## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

### **Fundamental Problem:**
This session represents a critical clinical error: the application of CBT to a patient with Major Neurocognitive Disorder due to Alzheimer's disease. CBT requires intact cognitive capacity for metacognition, thought monitoring, and behavioral experimentation. This patient has documented deficits in memory, attention, executive function, and visuospatial abilities, making CBT contraindicated and ineffective.

### **Key Deficiencies:**

1. **Diagnostic Misunderstanding:** Therapist treats presentation as primary depression amenable to cognitive restructuring, despite clinical evidence of Alzheimer's disease (progressive decline over 3 years, treatment resistance to two antidepressants, documented cognitive deficits).

2. **Inadequate Assessment:** No systematic cognitive assessment despite documented deficits. No exploration of three-year progressive decline. No involvement of caregiver (wife) in assessment or treatment planning.

3. **Inappropriate Interventions:** CBT techniques applied without adaptation for cognitive deficits. Techniques suggested rather than taught. No caregiver involvement or external supports.

4. **Unrealistic Homework:** Four separate tasks assigned in first session. Vague, unstructured, with no written materials or external reminders. No assessment of feasibility. Second session does not adequately address patient's report that "it's hard to keep it up."

5. **Lack of Genuine Collaboration:** Therapist-directed approach with patient in passive role. No shared decision-making or active patient participation in problem-solving.

### **Immediate Actions Required:**

1. **Reconsider diagnosis and treatment approach** in light of cognitive deficits, treatment resistance, and progressive decline.
2. **Conduct comprehensive cognitive assessment** to confirm extent and nature of cognitive decline.
3. **Involve caregiver (wife)** in assessment and treatment planning.
4. **Adjust treatment approach** to match cognitive capacity or consider alternative interventions (caregiver support, environmental modifications, neuropsychological evaluation, medication management).
5. **Establish realistic, measurable treatment goals** appropriate for progressive neurocognitive disorder.

### **Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit:**
**Low.** Patient is unlikely to benefit from current approach. Homework is unrealistic given cognitive deficits. Underlying treatment rationale is misaligned with diagnosis. Patient's second-session report ("it's hard to keep it up," "I don't really feel much better yet") indicates current approach is not working. Without significant changes, patient will likely experience continued symptoms, homework non-compliance, and demoralization.

### **Whether Session Qualifies as Evidence-Based CBT:**
**No.** While therapist uses some CBT techniques, they are applied inappropriately to a patient with Alzheimer's disease, for whom CBT is not evidence-based. Interventions are not adapted for cognitive deficits, caregiver is not involved, and homework is unrealistic. This approach is not evidence-based and is unlikely to help the patient.
