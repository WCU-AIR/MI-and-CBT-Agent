## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.4/5**

**Assessment:** This session demonstrates **poor Motivational Interviewing practice** and represents a significant departure from MI principles. The psychiatrist adopts a directive, expert-driven stance throughout, offering multiple specific recommendations without first exploring Marcus's own ideas, values, motivations, or preferences. This approach fundamentally undermines the collaborative partnership central to MI and is unlikely to sustain long-term motivation or behavior change.

**Key Strengths:**
- Basic engagement maintained; Marcus remains engaged throughout
- Respectful tone; not overtly hostile or dismissive
- Specific, manageable behavioral goal identified (reducing soda by one per day)
- Recognition that starting small is preferable to attempting large changes
- Clear session structure with beginning, middle, and end

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Shift from telling to asking:** Replace advice-giving with open-ended questions designed to draw out Marcus's own ideas and motivations
- **Build genuine partnership:** Seek Marcus's input first; co-create solutions; affirm his expertise about himself
- **Explore intrinsic motivation:** Ask questions connecting diabetes management to what matters most to Marcus—his values, relationships, and future
- **Validate emotional experience:** Reflect and explore Marcus's overwhelm, doubt, and anxiety rather than bypassing with solutions
- **Cultivate change talk strategically:** When Marcus offers change talk, pause and explore further to strengthen motivation
- **Reduce prescriptive language:** Eliminate phrases like "It would probably help if you..." and "You should..."; replace with autonomy-supportive language

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Client Motivation:**
**Unlikely.** While Marcus has agreed to a specific behavioral goal, this appears to be compliance with clinician's recommendation rather than genuine commitment rooted in his own values and reasons for change. Marcus's repeated expressions of overwhelm, doubt, and lack of confidence were not addressed or resolved; instead, they were met with prescriptive solutions, potentially reinforcing his sense that he cannot manage this independently. For sustained behavior change, Marcus needs to develop intrinsic motivation—a sense that managing his diabetes matters to him personally, that he has the ability to do it, and that he is in control. This session did not facilitate that development.

**Recommendation:** Focused training on MI principles and techniques is essential to improve this clinician's ability to evoke and sustain client motivation for change.
