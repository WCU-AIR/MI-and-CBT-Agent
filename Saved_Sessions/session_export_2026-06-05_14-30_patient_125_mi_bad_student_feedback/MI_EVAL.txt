## Summary of Clinical Findings

### Strengths
- **Maintains focus on target behavior:** The psychiatrist consistently keeps discussion centered on marijuana use without allowing the session to wander aimlessly.
- **One moment of collaborative exploration:** The statement "Maybe we can explore what 'using less' might look like for you and what benefits or challenges that could bring" demonstrates brief awareness of a more collaborative approach.
- **Occasional acknowledgment of client perspective:** Statements like "I appreciate you sharing that" and "It sounds like quitting completely feels overwhelming" show minimal attempt to recognize Sofia's viewpoint.

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Fundamental violation of MI spirit:** This session is characterized by confrontation, prescription, and control rather than partnership, evocation, and collaboration. The psychiatrist operates from an expert-driven, authoritarian stance that directly contradicts MI principles.
- **Absence of genuine empathy and acceptance:** The psychiatrist judges Sofia's perspective as excuse-making and communicates conditional regard based on compliance. There is no unconditional positive regard.
- **Failure to evoke change talk:** Rather than asking questions that help Sofia discover her own motivations for change, the psychiatrist tells her what those motivations should be. This is the inverse of MI practice.
- **Undermining of autonomy:** The psychiatrist repeatedly prescribes what Sofia "must" do and explicitly states that quitting is "not optional." This removes Sofia's sense of choice and ownership.
- **Confrontation of sustain talk:** Rather than skillfully softening Sofia's arguments for the status quo, the psychiatrist directly challenges them, which increases defensiveness and entrenchment.
- **Dismissal of tentative change talk:** When Sofia expresses willingness to consider using less, the psychiatrist dismisses this as insufficient and prescribes complete abstinence instead. This discourages rather than amplifies change talk.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Likelihood of Change
This session is likely to be counterproductive. Sofia enters with ambivalence—recognizing some problems with her use but also seeing benefits and feeling uncertain about change. The psychiatrist's confrontational, prescriptive approach will likely increase Sofia's defensiveness and entrenchment in sustain talk. Sofia is likely to feel judged, misunderstood, controlled rather than supported, and defensive. The likelihood of Sofia making meaningful change following this session is low. Without genuine internal motivation, sustained behavior change is unlikely.

---

## Recommendations for Clinical Improvement

**HIGHEST PRIORITY:** Shift from expert-driven, confrontational stance to collaborative partnership. Replace directive language ("You need to," "You have to," "You must") with collaborative language ("I'm wondering," "Help me understand," "What's your sense of").

**CRITICAL ACTIONS REQUIRED:**
1. Develop genuine empathy and acceptance through reflective listening without judgment
2. Master evocative questioning to help clients discover their own reasons for change
3. Amplify change talk rather than dismiss it as insufficient
4. Soften sustain talk through reflection and reframing, not confrontation
5. Consistently respect and support client autonomy
6. Explore and validate legitimate concerns rather than dismissing them
7. Engage in formal Motivational Interviewing training (MINT-certified program recommended)
8. Participate in ongoing clinical supervision with MI-competent supervisor

**Overall Recommendation:** The psychiatrist should engage in formal MI training and ongoing supervision before continuing to see clients for motivational interviewing. The current approach is counterproductive and potentially harmful to client outcomes.
