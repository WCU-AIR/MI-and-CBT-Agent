## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 1.2/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **critical failure in Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates fundamental misunderstanding of MI principles and spirit across all nine competency domains. The approach is directive, confrontational, and expert-driven—antithetical to evidence-based MI practice.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Repeated yes/no questions eliminate genuine dialogue
- Controlling language ("You have to," "That's not acceptable") undermines autonomy
- Dismissal of client's legitimate concerns about peer relationships
- Confrontation of resistance rather than strategic MI responses
- Failure to evoke client's intrinsic motivation
- No genuine partnership or collaborative problem-solving
- Missed opportunities to explore client's values, goals, and barriers

**Impact on Client:**
While client produces surface-level compliance statements, underlying ambivalence and resistance persist throughout. Psychiatrist has not helped client develop own reasons for change or confidence in ability to change. Therapeutic alliance is damaged, reducing likelihood of sustained behavior change.

**Recommendations:**
Formal MI training, deliberate practice with feedback, ongoing supervision, and fundamental shift from directive to collaborative stance are essential. Psychiatrist must develop genuine empathic understanding, learn to evoke rather than impose, and actively support client autonomy. Current approach is likely to decrease intrinsic motivation and increase defensiveness.
