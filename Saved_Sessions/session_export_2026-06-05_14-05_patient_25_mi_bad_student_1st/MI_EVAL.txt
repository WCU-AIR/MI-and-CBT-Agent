## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine competency domains. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, authoritarian, expert-driven approach directly contradictory to MI's core spirit of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation.

### Strengths
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol reduction)
- Explicitly communicates concern about health risks

### Major Deficiencies
1. **Complete absence of acceptance and empathy** – Judgmental language, dismissal of client perspective, negation of positive health behaviors
2. **Suppression of autonomy and collaboration** – Explicit rejection of negotiation, removal of choice, treatment of client as subordinate
3. **Imposition rather than evocation** – Tells rather than asks; client's intrinsic motivations never explored
4. **Confrontation of resistance** – Direct confrontation amplifies rather than softens resistance; client explicitly states pressure backfires
5. **Repetitive, rigid approach** – Same demand repeated 10+ times without strategic variation or responsiveness

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**This session is likely to DECREASE client motivation for change.** The confrontational, coercive approach triggers psychological reactance. Client explicitly demonstrates this: "pressure just makes me dig in my heels" and "being told it's non-negotiable just makes me feel boxed in." Research demonstrates this approach is counterproductive—clients experiencing coercion are less likely to change, more likely to drop out, and more likely to relapse.

### Recommendations for Immediate Remediation

1. **Foundational shift**: Adopt MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation) before next session
2. **Develop reflective listening**: Practice accurate empathic reflections; aim for 1:3 ratio of directives to collaborative questions
3. **Evoke rather than impose**: Replace directives with open-ended questions about values, goals, concerns, and resources
4. **Support autonomy**: Explicitly acknowledge client's right to choose; use collaborative language
5. **Cultivate change talk**: Amplify client's own motivation statements through follow-up questions
6. **Soften sustain talk**: Use double-sided reflections and reframing instead of confrontation
7. **Respond to client feedback**: When client states approach is not working, listen and adjust
8. **Formal MI training**: Complete refresher training or formal MI certification before conducting additional MI sessions

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change

**Very Low.** Without fundamental shift toward MI principles, this psychiatrist is unlikely to effectively engage clients in behavior change and may actively harm the therapeutic relationship.
