### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated through genuine invitation for Anya's input ("what would you like to focus on?"), seeking permission before offering psychoeducation ("Would it be okay if I shared some brief information?"), and co-constructing solutions. However, subtle shift toward clinician-directed problem-solving occurs in latter half of session. Once psychoeducation is introduced, conversation becomes more guided; psychiatrist gently steers toward timer solution rather than fully exploring what Anya might naturally generate.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Anya's right to choose and emphasizes her agency throughout. Uses exploratory framing rather than prescriptive language. Explicitly asks permission before offering guidance. However, psychoeducation about breaks, while presented as optional, is framed as evidence-based guidance that implicitly suggests this is the "right" approach. Does not explicitly acknowledge that Anya might choose to reject or significantly modify suggestions without subtle pressure toward compliance.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation of Anya's own motivations and past coping strategies present, but inconsistent and relatively surface-level. Asks about reasons for doing well in school and past stress management techniques. However, lacks deeper evocation of intrinsic values, identity, and self-discovery. Does not ask what Anya truly wants beyond parental expectations or what matters most to her. Moves relatively quickly to offering psychoeducation rather than asking Anya to generate her own ideas about solutions.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally collaborative stance present in early session but gradually shifts toward expert-driven role as session progresses. Early agenda-setting is strong; however, introduction of psychoeducation positions psychiatrist as expert with knowledge about what will help. Statements like "I'm really impressed with how you're thinking this through" affirm Anya but subtly position clinician as evaluator. Power dynamic gradually becomes less equal; opportunity exists to maintain more consistent co-creation and mutual exploration throughout.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. While Anya generates some change talk naturally ("I think I could try that, yeah," "I'm feeling pretty good about giving it a shot"), psychiatrist does not strategically evoke, amplify, or reinforce this language. No systematic effort to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. When Anya demonstrates confidence (8/10), psychiatrist acknowledges but does not deepen or build on it. Missed opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation through intentional cultivation of change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist skillfully handles minimal sustain talk present through double-sided reflections and validation rather than confrontation. When Anya expresses concern about having to "get stuff done," clinician validates both desire for relief and responsibility without reinforcing either/or thinking. Avoids challenging hesitations and maintains collaborative stance. However, limited sustain talk in this session restricts full evaluation of this competency. Skill demonstrated but not extensively tested.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current Competency Level:** Intermediate MI competence with solid foundational skills but significant gaps in strategic technique application.

**Primary Strengths:**
- Warm, respectful, affirming tone with consistent unconditional positive regard
- Strong early agenda-setting with genuine invitation for client input
- Solid reflective listening capturing content and emotional tone
- Collaborative problem-solving resulting in specific, doable action step
- Skillful handling of sustain talk through validation and double-sided reflection
- Consistent affirmation of autonomy and agency

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Significant drift from referral context:** Alcohol screening referral never addressed; substance use, peer influences, and risk factors unexplored
- **Minimal strategic cultivation of change talk:** Core MI competency largely absent despite natural change talk from client
- **Shallow evocation of intrinsic motivation:** Session focuses on practical strategies without exploring deeper values, identity, or purpose
- **Gradual shift to expert-driven stance:** Power dynamic becomes less equal as session progresses
- **Limited depth of empathy:** Reflections remain surface-level without capturing deeper emotional nuance

**Session Outcome:** Successfully engages Anya with concrete action plan (8/10 confidence), but clinical impact limited by failure to address referral question and reliance on externally guided rather than intrinsically generated motivation.

**Overall Session Score: 3.4/5**

## Recommended Priority Actions

1. **Return to and address the alcohol screening referral** in next session using curious, nonjudgmental exploration
2. **Strategically cultivate change talk** by intentionally evoking and amplifying client's own language about motivation and commitment
3. **Deepen empathy** by exploring underlying emotions (perfectionism, anxiety, fear) rather than surface-level content
4. **Maintain equal partnership** by positioning client as expert on their own experience
5. **Evoke intrinsic motivation and values** before introducing problem-solving strategies
