| **Total Score (Sum of Items 1–11)** | **8/66** | **CRITICAL DEFICIENCIES IDENTIFIED** This evaluation represents a fundamental failure to deliver evidence-based cognitive-behavioral therapy. Therapist scored 0/6 on six of eleven criteria (Agenda, Guided Discovery, Focusing on Key Cognitions/Behaviors, Strategy for Change, Application of CBT Techniques, Homework), indicating complete absence of essential CBT components. Therapist scored 1-2/6 on remaining criteria, indicating minimal or inadequate performance. **Strengths:** Therapist displayed basic warmth, did not behave in hostile or demeaning manner, allowed patient to speak, acknowledged statements with simple reflections, maintained non-judgmental tone. These foundational skills prevent harm but are insufficient for effective treatment. **Critical Deficiencies:** (1) **Structural/Strategic Failures:** No agenda established; sessions entirely unstructured and reactive; no coherent treatment strategy or conceptualization; no systematic assessment of cognitions, behaviors, or maintaining factors; no integration of cultural context, grief, or spiritual beliefs. (2) **CBT-Specific Failures:** Zero application of evidence-based CBT techniques (no cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, thought records, behavioral experiments); no guided discovery or Socratic questioning; no homework assigned or reviewed; no focus on key cognitions (worthlessness, burden beliefs, hopelessness) or behaviors (avoidance, inactivity, isolation). (3) **Therapeutic Process Failures:** Minimal feedback elicitation; minimal collaboration; repeated ineffective interventions without adjustment when patient reported they didn't work; inappropriate self-disclosure centering therapist's experience; dismissive normalization minimizing patient's suffering. (4) **Cultural/Clinical Sensitivity Failures:** Complete failure to acknowledge or integrate Hmong cultural background, refugee trauma, or spiritual practices; no exploration of grief related to husband's death; no recognition that somatic symptoms (fatigue, pain, insomnia) are culturally-congruent expressions of depression in Hmong populations; no adaptation of language, pace, or approach for patient with limited English proficiency and functional illiteracy; repeated generic advice ignoring patient's stated barriers and complexity. **Impact on Patient Care:** This approach is unlikely to produce therapeutic change and may harm patient. Ka Fang will leave treatment without understanding depression, how it develops, or how to change it. She will not have learned skills to challenge thoughts about worthlessness or burden, increase activity despite pain, or process grief. She will not have structured plan for between-session work. Most importantly, she will have received message that her complex, culturally-embedded suffering can be addressed with generic advice and positive thinking—a message that, when it fails, may reinforce hopelessness and beliefs about inability to change. For vulnerable patient with limited insight, cultural displacement, grief, and somatic depression, this represents missed opportunity for meaningful intervention and potential reinforcement of belief that nothing will help. **RECOMMENDATION:** This session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT and is unlikely to produce therapeutic change. **Immediate, intensive training and supervision required before therapist continues treating patients.** |

## SUPERVISOR RECOMMENDATIONS FOR REMEDIATION

### Priority 1: Immediate Training Required
- **CBT Fundamentals:** Enroll in accredited CBT training program (e.g., Beck Institute, ABCT)
- **Agenda-Setting:** Practice establishing collaborative agendas in every session
- **Guided Discovery:** Study and practice Socratic questioning techniques
- **Case Conceptualization:** Learn to develop cognitive-behavioral case formulations

### Priority 2: Cultural Competency Training
- **Hmong Mental Health:** Consult resources on Hmong culture, refugee trauma, and mental health
- **Somatic Presentations:** Learn how depression presents somatically in non-Western cultures
- **Spiritual Integration:** Develop skills to incorporate patient's spiritual practices into treatment

### Priority 3: Supervision Structure
- **Weekly Supervision:** Engage in weekly supervision with CBT-trained supervisor
- **Session Recording:** Record and review sessions with supervisor for feedback
- **Skill Practice:** Role-play CBT interventions with supervisor before applying with patients

### Priority 4: Specific Skill Development
- Behavioral activation planning
- Cognitive restructuring and thought records
- Sleep assessment and intervention
- Grief processing and meaning-making
- Homework assignment and review
