## Summary Assessment

**Fundamental Deficiency:** This session represents a **complete departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist demonstrates a confrontational, expert-driven, coercive approach directly contradicted by MI evidence and theory.

**Critical Failures:**
- Complete absence of acceptance and empathy
- Coercive rather than collaborative approach
- Failure to evoke change talk; active suppression of client motivation
- Reinforcement of sustain talk through confrontation
- Misalignment with client's stage of change (contemplation)
- Damage to therapeutic relationship and trust

**Likely Impact:** This session is counterproductive. Research shows confrontational, expert-driven approaches decrease intrinsic motivation and increase dropout rates. Tyler is likely to experience increased psychological reactance, decreased intrinsic motivation, damaged therapeutic relationship, and increased dropout risk. His final statement ("I'm just not ready for that yet") suggests he is becoming more entrenched in his position—evidence the confrontational approach is backfiring.

**MI Competency Level:** Minimal. While target behavior focus is maintained (after initial confusion), psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support, evocation, partnership, cultivation of change talk, or skillful handling of sustain talk.

**Critical Recommendation:** Seek formal Motivational Interviewing training and clinical supervision before continuing substance use treatment. Current approach, while well-intentioned, is likely to harm rather than help clients.
