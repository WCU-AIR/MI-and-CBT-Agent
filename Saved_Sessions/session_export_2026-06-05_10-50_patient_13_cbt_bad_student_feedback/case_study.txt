--- DISORDER ---
Avoidant Personality Disorder; Social Anxiety Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Mathilda Herbert was a 23-year-old woman referred for psychiatric consultation to help her "break out of her shell."
She had recently moved to a new city to take classes to become an industrial lab technician and moved in with an older cousin, a psychotherapist, who encouraged her to "get out and enjoy her youth."

Although previously prescribed medications for anxiety, Ms. Herbert described her primary problem as "shyness."
She found school difficult due to constant perceived criticism. She avoided being called on in class, worried she would "say something stupid" and blush, fearing ridicule. She avoided speaking on telephones and dreaded public speaking.

Her social interactions were similarly restricted. She described herself as a people pleaser, hiding her feelings behind a cheerful, compliant demeanor. She had a few "warm and lifelong" friends but felt lonely since her recent move. She had not dated since a past negative relationship with an "angry alcoholic" boyfriend two years earlier.

Ms. Herbert grew up in a metropolitan area with her parents and three older siblings. She described her home environment as competitive and critical, with a hyperactive brother and perfectionist sisters. Her mother was anxiously compliant like her, and her father was highly successful and often critical. Her parents divorced in her senior year of high school, and she attended a local community college to reduce competition and support her mother.

Strengths included excellent academic performance in chemistry, mastery of outdoor skills from family camping trips, and enjoyment of volunteering and babysitting.

During evaluation, Ms. Herbert was well-dressed, of short stature, attentive, coherent, and goal-directed. She smiled frequently, especially when discussing topics that would frustrate most people. She became visibly upset when the psychiatrist linked her anxiety to her father, indicating sensitivity to perceived criticism. Explicit discussion of these concerns improved the flow of the interview.

Discussion: Ms. Herbert's shyness manifests as persistent social avoidance, reduced academic performance, limited social connections, and difficulty dating. She meets criteria for avoidant personality disorder (persistent social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy, hypersensitivity to negative evaluation) and social anxiety disorder (fear of social scrutiny, enduring but anxious participation in social situations). Symptoms have been present from a young age and have negatively impacted multiple aspects of her life.
