### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk rather than skillfully softening it, which strengthens client's resistance. Client's sustain talk includes: "It's not a lot though," "It's not like I was gonna sell it," "It's California, no one cares," "I don't really think it's a big deal," "It's not like I'm hurting anyone," "I just don't see why I gotta stop now," "I'm not sure I totally buy into all the health scare stuff." Psychiatrist's response to each is direct argument: "Even occasional use is harmful," lectures about health risks, fear-based consequences. This violates MI principle; research shows direct argumentation against sustain talk strengthens psychological reactance. Skillful softening would involve double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic responses. Instead, psychiatrist argues directly, predictably reinforcing client's defenses. By session end, client is more entrenched in position, complying only to escape session. Direct confrontation of sustain talk is fundamental MI error.

## **OVERALL CLINICAL SUMMARY**

### **Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

---

### **Strengths**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (marijuana use) throughout session
- Provides concrete behavioral recommendations (avoid triggers, stay busy, sleep, eat well)
- Demonstrates some structural organization of treatment plan

---

### **Critical Deficiencies**

**This session represents a fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing practice.**

1. **Authoritarian stance replacing MI spirit:** Psychiatrist explicitly states "I'll be telling you exactly what steps you should take" and "This isn't about what you think; it's about what you have to do." Fundamentally incompatible with MI principles.

2. **Confrontation rather than collaboration:** Repeated confrontational statements ("That's the problem right there," "You're missing the point," "You're just making excuses," "Why are you being so stubborn?") predictably strengthen resistance rather than evoking motivation.

3. **Absence of reflective listening:** Virtually no reflective listening demonstrates understanding or explores client's perspective. Damages rapport and prevents genuine exploration.

4. **Imposition rather than evocation:** Lectures about consequences (lung damage, brain damage, future risk) without exploring what matters to client or evoking his own reasons for change.

5. **Coercive language undermining autonomy:** "You have to" appears 17+ times, creating coercive environment that produces only external compliance, not intrinsic motivation.

6. **Strengthening of resistance through direct confrontation:** Each time client minimizes or justifies use, psychiatrist argues against it, strengthening psychological reactance and defensive resistance.

7. **Failure to understand client's world:** Client explicitly states "I'm not sure you get what it's like for me." No genuine effort to understand peer context, values, or perspective on marijuana use in California.

---

### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change**

**This session is likely to DECREASE intrinsic motivation and INCREASE resistance.**

- **No genuine behavior change likely:** Client demonstrates surface compliance ("Yeah, yeah, I got it") but explicitly states "I'm not sure I totally buy into all the health scare stuff, but whatever. I'll quit if that's what I gotta do." Compliance is to escape session, not from developed internal motivation.

- **Increased defensiveness:** Client becomes more defensive as session progresses, moving from minimization to explicit rejection of psychiatrist's framing ("I'm not stupid," "You're acting like I'm some kind of addict").

- **Damaged therapeutic relationship:** Psychiatrist has failed to establish rapport or understanding, undermining any future therapeutic work.

- **Psychological reactance:** Confrontational approach triggers resistance. Client becomes more entrenched in position rather than moving toward change.

- **Temporary compliance without commitment:** Any behavioral change will be externally motivated and likely to revert once external pressure (juvenile justice system) is removed.

---

### **Critical Conclusion**

**This session receives 1.1/5 because it represents fundamental rejection of Motivational Interviewing principles.** Psychiatrist explicitly adopts authoritarian, expert-driven stance directly contrary to MI spirit. Session is characterized by confrontation, coercion, imposition of external reasons for change, and absence of reflective listening or genuine partnership.

**Most concerning:** This approach is likely to be **counterproductive.** Research on MI consistently shows that confrontation, direct argumentation against client perspectives, and coercive language strengthen resistance and decrease intrinsic motivation.

**Immediate action required:** Psychiatrist needs formal training in Motivational Interviewing and clinical supervision to fundamentally shift approach from authoritarian direction to collaborative partnership. Without this change, sessions with resistant clients—particularly adolescents—will continue to be ineffective and potentially harmful.
