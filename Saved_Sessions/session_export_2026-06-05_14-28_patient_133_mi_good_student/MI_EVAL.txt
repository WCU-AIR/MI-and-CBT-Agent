# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Validates Yasmin's experience ("It sounds like you're carrying a lot of tension") and normalizes her struggle. However, could deepen acceptance by more explicitly affirming her ambivalence about weight loss as legitimate rather than something to move past. Praise-giving ("I'm really impressed") borders on performance-based affirmation rather than pure acceptance of inherent worth.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening with several complex, accurate reflections capturing Yasmin's ambivalence and emotional experience ("It's like you're standing at a crossroads..."). Good emotional attunement to her fears about exercise and perfectionism. However, misses opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance—particularly the shame and cultural pressures underlying weight loss desire, and the fear of failure beneath her worry about slipping into old habits.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, strategic focus on sustainable, non-diet approach to health and body dissatisfaction. Session consistently returns to this target with logical progression from problem exploration to past successes to concrete action steps. Scaling questions effectively assess readiness. However, direction could be sharpened by more explicitly clarifying what "non-diet approach" means in practical terms and establishing clearer markers of progress beyond the scaling question.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaborative stance with frequent invitations for Yasmin's input ("What would you like to focus on?", "What do you think might be the most important part?"). Genuinely incorporates her responses and affirms her proposed check-in journal idea. However, collaboration could be deepened by more explicitly positioning Yasmin as the expert on her own change process and co-constructing definitions rather than affirming pre-existing frameworks.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and supports Yasmin's autonomy with language emphasizing choice and client ownership ("It's okay to take your time there"). Does not prescribe solutions or pressure toward change. However, could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her agency in moments of potential setback and by neutrally supporting her right to pursue or not pursue weight loss, rather than implicitly supporting a shift away from it.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. While Yasmin produces genuine change talk throughout ("I want something more sustainable," "when I focus on enjoyment rather than goals, I stick with it longer"), psychiatrist primarily reflects rather than amplifies or evokes more. Relies heavily on telling rather than asking. Misses opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation through questions like "What would it feel like to wake up without this preoccupation?" Does not systematically explore desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change. Insufficient amplification of her powerful insights about enjoyment vs. goals.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial partnership with collaborative opening but increasingly adopts expert/guide role through interpretation, analysis, and affirmation. Statements like "I'm really impressed by your thoughtful approach" and "Your awareness...shows real readiness" represent clinician's evaluation rather than co-exploration. True partnership would involve more positioning of Yasmin as expert on her own experience and more explicit invitation into collaborative problem-solving about how to work together.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Major gap in MI practice. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal strategic cultivation of change talk. While Yasmin produces multiple instances of genuine change talk, psychiatrist reflects rather than amplifies or reinforces. Does not systematically use strategic questioning to evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. Misses opportunities to ask follow-up questions deepening her insights. Does not strengthen commitment talk when she rates confidence at 7/10. Session relies on clinician's affirmations rather than evoking client's own language and reasoning for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic skill in avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk but does not consistently or strategically soften it. When Yasmin expresses doubt ("worried I might forget or slip") or lingering weight loss desires, psychiatrist normalizes concerns rather than using strategic techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing. Responds with reassurance rather than strategic exploration. Does not help her integrate ambivalence or move toward change talk. Sustain talk is primarily validated rather than strategically redirected.

## **Overall Session Score: 3.4/5**

---

### Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Consistent acceptance, warmth, and genuine respect creating psychological safety
- Good reflective listening with accurate complex reflections
- Strong collaborative opening establishing partnership
- Practical, manageable goal-setting with concrete first step
- Appropriate use of scaling questions
- Effective drawing out of past success (walking example)

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient evocation of change talk** – Most significant gap; relies on reflection rather than strategic amplification
- **Underutilization of strategic questioning** – Lacks systematic exploration of intrinsic motivation and values
- **Expert role creeping in** – Increasingly interprets and evaluates rather than positioning client as expert
- **Minimal amplification of change talk** – Does not deepen or extend client's own insights
- **Inadequate softening of sustain talk** – Normalizes rather than strategically redirects
- **Lack of framework clarification** – Does not collaboratively define "non-diet approach" in practical terms
- **Limited exploration of ambivalence** – Misses opportunity to help integrate conflicting desires

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Yasmin leaves with reasonable clarity and a 7/10 confidence rating, suggesting adequate engagement. However, motivation is somewhat externally supported (by clinician affirmations and concrete plan) rather than deeply rooted in intrinsic values and self-discovery. Risk of insufficient internal foundation when encountering obstacles or doubt.

**Recommendation:**
Clinician demonstrates adequate foundational MI skills but significant gaps in advanced evocation techniques. Focused practice on strategic questioning to cultivate change talk, amplification of client insights, and strategic softening of sustain talk is essential for developing full MI competence.
