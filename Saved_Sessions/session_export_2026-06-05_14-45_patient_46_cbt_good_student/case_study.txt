--- DISORDER ---
Histrionic Personality Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Karmen Fuentes, a 50-year-old married Hispanic woman, presented to the psychiatric emergency room (ER) at the urging of her outpatient psychiatrist after reporting thoughts of overdosing on Advil.

She explained that her back had been painful since a recent fall at the family-owned grocery store where she worked. The fall left her feeling downcast and upset, although she denied other depressive symptoms aside from a low mood. She spoke extensively about the fall and a prior fall years earlier, expressing feelings of abandonment by a neurosurgeon and frustration over diminished ability to exercise and weight gain.

When discussing suicidal comments, she described them as threats directed at her husband to “teach him a lesson” for perceived lack of compassion. She minimized the seriousness of her statements and laughed when the interviewer expressed concern. She was friendly, flirtatious with the male interviewer, and consistently acknowledged staff by name, maintaining attention to her appearance.

Her outpatient psychiatrist reported no prior suicidal ideation in the past three years of treatment, and her husband stated that her suicidal comments were habitual attempts to worry him rather than genuine intent. Ms. Fuentes had received trials of multiple antidepressants (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, paroxetine) without benefit.

She described being sexually active with older men in high school, finding dating enjoyable, and emphasized the importance of her appearance and grooming throughout her life. She lived with her 73-year-old husband; her 25-year-old son lived nearby with his family. She reported a history of being “unsupported” by her husband and expressed a need for attention and care.

Discussion: Ms. Fuentes demonstrates excessive emotionality and attention-seeking behavior, with rapidly shifting moods, theatricality, seductive behavior, self-dramatization, and use of physical appearance to gain attention. Her suicidal gestures serve primarily to elicit concern rather than reflect major depressive disorder. She meets at least six of the eight symptomatic criteria for DSM-5 histrionic personality disorder (HPD). No clear evidence of comorbid personality disorder was noted.

Diagnosis: Histrionic Personality Disorder
