### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Explicitly rejected from session opening. Psychiatrist states "I'll be telling you exactly what steps you must take," establishing hierarchical expert-authority relationship rather than partnership between equals. Operates from assumption that clinician possesses all expertise and wisdom; patient is passive recipient of directives. ## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.3/5**

### Summary of Findings:

This session represents a **fundamental violation of Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist operates from an expert-authority, controlling stance that treats the patient's legitimate questions and concerns as obstacles to compliance rather than as expressions of her own values and wisdom about her health.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Explicit rejection of MI spirit and collaborative partnership
- Dismissal of legitimate safety concerns (drug interaction)
- Failure to recognize and amplify substantial patient change talk
- Controlling language and pressure tactics throughout
- Implicit questioning of patient integrity and trustworthiness
- Rejection of patient's autonomous contributions
- Complete absence of reflective listening
- Exploration of ambivalence replaced by pressure for compliance

**Strengths (Limited):**
- Critical safety information eventually provided (serotonin syndrome risk)
- Clear directive toward appropriate action (contact prescriber)
- Patient ultimately commits to action despite poor MI practice

### Impact on Therapeutic Alliance and Patient Engagement:

While patient ultimately agrees to call doctor (suggesting resilience in intrinsic motivation), this session is likely to **damage long-term engagement with mental health treatment**. The psychiatrist's controlling, dismissive stance sends clear message: your questions are not welcome, your concerns are not valid, your autonomy is not respected. This will reduce patient's willingness to raise concerns in future appointments, ask questions about treatment, or engage authentically with care.

**The Irony:** Patient demonstrates exactly the kind of engaged, responsible, safety-conscious behavior clinicians should cultivate. A skilled MI clinician would recognize these as strengths and amplify them. Instead, this psychiatrist treats them as obstacles.

**Session Outcome:** Achieves short-term compliance but likely damages long-term engagement and trust. Patient may follow through on this specific action but is less likely to return for future appointments, be honest about concerns, or engage authentically with treatment.

### Urgent Recommendation:

**This clinician requires immediate, intensive training in Motivational Interviewing principles and practice.** The current approach is not only ineffective—it is actively harmful to the therapeutic alliance and patient engagement. Without significant change, this clinician's patients are likely to disengage from treatment, become less honest about their concerns, and have worse clinical outcomes.
