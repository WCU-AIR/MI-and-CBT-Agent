## Summary Assessment

**Fundamental Deficiencies:**

This session demonstrates critical departures from motivational interviewing principles. While the psychiatrist demonstrates basic courtesy and maintains focus on smoking as target behavior, the overall approach is characterized by **advice-giving, prescriptive language, and expert-driven problem-solving** rather than collaborative exploration and evocation that define MI.

**Critical Gaps:**

- **Minimal evocation:** Advice-giving replaces the core MI skill of drawing out client's own motivations, values, and resources
- **Poor handling of ambivalence:** Rosa's profound internal conflict is treated as obstacle to overcome through planning rather than core material to explore
- **Conditional acceptance and autonomy support:** Framing communicates "You're not ready enough yet" rather than unconditional support
- **Shallow empathy:** Factual reflections lack emotional depth and miss opportunities to capture meaning and values
- **Minimal partnership:** Unilateral decision-making and expert role undermine genuine power-sharing
- **Inadequate change talk amplification:** Multiple instances of client motivation missed or minimized
- **Inadequate sustain talk softening:** Significant barriers and fears largely unaddressed without strategic reframing

**Impact on Client:**

While Rosa leaves with concrete plan (cut back this week), this plan is psychiatrist's idea, not Rosa's own discovery. Research demonstrates client-generated plans are significantly more likely to succeed. Failure to evoke and amplify change talk means session does not strengthen intrinsic motivation. Conditional framing may reinforce self-doubt. Lack of genuine partnership reduces Rosa's sense of ownership and autonomy—both critical predictors of sustained behavior change.

**Highest Priorities for Improvement:**

1. Eliminate advice-giving; replace with open-ended questions that evoke client's own ideas
2. Systematically amplify change talk through deep exploration
3. Explore and honor ambivalence rather than bypass it
4. Build genuine partnership and unconditional autonomy support
5. Deepen empathy through complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and values

**Conclusion:**

This psychiatrist is not practicing motivational interviewing despite using MI language. Fundamental reorientation required: from telling to asking, from prescribing to evoking, from expert role to partnership, from installing motivation to drawing it out.
