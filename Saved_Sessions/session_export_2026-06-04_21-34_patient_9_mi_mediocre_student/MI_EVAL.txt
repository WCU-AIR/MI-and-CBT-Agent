# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal acceptance demonstrated. While opening is warm, clinician quickly pivots to problem-focused questioning without establishing genuine unconditional positive regard. Repeatedly prescribes solutions rather than affirming client autonomy. Offers conditional approval contingent on compliance with clinician's agenda rather than communicating acceptance of the client as a person. Fails to normalize ambivalence or validate client's struggle as understandable.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level reflective listening only. Basic factual reflections capture content but miss emotional complexity and nuance. Does not explore deeper feelings when client states "I'm kind of stuck." Relies on closed, problem-focused questions rather than open-ended exploratory inquiry. No evidence of stepping into client's world or understanding perspective from inside. Fails to use complex reflections that capture both sides of ambivalence simultaneously.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on reducing phone use, particularly nighttime usage. Session does not wander and maintains consistent thread. However, direction is clinician-driven rather than collaboratively developed. Clinician predetermines target behavior without first exploring client's own goals and values. Does not ask what client wants to work on or what would be most important to her.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Severely limited collaboration. Clinician adopts expert stance, diagnosing problems and prescribing solutions. Leading questions presuppose clinician already knows what would help. Uses "you should" and "you might want to" language framing recommendations as clinician's ideas, not joint decisions. When client expresses hesitation, clinician overrides concern rather than exploring collaboratively. True partnership absent throughout session.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually absent. Clinician repeatedly pressures client toward change while minimizing expressed hesitations. Uses "but" statements that negate client concerns and push toward clinician's preferred direction. Frames compliance as expected outcome rather than genuinely supporting client's right to choose. Assumes client will attempt intervention and will need backup strategies if she fails. No acknowledgment that client might validly choose not to change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal evocation of client's own motivations, values, and reasons for change. When client provides change talk (grades slipping, sleep problems), clinician does not amplify or explore. Asks scaling questions but does not explore why numbers are low or what would increase them. Never asks about client's values, hopes, or what she wants her life to look like. Immediately pivots to problem-solving when ambivalence emerges, prematurely foreclosing exploration of intrinsic motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Fundamentally absent. Clinician functions as expert authority rather than equal collaborator. Adopts hierarchical stance positioning self as authority who knows what client should do. Client's contributions do not substantially influence session direction or content. Expertise and wisdom about change positioned with clinician rather than with client. No genuine invitation of client's expertise about her own life.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Multiple instances of change talk from client (grades slipping, sleep problems, thoughts about setting limits, recognizing need to cut back) are largely unrecognized and unamplified. When client mentions grades slipping, clinician does not explore further. When client rates importance at 5, clinician does not explore what would move it higher. When client says "part of me thinks it'd be good to cut back," clinician does not explore this part. Clinician focuses on barriers and prescriptions rather than strategically evoking more change language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Poor skill in handling sustain talk. Clinician either ignores sustain talk or responds in ways that may reinforce it. Uses "but" statements that directly contradict client concerns rather than honoring them. When client expresses worry about frustration and missing out, clinician dismisses with "but maybe you should still give it a shot." Does not use reframing, ask-tell-ask technique, or other MI strategies to gently explore and redirect sustain talk. Overrides concerns with prescriptive advice rather than exploring collaboratively.

## Summary Assessment

### Session Embodies MI Spirit?
**No.** This session does not embody the spirit of Motivational Interviewing. The spirit of MI is partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. This session demonstrates hierarchy, conditional approval, dismissal of concerns, and prescription.

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation?
**Low.** Client has agreed to try intervention, but language suggests compliance rather than commitment ("I'm not promising it'll make a big difference"). Client expresses worry about failure, skepticism about effectiveness, and concern about missing out. These are not markers of intrinsic motivation. If client fails to maintain intervention, she may interpret as personal failure and become more entrenched in ambivalence.

### Critical Deficiencies
1. **Fundamental MI spirit violation:** Functions as expert prescriber rather than collaborative partner
2. **Absence of autonomy support:** Pressures client toward change; overrides concerns
3. **Weak evocation:** Fails to draw out client's own reasons for change
4. **Poor ambivalence exploration:** Immediately pivots to problem-solving rather than exploring both sides
5. **Minimal empathy:** Surface-level listening; fails to capture emotional complexity

### Urgent Recommendations
- Abandon expert role; adopt genuinely collaborative stance
- Support autonomy by honoring client concerns rather than overriding them
- Evoke and amplify change talk rather than providing advice
- Explore ambivalence thoroughly before action planning
- Develop deeper empathic understanding through complex reflections
- Seek formal MI training and supervision before continuing MI practice

---

**Clinical Recommendation:** This clinician requires immediate Motivational Interviewing training and supervision. Current practice does not meet MI fidelity standards and may undermine client motivation for change.
