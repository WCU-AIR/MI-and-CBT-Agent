## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Quality Assessment:** Poor (0.8/6 average)

**Key Findings:**
- Complete absence of CBT structure and technique
- Advice-giving without collaboration or guided discovery
- Superficial understanding of clinical presentation
- Inappropriate self-disclosure and boundary violations
- No functional analysis or treatment strategy
- Inadequate homework implementation

**Immediate Supervision Priorities:**
1. Establish CBT session structure (agenda, guided discovery, homework, feedback)
2. Conduct functional analysis of patient's opioid use and depression
3. Implement evidence-based CBT techniques (behavioral activation, cognitive restructuring, relapse prevention)
4. Eliminate self-disclosure; maintain professional boundaries
5. Develop specific, measurable treatment plan

**Recommended Training:**
- Formal CBT training program (Beck Institute or ABCT)
- Weekly supervision with CBT expert
- Session recording and review with feedback
- Role-play practice of CBT techniques

**Clinical Risk:** Moderate. While not harmful, current approach is ineffective for opioid use disorder and depression. Patient at risk for continued substance use and worsening depression without evidence-based intervention.
