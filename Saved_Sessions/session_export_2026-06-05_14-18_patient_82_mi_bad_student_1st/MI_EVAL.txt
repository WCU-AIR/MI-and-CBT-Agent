## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

**MI Competency Level:** Not practicing Motivational Interviewing. Approach is directive, judgmental, and confrontational—fundamentally anti-MI.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance and empathy; explicit judgment and shame-inducing language
- Authoritarian stance replacing genuine collaboration and partnership
- Systematic violation of client autonomy through demands and removal of choice
- Failure to evoke or amplify change talk; suppression of client motivation
- Confrontational approach to sustain talk that strengthens rather than softens resistance
- Failure to work with client's stated priorities; imposition of psychiatrist's agenda

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is actively counterproductive. Psychiatrist's coercive approach triggers psychological reactance. Client explicitly states: "being told it's non-negotiable and I have to stop right now makes me want to push back even more." Therapeutic relationship damaged. Likelihood of client returning for treatment or engaging in genuine change work significantly reduced.

**Immediate Action Required:**
This psychiatrist requires formal MI training and ongoing clinical supervision before continuing to work with clients using this approach. Current practice is contraindicated by research and violates ethical principle of beneficence. Coercive, confrontational approaches to substance use treatment increase dropout and treatment failure.

**Recommended Priority Interventions:**
1. Fundamental shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance
2. Development of genuine empathic listening and reflective responses
3. Elimination of shame-inducing, judgmental language
4. Learning to evoke rather than impose motivation for change
5. Formal MI training through MINT-certified program with ongoing supervision
