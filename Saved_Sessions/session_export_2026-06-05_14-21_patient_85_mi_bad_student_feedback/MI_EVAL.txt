# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates solid acceptance throughout most of the session with warm, non-judgmental reflections and explicit validation of Malik's perspective. Opening question ("I want to hear from you first") communicates respect for his viewpoint. However, the initial directive exchange ("You really need to take this seriously," "You're just making excuses") represents a temporary lapse in unconditional positive regard. Recovery is evident in later exchanges with improved tone and language. No labeling or shaming language is used in the corrected portion.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong empathic understanding through accurate reflective listening, particularly the complex reflection capturing Malik's core conflict: "It sounds like your friendships are really important to you, and that your mom's concerns feel like pressure. It's understandable that you're feeling stuck between these two." Demonstrates perspective-taking about adolescent social dynamics. However, missed opportunities exist to deepen empathic exploration—could have explored emotional weight of conflict more fully when Malik mentioned making his mom mad. Empathy is accurate but somewhat surface-level in places.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains general focus on substance use and family conflict but lacks strategic clarity about the ultimate goal. Direction is somewhat diffuse—unclear whether goal is abstinence, harm reduction, or improved communication. By final exchange, direction emerges toward reduced/selective use, but this is implicit rather than collaboratively established. Psychiatrist never explicitly clarifies direction with Malik or checks alignment with his values. Direction emerges clinician-guided rather than truly client-centered.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaborative stance through genuine curiosity and equal positioning. Opening question explicitly positions Malik as expert on his own experience. Consistent use of collaborative language ("Let me ask you," "I'm curious," "Thanks for sharing") maintains exploratory stance. When Malik expresses uncertainty, psychiatrist asks "What are some small steps you might consider?" preserving his agency. Minor gap: agenda and goals are not explicitly co-constructed with Malik. Earlier directive exchange represents significant departure from collaboration.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist consistently supports Malik's autonomy through exploratory language ("you might consider") and acceptance of his timeline ("I'm not ready to just stop right now"). Validates his concern about peer perception without dismissing it as invalid. Does not push back when Malik expresses hesitation. However, could be strengthened by explicitly affirming Malik's right to make his own decisions and acknowledging that he might choose not to change. Earlier directive exchange ("You need to stop," "Just quit now") directly violates autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist asks some exploratory questions but inconsistently evokes Malik's intrinsic motivation and values. Misses critical opportunities—when Malik says "sometimes it does make my mom mad," could have explored this discrepancy more deeply. When Malik produces emerging change talk ("I guess I could try..."), psychiatrist doesn't amplify or deepen it. Does not systematically explore Malik's values beyond peer acceptance. Earlier directive exchange ("Why didn't you tell your mom you'd quit already?") is anti-evocative, imposing reasons rather than drawing them out.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong partnership by positioning Malik as expert on his experience, demonstrating genuine curiosity, and inviting collaborative problem-solving. Accepts Malik's timeline and works within his readiness, respecting his wisdom about himself. Uses exploratory questions rather than prescriptive advice. However, partnership could be strengthened by explicitly acknowledging Malik's insights and wisdom, and by inviting him to co-construct session agenda and goals. Earlier directive exchange represents significant departure from partnership stance.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is a significant weakness. Malik produces emerging change talk ("I guess I could try hanging out without always using weed," "Maybe if I picked a couple times..."), but psychiatrist does not capitalize on these moments. When change talk emerges, psychiatrist asks follow-up questions without amplifying or deepening it. Misses opportunities to evoke change talk earlier—when Malik acknowledges conflict with mother, could have explored values-based reasons for change. Does not systematically explore Malik's ability to change. Minimal reinforcement or celebration of change talk. Critical gap in MI technique.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist appropriately avoids confronting or arguing against Malik's sustain talk ("They don't think it's a problem," "I don't want them thinking I'm weak," "I'm not ready to quit"). Uses reflective listening and exploration rather than direct challenge. However, misses opportunities to strategically soften sustain talk through double-sided reflections. When Malik blames mother ("If she could chill out..."), could have gently reframed toward internal locus of control. Approach is somewhat passive—does not actively pivot Malik's language toward change as skillfully as possible.

---

### **Overall Session Score: 3.4/5**

---

## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Strong opening stance with genuine curiosity and collaborative invitation
- Consistent acceptance and warmth in corrected portion of session
- Solid reflective listening capturing core conflict
- Respect for autonomy and client timeline
- Avoidance of confrontation and maintenance of therapeutic alliance
- Collaborative problem-solving approach

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Minimal change talk cultivation** – Emerging change talk not amplified or deepened
- **Missed discrepancy exploration** – Conflict between values and behavior not systematically explored
- **Lack of values exploration** – Does not systematically discover what matters to Malik beyond peer acceptance
- **Insufficient softening of sustain talk** – Limited use of double-sided reflections and reframing
- **Unclear direction** – Change goal not collaboratively defined or explicitly established
- **Limited depth in empathy** – Sometimes moves past opportunities to sit with ambivalence
- **Directive exchange present** – Initial portion demonstrates confrontational, anti-MI approach

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** Moderate. While collaborative stance creates safe environment for exploration, without strategic cultivation of change talk and values-based discrepancy exploration, session unlikely to significantly strengthen Malik's intrinsic motivation. Malik leaves with emerging openness but without deep exploration of why he might want to change.

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist demonstrates foundational MI competence. Focus training on: (1) mastering change talk cultivation and amplification, (2) systematic values exploration, (3) strategic double-sided reflections, and (4) eliminating directive responses. With focused practice, clinician could advance from adequate (3/5) to good (4/5) MI practice.
