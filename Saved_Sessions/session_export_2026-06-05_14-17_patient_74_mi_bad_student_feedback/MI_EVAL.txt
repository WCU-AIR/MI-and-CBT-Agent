# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist repeatedly labels Linda's concerns as "excuses" (5+ instances), communicates explicit disapproval, and frames her ambivalence as a character flaw rather than normal human struggle. No affirmation of Linda's strengths (self-referral, health awareness, repeated quit attempts, willingness to engage). Repeated use of judgmental language ("stop making excuses," "avoiding responsibility") undermines dignity and worth. No warmth, support, or communication that Linda is valued as a person independent of smoking status.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand Linda's internal frame of reference. When Linda expresses fear, psychiatrist does not explore it. When Linda describes social/identity dimensions of smoking, psychiatrist dismisses them as irrelevant. Zero complex reflections capturing emotional nuance. Psychiatrist never reflects Linda's ambivalence, explores past failures to understand barriers, or validates emotional weight of struggle. Ignores Linda's explicit feedback that "talking about it like this, with someone who listens, it does help me start thinking that maybe I could try again." Continues same confrontational approach despite this clear signal.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on smoking cessation and quit date as concrete goal, showing some directional clarity. However, execution is fundamentally misaligned with MI principles. Direction is imposed coercively and unilaterally rather than collaboratively developed. Psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment to specific timeline (this week) without exploring readiness, barriers, or preferences. Never explores underlying barriers to previous quit attempts, never discusses strategies Linda prefers, never helps clarify her own goals and values. Lacks progressive clarification—simply repeats same demand rather than strategic guidance. No movement toward deeper understanding of ambivalence or gradual building of commitment.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Explicitly hierarchical, expert-driven stance antithetical to collaboration. Repeated authoritative language: "You have to," "You must," "You need to," "I'm telling you," "No more delays." Never asks Linda's opinion about what might work, never seeks input on strategies, never invites co-construction of solutions. When Linda offers perspective ("it's not that simple," "I need more support"), psychiatrist dismisses as "overthinking" and "stalling" rather than treating as valuable information. Linda's contributions do not shape session direction. No shared decision-making—all interventions imposed unilaterally. Power dynamic entirely one-directional: psychiatrist as authority, Linda as non-compliant patient.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively undermines autonomy at every turn. Pressures Linda to make specific commitment (quit date this week) and frames hesitation as failure of responsibility rather than legitimate exercise of choice. Phrases like "you can't keep saying you're scared," "stop overthinking," "no more delays" communicate that Linda's autonomy is not respected. When Linda expresses "not ready" and needs "more time and understanding," psychiatrist escalates pressure rather than respecting boundary. False binary presented ("Will you pick a day this week to quit or not?") eliminates genuine choice. No acknowledgment that Linda has right to make decisions about timing, methods, or pace. Dismisses Linda's insight that supportive conversation helps her think about change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation; relies entirely on telling. Linda spontaneously offers multiple change talk statements: "It scares me sometimes," "How much money I'm throwing away," "I have tried quitting before, plenty of times," "It's frustrating because every time I try, I end up going back," "Talking about it like this, with someone who listens, it does help me start thinking that maybe I could try again." Psychiatrist does not explore, amplify, or build upon any of this. Responds to Linda's statements with directives about what she "should" do rather than asking questions to deepen motivation. Never asks open-ended questions designed to evoke Linda's own reasons for change, values, past successes, or resources. No curiosity about what has worked before, what barriers exist, or what Linda thinks might help. Entire approach is "telling" rather than "asking."

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Explicitly assumes expert role and dismisses Linda's self-knowledge and contributions. Positions psychiatrist as sole expert on smoking cessation; never acknowledges that Linda knows her own life, barriers, strengths, and readiness better than anyone else. Never says "You know yourself best," "What do you think would work for you?", or "Let's figure this out together." Actively dismisses Linda's self-knowledge: when she says not ready, told she's making excuses; when she says needs support, told to use willpower; when she says talking helps, ignored and pressured further. Psychiatrist's contributions do not invite Linda's input or build on her ideas. Every response is standalone directive unconnected to Linda's previous statements. No co-construction of understanding or collaborative problem-solving. Implicit message: "You have failed repeatedly, so you cannot be trusted to make decisions about your own change process. I will decide for you." This is paternalism, not partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No strategic cultivation of change talk; Linda's spontaneous change talk goes unrecognized and unreinforced. Psychiatrist ignores Linda's desire for change ("I know it's dangerous," "I want to quit," "I do want to change"), her reasons ("It's frustrating because every time I try, I end up going back"), and her ability/confidence building ("Talking about it like this, with someone who listens, it does help me start thinking that maybe I could try again"). Psychiatrist's response to each change talk statement is to ignore it and redirect to demands for immediate action. Never reflects Linda's change talk back to her, never asks follow-up questions to deepen it, never celebrates her recognition of problem, never builds on her statement that support helps. Confrontational approach actually suppresses change talk—by session end, Linda primarily offers sustain talk ("I'm not ready," "I need more time") in response to pressure. Psychiatrist has inadvertently reinforced resistance rather than motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Poor handling of sustain talk through direct confrontation rather than skillful softening. Linda offers sustain talk throughout ("It's really part of my life," "It's kind of how I connect with my friends," "Sometimes it feels like part of who I am," "I'm not quite ready," "I'm worried that even if I start something, I might just fail again"). Psychiatrist's response is direct confrontation: "It's not about how long you've smoked or who you smoke with," "You're just making excuses," "You can't keep saying you're scared and not ready." This hardens rather than softens resistance. In MI, sustain talk should be acknowledged and gently redirected, not attacked. Psychiatrist does not become overtly hostile (tone 2 rather than 1), but repeated pressure and confrontation actually strengthen Linda's sustain talk rather than soften it. By final exchange, Linda is firmly entrenched: "No, I'm not ready to pick a quit date this week." Psychiatrist has transformed ambivalence into active resistance.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Minimal to no MI competence. While psychiatrist maintains focus on target behavior and does not become overtly hostile, approach is fundamentally misaligned with MI spirit and principles.

**Session Embodies MI Spirit:** ☐ Yes     ☒ No

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** ☐ High     ☐ Moderate     ☒ Low/Actively Counterproductive

---

### Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Consistent focus on target behavior (smoking cessation)
- Explicit communication that smoking is serious health risk
- Availability for follow-up sessions

**Critical Deficiencies:**
1. **Absence of acceptance and empathy:** Repeated judgment of Linda as making excuses; no validation of legitimate fears or acknowledgment of change difficulty
2. **Coercive directiveness:** Unilateral demands for specific action without exploring readiness or preferences; violates autonomy and collaboration
3. **No evocation:** Tells rather than asks; ignores Linda's spontaneous change talk; dismisses her self-knowledge
4. **Confrontation of sustain talk:** Directly attacks Linda's concerns, strengthening defensiveness rather than softening resistance
5. **Misunderstanding of ambivalence:** Treats normal contemplation-stage ambivalence as character flaw requiring correction

---

### Impact Assessment

**Session Outcome:** ACTIVELY COUNTERPRODUCTIVE

Linda enters with genuine change talk and openness to support. By session end, she is firmly entrenched in resistance: "No, I'm not ready to pick a quit date this week." Psychiatrist has transformed ambivalence into active resistance and damaged therapeutic alliance.

**Likelihood of Return:** Low. If Linda returns, defensiveness will be stronger.

**Probability of Successful Behavior Change:** Significantly reduced by this approach.

---

### Critical Recommendations (Priority Order)

1. **Shift from confrontation to collaboration** – Treat Linda as equal expert on her own life
2. **Develop empathic listening and reflective responses** – Demonstrate genuine understanding, especially of fears and ambivalence
3. **Learn to evoke rather than tell** – Ask questions to draw out Linda's own motivation, values, and resources
4. **Respect autonomy and eliminate pressure** – Stop demanding specific timeline; explore what would help her feel ready
5. **Recognize and amplify change talk** – Listen for and deepen Linda's own statements about desire, ability, reasons, and commitment to change

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Immediate MI training and skill development required. Current approach is contraindicated and actively harmful to client engagement and behavior change outcomes.
