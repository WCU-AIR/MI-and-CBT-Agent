| **Total Score (Sum of Items 1–11)** | **18/66** | **CRITICAL DEFICIENCIES:** This session represents a fundamental failure to deliver evidence-based CBT for a patient in acute mania with psychotic features. While the therapist demonstrated adequate interpersonal warmth (Item 4: 3/6), clinical work was severely deficient across nearly all domains. Most critically, the therapist applied standard cognitive-behavioral techniques to a patient whose acute psychiatric presentation requires pharmacological stabilization, psychoeducation, behavioral structure, and safety planning *before* cognitive interventions. The session lacked essential CBT structure (no explicit agenda, no prioritization, no coherent strategy). The therapist failed to assess or address critical safety concerns (sexually inappropriate behavior), did not review medication adherence or sleep deprivation, and did not provide psychoeducation about mania. Homework was vague and inappropriate. **This session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT.** The therapist requires immediate, intensive supervision and training in: (1) CBT for bipolar disorder and psychosis, (2) clinical assessment and safety planning, (3) psychoeducation and medication management collaboration, (4) structured session planning, and (5) appropriate use of Socratic questioning. The therapist should not continue treating patients with acute mania or psychosis without significant skill development and close supervision. |

## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Immediate Actions Required:**

1. **Do not continue treating this patient without direct supervision.** The clinical presentation (acute mania with psychotic features) requires specialized assessment and management beyond the scope of this therapist's current competence.

2. **Urgent consultation with prescribing psychiatrist** is needed to assess medication adequacy, sleep deprivation level, and safety concerns (sexually inappropriate behavior).

3. **Safety assessment must be completed** before next session, including: suicidal/homicidal ideation, risky sexual behavior, reckless spending, substance use, and medication adherence.

4. **Therapist requires intensive training** in CBT for bipolar disorder, psychosis, and acute psychiatric presentations before continuing independent clinical work.

5. **Recommend structured supervision** with weekly session review, role-play practice, and competency assessment before therapist resumes independent practice.
