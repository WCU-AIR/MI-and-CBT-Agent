## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### **Strengths:**
- **Exemplary empathic understanding** across cognitive, emotional, and somatic domains with accurate reflections
- **Masterful use of guided discovery** and Socratic questioning enabling patient self-discovery rather than imposing solutions
- **Strong collaborative engagement** in agenda-setting, prioritization, and homework planning
- **Warm, professional interpersonal effectiveness** establishing genuine therapeutic rapport
- **Competent cognitive technique application** (thought records, reframing, perspective-taking)
- **Specific, achievable behavioral homework** with clear rationale and SMART goal refinement
- **Logical session flow** with appropriate depth on identified priority

### **Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Narrow treatment strategy** addressing only financial worry cognition, not broader adjustment disorder (breakup grief, social withdrawal, academic impairment, behavioral avoidance)
- **Insufficient behavioral focus** with heavy cognitive weighting; key behavioral patterns (avoidance, social withdrawal) not comprehensively addressed
- **Incomplete CBT technique application** missing psychoeducation, behavioral experiments, thought records as homework, exposure work, and systematic avoidance analysis
- **Limited cultural competence** with minimal acknowledgment of contextual stressors (racial discrimination, first-generation status, midnight-shift work)
- **Lack of comprehensive treatment planning** with no articulated overall strategy for adjustment disorder across multiple domains
- **Incomplete homework scope** addressing only one presenting problem; no behavioral activation for mood; no thought records for independent practice
- **Absent time management structure** with no explicit time allocation, mid-session checks, or discussion of addressing secondary problem

### **Clinical Impact:**
This session will likely produce modest short-term benefit through information-gathering and cognitive reframing of the financial worry. However, without addressing the broader adjustment disorder (unprocessed breakup grief, social withdrawal, behavioral avoidance, underlying beliefs), the patient will likely continue struggling with depressed mood, social isolation, and academic impairment. The session represents partial, not comprehensive, evidence-based CBT for adjustment disorder.

### **Recommendations for Improvement:**
1. Develop comprehensive treatment strategy addressing all adjustment disorder domains (breakup processing, behavioral activation, financial management)
2. Integrate behavioral interventions as central—assign behavioral activation, develop exposure plans for avoidance
3. Assign thought records as standard homework for independent cognitive skill practice
4. Provide explicit psychoeducation about CBT model and rationale
5. Propose behavioral experiments to test catastrophic predictions
6. Explicitly address avoidance patterns and develop graded exposure plan
7. Establish explicit time management with mid-session checks and topic transitions
8. Incorporate cultural competence by acknowledging contextual stressors
9. Develop multi-domain homework addressing cognitive, behavioral, and emotional targets
10. Establish explicit follow-up and homework review process
