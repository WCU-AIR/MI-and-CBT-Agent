# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Strong validation of frustration ("It sounds really frustrating to put in effort and still feel stuck") and affirmation of courage are evident. However, missed opportunity to explore deeper fears beneath concerns about being a burden to counselor. Could have more explicitly normalized weight loss plateau as common, expected experience rather than only validating emotional response.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking demonstrated throughout. Complex reflections capture emotional nuance ("You're carrying a lot not just physically but emotionally as well"). Sophisticated understanding of ambivalence evident in response to confidence rating. However, opportunities missed to explore deeper emotional layers, particularly around shame and self-blame embedded in statements like "I don't know what I'm doing wrong." Could have more deeply empathized with perfectionism and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial direction is clear and appropriately focused on weight loss plateau and barriers. However, session becomes progressively less focused on primary presenting concern as it advances. Significant pivot occurs midway through toward support-seeking and emotional processing. By session end, focus has shifted entirely to what kind of listening client needs rather than exploring specific weight loss barriers, workout routines, nutrition, or concrete strategies. Session drifts without returning to primary goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated throughout. Opening immediately establishes partnership ("I want to make sure this time is all about what matters most to you"). Frequent use of collaborative language and invitation for client input evident. Validates self-generated solutions (school counselor suggestion) rather than prescribing. Minor gap: could have more explicitly invited partnership in agenda-setting regarding weight loss specifically, allowing focus shift to occur somewhat passively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect for client choice and avoidance of prescriptive language throughout. Liyana's autonomy is well-supported; psychiatrist does not pressure regarding parents or specific actions. Validates her judgment about family dynamics. Minor subtlety: affirmation of "courage" in continuing to try could be reframed to more explicitly acknowledge her right to choose different approaches. Overall, autonomy is appropriately respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial evocation of values is strong ("What is it about losing weight that matters most to you right now?") and elicits meaningful change talk about self-acceptance and confidence. However, this change talk is not strategically deepened or amplified. Session shifts focus away from exploring intrinsic motivation for weight loss toward emotional support needs. No exploration of ability talk, reasons talk, or need talk regarding weight loss. Significant missed opportunity to sustain and build motivation for primary change goal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative partnership with consistent power-sharing and validation of client expertise. Opening establishes shared power and invites client priorities. Clinician positions Liyana as expert on her own experience and avoids expert role in key moments. Minor gaps: statements like "It's understandable to feel uncertain" are somewhat didactic; interpretation of confidence rating ("A 4 shows that there's some hope") could have invited client to define meaning rather than clinician interpreting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. While some change talk is elicited early ("I want to feel okay in my own skin"), it is not strategically deepened or amplified. No follow-up questions to explore values further. No exploration of ability talk regarding weight loss confidence or strategies. No systematic exploration of reasons, need, or commitment to weight loss change. Critical gap: session pivots to evoking change talk about seeking support rather than about weight loss itself. Change talk about reaching out to counselors emerges, but weight loss motivation remains largely unexplored and unamplifed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates adequate but inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Generally avoids reinforcing discouragement through validation and redirection. However, handling is passive rather than strategic. No double-sided reflection used to acknowledge both commitment and discouragement. No reframing of self-blame or normalization of plateaus as expected, temporary experience. When Liyana expresses hopelessness ("I don't see how to get out of it"), psychiatrist validates but does not gently challenge or explore. Sustain talk is accepted rather than actively softened.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 3.4/5**

### Key Strengths:
- ✓ Excellent opening and agenda-setting establishing partnership
- ✓ Consistent warmth, acceptance, and unconditional positive regard
- ✓ Good reflective listening capturing emotional nuance and connections
- ✓ Strong respect for autonomy and collaborative stance
- ✓ Validation of past successes and resilience

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ Insufficient cultivation and amplification of change talk about weight loss
- ✗ Premature focus shift from primary concern (weight loss) to secondary concern (emotional support)
- ✗ Lack of strategic softening of self-blame and normalization of plateaus
- ✗ No exploration of concrete weight loss barriers or strategies
- ✗ Missing psychoeducation about weight loss plateaus
- ✗ Inadequate exploration of ability talk and self-efficacy for weight loss

### Impact on Client Change Likelihood:
**Moderate.** Session successfully builds emotional safety and relational foundation, which is necessary but not sufficient for behavior change. Liyana will likely feel more supported and less alone, increasing willingness to continue trying. However, without deeper exploration of motivation, barriers, and self-blame, her confidence (currently 4/10) is unlikely to increase substantially. Motivational foundation remains underdeveloped.

### Recommended Priority Actions:
1. Systematically cultivate change talk using DARN-CAT framework with deliberate amplification
2. Maintain focus on primary change goal while addressing support needs through explicit agenda-setting
3. Normalize weight loss plateaus and actively reframe self-blame rather than only validating
4. Explore concrete barriers and invite client-generated solutions
5. Use double-sided reflection to soften sustain talk and hopelessness
6. Strengthen ability talk and self-efficacy exploration regarding weight loss
