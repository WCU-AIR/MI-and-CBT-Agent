## Overall Session Assessment

### **Global Score: 1.0/5 – Fundamental Failure in MI Practice**

---

### Summary of Critical Findings

This session represents a **complete failure in Motivational Interviewing practice** and a violation of evidence-based principles for behavior change counseling. The psychiatrist demonstrates zero competency across all nine MI domains. The approach is authoritarian, coercive, judgmental, and actively harmful.

**Strengths (Minimal):**
- Clear focus on target behavior (smoking cessation)
- Factually accurate health information provided

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation)
- Authoritarian, coercive approach with unilateral demands and threats
- Judgment and contempt communicated throughout ("excuses," "weakness")
- Suppression of change talk and amplification of sustain talk
- Therapeutic abandonment through unilateral termination
- Likely counterproductive outcomes through psychological reactance

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**This session is iatrogenically harmful.** Rather than evoking intrinsic motivation, the psychiatrist's approach is likely to:

- **Strengthen resistance** through psychological reactance
- **Damage therapeutic relationship** and reduce trust
- **Reduce intrinsic motivation** by relying on external pressure
- **Produce shame and defensiveness** rather than openness
- **Reduce likelihood of future treatment engagement**

The patient leaves more entrenched in ambivalence, more defensive, and less likely to engage in future treatment. This is the opposite of the intended outcome.

---

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Fundamental reorientation to MI principles** – Complete retraining required
2. **Replace confrontation with acceptance and validation** – Normalize ambivalence
3. **Develop evocative questioning skills** – Ask rather than tell
4. **Amplify and reinforce change talk** – Strengthen patient's own motivation
5. **Support autonomy and client-centered decision-making** – Affirm patient's right to choose
6. **Use double-sided reflections** – Acknowledge both sides of ambivalence
7. **Eliminate judgment, contempt, and labeling** – Remove all judgmental language
8. **Maintain collaborative relationship** – Adopt true partnership stance
9. **Develop skill in handling resistance** – Use MI-consistent strategies
10. **Seek ongoing clinical supervision** – Engage with MI-certified trainer for minimum 6 months

---

### Critical Ethical Concern

**Urgent action recommended:** The psychiatrist should not continue conducting MI sessions without intensive retraining and supervision. Continued practice with this approach will harm patients and undermine treatment outcomes. Immediate referral to certified MI training program is essential.
