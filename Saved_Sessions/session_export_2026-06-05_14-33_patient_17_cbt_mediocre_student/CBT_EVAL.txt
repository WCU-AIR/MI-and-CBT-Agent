## SUPERVISOR NOTES

### Strengths Demonstrated
- Maintains warm, respectful, non-judgmental tone
- Uses open-ended questions to encourage elaboration
- Demonstrates basic empathic listening skills
- Attempts to explore patient's emotional experience
- Assigns homework (though poorly executed)
- Patient appears comfortable and willing to engage

### Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Intervention
1. **Failure to recognize acute psychiatric crisis** – Treats manic episode with psychosis as emotional problem
2. **Validation of delusions** – Non-directive approach normalizes grandiose beliefs and religious preoccupations
3. **Absence of reality-testing** – No collaborative examination of evidence for delusional beliefs
4. **No safety assessment** – Fails to assess suicide/homicide risk, medication compliance, or acute dangers
5. **Misaligned focus** – Addresses secondary emotions rather than core manic symptoms
6. **No psychoeducation** – Patient receives no education about bipolar disorder or symptom-illness connection
7. **Absence of structured CBT** – No thought records, behavioral experiments, cognitive restructuring, or evidence-based interventions
8. **Poor agenda-setting** – No specific, prioritized treatment goals established
9. **Inadequate feedback loops** – Only one cursory check-in; no systematic verification of understanding
10. **Generic homework** – Vague assignments without clear rationale or connection to symptom reduction

### Immediate Actions Required
- **Intensive supervision** on CBT for bipolar disorder with psychotic features
- **Structured training** in recognizing and treating acute mania
- **Role-plays** of reality-testing, psychoeducation, and Socratic questioning
- **Case review** with focus on case conceptualization for acute psychiatric crises
- **Supervised practice** before continuing to treat bipolar patients
- **Study** of evidence-based CBT protocols for bipolar disorder

### Risk Level
**HIGH** – Trainee should not continue treating bipolar patients without significant additional training and close supervision. Risk of harm to patients is significant.
