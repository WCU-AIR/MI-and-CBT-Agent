--- DISORDER ---
Major Depressive Disorder
--- CASE STUDY ---
 Helena Bates was a 27-year-old single white woman who presented for psychiatric evaluation and treatment for depression.
 She had recently begun an intensive outpatient program following her first-ever psychiatric hospitalization, which occurred after an impulsive overdose following the breakup of a two-year romantic relationship.
She reported that her mood had been “increasingly sad and hopeless” for 1–2 months leading up to the breakup.
 Throughout those weeks, she experienced moderate depression most of the day on most days, with no daily variation and intact mood reactivity.
She had gained about 10 pounds from “overeating comfort food and junk.”
 She denied irritability or argumentativeness.
 She reported “no” self-esteem and described a significant lack of motivation and difficulty concentrating on routine tasks.
 She experienced intrusive, racing thoughts and periods of “nonstop thinking” about her ex-boyfriend, including purchasing a full-page newspaper ad as an “open letter” to him.
 She also reported staying awake journaling or calling friends until 5:00 or 6:00 AM, and yet reported no fatigue during the following day, despite only 2–3 hours of sleep. These symptoms preceded her hospitalization.
She was horrified by her own suicide attempt and denied any prior history of suicidal thoughts or gestures.
 She had no history of self-injury or substance misuse.
Past psychiatric history included counseling in high school for “moodiness” and a brief trial of escitalopram and psychotherapy during college for depression.
 These were discontinued after a few weeks when symptoms improved.
At the time of hospitalization, she was started on vilazodone and quetiapine.
Family history was notable for a brother with substance use issues, a sister treated for panic attacks and depression, and multiple extended family members with suspected depression.
Mental status exam revealed a pleasant, appropriately dressed, moderately overweight woman, who made good eye contact.
 Speech was somewhat rapid and verbose but not pressured.
 No abnormal movements were noted, though she used exaggerated gestures.
 Mood was depressed and affect tense and dysphoric, but with a full range and normal responsivity.
 Thought processes were circumstantial but coherent, linear, and logical.
 She reported passive thoughts that she might be better off dead, but denied intent or plan.
 She had no hallucinations, delusions, or homicidal ideation.
 Insight and judgment were grossly intact, though some recent behaviors suggested impaired judgment.
Discussion:
 Ms. Bates met criteria for major depressive disorder with at least five depressive symptoms. She also displayed subthreshold hypomanic symptoms (racing thoughts, rapid speech, decreased need for sleep, and increased goal-directed activity), present prior to medication initiation.
 Thus, she met criteria for Major Depressive Disorder with mixed features.
 Her presentation had been previously mischaracterized as borderline personality disorder, but her history lacked the chronic instability or interpersonal chaos required for that diagnosis.
