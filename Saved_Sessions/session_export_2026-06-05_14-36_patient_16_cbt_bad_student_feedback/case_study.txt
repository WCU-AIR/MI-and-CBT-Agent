--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar I Disorder, Current Episode Manic, Severe, with Psychotic Features, with Peripartum Onset

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ursula Norman, a 32-year-old nurse, was brought to the ED 6 days postpartum due to bizarre behavior and fixed beliefs that she had smothered her newborn. Her husband reported that after a normal pregnancy and delivery, Ms. Norman became irritable, anxious, and experienced rapid mood shifts between elation and tearfulness beginning on postpartum day 3. She exhibited extreme sleep deprivation, agitation, overactivity, distractibility, flight of ideas, and hyperreligiosity. She held multiple delusional beliefs, including identifying “evil” in people and perceiving special powers, and repeatedly insisted she had already killed her child.

Ms. Norman’s psychiatric history included three major depressive episodes in her teens and early 20s, and a prior manic episode requiring hospitalization. Family history was notable for her mother having a postpartum psychiatric admission. Physical and laboratory exams were normal.

Discussion: Ms. Norman presents with severe mania with psychotic features in the immediate postpartum period, qualifying as a psychiatric emergency. Her presentation is consistent with postpartum psychosis: abrupt onset, rapid fluctuation of symptoms, severe affective dysregulation, delusions, and risk to self and child. Given her history of bipolar disorder, she is at high risk for postpartum episodes. Early recognition and hospitalization are critical for maternal and infant safety.
