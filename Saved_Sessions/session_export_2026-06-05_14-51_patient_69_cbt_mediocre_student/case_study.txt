--- DISORDER ---
Opioid Use Disorder; Tobacco Use Disorder; Alcohol Use Disorder (in remission); Major Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Peter Winters, a 46-year-old married white minister, was referred to outpatient psychiatry for depressive symptoms and opioid misuse related to chronic right knee pain.

Seventeen months prior, he injured his right knee playing basketball. Initially treated with hydrocodone-acetaminophen provided by his mother, he found relief. After finishing the medication, his pain persisted. He was prescribed a 1-month supply by the emergency room, which resolved pain, but recurrence led him to take higher doses than prescribed. He experienced dysphoria, muscle aches, and cravings when abstinent, began “enjoying the high,” frequently visited ERs for opioids, misrepresented pain severity, and stole pills from his mother. His opioid-seeking behavior negatively impacted work and home life.

History: Two prior major depressive episodes successfully treated with escitalopram; alcohol use disorder in his 20s, remitted; heavy tobacco use (2 packs/day); strong family history of addiction and paternal depression. Married 20 years with two school-aged children; minister for 15 years. Physical exam and labs were normal.

Mental status: cooperative, brief responses (“yes”/“no”), normal speech, dysphoric/constricted affect, mood “lousy,” no psychosis, no suicidal or homicidal ideation, memory intact, no psychomotor abnormalities.

Discussion: Mr. Winters demonstrates a prescription opioid use disorder with compulsive misuse, cravings, tolerance, withdrawal, and significant life impairment. Risk factors include personal/family history of addiction, depression, and heavy tobacco use. Tobacco and alcohol use disorders are also present, the former active and the latter in remission. Mood symptoms may reflect an underlying major depressive disorder, exacerbated by opioid use. Somatic symptom disorder with predominant pain may also contribute.
