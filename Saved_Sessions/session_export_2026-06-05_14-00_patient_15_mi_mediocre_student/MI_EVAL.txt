## **OVERALL SESSION ASSESSMENT**

### **Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **significant deficiency in Motivational Interviewing practice**. While psychiatrist maintains basic organizational structure and acknowledges some barriers, session is fundamentally misaligned with MI principles and spirit. Psychiatrist operates from expert-driven, prescriptive model rather than evocative, collaborative approach.

**Current MI Competency Level:** Below-average to poor (1–2 range on most criteria). Basic reflective listening present but lacks depth and is undermined by pervasive prescriptive language, minimal collaboration, and failure to evoke genuine client motivation.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- ☐ Pervasive prescriptive language ("should," "would help if," "might want to") contradicts MI foundation
- ☐ Absence of genuine collaboration; expert authority maintained throughout
- ☐ Failure to evoke change talk; prescriptive advice-giving replaces strategic questioning
- ☐ Inadequate handling of ambivalence; pressure applied rather than exploration
- ☐ Minimal autonomy support; subtle pressure undermines client choice
- ☐ Shallow empathy; emotional attunement lacking despite client's expressed frustration and overwhelm

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Motivation:** **Low to moderate.** Client agreement to snack-swapping appears to be compliance rather than genuine intrinsic commitment. Psychiatrist missed critical opportunities to help client discover own reasons for change and collaboratively problem-solve barriers. Without addressing fundamental MI deficiencies, likelihood of sustained behavior change is limited.

**Does This Session Embody MI Spirit?** **No.** Session demonstrates expert-driven, prescriptive approach contradicting MI spirit of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. Fundamental stance shift required from "I know what you should do" to "You know your life best—what do you think might work?"

---

### **PRIORITY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT**

**1. HIGHEST PRIORITY – Shift from Prescriptive to Evocative Stance**
Replace "should," "would help if," "might want to" language with genuine open questions. This is most fundamental MI skill and most critical deficiency.

**2. SECOND PRIORITY – Develop Genuine Collaborative Partnership**
Position client as expert on own life. Use language emphasizing shared decision-making. Explicitly defer to client's knowledge.

**3. THIRD PRIORITY – Amplify and Explore Change Talk**
When client expresses change talk, pause and deepen through reflection and strategic questioning to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

**4. FOURTH PRIORITY – Explore and Normalize Ambivalence**
Recognize ambivalence as normal contemplation. Use double-sided reflections and curious exploration rather than minimization.

**5. FIFTH PRIORITY – Develop Genuine Empathy and Emotional Attunement**
Move beyond factual reflections to capture emotional nuance. Reflect frustration, time pressure, and overwhelm explicitly.
