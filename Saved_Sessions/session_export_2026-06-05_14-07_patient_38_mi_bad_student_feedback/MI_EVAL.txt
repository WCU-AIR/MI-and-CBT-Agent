### Direction

Directive language dominates ("Just do it, okay?" "You must do it now. Don't wait." "I expect you to make that appointment"). Brief topic drift when introducing tobacco use. Direction should guide client toward exploring own reasons for change; instead, clinician imposes predetermined outcome through ultimatums and commands.

### Collaboration

Psychiatrist dismisses Elena's concerns without exploring them jointly ("You can't just worry about side effects without thinking about the bigger picture"). Elena's contributions treated as obstacles rather than incorporated into decision-making.

When Elena expresses recognition of risk ("Wow, I didn't realize it was so dangerous"), clinician responds with demands rather than exploration ("Good, you need to take this seriously. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Poor handling of sustain talk through direct confrontation rather than skillful redirection. Elena's concerns about vaccine safety, efficacy doubts, fear of harm, disease minimization, and need for partner discussion met with contradiction ("Look, you can't just worry about side effects") and pressure ("You really have to stop hesitating") rather than validation. No double-sided reflections to validate both sides of ambivalence. One moment where directiveness appears to reduce sustain talk ("Uh, okay. I didn't really realize it was so dangerous"), but this represents compliance through pressure, not genuine resolution of ambivalence. Information provided but through lecturing rather than collaborative exploration.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.2/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **severe departure from motivational interviewing principles** and demonstrates a fundamentally contraindicated confrontational, coercive approach. The psychiatrist violates nearly every core MI principle through judgmental language, shame-based framing, systematic autonomy undermining, hierarchical control, and pressure-based tactics. While Elena shows surface-level compliance ("I'll try to make that appointment by the end of the week"), this represents **compliance through coercion, not genuine intrinsic motivation for change**. Her final statement—"It's just... I'm still a bit anxious about it"—reveals underlying ambivalence remains unresolved.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- No genuine empathic listening or reflective responses
- Elimination of client autonomy through threats and ultimatums
- Replacement of partnership with hierarchical control
- Imposition of reasons for change rather than evocation of intrinsic motivation
- Confrontation of sustain talk rather than skillful softening

**Impact on Therapeutic Relationship and Change Likelihood:**
- Increases reactance and resistance
- Damages trust in healthcare provider
- Teaches client to override own deliberation and family relationships
- Reduces likelihood of sustained behavior change
- Models poor decision-making processes

**Urgent Recommendation:** This clinician requires formal MI training (MINT-certified workshop), supervised practice, and session recording review before continuing MI-based clinical work. Current approach is contraindicated and potentially harmful to patients.
