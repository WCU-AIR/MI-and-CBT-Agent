## Overall Session Assessment

### **Overall Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **critical failure of Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, directive counseling that violates nearly every core MI principle. The opening statement—"I'll be telling you exactly what steps to take"—immediately establishes a hierarchical expert-driven stance rather than partnership.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- ✗ Absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- ✗ Complete lack of empathic understanding and reflective listening
- ✗ Suppression of client autonomy through controlling language
- ✗ Failure to evoke client's intrinsic motivation
- ✗ Threat-based motivation and blame-focused approach
- ✗ No genuine collaboration or joint problem-solving
- ✗ Confrontational response to resistance

**Impact on Client:**

While short-term behavioral agreement is achieved, this appears to be **compliance under pressure** rather than genuine commitment. Client language ("Yeah, okay. I heard you. I'm not saying it's gonna be easy") suggests external motivation rather than intrinsic commitment. Research indicates this type of compliance is fragile and likely to collapse when external pressure is removed.

**Critical Concern:**

This session **fails to address underlying ambivalence and overwhelm** driving client's resistance. Rather than exploring competing demands and helping client discover own reasons for change, psychiatrist adds pressure, likely increasing overwhelm and damaging therapeutic relationship.

## Recommendations for Improvement

### **HIGHEST PRIORITY: Immediate Retraining Required**

1. **Establish Genuine Partnership** – Replace hierarchical language with collaborative stance. Use "What do you think?" instead of "You have to."

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills** – Aim for 2+ reflections per directive statement. Demonstrate understanding of client's perspective before offering solutions.

3. **Eliminate Judgmental Language** – Remove all pejorative labels ("laziness," "excuses," "slacking"). Replace blame-focused language with curiosity-focused language.

4. **Evoke Intrinsic Motivation** – Ask open-ended questions about client's own reasons for change, values, and what would be different if behavior changed.

5. **Validate Ambivalence** – Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sustain talk and change talk. Create space for client to move toward change on own.

6. **Support Autonomy** – Explicitly affirm client's right to choose. Use language: "This is your choice," "Ultimately, this is up to you."

7. **Amplify Change Talk** – When client expresses change talk, pause and explore it with follow-up questions to deepen commitment.

8. **Use MI-Consistent Resistance Techniques** – Replace confrontation with reflective listening and reframing when addressing sustain talk.

9. **Collaborate on Plans** – Involve client in problem-solving. Ask what they think would work before suggesting solutions.

10. **Seek Formal MI Training** – Pursue MINT-certified training, workshops, and peer consultation. Commit to 20+ hours of formal training with recorded session review by MI-trained supervisor.

---

### **Clinical Recommendation:**

**This psychiatrist should not continue using this approach without significant retraining and supervision.** Current practice is likely to produce poor long-term outcomes, increased client disengagement, and potential harm to therapeutic relationship. Immediate enrollment in comprehensive Motivational Interviewing training is strongly recommended.
