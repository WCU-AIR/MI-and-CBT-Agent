## **OVERALL SESSION ASSESSMENT**

### **Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

---

### **Summary**

#### **Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on specific health behavior (flu vaccination)
- Demonstrates awareness that family impact (protecting grandparents) could be a motivator
- Shows persistence in addressing health concern

#### **Critical Deficiencies:**

This session represents a **fundamental violation of Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly every domain. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, authoritarian, expert-driven approach that is antithetical to MI spirit:

- **Complete absence of empathy and acceptance:** No reflective listening, no validation, no warmth. Client is judged rather than accepted.
- **Systematic undermining of autonomy:** Controlling language, threats, guilt, and shame pressure compliance. Client's explicit need for autonomy is dismissed.
- **Failure to evoke:** Psychiatrist tells rather than asks. Multiple instances of spontaneous change talk are ignored entirely.
- **No collaboration or partnership:** Psychiatrist assumes complete expert authority and rejects client's input.
- **Repeated, scripted responses:** Same opening monologue recycled verbatim across multiple turns, indicating psychiatrist is not listening or adapting.
- **Misuse of family responsibility:** Family impact is weaponized through guilt and shame rather than explored collaboratively.

#### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

This session is **actively counterproductive** to evoking client motivation. Jordan enters with ambivalence and emerging change talk (motivated by protecting grandparents). By session end, he is more entrenched in resistance and explicitly states his need for autonomy. Psychiatrist's response is to intensify control, likely further entrenching resistance.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Increased resistance to behavior change
- Damaged therapeutic relationship and decreased trust
- Decreased likelihood of vaccination
- Potential harm to future healthcare engagement

#### **Critical Clinical Note:**

**This session does not represent Motivational Interviewing.** It represents a confrontational, authoritarian approach explicitly contraindicated in MI training and research. Psychiatrist appears to have confused "being directive about health goal" with "being controlling and confrontational." In MI, clinicians can maintain clear direction while preserving collaborative, evocative, autonomy-supportive stance.

**Immediate recommendation:** Formal MI training and ongoing supervision essential. Continuing this approach will harm clients and undermine health outcomes.
