--- DISORDER ---
Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID)
--- CASE STUDY ---

Uma, an 11-year-old girl in a gifted and talented school, was referred to an eating disorder specialist due to concerns that she had drifted below the 10th percentile for weight. She had previously been treated by a child psychiatrist for perfectionistic traits causing significant anxiety, with sessions focusing on anxiety rather than eating behavior.

Eating difficulties began at age 9, when Uma refused to eat and reported fear of vomiting. At that time, her pediatrician monitored her yearly, explaining such phases were normal. At age 9, she was above the 25th percentile for height and weight (52 inches, 58 pounds), but by age 11, her growth had flattened, and she dropped to the 5th percentile (52.5 inches, 55 pounds).

Uma is the only child of two professional parents who divorced 5 years earlier. She lives with her mother on weekdays and father on weekends. She was born prematurely at 34 weeks, with early developmental delays but was developmentally normal by age 2. Yearly physical exams were unremarkable except for the recent decline in growth. Uma has always been petite but never below the 25th percentile before. She is talented academically and well-liked by teachers, but recently stopped socializing, coming home directly from school, stating she felt calmer at home.

For the past 2 years, Uma has eaten very small amounts of food slowly. Parents attempted to encourage eating with different foods and restaurants, but she gradually refused to eat outside either parent's home. Mealtimes involved rearranging food on her plate, cutting food into small pieces, and crying if urged to eat. Fear of vomiting originated from a single incident at age 4 and has led to avoidance of eating in public. She denies concern about appearance and recognizes the seriousness of low weight, expressing a desire to gain weight.

Discussion: Uma is an 11-year-old with a significant flattening of height and weight due to minimal food intake, triggered by fear of vomiting. She demonstrates social withdrawal and anxiety but does not fear weight gain or deny the seriousness of her low weight, distinguishing her condition from anorexia nervosa. Diagnosis of ARFID is consistent with DSM-5 criteria, with a subtype related to fear of vomiting. Differential diagnoses include anxiety disorders, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and medical/neurological issues, but ARFID is the most parsimonious explanation.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---
- Tearful during discussion of food or weight
- Avoids social interaction with peers
- Compliant but anxious during assessment
- Focused on rearranging and cutting food
- Prefers eating at home; refuses public eating
- Exhibits perfectionistic tendencies

--- ARFID SYMPTOMS from DSM-5 ---
- Avoidance or restriction of food intake
- Persistent failure to meet nutritional/energy needs
- Significant weight loss or failure to achieve expected growth
- Dependence on supplemental feeding or specific food preparation
- Marked interference with psychosocial functioning
- Fear of vomiting triggers food avoidance
- No concern with body weight or shape
