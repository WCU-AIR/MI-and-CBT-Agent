# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist establishes initial warmth but quickly shifts to problem-focused stance. Makes assumptions about Marcus's concerns ("It sounds like you might be worried") that Marcus explicitly corrects. Lacks genuine affirmation of Marcus's autonomy, strengths, or perspective. Subtle disapproval conveyed through cautionary language ("habits can sneak up on you"). Generic closing reassurance ("You're doing fine") does not constitute authentic acceptance of his precontemplative stage.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Basic reflective listening present in isolated moments ("so it sounds like fitting in and having fun are big reasons you drink"), but lacks consistency and depth. Misses critical opportunity to explore emotional core when Marcus discusses belonging and identity. Reflects surface-level understanding rather than genuine perspective-taking. Projective empathy evident ("I understand that it might feel annoying") rather than genuine exploration of Marcus's actual experience. No complex reflections capturing ambivalence or underlying vulnerability.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains adequate focus on alcohol use throughout session without major topic drift. However, direction is prescriptive rather than collaborative. Psychiatrist moves quickly from exploration to advice-giving without strategically clarifying Marcus's own values, goals, or reasons for change. Session lacks exploration of what Marcus wants for his future or what matters most to him. Direction is present but externally imposed rather than internally discovered by client.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Minimal collaboration present. Psychiatrist operates from expert-driven, hierarchical stance throughout. Makes unilateral decisions about topics, problems, and solutions without genuine joint problem-solving. Introduces alcohol use as topic and assumes Marcus's emotional state without asking what he thinks is important. Offers prescriptive advice (pacing, water, limits) without co-construction. Scaling question functions as compliance check rather than genuine exploration. Unilateral session closure without asking Marcus what he finds helpful. Marcus's input does not substantially shape session direction.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy support largely absent despite absence of explicit coercion. Opening presupposes change is the goal rather than Marcus's right to choose. Cautionary language ("habits can sneak up on you") implies Marcus should be concerned, undermining his autonomy to assess his own situation. Advice-giving without permission or inquiry about Marcus's preferences. Scaling question functions as subtle pressure to commit to change. One positive: avoids harsh confrontation, which Marcus appreciates. However, absence of confrontation does not equal active autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation largely absent. Psychiatrist relies on telling and suggesting rather than asking and exploring. Misses opportunity to deeply explore why fitting in and belonging matter to Marcus. Advice-giving replaces evoking Marcus's own ideas ("maybe you could try to be a bit more mindful"). Leading questions impose direction rather than evoking his thinking. Failure to explore hints of ambivalence present in session. No exploration of Marcus's values, long-term goals, or what he wants for his future. Scaling question measures readiness rather than evoking intrinsic motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership fundamentally absent. Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role and maintains hierarchical control throughout. Identifies alcohol use as problem without exploring whether Marcus sees it as problem. Generates solutions unilaterally rather than co-creating them. Dismisses Marcus's expertise about himself by reframing his statements as low motivation rather than honoring his self-knowledge. Hierarchical language ("It would probably help if...") positions psychiatrist as authority. Unilateral session closure without asking Marcus what would be useful. No genuine power-sharing; psychiatrist's agenda remains constant regardless of Marcus's responses.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk minimally evoked and not strategically reinforced. Limited change talk present ("I guess having water or whatever doesn't sound too bad") is not explored or amplified. No strategic questioning designed to elicit change talk about desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. Missing questions about what would be good about adjusting drinking or confidence in ability to change. Weak commitment language ("I can try to keep things chill") not reinforced. Advice-giving undermines opportunity for Marcus to generate his own change talk. No exploration of values-based change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk present throughout ("It's not a big deal, everyone does it"; "I don't see why I should be worried"; "I'm not looking to change a lot") but not skillfully softened or redirected. Psychiatrist responds to sustain talk with cautionary language that may entrench defensiveness rather than using double-sided reflections. Accepts low motivation rating (2-3) without exploring ambivalence beneath it. No reframing of sustain talk. No exploration of function underlying Marcus's minimization. Absence of confrontation is positive but does not constitute skillful softening of sustain talk.

## Overall Session Score: 1.9/5

---

### Summary of Strengths

- **Warm opening tone:** Establishes initial rapport and non-threatening atmosphere allowing Marcus to engage
- **Avoids harsh confrontation:** Does not use aggressive challenge or labeling; Marcus explicitly appreciates this approach
- **Maintains focus:** Session consistently returns to alcohol use without major topic drift
- **Basic reflective listening:** One solid reflection accurately captures Marcus's stated motivation
- **Client engagement maintained:** Despite low motivation, Marcus continues to participate and respond

---

### Summary of Areas for Improvement

- **Expert-driven stance dominates:** Hierarchical, problem-focused approach undermines MI spirit and reduces likelihood of evoking intrinsic motivation
- **Minimal evocation of intrinsic motivation:** Relies on advice-giving and leading questions rather than drawing out Marcus's own reasons for change and values
- **Sustain talk not strategically addressed:** Significant sustain talk present but not softened through double-sided reflections or exploration of ambivalence
- **Limited autonomy support:** Does not actively affirm Marcus's right to choose or his self-knowledge about his situation
- **Weak change talk not amplified:** Limited change talk that emerges is not explored or reinforced
- **Collaboration absent:** Marcus's input does not substantially shape session direction; psychiatrist's agenda remains constant
- **Superficial empathy:** Misses opportunities for complex, emotionally attuned reflections capturing underlying needs for belonging and autonomy

---

### Clinical Impact

**Likelihood of sustained behavior change: LOW**

Marcus has agreed to surface-level strategy (water between drinks) primarily to comply with psychiatrist's suggestion, not from autonomous discovery of reasons to change. This represents **compliance without commitment** — research shows compliance-based change is short-lived, especially in adolescents developmentally oriented toward autonomy and peer influence. Expert-driven approach may reinforce defensiveness and resistance in future interactions. Session failed to explore underlying function of drinking (need for belonging, peer connection, identity), making any behavioral strategy unlikely to persist when peer influence is present.

---

### Key Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative partnership** — Actively seek Marcus's input; allow his responses to shape session direction
2. **Evoke intrinsic motivation through values exploration** — Move beyond surface reasons to explore deeper values, goals, and what he wants for his life
3. **Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk** — Acknowledge both reasons for drinking and potential concerns without judgment
4. **Amplify and explore change talk when it emerges** — Follow up with curiosity on weak change talk to help Marcus elaborate and strengthen his own change language
5. **Actively support autonomy** — Explicitly affirm Marcus's right to choose and his expertise about himself
6. **Deepen empathy through complex reflections** — Capture emotional nuance and underlying needs beneath surface statements
7. **Avoid advice-giving without permission and collaboration** — Ask permission and collaborate on generating ideas before offering strategies
8. **Explore ambivalence rather than accepting low motivation** — When Marcus rates change importance as 2-3, explore what's beneath this rating
9. **Maintain MI spirit** — Before each session, check in with yourself on partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation
10. **Develop skill in handling precontemplation** — Recognize Marcus is in precontemplation; goal is to build discrepancy and explore ambivalence, not convince him to change

---

### Immediate Next Steps

1. Commit to asking permission before giving advice in next 5 sessions
2. Practice values-exploration questions in every session
3. Learn to use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk
4. Develop pre-session MI spirit check-in to maintain partnership and evocation focus
