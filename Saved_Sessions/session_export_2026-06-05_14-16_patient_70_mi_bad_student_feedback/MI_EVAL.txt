### Evocation

When client offers emerging change talk ("save money," "not smell like smoke"), psychiatrist dismisses it as insufficient rather than amplifying. Few open questions asked; when asked, not followed by genuine curiosity or exploration. No questions designed to help client discover own values or motivations.

## Summary of Findings

### Critical Deficiencies

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and is **clinically harmful** to the client. The psychiatrist operates from a confrontational, expert-driven model directly contraindicated for ambivalent, resistant clients.

**Core Problems:**
- **Acceptance:** Judgmental language and disapproval of ambivalence
- **Empathy:** No reflective listening or genuine understanding of client perspective
- **Autonomy:** Systematic undermining through controlling directives
- **Evocation:** Telling rather than asking; suppression of client's emerging motivation
- **Partnership:** Expert positioning with no genuine power-sharing
- **Change Talk:** Active suppression rather than amplification
- **Sustain Talk:** Direct confrontation that strengthens resistance

### Client Impact

Client enters with ambivalence but behavioral compliance. By session end, client is increasingly dismissive ("Yeah, whatever") and explicitly states "I'm not ready" and "I'm not making any promises." **The psychiatrist's approach has decreased rather than increased client motivation for change.**

Client's explicit feedback—"Every time I hear that stuff, it just feels like pressure"—is ignored, and the psychiatrist intensifies the same counterproductive approach.

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Fundamental Reorientation Required:** Shift from confrontational to collaborative, client-centered model
2. **Formal MI Training:** Attend MINT-certified MI training immediately
3. **Structured Supervision:** Engage in monthly MI supervision with recorded session review
4. **Stop Confronting:** Eliminate direct arguments against sustain talk
5. **Amplify Change Talk:** When client offers own reasons for change, explore and deepen them
6. **Respect Autonomy:** Remove "you have to/must/need to" language; replace with collaborative language
7. **Develop Genuine Curiosity:** Ask questions you genuinely want answered, not rhetorical pressure questions
8. **Validate Experience:** Normalize struggle and affirm client worth
9. **Establish Partnership:** Position client as expert on own life; invite their ideas to shape session

### Prognosis Without Intervention

Without fundamental reorientation, this psychiatrist will continue to harm clients through an approach that activates resistance rather than evoking motivation. Clients will become more defensive, disengaged, and unlikely to return for follow-up or engage authentically in treatment.

**This is not a minor adjustment—it requires complete reorientation of clinical approach and formal MI training.**
