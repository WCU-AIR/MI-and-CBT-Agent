## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Therapist Performance Summary:**

The therapist demonstrated foundational interpersonal skills (warmth, genuineness, non-judgmental stance) but failed to deliver evidence-based CBT. The session score of 9/66 reflects critical gaps in CBT competence, most notably the complete absence of safety planning for a high-risk adolescent with recent high-lethality suicidal behavior.

**Key Concerns:**

1. **Safety Planning (URGENT):** No safety plan developed despite patient's overdose requiring intubation within hours of suicidal ideation. This is a clinical failure with potential life-or-death consequences.

2. **Lack of CBT Implementation:** No cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, behavioral experiments, exposure, problem-solving, emotion regulation skills, or homework assigned. Session was supportive counseling, not CBT.

3. **Repetitive, Ineffective Advice:** Therapist recommended positive thinking five times despite patient explicitly stating it felt "fake." No adaptation or exploration of why this approach wasn't working.

4. **Failure to Identify Core Maintaining Factors:** Therapist did not explore rumination, catastrophic thinking, behavioral avoidance, or hopelessness—the central factors maintaining Hannah's distress.

5. **Excessive Self-Disclosure:** Five+ personal anecdotes shifted focus away from patient and minimized severity of her presentation (comparing adjustment disorder to exam stress and pet loss).

6. **No Guided Discovery:** Therapist told rather than asked. Socratic method entirely absent.

7. **No Collaboration:** Patient positioned as passive recipient of advice rather than active partner in treatment.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

- Assign immediate supervision focused on safety planning and CBT protocols for adolescent suicidal behavior.
- Provide training in cognitive conceptualization, thought records, behavioral activation, and emotion regulation skills.
- Practice Socratic questioning and guided discovery in role-play.
- Require therapist to develop and implement safety plan with patient before next clinical session.
- Consider pairing therapist with experienced CBT supervisor for co-therapy or observation until competence improves.

**Prognosis for Patient Care:**

Without immediate intervention and skill development, patient outcomes are at risk. Hannah requires evidence-based CBT to prevent suicidal recurrence and build coping skills for managing disappointment and anger. Current approach is inadequate.
