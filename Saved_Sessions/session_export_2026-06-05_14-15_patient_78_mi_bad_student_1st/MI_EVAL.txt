### Summary of Clinical Findings

**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on specific target behavior (calorie tracking app)
- Shows up and attempts to address patient's health concern
- Prevents session from becoming completely aimless through behavioral focus

**Critical Deficiencies:**

This session represents a **near-complete absence of Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist operates from an authoritarian expert model directly contrary to MI principles and actively harmful to patient well-being.

**Core Failures:**

1. **Rejection of Emotional Experience:** Patient explicitly states she feels alone, overwhelmed, and scared. Rather than validating these as important barriers to change, psychiatrist dismisses them as "excuses." Patient requests support and understanding; clinician refuses.

2. **Suppression of Intrinsic Motivation:** Patient demonstrates change talk and is already engaging in health behaviors (regular workouts). Psychiatrist responds with pressure and demands, causing motivation to diminish over session. Patient moves from "I want to do better" to "I'm just tired" and "I'm just scared."

3. **Coercive Pressure Rather Than Collaboration:** Repeated ultimatums ("Are you going to do it or not?" asked 6+ times), demands ("you must," "you have to"), and framing non-compliance as personal failure ("you're just choosing to fail") constitute psychological coercion, not MI.

4. **Failure to Understand Patient's World:** Never explores patient's experience, barriers, values, or strengths. Patient mentions feeling alone at home—critical contextual information dismissed as irrelevant.

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is **actively harmful** to patient's motivation and likelihood of change. Patient enters with ambivalence and leaves with increased hopelessness and withdrawal. Change talk has diminished; sustain talk has strengthened. Patient has experienced clinician as rejecting and coercive rather than supportive.

**Research Implications:**

This approach predicts poor outcomes: decreased intrinsic motivation, increased resistance, lower treatment engagement, higher dropout rates. Patient's final statement ("I'm just scared it's going to make me feel worse, not better") reflects damage done by interaction. She is now more afraid of change, not less.

---

### Critical Recommendations

**Immediate Actions Required:**

1. **Fundamental Shift to Collaborative Partnership:** Replace all directive language ("you must") with exploratory language ("we could," "let's explore together")

2. **Develop Genuine Empathic Understanding:** Use reflective listening to demonstrate accurate understanding of emotional experience before offering guidance

3. **Validate Rather Than Dismiss:** Treat patient's feelings (loneliness, overwhelm, fear) as valid information about barriers, not character flaws

4. **Evoke Rather Than Impose:** Ask open-ended questions to draw out patient's own reasons for change, values, and ideas

5. **Amplify Change Talk:** When patient expresses motivation, reflect it back and build on it rather than meeting it with pressure

6. **Soften Resistance:** Use double-sided reflections and reframing rather than direct confrontation

7. **Respect Autonomy:** Eliminate ultimatums; offer genuine choice and respect patient's right to decide

8. **Establish Unconditional Positive Regard:** Communicate that patient is valued as person regardless of compliance; affirm efforts and strengths

9. **Formal MI Training:** Seek certified Motivational Interviewing training (2-3 day intensive), supervised practice, and ongoing consultation

10. **Ongoing Supervision:** Join MI consultation group; record and review sessions with supervisor

### Competency Assessment

**Current MI Competency Level:** NOT PRACTICING MOTIVATIONAL INTERVIEWING

This clinician is not practicing MI. The approach is directive, coercive, and rejecting. It violates core MI principles of collaboration, autonomy support, evocation, and acceptance. This requires fundamental reorientation to MI philosophy and practice, not refinement.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** ACTIVELY UNDERMINING MOTIVATION

Patient is less motivated at end than beginning. She is more hopeless, more withdrawn, and more afraid of change. This approach predicts poor treatment engagement, high dropout rates, and poor outcomes.

**This session demonstrates practices inconsistent with evidence-based care and potentially harmful to patient.** The psychiatrist should not continue using this approach without immediate training and supervision in Motivational Interviewing or other evidence-based collaborative approaches.
