## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (smoking reduction)
- Concrete, actionable plan developed with specific targets
- Basic warmth and respect; clinician does not demean
- Acknowledgment that change is difficult; normalizes slipping up
- Aisha agrees to plan and expresses willingness to try
- Well-organized, goal-directed session

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- Excessive directiveness undermines autonomy and collaboration
- Minimal evocation of intrinsic motivation; spontaneous change talk not amplified
- Weak partnership; clinician assumes expert role throughout
- Unaddressed ambivalence; minimization allowed to stand unchallenged
- Missed opportunities for depth when Aisha offers change talk or core values
- Sustain talk not softened; barriers met with advice rather than exploration
- Agenda set unilaterally without exploring client readiness or priorities

**Impact on Sustained Change:**
Aisha's confidence is only 5/10, and intrinsic motivation has not been deeply explored or strengthened. She is likely following psychiatrist's recommendations due to compliance with authority rather than discovering her own compelling reasons for change. Low confidence combined with fragile motivation suggests **moderate to low likelihood of sustained behavior change**. When facing evening stress (primary trigger), she may revert to baseline behavior. Failure to address minimization means sustain talk remains active and could erode motivation over time.

**Immediate Priority Recommendations:**
1. Shift from expert/prescriber to collaborative/evocative partner
2. Learn to amplify change talk through strategic follow-up questions
3. Develop skill in handling ambivalence with double-sided reflections
4. Replace "should" language with open-ended questions
5. Explore and deepen client's core values and intrinsic motivation
