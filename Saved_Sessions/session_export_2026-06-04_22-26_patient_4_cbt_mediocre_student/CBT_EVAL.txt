## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR

**Strengths:**
- Demonstrated basic warmth, professionalism, and non-judgmental stance
- Accurately reflected patient's explicit concerns
- Attempted multiple CBT interventions (cognitive reframing, positive thinking, behavioral activation)
- Patient remained engaged and cooperative
- Assigned between-session work

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **No CBT conceptualization:** Failed to identify safety-seeking behavior and hypervigilance as core maintaining factors
- **Superficial cognitive work:** Cognitive restructuring reduced to vague "think positive" suggestions without evidence examination
- **Lack of structure:** No explicit agenda, priorities, or time management
- **Minimal collaboration:** Offered solutions rather than collaboratively exploring them
- **Weak guided discovery:** Relied on direct advice rather than Socratic questioning
- **Vague homework:** Lacked specificity, rationale, and connection to core treatment goals
- **Missing techniques:** No thought records, behavioral experiments, exposure work, or psychoeducation

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Intensive supervision on CBT fundamentals (assessment, case formulation, maintaining factors)
2. Structured training in cognitive restructuring with thought records and behavioral experiments
3. Practice with guided discovery and Socratic questioning
4. Training in agenda-setting and session structure
5. Observation of expert CBT sessions
6. Role-play practice with feedback

**Risk Assessment:**
- **Likelihood of therapeutic change:** Low. Patient may experience temporary relief from supportive environment but unlikely to achieve lasting symptom improvement without targeting core maintaining factors.
- **Risk of harm:** Moderate. Suggested interventions (distraction, positive thinking) may reinforce safety-seeking cycle. Continued ineffective practice without supervision risks patient demoralization and symptom escalation.
