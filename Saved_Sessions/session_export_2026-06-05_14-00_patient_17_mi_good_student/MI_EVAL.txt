### Evocation

**Comments:** Skillfully evokes Anika's own motivations, values, and resources throughout most of session. Effective questions draw out intrinsic reasons: "What do you think makes you feel confident about giving it a solid try?" Clinician asks about past successes rather than suggesting coping strategies. However, missed opportunities for deeper evocation: doesn't explore what it would feel like to handle pressure without vaping or what strengths Anika possesses for change. Session focuses heavily on extrinsic motivation (basketball, avoiding suspension) without exploring personal values around health, autonomy, or self-respect that might fuel longer-term change.

Inclusive language emphasizes shared work: "You're building a toolkit," "You're showing great insight." Clinician affirms Anika's wisdom in generating strategies rather than co-opting them. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces Anika's change talk throughout. Key statements amplified: desire ("I wanna play well"), ability ("I'm pretty sure I can stop for a month"), reasons ("I play better when I'm not high"), commitment ("I'm gonna try to stick with the plan"). Reflective listening strategically amplifies rather than letting change talk pass unnoticed. However, missed opportunities: when Anika identifies stress as trigger, clinician could evoke more ability talk: "What else could you do? What have you done before when stressed without using?" Additionally, doesn't strengthen tentative commitment language ("try" vs. "will"). Doesn't ask about concrete benefits: "What will be different about your basketball performance when you're not vaping?"

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Initial double-sided reflection skillfully acknowledges both minimization and concerns without reinforcing harm denial. However, inconsistent follow-through: when Anika says "It's not like smoking cigarettes—it feels way safer," clinician reflects without gently exploring assumption. Most critically, when Anika identifies stress as key barrier ("When I'm stressed, vaping feels like the easiest way to feel better"), clinician moves to solution-building rather than deeply exploring this sustain talk. Premature pivot misses opportunity to address emotional function of use. Additionally, doesn't address implicit sustain talk in seasonal goal—doesn't explore what happens after basketball season or whether goal represents true change or temporary compliance.

## Summary of Strengths

- **Exceptional autonomy support:** Consistently positions client as decision-maker with empowering language
- **Strong collaborative stance:** Genuine curiosity; follows client's lead; affirms client's own ideas
- **Solid reflective listening:** Accurate perspective-taking; dual-sided reflections capturing ambivalence
- **Effective change talk reinforcement:** Strategically amplifies client's reasons, ability, and commitment
- **Warm, nonjudgmental acceptance:** Genuine affirmations tied to observable strengths
- **Clear, appropriate direction:** Maintains focus on target behavior with progressive goal-clarification
- **Good partnership:** Positions clinician as supporter; affirms client expertise; shares power

---

## Summary of Areas for Improvement

- **Sustain talk management:** Inconsistent handling of minimization; doesn't deeply explore stress-related barriers before problem-solving
- **Depth of evocation:** Focuses on extrinsic motivation without exploring deeper intrinsic values
- **Emotional exploration:** Moves to solution-building before sitting with emotional difficulty
- **Commitment language:** Doesn't strengthen tentative commitment ("try") to firmer commitment
- **Long-term perspective:** Accepts seasonal goal without exploring post-season relapse risk
- **Verification of authenticity:** Doesn't check whether stated confidence/goals feel genuinely owned or represent compliance

---

## Key Recommendations for Skill Development

1. **Deepen exploration of stress and emotional regulation** before moving to coping strategies
2. **Strengthen handling of sustain talk** through gentle, curious exploration rather than simple reflection
3. **Explore and clarify true goals** beyond seasonal compliance
4. **Evoke deeper intrinsic values** beyond basketball performance
5. **Verify authenticity** of stated confidence and goals through collaborative checking-in
6. **Strengthen commitment language** by gently exploring movement from "I think I can" to "I'm going to"
7. **Explore concrete benefits** of change using vivid, sensory language
8. **Co-construct solutions** more actively rather than simply affirming client ideas
9. **Normalize relapse and struggle** as part of change process
10. **Invite true collaboration** on follow-up planning rather than suggesting timelines

---

## Clinical Impression

This session demonstrates **solid, competent MI practice** with strong autonomy support, collaboration, and acceptance. The psychiatrist skillfully maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and effectively reinforces change talk. However, the session has **significant limitations in depth and strategic focus** that reduce its effectiveness in evoking sustainable motivation for change. Most critically, the clinician inconsistently handles sustain talk, doesn't deeply explore the emotional function of substance use, accepts a seasonal goal without exploring post-season relapse risk, and doesn't verify whether stated goals feel genuinely owned. The session will likely result in short-term abstinence during basketball season but carries **high risk for relapse afterward** due to insufficient exploration of intrinsic motivation and underlying emotional drivers. With focused skill development in the areas outlined above, this clinician could significantly enhance their effectiveness in evoking lasting behavior change.
