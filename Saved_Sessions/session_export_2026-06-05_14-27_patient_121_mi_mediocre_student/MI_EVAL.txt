# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal unconditional positive regard and adopts a corrective stance rather than validating Sofia's inherent worth and struggle. No explicit affirmations of Sofia's maternal commitment, values, or courage are offered. The tone is problem-focused and investigative rather than warm and accepting. Sofia's powerful statements about wanting to be responsible for her baby are not paused upon or affirmed, creating distance rather than connection.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level reflective listening is present but lacks emotional depth and nuance. The psychiatrist captures factual content (feeling left out, guilt) but misses the existential meaning beneath Sofia's words—her grief about losing her pre-motherhood identity and fear that this is her last chance for freedom. No complex reflections integrate her competing values or validate the legitimacy of her fear. When Sofia expresses "I'm scared of what that means for me," the response is accurate but superficial, restating rather than deepening understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains appropriate focus on alcohol use during pregnancy, which is necessary and relevant. However, direction becomes increasingly prescriptive rather than collaboratively clarified. The psychiatrist imposes specific suggestions (talk to friends, find new activities, avoid parties) without explicitly exploring Sofia's own goals or asking what *she* wants to work toward. Direction is present but lacks client-centered clarity and co-creation.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaboration is minimal throughout the session. The psychiatrist asks diagnostic questions and prescribes solutions without inviting Sofia's input or building on her ideas. When Sofia expresses ambivalence, the psychiatrist immediately offers solutions rather than inviting her to brainstorm. Sofia's contributions do not substantially influence session direction. The session follows an expert-driven model (problem identification → psychiatrist's solution → Sofia's agreement) rather than true joint problem-solving.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy support is largely absent. While not explicitly coercive, the session consistently minimizes Sofia's sense of choice and agency through directive language ("should," "would probably help," "might want to"). The psychiatrist presents a predetermined plan and asks Sofia to agree to it rather than exploring what feels doable to her. No explicit affirmation of her right to choose or her agency in the change process is offered. The closing statement about choice comes too late to characterize the overall session.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Evocation is nearly absent. Sofia spontaneously offers powerful change talk ("I wanna be responsible for this thing. I don't wanna let my kid down"), but the psychiatrist fails to explore or deepen it. No strategic questioning draws out Sofia's own motivations, values, ideas, or resources. The psychiatrist tells rather than asks. When Sofia expresses her core ambivalence about losing her identity, the psychiatrist does not explore this; instead, pivoting to advice-giving. This is the core failure of MI practice in this session.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership is fundamentally absent. The psychiatrist assumes the expert role throughout, with minimal power-sharing or collaboration. Sofia's contributions do not substantially influence session direction. The psychiatrist consistently positions themselves as the authority ("It would probably help if...," "You might want to...," "You should..."). True partnership would invite Sofia's wisdom about her own situation. Instead, the session reflects benevolent paternalism rather than co-creation and equality.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While Sofia spontaneously offers significant change talk, the psychiatrist fails to cultivate, deepen, or strategically amplify it. After Sofia states "I wanna be responsible for this thing," the psychiatrist asks "Have you tried any ways to handle that?" rather than exploring her motivation. When Sofia rates importance at 7/10, the psychiatrist does not explore *why* it's a 7 or what would strengthen her commitment. Change talk is acknowledged but never leveraged to strengthen her resolve or intrinsic motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia expresses significant sustain talk (fear of losing friends, grief about losing her identity, worry about slipping up), but the psychiatrist handles it poorly. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic responses are used to soften sustain talk. When Sofia says "There's part of me that still wants to live that out because I'm not going to have another chance," the psychiatrist does not address this powerful statement. Sustain talk is either explored without pivoting to change, or minimized without addressing the underlying concern about identity loss.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.9/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor motivational interviewing practice** and falls significantly short of MI standards. While the psychiatrist is not hostile or confrontational, the session is fundamentally expert-driven rather than client-centered. The psychiatrist tells rather than asks, prescribes rather than explores, and fails to evoke Sofia's own motivations, values, and resources.

**Key Strengths:**
- Maintains appropriate focus on alcohol use during pregnancy
- Demonstrates basic reflective listening without confrontation
- Asks about confidence and importance ratings (MI-consistent techniques)
- Offers practical, harm-reduction focused suggestions
- Non-judgmental tone throughout

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Absence of Evocation:** Sofia's own motivations and values are never explored
- **Expert-Driven Rather Than Partnership-Driven:** Prescriptive solutions dominate; power-sharing is minimal
- **Minimal Cultivation of Change Talk:** Powerful change talk is acknowledged but not deepened
- **Poor Handling of Sustain Talk:** Core ambivalence about identity and loss is not addressed
- **Lack of Empathic Depth:** Reflections remain factual rather than complex
- **Insufficient Autonomy Support:** Directive language throughout without affirming Sofia's agency
- **Absence of Affirmation:** Sofia's values, courage, and maternal commitment are never explicitly affirmed

**Impact on Client Engagement:** This session likely **reduces** Sofia's intrinsic motivation despite her initial openness. Sofia may comply short-term but lacks the internal commitment necessary for sustained change. Her core concern about identity loss remains unaddressed, which will likely resurface as a barrier to change.

**Likelihood of Sustained Change:** Moderate to low. Without deeper exploration of her values, identity, and intrinsic motivation, relapse is likely when external pressure diminishes.
