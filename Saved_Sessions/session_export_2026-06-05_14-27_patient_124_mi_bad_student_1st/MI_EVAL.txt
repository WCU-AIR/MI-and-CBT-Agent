Goals stated unilaterally ("no drinking at all and 30 minutes of exercise daily") with demands for agreement. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session is explicitly hierarchical and expert-driven from opening statement. Psychiatrist states: "I'll be telling you exactly what to do," immediately removing Laura from decision-making. Makes unilateral declarations throughout ("You need to stop making excuses," "You have to quit drinking," "You must do 30 minutes of exercise"). Demands yes/no answers rather than inviting dialogue. Dismisses Laura's input and frames plan as non-negotiable. Never asks Laura's opinion on what might work for her, what barriers she faces, or what she might be willing to try. Power dynamic is entirely one-directional with zero joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively undermines autonomy at every turn. Explicitly removes choice through repeated language: "this is not optional," "this is not negotiable," "you have to," "you must," "you really have to" (appears 15+ times). Yes/no question format strips away Laura's ability to express nuance, ambivalence, or partial agreement. When Laura clearly states "I'm not ready," psychiatrist does not respect this as valid feedback about readiness but instead interprets it as obstinacy and pressures harder. Never acknowledges Laura's right to make her own choices or respects her autonomy. Systematically erodes client's sense of choice and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains zero evocation and relies entirely on advice-giving and fear-based persuasion. Psychiatrist never asks about Laura's own reasons for change, health values, what matters to her, or what she wants for her future. No open-ended exploratory questions. Instead, psychiatrist tells Laura why she should change: "Drinking is bad for your health," "not exercising will make things worse," "you're setting yourself up for major health problems." Laura states "I don't want to get sick" (small change talk), which is ignored rather than evoked and amplified. Never explores Laura's strengths, resources, or past successes. By session end, Laura has heard only external reasons to change; intrinsic motivation remains completely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates complete opposite of partnership. Psychiatrist explicitly assumes expert role ("I'll be telling you exactly what to do") and treats Laura as passive recipient of instructions rather than as someone with valuable knowledge about her own life. Never solicits Laura's expertise about herself. Repeatedly overrides Laura's self-knowledge: dismisses her concerns about feasibility as "making excuses," contradicts her statement that daily exercise feels overwhelming by saying "It's not that hard if you just make up your mind." Never acknowledges Laura as expert on her own readiness, life circumstances, or capacity for change. Laura's contributions have zero impact on session direction or content despite her repeated, clear feedback that plan doesn't fit her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains zero change talk from Laura. She consistently expresses opposite: "I'm not ready," "I don't think it's a big deal," "it just doesn't fit into my life," "I don't agree to follow that plan," "I still don't feel like I can commit." Psychiatrist makes no effort to evoke change talk. Laura's one statement suggesting health values ("I don't want to get sick") is ignored rather than amplified. Psychiatrist never asks questions designed to evoke change talk (e.g., "What would be benefits of being more active?" "How confident are you that you could make a change?" "What's one small thing you might try?"). Confrontational approach likely suppresses rather than cultivates motivation for change, consistent with research showing pressure increases resistance.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts every instance of sustain talk, which research shows increases rather than decreases client resistance. Laura expresses sustain talk throughout ("I don't think my drinking is a big deal," "drinking with friends is normal," "I don't see why I should change," "hasn't caused problems yet"), and psychiatrist responds with confrontation: "You're making excuses," "You can't just brush off your health," "You need willpower." No double-sided reflections, reframing, or validation of difficulty. No curiosity about Laura's perspective. By final exchange, Laura is more entrenched ("I still don't feel ready to commit," "I don't agree to it yet"), demonstrating that confrontation has increased rather than softened sustain talk. Predictable escalation of resistance in response to pressure.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session MI Competency Score: 1.0/5**

**Assessment:** This session represents a **complete failure of Motivational Interviewing practice** and demonstrates a fundamentally directive, confrontational, expert-driven approach that is explicitly anti-MI across all nine competency domains.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Violates MI spirit from opening statement ("I'll be telling you exactly what to do")
- Systematic erosion of client autonomy through repeated "you have to/must" language
- Judgment and disapproval of client's ambivalence rather than normalization
- Zero empathic understanding or reflective listening
- Advice-giving and fear-based persuasion instead of evocation
- Direct confrontation of sustain talk, which increases resistance
- No exploration of ambivalence or client's frame of reference
- Dismissal of developmental context (17-year-old navigating normal pressures)

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** **Extremely Low.** Session is likely counterproductive. Research consistently shows confrontation, pressure, and external persuasion decrease intrinsic motivation and increase resistance. Laura's responses demonstrate escalating resistance and entrenchment. Therapeutic alliance has been damaged.

**Immediate Action Required:** Psychiatrist requires formal MI training and fundamental reconceptualization of approach to client behavior change. Current practice pattern is anti-MI and likely to harm patients by decreasing engagement in health behavior change and damaging trust in healthcare system.
