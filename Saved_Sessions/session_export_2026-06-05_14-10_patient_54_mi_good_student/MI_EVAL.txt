#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Primary strategy is reflection and validation, which is appropriate but limited. ## Overall Summary

### **Overall Session Score: 3.9/5**

---

### Strengths

- **Genuine collaborative partnership:** Establishes true partnership from opening question; positions Isabella as expert on her own priorities; co-constructs solutions rather than imposing them
- **Consistent empathic validation:** Accurate reflections capture ambivalence, fear, and loneliness without judgment; creates psychological safety for client with fear-based avoidance
- **Strong autonomy support:** Explicitly respects Isabella's pace; affirms her right to choose; avoids pressure or directive language; honors her concerns about feeling pressured
- **Skillful resource evocation:** Draws out Isabella's own strategies from job interview; invites her to apply them rather than imposing solutions; validates her self-awareness
- **Strategic amplification of change talk:** Reflects and reinforces motivations and demonstrated capability; helps Isabella move from vague hesitation to concrete planning
- **Appropriate pacing and respect:** Honors hesitation; validates small steps (exploring activities) without pressure to commit to attendance; maintains collaborative power-sharing
- **Progressive client engagement:** Isabella's responses become increasingly specific and engaged throughout session, suggesting effective building of intrinsic motivation

---

### Areas for Improvement

- **Deepen empathic reflection:** Move beyond surface-level reflections to explore deeper emotional nuances; when Isabella mentions fear of not fitting in, ask follow-up questions about what that specifically means to her
- **Reduce subtle clinician interpretation:** Several moments where clinician interprets Isabella's experience rather than evoking her own interpretation; shift from "That shows resilience" to "What does that tell you about yourself?"
- **More strategic cultivation of change talk:** While change talk is amplified, missed opportunities to deepen it through targeted follow-up questions (e.g., "What makes that feel okay to you?" or "What would need to happen for that to move to a 4?")
- **Stronger softening of sustain talk:** Primarily validates sustain talk rather than strategically pivoting toward change talk; implement consistent use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge both hesitation and openness
- **Reduce prescriptive language:** Phrases like "gently stretching your comfort zone" subtly impose clinician's agenda; replace with collaborative framing that invites Isabella's determination
- **Invite Isabella's determination of significance:** Reduce statements where clinician judges what's important; ask "What does that mean to you?" rather than "It's important to recognize..."

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

The psychiatrist's MI approach is largely effective in maintaining Isabella's engagement and building motivation for change. The consistent validation, autonomy support, and collaborative stance create psychological safety essential for a client presenting with fear and avoidance. Isabella's progression throughout the session—from hesitant agreement to concrete planning and identification of her own support needs—demonstrates the clinician's MI approach is working to build intrinsic motivation.

However, the session's potential impact is somewhat limited by occasional subtle shifts toward clinician interpretation and direction. These moments, while minor, risk undermining collaborative partnership and may reduce Isabella's sense of ownership over her change process. The limited strategic softening of sustain talk means her fears remain somewhat unaddressed, which could limit confidence in taking action. The session likely increases Isabella's willingness to explore activities and may support initial engagement, but more consistent strategic use of MI techniques would enhance likelihood of sustained behavior change and deeper motivation.

---

### Recommendations for Skill Development

1. **Deepen empathic reflection** through follow-up questions exploring emotional nuance and specific fears
2. **Reduce subtle interpretation** by replacing clinician judgments with open questions inviting Isabella's own meaning-making
3. **Strategically deepen change talk** using targeted follow-up questions after Isabella offers motivation or capability statements
4. **Use double-sided reflections** consistently to soften sustain talk while maintaining empathy and pivoting toward change talk
5. **Explicitly invite Isabella's determination** of what's important rather than clinician judgment
6. **Strengthen softening of sustain talk** by reframing fears as surmountable and connecting them to demonstrated capability
7. **Reduce prescriptive language** and replace with collaborative framing that invites her perspective
8. **Deepen exploration of ambivalence** by explicitly asking about both sides of her mixed feelings

---

**Overall Assessment:** This is a competent MI session demonstrating solid foundational skills in collaboration, empathy, and autonomy support. The clinician successfully builds rapport and evokes change talk; Isabella's engagement increases throughout. However, the session falls short of exemplary MI practice due to occasional subtle shifts toward clinician interpretation, inconsistent strategic softening of sustain talk, and missed opportunities to deeply cultivate Isabella's own change talk. With focused attention to the recommendations above—particularly reducing subtle interpretation, using double-sided reflections consistently, and asking targeted follow-up questions—this clinician would demonstrate excellent MI practice.
