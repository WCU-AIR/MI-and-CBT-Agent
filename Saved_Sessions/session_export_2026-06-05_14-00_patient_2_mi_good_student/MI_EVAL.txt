# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Strong affirmations of Ahmed's practical approach ("That balance you've found is a strength in itself") communicate genuine respect for his autonomy without moral judgment about cigar use. However, earlier acknowledgment of Ahmed's core values around family and work stability would have strengthened the acceptance foundation and created earlier alignment between the clinician's stance and Ahmed's intrinsic motivations.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking are demonstrated throughout. The clinician accurately captures Ahmed's surface-level minimization and underlying values ("your motivation to stay on top of your dental health is deeply connected to wanting to avoid surprises that could interfere with your ability to care for your family"). Nuanced reflection of Ahmed's practical, non-anxious temperament is evident. However, empathy could be deeper and more emotionally attuned. Ahmed mentions gum irritation multiple times but is never asked to explore what that irritation feels like or whether it causes concern—a missed opportunity to deepen understanding of his ambivalence.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Direction is present but loose and somewhat diffuse. While dental health is discussed, the session lacks strategic focus on the specific health concern (gum and throat irritation and its connection to cigar use) that prompted the referral. The conversation drifts toward Ahmed's general approach to health management rather than maintaining focus on the target behavior. By session end, Ahmed has committed only to monitoring gums and keeping appointments—harm-reduction steps that do not address the underlying behavior (cigar use) or the clinical concern that prompted the referral. More directed exploration of the connection between mini-cigars and gum irritation would strengthen focus.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance is demonstrated throughout. The opening question ("What would you like to focus on?") immediately establishes partnership and invites Ahmed's input into the agenda. The clinician consistently seeks Ahmed's perspective rather than imposing one and frames Ahmed as the authority on his own experience. When Ahmed states he is not ready to quit cigars, the clinician does not argue but collaboratively identifies what Ahmed is willing to do. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly inviting Ahmed's ideas about solutions and co-constructing next steps rather than the clinician suggesting them.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout the session. The clinician consistently emphasizes Ahmed's right to choose and frames him as the decision-maker ("It's clear you know what you can handle and are prioritizing what feels realistic and important to you right now"). When Ahmed explicitly states he is not ready to quit cigars, the clinician does not pressure, persuade, or manipulate. Prescriptive language ("you should," "you need to") is avoided in favor of collaborative framing ("you're open to," "you're choosing"). Ahmed's self-efficacy is reinforced consistently. This represents a clear strength of the session.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Evocation—drawing out the client's own motivations, values, and reasons for change—is minimal and inconsistently applied. While the clinician asks some evocative questions early ("What are some reasons you might want to keep your teeth and mouth as healthy as possible?"), Ahmed deflects and the clinician does not pursue this missed opportunity. Most critically, Ahmed mentions gum irritation multiple times but is never asked to explore what that means to him or whether it concerns him. By session end, Ahmed has not articulated a single personal reason for changing his cigar use. This represents a fundamental gap in MI practice and significantly limits the session's effectiveness in evoking motivation for behavior change.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance is maintained throughout most of the session with genuine power-sharing. Ahmed's contributions substantially shape the direction of conversation. The clinician does not assume an expert role or attempt to override Ahmed's perspective. However, there is a subtle shift toward the end of the session where the clinician becomes slightly more directive. After Ahmed rates his confidence at 8/10, the clinician asks "Is there anything that might help support you?" rather than inviting Ahmed to generate his own solutions. A more partnership-oriented approach would ask Ahmed to identify what will help him stick with his commitments.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Critical deficiency. The clinician does not strategically evoke, amplify, or reinforce change talk. Ahmed's only explicit change talk consists of two confidence/importance ratings (6/10 and 8/10), which are not built upon or explored. When Ahmed articulates sustain talk ("I don't think it's a big deal," "I'm not ready to quit"), the clinician reflects and accepts without strategically evoking competing change talk. The clinician never asks questions designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change regarding cigar use. By session end, Ahmed has not articulated a single personal reason to reduce cigar use—only agreement to monitor gums and keep appointments, which do not constitute change talk about the target behavior.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ineffective response to sustain talk. Ahmed articulates multiple statements against change ("I don't think it's a big deal," "I'm not ready to quit or anything because I don't really see the point"), and the clinician's typical response is to reflect and accept without strategically redirecting. Double-sided reflections that acknowledge both Ahmed's enjoyment of cigars and his gum irritation are absent. The clinician never uses reframing, amplification of concern, or strategic questioning to soften Ahmed's minimization. When Ahmed says his gums are "nothing serious," the clinician does not explore his threshold for concern. This represents a missed opportunity to gently introduce competing perspectives without confrontation.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- **Exceptional autonomy support** with consistent reinforcement of Ahmed's right to choose
- **Warm, nonjudgmental acceptance** that maintains unconditional positive regard throughout
- **Solid reflective listening** that accurately captures Ahmed's perspective and values
- **Genuine partnership** with shared power and Ahmed's contributions shaping the session
- **Strong foundational MI stance** embodying partnership, acceptance, and compassion

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Minimal evocation of change talk** with missed opportunities to explore Ahmed's gum irritation and intrinsic motivations
- **Weak cultivation of change talk** with no strategic amplification or reinforcement of Ahmed's own language favoring change
- **Ineffective softening of sustain talk** without double-sided reflections or exploration of Ahmed's own concerns
- **Loose strategic direction** lacking focus on the specific health concern (gum/throat irritation and cigar connection)
- **Limited depth of empathy** regarding emotional nuance and ambivalence exploration

### Clinical Impact
While the warm, respectful relationship is a strong foundation, the session does not move Ahmed toward examining his own reasons for changing cigar use or recognizing the connection between his behavior and gum irritation. Ahmed leaves with his minimization intact and no personal commitment to reduce use. The session prioritizes acceptance and autonomy support at the expense of evocation and cultivation of change talk—limiting its effectiveness as true motivational interviewing.
