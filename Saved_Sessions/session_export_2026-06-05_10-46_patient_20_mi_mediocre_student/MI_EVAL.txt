No explicit affirmation of client's right to choose or exploration of what Anya genuinely wants versus what she feels obligated to do. Ambivalence about leaving social circle not addressed. False autonomy support statement ("it's your choice, but taking these steps could really help") undermines autonomy message by immediately following with directive. Client's self-determination not meaningfully respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2

Substantial spontaneous change talk by Anya (dreams of travel, desire for stability, motherhood values, regret about money lost) fails to be strategically evoked or amplified. No open-ended questions designed to deepen motivation: "What would be different if you stopped gambling?" "Tell me more about Egypt?" ### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Partnership fundamentally compromised. Psychiatrist consistently assumes expert role, determining agenda, identifying problems, and prescribing solutions. Power held by clinician, not shared with client. When Anya offers own ideas (avoiding pubs, cutting cards), clinician validates but then adds prescriptions rather than exploring deeply. Final summary presents plan as jointly constructed when largely clinician-generated. True partnership would involve asking "What do you think might work best for you?" and allowing client expertise about own life to substantially shape session direction.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inconsistent skill in handling sustain talk. Clinician avoids confrontation and does not reinforce status quo arguments, which is appropriate. However, sophisticated MI techniques absent: no double-sided reflections acknowledging both sustain talk and change talk; no reframing of barriers; insufficient exploration of meaning behind sustain talk. When client expresses fear ("it's scary"), doubt ("I'm not 100% sure"), or social pressure ("my friends are all into it"), clinician does not explore these barriers or use them as opportunities to strengthen motivation. Sustain talk largely ignored rather than strategically addressed.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 2.2/5 – Below Average MI Competence**

### Key Strengths:
- Non-confrontational stance maintained throughout
- Acknowledgment of client's stated values (travel, family, stability)
- Basic session structure with beginning, middle, and end
- Openness to client's agenda (gambling introduced by client)
- Use of scaling questions (confidence, importance)

### Critical Areas for Improvement:

**1. Shift from Expert to Collaborative Stance (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
Clinician must begin by genuinely asking what client thinks the problem is before prescribing solutions. Replace "Maybe you should think about..." with "What do you think might work for you?"

**2. Strategically Evoke and Amplify Change Talk**
When client expresses dreams, values, or desire for change, pause and deepen with follow-up questions rather than moving to problem-solving. Example: "Tell me more about Egypt. What would that dream mean for your life?"

**3. Use Double-Sided Reflections for Ambivalence**
Validate both sides of client's conflict: "Part of you wants to travel and build stability, and another part values the fun and friendship you have now. Both make sense."

**4. Explore and Validate Sustain Talk**
When client expresses fear or doubt, explore with curiosity: "What specifically worries you most about making this change? What would help you feel less scared?"

**5. Explicitly Support Autonomy**
Regularly affirm client's right to choose. Replace directive language with "What feels right to you?" and "What do you think?"

**6. Deepen Empathic Understanding**
When client expresses emotion or values, explore meaning rather than moving to problem-solving. Capture emotional nuance in reflections.

**7. Co-Create Change Plan**
Ask client to generate strategies before offering suggestions. Build plan from client's top priorities, not clinician's prescriptions.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change:
While client leaves with concrete plan, session unlikely to significantly strengthen intrinsic motivation. Client-generated solutions more likely to be implemented and sustained than clinician-prescribed ones. Powerful change talk not developed; legitimate ambivalence not addressed. Client may experience plan as something psychiatrist wants rather than something she has chosen, reducing ownership and follow-through likelihood.

---

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should prioritize developing genuine collaborative partnership, strategic evocation of change talk, and deeper exploration of client ambivalence in future sessions. Deliberate practice with MI techniques and supervision strongly recommended.
