Client offers multiple instances ("I guess so," "Maybe it could help a little," "I guess I could try for a bit") but clinician fails to explore, amplify, or reinforce. Strategic questioning to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment entirely absent. Clinician tells why change matters rather than asking client to articulate own reasons. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk addressed primarily through prescription and persuasion rather than MI-consistent techniques. "Yes, but" responses negate reflections and pivot to persuasion. Double-sided reflections absent. No reframing to soften resistance. Clinician does not explore function of sustain talk (fear, shame, overwhelm, lack of understanding). Practical solutions offered without understanding root of resistance.

## **OVERALL SESSION SCORE: 1.9/5**

### **SUMMARY**

This session demonstrates **poor to below-average MI practice** with significant deficiencies in core MI competencies. While the psychiatrist maintains a respectful tone and demonstrates basic reflective listening, the session is fundamentally characterized by an **expert-driven, prescriptive approach** that is inconsistent with MI spirit and principles.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Evocation and Partnership nearly absent:** Clinician tells rather than asks; prescribes rather than explores
- **Change talk uncultivated:** Client's emerging willingness is acknowledged but not deepened or amplified
- **Sustain talk addressed through persuasion:** Resistance met with "yes, but" statements rather than MI-consistent techniques
- **Autonomy support undermined:** Pseudo-autonomy statements mask subtle pressure toward compliance
- **No connection to intrinsic values:** Device use remains externally imposed rather than internally motivated

**Impact on Behavior Change:** While client agrees to try device use for one week, this appears to be **compliance-based rather than intrinsically motivated**. Research indicates compliance-based agreements are less likely to be sustained long-term. Without genuine evocation of client's own reasons for change and connection to personal values, likelihood of sustained behavior change is low.

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Shift from expert role to genuine collaborative partnership
2. Replace telling with strategic questioning to evoke change talk
3. Amplify and reinforce emerging change talk through exploration
4. Use double-sided reflection to honor ambivalence without persuasion
5. Explore function of sustain talk rather than prescribing solutions
6. Connect health behaviors to client's own values and goals
7. Genuinely support autonomy by validating refusal as legitimate choice
8. Develop deeper empathy through complex reflections capturing emotional nuance
9. Eliminate "yes, but" responses; replace with genuine curiosity
10. Build collaborative plans by inviting client input on solutions

**Path Forward:** With focused practice on core MI competencies—particularly evocation, partnership, and cultivation of change talk—the psychiatrist can significantly improve likelihood of evoking sustained behavior change rooted in client's intrinsic motivation.
