## Summary of Clinical Findings

### Strengths
- Maintains warm, non-confrontational therapeutic tone throughout
- Avoids arguing with client's sustain talk or attempting to convince through logic
- Provides concrete, appropriately scaled suggestions (5-minute walk, relaxation app) matched to client's low confidence (4/10)
- Explicitly offers ongoing support and availability
- Normalizes anxiety and difficulty of behavior change
- Demonstrates care and encouragement without being pushy

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**1. Fundamental Misalignment with MI Principles**
This session represents a **directive, expert-driven approach rather than collaborative and evocative MI**. The psychiatrist tells the client what to do rather than drawing out the client's own motivation and wisdom.

**2. Ignores Client's Stated Priority (CRITICAL)**
Client explicitly identifies exam anxiety and avoidance as primary concern. Psychiatrist redirects to generic health habits without negotiation, violating MI principle of honoring client autonomy and agenda.

**3. Minimal Evocation of Change Talk (CRITICAL)**
Client's powerful statements about motivation are largely ignored:
- "If I pass this test, I can finally have that weight lifted off my shoulders"
- "I'd like to have that same feeling as when I succeeded at work"
- "Maybe just having that faith in myself that I can do it"

These are not explored, amplified, or used to build intrinsic motivation.

**4. Missed Critical Resource**
Client's past success (work presentation) mentioned but never explored in depth. This is a powerful resource for building confidence and self-efficacy that remains undeveloped.

**5. Superficial Empathy**
Basic reflections lack emotional depth. Client's fear of judgment, perfectionism, ambivalence, and self-doubt never deeply understood or reflected.

**6. Prescriptive Rather Than Exploratory**
Session filled with "shoulds" and "maybes" that represent prescription rather than exploration, despite softer language.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

Client agreement to plan appears to be **compliance rather than genuine commitment**. While Hana agrees to walks and relaxation tracking, this reflects external motivation rather than intrinsic motivation. **Core issue (exam avoidance) never directly addressed**. By session end, no explicit discussion of when client will take exam, what she needs to feel confident, or how to address self-doubt. Plan focuses on generic stress management rather than building self-efficacy for target behavior. Because psychiatrist did not evoke client's own motivations or explore past success, client leaves with externally imposed plan rather than sense of ownership and agency. This reduces likelihood of sustained behavior change and increases risk of plan abandonment when motivation wanes or obstacles arise.

---

## Key Recommendations for Development

1. **Redirect focus to client's stated priority** – Begin by honoring exam anxiety concern and negotiate agenda collaboratively
2. **Evoke and amplify change talk** – Pause when client expresses motivation; explore deeply with open-ended questions
3. **Explore and build on past success** – Treat work presentation as critical resource; explore in depth to build confidence
4. **Shift from prescription to exploration** – Replace "should" language with open-ended questions inviting client's own solutions
5. **Develop complex, empathic reflections** – Move beyond factual restatements to capture emotional nuance and ambivalence
6. **Explore and soften sustain talk strategically** – Use double-sided reflections and exploration rather than simple reassurance
7. **Collaborate on agenda-setting and solution-building** – Explicitly invite co-creation; let client's ideas shape direction
8. **Strengthen autonomy support** – Consistently emphasize client's right to choose and ownership of change process
9. **Engage in deliberate MI practice** – Record sessions; review for adherence to MI principles; seek supervision with MI expert
10. **Reframe role from expert to facilitator** – Shift mindset: client is expert on their own life; clinician facilitates access to client's wisdom

---

## Conclusion

This clinician demonstrates **foundational warmth and non-confrontation but requires substantial training and deliberate practice to develop true MI competence**. Without development of core MI skills—particularly evocation, collaboration, and honoring client agenda—sessions will remain supportive but ineffective at evoking intrinsic motivation for change. The session illustrates how a well-intentioned, caring clinician can inadvertently undermine client autonomy and intrinsic motivation through expert-driven, prescriptive approaches. Focused training on MI principles and deliberate practice with feedback is essential for this clinician to develop competence in this evidence-based approach.
