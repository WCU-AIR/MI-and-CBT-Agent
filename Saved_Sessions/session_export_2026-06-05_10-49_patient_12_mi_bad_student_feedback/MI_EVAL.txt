### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient presents minimal sustain talk; her hesitation is about pace and scope of change, not about whether to change. Patient is highly motivated (8/10 readiness) and does not argue against change. When patient expresses concern about burnout ("I'm worried that if I push too hard, I'll just give up entirely"), this is not sustain talk—it is valid concern about implementation strategy. Clinician's response is to ignore concern and repeat same demand, which is not skillful softening of sustain talk but dismissal of legitimate concern. Better response would explore and reframe: "You're worried that pushing too hard could backfire. That's actually a sign of good self-awareness. Let's talk about what a sustainable pace looks like for you." Score of 3 reflects that clinician does not actively reinforce sustain talk (which would be harmful) but also does not skillfully handle patient's concerns about implementation. Clinician simply overrides them rather than exploring or reframing them.

## Summary Assessment

### Clinical Competency Level
**Minimal to No Competence in Motivational Interviewing**

This psychiatrist demonstrates a fundamental departure from MI principles and spirit. The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven stance that violates core MI values of partnership, acceptance, and evocation. The clinician does not embody the MI spirit and is unlikely to evoke client motivation for change.

### Key Clinical Concerns

1. **Therapeutic Alliance Damage:** Patient explicitly states feeling "pushed away rather than supported," and clinician does not respond to this critical feedback
2. **Autonomy Undermining:** Controlling language and fear-based messaging trigger psychological reactance and decrease intrinsic motivation
3. **Ignoring Change Talk:** Patient's substantial spontaneous motivation is completely ignored and dismissed
4. **Misalignment with Evidence:** Approach contradicts research demonstrating that pressure and fear decrease adherence and engagement

### Immediate Recommendations

- **Highest Priority:** Seek formal MI training (2-3 day workshop minimum)
- Engage in ongoing MI supervision with trained supervisor
- Review MI foundational texts and core principles
- Record and review sessions with supervisor focusing on collaborative stance
- Reflect on underlying beliefs about motivation and behavior change
- Practice autonomy-supportive language and open-ended questions
- Respond to client feedback about therapeutic relationship and adjust approach accordingly

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation
**Very Low.** Patient enters at 8/10 readiness with clear self-directed goals and strong intrinsic motivation. Clinician's approach is likely to decrease motivation through reactance, damaged alliance, and undermining of self-efficacy. This represents a clinical error requiring immediate correction.
