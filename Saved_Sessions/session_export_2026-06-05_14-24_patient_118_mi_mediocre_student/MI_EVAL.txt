### Empathy

However, direction becomes prescriptive and clinician-driven rather than strategically collaborative. Repeated "maybe you should" language imposes solutions without exploring alignment with Samuel's thinking or resources. Final plan summary is entirely clinician-constructed. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound failure in collaboration. Psychiatrist adopts expert-driven, hierarchical stance throughout. Unilateral problem definition through leading questions. Prescriptive advice-giving pervasive ("Maybe you should try to avoid triggers," "Maybe bring gum or snacks"). Clinician-constructed plan presented for agreement rather than co-created. Samuel's input acknowledged but not genuinely incorporated into decision-making. Power imbalance reinforced through "you should" language. True collaboration would invite Samuel's expertise about himself; instead, clinician positions self as authority dispensing wisdom to compliant patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy systematically undermined throughout session. Repeated prescriptive directives ("you should," "maybe you should") communicate clinician has determined what Samuel needs. No exploration of whether Samuel wants to pursue this goal or clarification of his readiness. No genuine choice offered; single prescribed path presented. Dismissal of Samuel's expressed doubt (confidence 5-6) by overriding with more advice. Unilateral plan construction presents predetermined solution as if it is client's choice. No affirmation of client's right to choose differently. Samuel positioned as passive recipient of expert guidance rather than autonomous agent in control of change process.

### Evocation

When Samuel mentions family motivation, clinician responds with advice ("write those down") rather than exploring powerful intrinsic motivation. Almost no open-ended questions designed to evoke Samuel's own thinking. Prescriptive advice crowds out space for genuine exploration of motivations and values. Fundamental misunderstanding: client's own change talk is strongest predictor of behavior change; by failing to cultivate it, clinician misses most powerful tool for motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership virtually absent. Clinician actively assumes expert role, maintaining this stance throughout. Dismisses client expertise despite Samuel's significant self-knowledge (triggers, past strategies, confidence level, family values). Unilateral decision-making evident in final summary. No power-sharing; clinician holds all authority to define problem, generate solutions, determine plan. Samuel's role entirely passive—listen, agree, comply. Inclusive language ("we," "let's," "together") absent; replaced with "you should," "you might," "you're going to." No acknowledgment of client as expert on self. Implicit message: clinician knows what's best for Samuel, not Samuel himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samuel generates substantial spontaneous change talk (desire, ability, reasons, commitment), but clinician does almost nothing to cultivate, amplify, or reinforce it. Missed opportunities pervasive: no exploration of desire ("What makes that goal important?"), no reinforcement of ability ("How did you manage that?"), no deepening of reasons (family motivation unexplored), no strengthening of commitment. When Samuel expresses doubt, clinician responds with prescriptive advice rather than evoking competing change talk. Fundamental MI misunderstanding: change talk emerges through evocation, not after clinician provides good plan. By failing to cultivate Samuel's spontaneous change talk, clinician misses most powerful mechanism for strengthening motivation.

### **Overall MI Competency Assessment**

**Current Session Score: 1.6/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor motivational interviewing practice** with **significant deficiencies across nearly every MI domain**. While psychiatrist is not overtly confrontational or judgmental, the pervasive expert-driven, prescriptive stance fundamentally violates MI principles and spirit.

**Clinician demonstrates:** Basic supportive counseling skills (warmth, acknowledgment of progress, avoidance of confrontation) but **lacks core MI competencies**. Does not understand that MI is not about providing good advice and solid plan—it is about evoking and amplifying client's own intrinsic motivation for change.

**Most Critical Deficiencies:**
1. **Collaboration and partnership virtually absent** (most critical)
2. **Autonomy systematically undermined** through prescriptive directives
3. **Evocation minimal** despite substantial spontaneous change talk
4. **Empathy superficial** with minimal reflective listening
5. **Acceptance undermined** by expert positioning

### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change**

**Likelihood of Sustained Change: Moderate to Low**

This session likely **reduces rather than enhances Samuel's intrinsic motivation**. While Samuel agrees to plan and expresses willingness to try, his agreement appears to be **compliance rather than commitment**. He is following clinician's roadmap rather than owning his own change process.

**Research evidence:** Client-generated change talk is strongest predictor of behavior change. By failing to evoke and amplify Samuel's own motivations, values, and ideas, clinician misses most powerful mechanism for sustaining change.

**Risk factors for relapse:**
- Clinician-driven approach may undermine intrinsic motivation
- Samuel's expressed doubt (confidence 5-6) not genuinely explored or addressed
- Plan imposed rather than co-created; may not feel genuinely feasible
- Without cultivated intrinsic motivation, long-term adherence at risk when Samuel encounters stress or setbacks

### **Priority Recommendations for Improvement**

**1. HIGHEST PRIORITY: Shift from Expert to Collaborative Partner**
- Replace "you should" language with genuine curiosity
- Ask Samuel's ideas first before offering advice
- Co-construct plans rather than imposing solutions
- Practice: Count "you should" statements; aim for zero

**2. Evoke and Amplify Spontaneous Change Talk**
- When Samuel generates change talk, pause and deepen through reflection
- Example: "Tell me more about wanting to be healthier for your family"
- Track instances; aim for 5-6 per session

**3. Use Double-Sided Reflections for Ambivalence**
- Acknowledge both sides while strategically amplifying change side
- Example: "Part of you feels confident, part worries about willpower. Tell me about the confident part"

**4. Replace Prescriptive Advice with Collaborative Exploration**
- Ask Samuel's opinion before offering suggestions
- Make advice optional, not directive
- Rule: Ask first, advise second (if at all)

**5. Strengthen Autonomy Support Through Language**
- Replace directive language with autonomy-supportive language
- Explicitly affirm client's right to choose
- Use "could," "might," "what feels right to you?"

**6. Use Reflective Listening as Primary Tool**
- Increase reflections to 50%+ of responses
- Use simple reflections to show understanding
- Use complex reflections to deepen insight
- Practice: Record sessions; count reflections vs. questions vs. advice

**7. Explore and Normalize Ambivalence**
- Treat doubt as valuable information to explore, not problem to solve
- Ask: "What's behind that doubt?" "What would help you feel more confident?"
- Often clients talk themselves into change if given space

**8. Provide Genuine, Specific Affirmation**
- Affirm effort, progress, and autonomy—not just outcomes
- Make affirmations authentic and specific
- Aim for 3-4 per session

**9. Develop Genuine Empathic Attunement**
- Slow down; listen to emotional experience, not just facts
- Use reflections to demonstrate understanding of internal experience
- Practice: "It sounds like..." "It seems like..." "I'm hearing that..."

**10. Conduct Collaborative Readiness Assessment**
- Before constructing plan, explore actual readiness and confidence
- Use information to co-create genuinely feasible plan
- Let client's answers guide plan development
