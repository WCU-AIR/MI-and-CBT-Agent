### Acceptance

Effectively normalizes Jamal's struggle ("That tension between wanting to rest or avoid stress and the push to hurry is really understandable") and validates his awareness of impact. ### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking. Early reflections capture emotional nuance and internal conflict ("mornings feel heavy and a bit overwhelming"). Accurately reflects connection between reduced pressure and success. However, misses opportunities to deepen empathy by reflecting underlying concerns about family conflict when Jamal expresses desire to avoid arguing. Limited exploration of complex ambivalence; could have asked more about tension between wanting change and frustration with pressure before moving to confidence scale.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on target behavior (getting to school on time) throughout session. Follows logical MI progression: problem → barriers → past successes → confidence → action steps. Uses confidence scale strategically. However, could have more explicitly connected intrinsic motivations to target behavior. When Jamal mentions "not having people breathing down my neck," psychiatrist acknowledges but doesn't explore deeper personal benefits beyond reducing nagging. Subtly shifts focus to changing mother's behavior rather than maintaining focus on Jamal's controllable actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing. Opening question positions Jamal as expert on priorities. Uses open questions rather than expert analysis. Affirms Jamal's problem-solving when he identifies alarm strategy. Invites input on next steps and support needed. However, when discussing communication with mother, asks for "best way" rather than inviting expansive brainstorming. More collaborative approach would be: "You know your mom best—what are some ways you might talk to her?" This would invite co-creation rather than seeking singular solution.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates moderate autonomy support with notable gaps. Positive elements include framing confidence scale as Jamal's assessment and affirming his awareness. However, subtle pressure toward compliance is present ("It's great to hear you feel pretty confident...that you can try these steps"). Language like "As you move forward" assumes action rather than affirming choice. Doesn't explicitly emphasize Jamal's right to choose or ownership of change process. When mother's behavior affects motivation, could have affirmed: "Your mom's involvement is her choice, and how you respond is yours." Instead, focus shifts to managing reaction, subtly placing responsibility on Jamal to accommodate external pressure.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inconsistently demonstrates evocation. Positive instances include asking about past success ("Can you tell me about a time when you were able to get up and be on time?") and what made it possible. However, significantly underutilizes evocation to draw out intrinsic motivation and values. When Jamal states "For me, it's important to fix that," psychiatrist acknowledges but doesn't ask "Tell me more about why that matters to you." Limited exploration of personal benefits beyond reducing nagging. Minimal amplification of change talk; when Jamal produces statements like "I think I can do it if I really try," psychiatrist doesn't strategically deepen this language. Session remains focused on external barriers rather than evoking compelling personal reasons for change.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership with consistent power-sharing and co-construction of solutions. Client-directed agenda established immediately. Collaborative exploration positions Jamal as expert on his experience. Affirms his problem-solving wisdom rather than imposing solutions. Uses inclusive language appropriately. Invites Jamal to generate next steps and identify needed support. However, when discussing communication with mother, subtly shifts toward expert guidance by asking for "best way" rather than more explicitly positioning Jamal as expert on his own family dynamics. Could strengthen by asking: "You know your mom best—what do you think might work with her?"

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** **Critical weakness.** While Jamal produces change talk naturally ("For me, it's important to fix that," "I think I can do it if I really try"), psychiatrist demonstrates minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce it. After Jamal's commitment statement, psychiatrist reflects but doesn't ask follow-up to deepen change talk. When Jamal rates confidence at 5, psychiatrist normalizes but doesn't ask "What would it take to move to a 6?" Insufficient use of open questions designed to elicit reasons, ability, or need for change. Limited reinforcement of change talk statements. When Jamal expresses ambivalence, psychiatrist normalizes but doesn't ask "What makes you want to try despite these challenges?" This represents significant missed opportunity to strengthen intrinsic motivation—a core MI competency.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic awareness of sustain talk but handles inconsistently without strategic sophistication. Uses normalization and past-success exploration, which are appropriate. However, lacks double-sided reflections that acknowledge both obstacles and motivation. When Jamal says "my mom's always on my case, so it's hard not to get annoyed or think about giving up," psychiatrist normalizes but doesn't pivot toward change talk or reframe. Accepts external motivation framing without exploring intrinsic benefits. When mother's yelling identified as barrier, asks Jamal to manage reaction rather than exploring his agency and controllable actions. Minimal use of reframing to soften sustain talk. One effective softening: asks about past success when Jamal expresses ambivalence, which evokes capability and success.

## Summary Assessment

### **Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing and client-centered approach
- Solid reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and emotional attunement
- Effective elicitation of past success to build confidence and identify resources
- Clear, appropriate direction maintained throughout session
- Consistent nonjudgmental stance and unconditional positive regard
- Practical action planning with concrete, achievable next steps

### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient cultivation of change talk** (Score 2/5) – Most significant gap; minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce Jamal's own language favoring change
- **Limited exploration of intrinsic motivation** – Session remains focused on extrinsic motivation (avoiding nagging) without deeply exploring personal reasons, values, or identity-based motivation
- **Sustain talk not strategically softened** – Normalization used but more sophisticated techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing, explicit redirection toward agency) underutilized
- **Autonomy support could be more explicit** – Subtle pressure toward compliance present; insufficient affirmation of Jamal's right to choose and ownership of change process
- **Missed opportunities for deeper evocation** – Questions about values, identity, and personal benefits largely absent

### **Impact on Likelihood of Sustained Change:**
Jamal leaves with concrete plan and moderate confidence (6-7/10), which is positive. However, motivation remains partially rooted in external pressure rather than deeply connected to personal values. This external motivation is fragile and vulnerable to relapse when external pressure decreases or morning challenges resurface. Psychiatrist created safe space for change but did not fully leverage MI techniques to strengthen intrinsic motivation essential for lasting behavior change with ambivalent adolescent.
