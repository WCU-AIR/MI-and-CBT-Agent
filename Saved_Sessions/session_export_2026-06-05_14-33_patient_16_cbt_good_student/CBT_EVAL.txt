## SUPERVISOR NOTES

### Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Attention

1. **Failure to Recognize Psychiatric Emergency:** Therapist treated acute postpartum mania with psychotic features as anxiety/depression case amenable to standard CBT. This represents fundamental clinical error.

2. **Inadequate Safety Assessment:** No assessment of suicidal/homicidal ideation, access to means, ability to care for infant, or hospitalization need.

3. **Misidentification of Key Symptoms:** Focused on guilt and intrusive thoughts while missing critical manic symptoms (racing thoughts, sleep deprivation, fixed false beliefs, possible psychosis, agitation, hyperreligiosity).

4. **Inappropriate Intervention Strategy:** Cognitive restructuring alone insufficient for psychotic-level symptoms and does not address underlying manic episode.

5. **No Psychiatric Conceptualization:** Therapist did not articulate formulation connecting symptoms to postpartum mania or explain role of medication/hospitalization.

6. **Inadequate Psychoeducation:** Patient not educated about postpartum mania, biological basis of symptoms, or need for urgent psychiatric intervention.

7. **Assessment of Psychotic Features:** Patient's statement about "voice in my head" not explored; fixed, delusional quality of beliefs not assessed.

### Recommendations for Supervision

- **Immediate:** Discuss this case with therapist. Emphasize need for urgent psychiatric referral. Review safety assessment protocols.
- **Short-term:** Provide training on recognizing psychiatric emergencies, postpartum psychiatric disorders, and appropriate referral pathways.
- **Ongoing:** Develop therapist's competence in postpartum psychiatry. Establish consultation protocols with psychiatry colleagues. Create decision trees for when CBT is appropriate vs. when psychiatric intervention is required.
