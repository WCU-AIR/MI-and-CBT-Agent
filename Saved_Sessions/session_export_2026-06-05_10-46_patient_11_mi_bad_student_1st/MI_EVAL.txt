### Direction

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to address sustain talk but does so through confrontation and pressure, which amplifies rather than softens resistance. When Amina expresses barriers ("I don't have anyone to watch them," "It's hard to fit that in"), the psychiatrist responds with direct confrontation ("That's just an excuse"), intensified pressure ("You have to make time no matter what"), and fear-based arguments ("Your health will get worse"). This approach is entirely inconsistent with MI principles for handling ambivalence. A skillful approach would involve double-sided reflections, exploration of barriers, and collaborative problem-solving—none of which occur. The score is 2 rather than 1 because the psychiatrist does attempt to address sustain talk, though the approach is fundamentally wrong.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, expert-driven, confrontational counseling that violates core MI principles across all nine dimensions. The approach is characterized by explicit rejection of partnership, systematic dismissal of the client's perspective, coercive pressure tactics, complete absence of reflective listening, suppression of intrinsic motivation, and violation of autonomy.

**Critical Findings:**

- **Non-MI Counseling:** This is not MI with gaps—this is non-MI practice that would be rated 1/5 on any validated MI fidelity scale (MITI, MISC).
- **Therapeutic Relationship Damage:** The client is invalidated, pressured, and dismissed, damaging the therapeutic alliance.
- **Motivation Suppression:** The client's genuine intrinsic motivation (confidence at 10, willingness to engage) is actively suppressed through pressure rather than evoked.
- **Resistance Amplification:** The confrontational approach predictably amplifies client resistance rather than reducing it.

**Likelihood of Sustained Change:**

**Low.** This session is likely to decrease rather than increase Amina's motivation for change and engagement with treatment. Compliance made under pressure is unlikely to sustain, and the likelihood of her returning for future sessions is compromised.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Cease this approach immediately** until formal MI training is completed.
2. **Pursue accredited MI training** (e.g., MINT certification program).
3. **Develop reflective listening skills** as the foundation for all other MI competencies.
4. **Adopt a collaborative, partnership-based stance** in all clinical interactions.
5. **Seek ongoing supervision** from an MI-trained clinician to review recorded sessions and provide feedback.
