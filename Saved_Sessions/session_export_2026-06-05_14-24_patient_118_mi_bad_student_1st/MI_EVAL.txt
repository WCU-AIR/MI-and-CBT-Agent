## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Summary of Findings

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates **practice that is directly contraindicated in evidence-based behavioral change work**. The clinician employs a confrontational, coercive, expert-driven approach that violates MI principles across all nine domains.

### Critical Observations

**Strengths:**
- Consistent focus on target behavior without topic drift
- Clear communication of genuine concern about health risks

**Critical Deficits:**
- Complete absence of reflective listening (foundational MI technique)
- Systematic dismissal of client's genuine progress and change talk
- Explicit denial of client autonomy and right to self-determination
- Coercive language and pressure tactics throughout
- Zero collaborative goal-setting or power-sharing
- No exploration of client's own motivations, values, or resources
- Escalating pressure in response to client resistance rather than strategic adjustment

### Impact on Client Engagement and Outcomes

This session is **likely to decrease client motivation for change** and **damage the therapeutic relationship**. Research consistently demonstrates that:

- Coercive, pressure-based approaches increase psychological reactance and defensiveness
- Autonomy-supportive, collaborative approaches increase intrinsic motivation and sustained behavior change
- Confrontation hardens ambivalence rather than resolving it
- Dismissal of progress undermines confidence and engagement

Client entered session with genuine change talk, real accomplishments (journaling, reducing by two cigarettes, setting goals), and intrinsic motivation. Clinician's approach is likely to extinguish this motivation and create avoidance of future treatment engagement.

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Foundational MI Training:** Engage in formal, accredited Motivational Interviewing training (MINT-certified programs) with focus on MI spirit and core principles
2. **Implement Reflective Listening:** Practice reflective listening as foundation; aim for 2:1 reflection-to-question ratio
3. **Recognize and Amplify Change Talk:** Learn to identify and actively reinforce client's own language favoring change
4. **Adopt Collaborative Stance:** Replace directive language with collaborative language; support client autonomy explicitly
5. **Ongoing Supervision:** Establish regular supervision with MI-trained supervisor; record and review sessions for fidelity
6. **Personal Reflection:** Examine beliefs about client autonomy, change mechanisms, and clinician role; consider whether current approach aligns with evidence-based practice

### Clinical Responsibility Statement

**This session does not embody Motivational Interviewing principles and represents practice contraindicated in evidence-based behavioral health care.** Immediate, intensive training and supervision are necessary to align practice with MI standards and ethical clinical care. Continuing current practice pattern risks harm to clients and violates standards of behavioral health care.
