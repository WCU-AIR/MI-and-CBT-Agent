### Empathy

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, strategic focus on smoking reduction and stress management while remaining client-centered. Session progresses logically from exploring function → acknowledging ambivalence → exploring values → assessing confidence → eliciting past successes → identifying barriers → generating next steps. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated through client-centered agenda-setting and consistent seeking of input. Positions Natalia as expert on her own experience. Engages in collaborative problem-solving by asking what supports she imagines rather than prescribing solutions. Minor limitation: When introducing psychoeducation, subtly shifts power toward clinician as expert. Could have first explored her existing knowledge: "What have you already tried or heard about?" Doesn't explicitly invite critique of clinical information provided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes Natalia's freedom to choose and reinforces her ownership of change process. Validates her right to reject nicotine replacement: "Your hesitation shows your thoughtful consideration about what feels right for your body." Uses language emphasizing agency: "What do you think might be a first small step you'd feel comfortable trying?" Respects her stated readiness level and pace. Minor opportunity: Could more explicitly affirm right to reject options without needing justification.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Natalia's own motivations, values, and resources. Directly evokes intrinsic motivation regarding her son. Strategically elicits past successes, and Natalia makes her own connection to current situation: "Maybe if there were ways to get support or small steps to try, I could handle cutting back." Asks open questions allowing her to generate solutions. Missed opportunities: When she expresses doubt about readiness or rates confidence at 3/10, could ask more evocative questions ("What would it take to move from a 3 to a 4?") before providing information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively fosters power-sharing and positions Natalia's expertise as central. Opening question establishes partnership: "What's on your mind that you'd like to focus on?" Consistently seeks her input and treats her ideas as valuable. Uses inclusive language: "we can talk about what might fit best with your ideas." Builds on her insights and validates her thinking as foundation for the plan. Minor limitation: When introducing psychoeducation, positions self as information provider rather than first exploring her existing knowledge. Could have asked: "What do you already know about approaches to cutting back?"

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates skillful evocation and reinforcement of change talk across multiple domains. Elicits and amplifies desire ("I do want what's best for him"), ability (reinforces past success with scheduling and asking for help), reasons (evokes her own recognition of need to protect son), and commitment (concrete plans to track cigarettes and identify support). Natalia generates change talk: "I can't shake the feeling that maybe I should try cutting back." Missed opportunities: Could ask more evocative questions when exploring readiness and confidence before providing information. When she generates her own plan, could amplify more deeply with follow-up questions.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent handling of sustain talk. When Natalia normalizes smoking ("Some people have a glass of wine. I have a cigarette"), psychiatrist reflects empathically without directly addressing minimization. When she minimizes consequences ("He doesn't have asthma or anything serious"), psychiatrist acknowledges but doesn't use double-sided reflection to explore discrepancy. When expressing doubt about coping, psychiatrist reframes toward strengths rather than directly addressing sustain talk. Does validate her boundary about not being ready to quit completely. Significant gap: More strategic use of double-sided reflections and direct exploration of discrepancies between values and sustain talk would strengthen practice.

## Summary of Strengths

- **Exemplary empathic accuracy:** Complex, nuanced reflections consistently capture emotional content, underlying values, and core ambivalence
- **Strong collaborative stance:** Genuine client-centered agenda-setting with consistent positioning of client expertise as central
- **Skillful evocation of change talk:** Strategic questioning draws out intrinsic motivation, past successes, and client-generated solutions across multiple domains
- **Consistent autonomy support:** Explicit validation of client's right to choose with emphasis on ownership of change process
- **Warm, respectful acceptance:** Genuine unconditional positive regard with normalization of struggle and affirmation of client worth
- **Logical session structure:** Clear progression from exploration through concrete next steps while remaining client-centered

---

## Summary of Areas for Improvement

- **Inconsistent handling of sustain talk:** Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections when Natalia minimizes consequences or expresses doubt about ability to cope
- **Premature introduction of clinician expertise:** Positions self as information provider rather than first exploring client's existing knowledge
- **Missed opportunities for deeper evocation:** When client expresses doubt or low confidence, could ask more evocative questions before providing information
- **Limited exploration of discrepancies:** Doesn't strategically explore gap between stated values (protecting son) and minimization of consequences
- **Insufficient amplification of commitment language:** When client generates plan, could ask more follow-up questions to deepen commitment and specificity

---

## Clinical Impact

This session demonstrates competent MI practice likely to increase Natalia's engagement and move her toward contemplation of action. Her final statements show concrete commitment ("I could try keeping track" and "I could think about who I might feel okay talking to"). The psychiatrist's empathic accuracy and collaborative stance create psychological safety essential for behavior change. However, inconsistent handling of sustain talk means some minimizations and doubts were not fully addressed, potentially limiting depth of commitment. If psychiatrist had more strategically explored discrepancies, Natalia might have generated stronger change talk and higher confidence. Session succeeds in evoking motivation but leaves some motivational potential untapped.
