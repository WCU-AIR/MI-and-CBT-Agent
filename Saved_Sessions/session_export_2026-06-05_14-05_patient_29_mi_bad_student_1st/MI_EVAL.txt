### Impact Assessment

**Client Engagement Trajectory:** Declining throughout session. Client begins with openness ("I want to feel better") but progressively becomes more defensive and entrenched as psychiatrist increases pressure. By session end, client states "I'm not ready to say yes," representing hardening of position.

**Therapeutic Alliance:** Damaged. Client states "being told to just do it like that makes me feel kinda pushed away," indicating rupture in working relationship.

**Predicted Outcome:** Reduced likelihood of sustained behavior change. Session violates fundamental principles of behavior change psychology and will likely result in decreased intrinsic motivation, increased resistance, and lower treatment engagement.

---

### Immediate Recommendations

**Priority 1 – Fundamental Retraining Required:**
Psychiatrist requires formal Motivational Interviewing training and supervised practice with feedback. Current approach is fundamentally misaligned with MI principles and actively harmful to client outcomes.

**Priority 2 – Shift Therapeutic Stance:**
Replace expert-directive approach with collaborative partnership. Reframe opening to communicate respect for client autonomy and invitation to work together.

**Priority 3 – Develop Empathic Understanding:**
Implement reflective listening as primary clinical tool. Practice validating client's perspective and emotional experience before offering direction.

**Priority 4 – Eliminate Judgment and Pressure:**
Remove all accusatory language, threats, and coercive tactics. Replace with genuine curiosity about client's barriers and experience.

**Priority 5 – Learn to Evoke:**
Develop skill in drawing out client's own motivations, values, and past successes through open-ended questioning and strategic reflection.
