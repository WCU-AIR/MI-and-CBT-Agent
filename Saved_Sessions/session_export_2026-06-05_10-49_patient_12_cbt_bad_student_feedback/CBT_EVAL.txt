## SUPERVISOR SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Critical Findings

This session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT. The therapist's approach, while warm and non-threatening, fundamentally mismatches the patient's severe cognitive and communication limitations. The patient (Ashley, age 17, IQ 31, autism spectrum disorder) requires concrete, adapted interventions with explicit structure and family involvement. Instead, she received generic stress-management suggestions appropriate for typically developing adolescents.

### Immediate Priorities for Remediation

1. **Reconceptualize the patient's needs:** Recognize severe intellectual disability and autism as central to treatment planning, not peripheral factors
2. **Establish session structure:** Implement concrete, visual agendas at every session
3. **Adapt all interventions:** Replace abstract cognitive strategies with concrete, behavioral, and environmental approaches
4. **Involve the family:** Position mother as co-therapist; teach concrete skills; provide visual supports
5. **Assign homework:** Every session must include specific, achievable between-session practice
6. **Seek specialized training:** Pursue intensive training in CBT for intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorder

### Recommended Supervision Plan

- **Frequency:** Weekly individual supervision with specialist in developmental disabilities
- **Format:** Review session recordings; practice adapted CBT techniques; role-play with feedback
- **Training:** Attend workshops on CBT for intellectual disability; read current literature; consult with autism specialists
- **Competency milestones:** Demonstrate ability to (1) conduct functional analysis, (2) develop concrete treatment plans, (3) adapt CBT techniques, (4) involve caregivers, (5) assign and review homework

### Risk Assessment

**Current risk level: MODERATE-HIGH.** While therapist poses no direct harm, inadequate treatment may result in: (1) continued or worsening self-injury, (2) increased anxiety and isolation, (3) patient frustration and hopelessness, (4) missed opportunities for evidence-based intervention. Recommend continued supervision before independent treatment of patients with severe neurodevelopmental disability.
