### Autonomy Support

Psychiatrist repeatedly uses language minimizing choice: "you have to," "you need to," "you must," "you can't." When client expresses ambivalence or difficulty, psychiatrist responds with pressure rather than support: "You can't let losing phone privileges mess with you—that's just weakness." Uses threat and fear to attempt behavioral control: "if you don't comply, things will only get worse" (repeated multiple times). This is coercive, not autonomy-supportive. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client expresses sustain talk: "One of the officers has it out for me," "it feels like they're always watching me," "it's hard to just switch off how you feel when you think you're being picked on." Psychiatrist's approach is confrontational rather than skillful. Responds with direct confrontation: "No, you don't get to make excuses" and "feeling stressed doesn't cut it as an excuse." Psychiatrist does occasionally redirect toward change ("You have to get your act together if you want to get out in three weeks"), but through pressure and threat rather than skillful reframing. While sustain talk is somewhat reduced—client eventually backs down and agrees to try harder—this is achieved through pressure and shame, not collaborative technique. Likely to create resentment and undermine long-term change.

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Total MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Clinical Summary:**

This session represents a fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles and demonstrates harmful clinical practice. The psychiatrist scores 1/5 on seven of nine criteria (Acceptance, Empathy, Collaboration, Autonomy Support, Evocation, Partnership, and Cultivating Change Talk) and 2/5 on two criteria (Direction and Softening Sustain Talk). The session is characterized by:

- **Judgment and dismissal** of client's emotional experience
- **Imposed rather than collaborative** agendas
- **Coercion and threat** rather than autonomy support
- **Suppression rather than evocation** of intrinsic motivation
- **Confrontation rather than skillful handling** of resistance

**Critical Deficiencies:**

1. Complete absence of acceptance and empathy
2. Imposed agenda disconnected from client's actual concerns
3. Coercion and threat rather than autonomy support
4. No evocation of client's own motivation
5. Suppression of spontaneous change talk
6. Confrontational handling of sustain talk
7. No genuine partnership or power-sharing

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Behavior Change:**

**Low.** While client achieves surface-level compliance through pressure, this does not reflect genuine intrinsic motivation. Research indicates shame-based, confrontational approaches with adolescents increase defensiveness, strengthen resistance, and undermine long-term change. Client explicitly requested collaboration ("It helps if someone actually listens to where I'm coming from instead of just telling me to fix it"), which was not provided. Upon release in three weeks, client will not have developed genuine motivation for managing frustration or following rules—only learned to comply under pressure.

**Recommendation:**

This psychiatrist requires immediate, intensive training in Motivational Interviewing principles, supervised practice, and ongoing consultation. Current approach is not only ineffective but potentially harmful to client engagement and outcomes. Formal MI training (minimum 2-3 days) and supervised practice with feedback from MI-trained clinician is essential before continuing clinical work with this population.
