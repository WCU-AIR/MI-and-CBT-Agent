## Overall Session Score: 2.3/5

---

### **Summary of Strengths**

- Clear, consistent focus on physical activity as target behavior throughout session
- Non-confrontational stance; clinician does not argue with or criticize barriers
- Acceptance of client prioritization (activity before nutrition)
- Reasonable, practical, evidence-informed suggestions (10-minute walks, reminders, tracking)
- Warm opening and closing with genuine encouragement
- Appropriate use of scaling questions (1-10 confidence/importance)

---

### **Summary of Areas for Improvement**

- **Expert-driven stance:** Operates primarily from expert role, diagnosing problems and prescribing solutions rather than partnering with Amira
- **Insufficient evocation:** Accepts existing change talk but does not skillfully draw out deeper motivations, values, or resources
- **Prescriptive language:** Repeated "should," "probably," "might want to" subtly pressures toward compliance rather than supporting autonomy
- **Minimal collaboration:** Amira's contributions acknowledged but not genuinely integrated into co-constructed planning
- **Shallow empathy:** Basic reflective listening present but minimal deep emotional understanding or complex reflections
- **Limited exploration of ambivalence:** No double-sided reflections or exploration of mixed feelings about past attempts
- **Premature move to planning:** Quick shift from motivation-building to prescriptive planning, missing opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation
- **Missed affirmation opportunities:** Amira's self-awareness and wisdom not affirmed or integrated into plan

---

### **Clinical Recommendations**

1. **Shift from expert-driven to evocative stance:** Ask more questions before offering suggestions; practice "What do you think?" instead of "You should..."

2. **Deepen change talk strategically:** Explore desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment through follow-up questions that elaborate and strengthen motivation

3. **Use double-sided reflections:** Acknowledge both client's desire for change and their concerns to explore ambivalence rather than immediately problem-solving

4. **Affirm client wisdom:** Explicitly validate client's self-knowledge and integrate insights into the plan to build autonomy and self-efficacy

5. **Co-construct plans collaboratively:** Ask clients what they think would work and what feels doable; build plans together rather than designing and presenting for acceptance

6. **Support autonomy explicitly:** Replace prescriptive language with open questions emphasizing client choice and control

7. **Explore past attempts as wisdom:** Ask what clients learned from past experiences and how they might apply that learning to current attempt

8. **Use scaling questions strategically:** Follow up with evocative questions ("What would move you from 8 to 9?") rather than moving directly to planning

9. **Demonstrate genuine partnership:** Use inclusive language ("we," "together"); genuinely incorporate client ideas; show valuing of their expertise about themselves

10. **Develop deeper empathic attunement:** Move beyond factual reflections to complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and demonstrating deep understanding of internal experience

---

### **Overall Assessment**

This session demonstrates basic clinical competence and warm rapport but falls significantly short of skilled motivational interviewing. The fundamental gap is the shift from MI spirit to expert-driven problem-solving. While Amira leaves with a concrete plan and maintained motivation (8/10 confidence), the session has missed critical opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation, explore values and wisdom, and co-construct a plan reflecting her own self-knowledge. The most concerning gap is insufficient evocation—Amira's motivation is currently driven by her own insight rather than deepened understanding of why change matters or how it connects to her identity and life goals. When barriers emerge (she has already identified inconsistency and overwhelm as past challenges), Amira may lack the deep intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy that comes from having co-created her plan and explored her own wisdom.

**To improve:** Shift fundamentally from expert-driven to evocative stance. Ask more questions and offer fewer suggestions; explore ambivalence rather than bypass it; affirm client wisdom rather than prescribe solutions; genuinely partner with clients to co-construct change plans. With focused practice on evocation, autonomy support, and partnership, this clinician could move from adequate supportive counseling to skilled motivational interviewing that truly evokes and deepens client motivation for sustained change.
