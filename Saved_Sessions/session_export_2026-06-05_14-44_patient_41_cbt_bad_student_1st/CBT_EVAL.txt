## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Session Context:** 65-year-old male patient (Terry) presenting with marital crisis (wife threatened divorce after discovering fetishistic behavior), diagnosed fetishistic disorder, anxiety, insomnia, and shame. Multiple sessions reviewed in transcript.

**Key Observations:**

1. **Absence of CBT Framework:** This session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT. It is generic supportive counseling with motivational platitudes.

2. **Critical Clinical Gaps:**
   - No assessment of fetishistic behavior (onset, triggers, frequency, function, distress level)
   - No exploration of shame-based cognitions or catastrophic predictions
   - No marital assessment or communication pattern analysis
   - No understanding of what Terry wants to change or why
   - No exploration of barriers to change

3. **Therapeutic Stance Issues:**
   - Excessive self-disclosure shifts focus from patient to therapist
   - Advice-giving without collaboration or patient input
   - Premature reassurance without clinical foundation
   - Lack of genuine curiosity about patient's internal experience

4. **Most Urgent Training Needs:**
   - CBT-specific techniques (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, exposure, communication skills)
   - Systematic assessment and case formulation
   - Socratic questioning and guided discovery
   - Collaborative goal-setting and shared decision-making
   - Structured homework assignment and between-session task design

**Recommended Next Steps:**
- Immediate supervision with CBT-trained supervisor
- Review of CBT treatment manuals for sexual disorders
- Recorded session review with feedback
- Role-play practice of Socratic questioning and collaborative assessment
- Case consultation before next patient session
