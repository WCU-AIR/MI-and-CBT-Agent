## DETAILED CLINICAL NOTES

### Session Structure and Process Issues

**Lack of Agenda Setting (Item 1: Score 0/6)**
- First session opening: "Hey there! How's it going today?" — warm but no agenda-setting language
- No discussion of session goals, target problems, time allocation, or priorities
- Session drifted from topic to topic without explicit agreement on focus
- Second session opening: "Hey there! It's good to see you today. How have things been going for you lately?" — no reference to previous session goals or progress
- **Critical failure:** Without agenda, treatment becomes aimless and patients cannot track progress

**Minimal Feedback Elicitation (Item 2: Score 1/6)**
- No explicit questions checking understanding of therapist's reasoning
- No solicitation of reactions to interventions
- No summary of key points at session end
- Only implicit content questions asked
- When patient expressed doubt ("It's hard to believe I can actually quit"), therapist did not explore resistance
- No check-in about patient's experience of session
- No collaborative planning for next steps

**Surface-Level Empathy (Item 3: Score 2/6)**
- Therapist grasped explicit content but missed critical nuances
- Patient stated: "It's like the only thing that makes me forget the stress" — therapist missed emotion regulation function
- Patient disclosed: "I feel worthless, like I'm ruining everything" — therapist minimized with "don't be too hard on yourself"
- Patient expressed: "It's like a part of me is addicted, and the other part wants to fix things, but they're always fighting" — therapist compared to pizza craving instead of exploring ambivalence
- Patient mentioned cultural context (father gambled) — never explored
- Personal anecdotes centered therapist's experience rather than deepening understanding of patient's unique situation

**Blurred Professional Boundaries (Item 4: Score 3/6)**
- Excessive personal disclosure: school stress, exam anxiety, junk food cravings, dehydration, guitar playing
- Pattern suggests therapist views gambling disorder as equivalent to common life stressors
- Warmth felt performative; cheerleading ("You've got this!") rather than genuine empathic attunement
- Overconfident reassurance may communicate underestimation of disorder severity
- Appropriate boundaries maintained regarding exploitation, but peer-to-peer tone undermined professional stance

**Absent Collaboration (Item 5: Score 1/6)**
- Unilateral recommendations: "Try to drink plenty of water," "Maybe you could find something," "Maybe just take it one day at a time"
- Patient positioned as passive recipient rather than active partner
- No shared decision-making about treatment approach
- Ambivalence not explored collaboratively
- Patient's doubt about treatment not addressed through collaborative inquiry
- No discussion of treatment options or patient preferences

**Poor Pacing (Item 6: Score 2/6)**

- Time spent on peripheral topics: hydration, hobbies, guitar playing
- No systematic assessment of triggers, thoughts, consequences, previous attempts, motivation

- Second session lacked continuity; no reference to previous content or progress review
