--- DISORDER ---
Opioid Use Disorder, Severe; Opioid Withdrawal; Alcohol Use Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Raymond Xavier, a 29-year-old divorced semi-employed landscaper, presented with a complaint that his life was in a downward spiral. He had been homeless for over 6 months, living in his car, friends’ homes, and more recently in a tent in the woods. His parents offered to fund treatment directly.

History of Substance Use:

Opioids: Began acetaminophen-oxycodone (Percocet) at age 24 after a back injury. Initially felt “normal and happy.” Continued use 1–4 pills/day for 12 years. At age 26, tried heroin intranasally, progressing rapidly to intravenous use. Within 6 months, became homeless and unemployed, using up to 30 bags/day. Multiple relapses despite outpatient rehab and 10 detox/rehab admissions. Also used cocaine with heroin (“speedballs”) temporarily.

Alcohol: Heavy use, described as “getting in trouble” and needing to stop.

Benzodiazepines: Sporadic diazepam (Valium) use, viewed as coping rather than problematic.

Social/Occupational History:

Semi-employed as a landscaper, occasional odd jobs (office supplies, veterinary assistant, gas station attendant).

Estranged from daughter for over 2 years.

Engaged in theft, breaking and entering, and writing bad checks to support drug habit.

Mental Status:

Somewhat unkempt, cooperative but restless, rapid speech, impatient.

Pupillary dilation, sweating, piloerection, limb twitching, yawning, tearful, rhinitis.

Denied psychosis, suicidal or homicidal ideation.

Hopeful about Suboxone treatment to regain employment and parental role.

Laboratory Studies: Negative for HIV, hepatitis A, B, and C.

Discussion:
Mr. Xavier exhibits classic opioid withdrawal symptoms: diarrhea, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, mydriasis, sweating, yawning, restlessness, limb spasms, and dysphoric/anxious mood. His symptoms are acute and directly linked to opioid cessation. Alcohol use also contributes but does not account for the opioid-specific withdrawal signs.

DSM-5 Criteria:
Opioid Use Disorder, Severe:

Taking larger amounts than intended

Inability to cut down

Excessive time obtaining, using, recovering

Craving

Failure to fulfill role obligations

Social/interpersonal problems

Reduced activities

Physically hazardous use

Continued use despite consequences

Tolerance

Withdrawal

Alcohol Use Disorder: Meets criteria for significant alcohol-related problems.

Notable Considerations:

Antisocial behavior largely stems from substance acquisition; no evidence of pre-substance antisocial traits.

Classic presentation: onset in early adulthood, initial euphoria, “chasing the high,” progressive life disruption.

Remarkably, no bloodborne infections despite IV use.
