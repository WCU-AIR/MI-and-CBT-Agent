## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.4/5**

**Key Findings:**

This session demonstrates **poor alignment with Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit.** The psychiatrist operates from an expert-driven, directive stance that contradicts core MI values of partnership, autonomy support, and evocation. Most critically, the psychiatrist fails to address the client's direct question about citalopram-phenelzine drug interactions, instead redirecting to generic medication counseling. This fundamental misalignment with the client's stated agenda, combined with disregard for her time constraint and minimal evocation of her intrinsic motivation, results in a session unlikely to increase client engagement or commitment to change.

**Strengths Identified:**
- Warm, genuine opening greeting
- Avoids confrontation or judgment of client's resistance
- Attempts final summary to demonstrate understanding
- Accepts client's decision to leave without pressure

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Address client's direct question first** before redirecting to other topics
2. **Adapt approach to client's time constraint** rather than adding tasks
3. **Replace telling with asking** through open-ended questions
4. **Treat client as equal partner** rather than recipient of expert advice
5. **Evoke and amplify client's intrinsic motivation** rather than imposing directives
6. **Reflect emotional experience** underlying client's statements
7. **Use choice language** to support autonomy
8. **Explore sustain talk** with curiosity rather than ignoring it
9. **Establish clear boundaries** around medical information while offering alternatives
10. **Strengthen weak commitment language** through strategic follow-up questions

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

Ingrid enters with intrinsic motivation (desire for safety, proactive health management) but leaves with:
- Her direct question unanswered
- Frustration at being redirected repeatedly
- Weak, tentative commitment to follow-up ("I'll try to call")
- Reduced trust in the psychiatrist
- Decreased sense of autonomy and partnership

**Likelihood of Change:** Low. While Ingrid may call her pharmacist due to compliance with the psychiatrist's directive, this represents external motivation rather than strengthened intrinsic motivation. The session represents a missed opportunity to amplify her existing health-conscious values and autonomy.

**MI Spirit Assessment:** This session does **not** embody MI spirit. Significant retraining in core MI principles and techniques is needed to develop genuine competency in partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

**Recommendation:** Prioritize training in (1) client-centered listening and following the client's lead, (2) open-ended questioning and evocation techniques, (3) collaborative decision-making, and (4) autonomy-supportive language. Consider supervised practice with feedback on real or recorded sessions.
