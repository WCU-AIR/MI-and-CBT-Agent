### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Patient demonstrates multiple instances of change talk (pride in smoking cessation, desire to change, realistic planning about starting small), but psychiatrist rarely evokes, amplifies, or reinforces it. Instead, psychiatrist often contradicts patient's own motivation and realistic planning. Smoking cessation achievement negated ("but that's just the start"). Patient's desire to change not explored. Patient's realistic planning dismissed ("Half-measures won't cut it"). Psychiatrist does not ask questions designed to evoke change talk (benefits of change, importance of health, past accomplishments, confidence-building). Only final exchange creates space for change talk ("What do you think might be a good first step?"), with patient responding with realistic, sustainable change talk. Insufficient to establish pattern of evoking patient's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal in session; patient expresses ambivalence and realistic concerns rather than strong resistance. Psychiatrist does not actively reinforce sustain talk or agree with reasons against change. However, psychiatrist does not skillfully soften sustain talk through reframing or double-sided reflection. Instead, responds with pressure and blame ("Why haven't you been able to stick to a routine?"), framing difficulty as personal deficiency. Final exchanges show slight improvement with basic acknowledgment of patient's hesitation ("It sounds like there's some concern about jumping into strict routines right away") and redirection toward feasible change ("What do you think would be a manageable first step?"). Mixed handling of ambivalence—neither strongly reinforcing nor skillfully softening sustain talk.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor adherence to Motivational Interviewing principles** and is fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit. The psychiatrist operates almost entirely from a directive, expert-driven, controlling stance that violates core MI tenets. The patient's own wisdom, motivation, and realistic self-awareness are repeatedly dismissed in favor of psychiatrist-imposed prescriptions and pressure.

**Key Strengths:**
- Clear health focus maintained throughout
- Acknowledgment of smoking cessation achievement (though undermined)
- Concrete, measurable behavioral targets specified
- Final two exchanges demonstrate capacity for collaborative, autonomy-supportive practice

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Eliminate controlling language** and establish genuine collaboration (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
- **Develop empathic listening** and reflective responses
- **Evoke rather than impose** motivation for change
- **Amplify and reinforce** patient's change talk
- **Replace pressure and demands** with collaborative exploration
- **Establish goals collaboratively**, not unilaterally

**Impact on Patient Engagement:**
This session is likely to **decrease intrinsic motivation** and increase resistance. The controlling, judgmental approach activates psychological reactance. While the patient demonstrates appropriate ambivalence and realistic self-awareness, the psychiatrist's approach pushes toward defensiveness. The brief improvement in final exchanges is insufficient to overcome earlier damage.

**Training Recommendation:**
This psychiatrist requires **intensive MI training and supervision**, including:
- Comprehensive MI training program completion
- Weekly session recording and review with MI-trained supervisor
- Regular practice of specific MI skills in role-plays
- Feedback using validated MI fidelity scales
- Deliberate practice until collaborative, autonomy-supportive responses become automatic
