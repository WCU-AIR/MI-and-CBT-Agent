## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Strengths:**
- Warm, non-threatening demeanor appropriate for anxious child
- Consistent validation of emotions
- Age-appropriate, accessible language
- Genuine concern and kindness
- Belated attempt at structured agenda-setting with time allocation

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **No CBT-specific intervention:** Session contained no cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, exposure hierarchies, thought records, or safety behavior reduction
- **Reassurance-based approach:** Contraindicated for anxiety disorders; reinforces avoidance and prevents habituation
- **No case conceptualization:** Therapist never articulated understanding of maintaining factors (avoidance, safety behaviors, fear conditioning)
- **Absence of Socratic questioning:** Therapist told patient what to do rather than facilitating discovery
- **Minimal collaboration:** Patient positioned as passive recipient rather than active partner
- **No homework:** Between-session practice essential for anxiety treatment completely absent
- **Over-personalization:** Excessive therapist anecdotes blurred boundaries and shifted focus from patient

**Clinical Risk:**
Patient at 5th percentile for weight with two-year eating restriction history. At risk for medical complications (malnutrition, growth stunting, bone density loss). Ineffective psychological treatment delays necessary intervention and increases medical risk.

**Recommendation for Therapist Development:**
1. Pursue formal CBT training and certification
2. Seek weekly supervision from experienced CBT supervisor
3. Study evidence-based treatment for anxiety disorders and ARFID
4. Record and review own sessions
5. Practice exposure-based intervention with supervisor
6. Develop case conceptualization skills
7. Learn and apply Socratic questioning
8. Establish clear session structure and agenda-setting
9. Eliminate personal anecdotes; maintain professional boundaries
10. Assign specific, rationale-explained homework systematically
