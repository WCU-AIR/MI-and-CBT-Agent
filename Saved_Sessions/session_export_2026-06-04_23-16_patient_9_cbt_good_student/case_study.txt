--- DISORDER ---
Antisocial Personality Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ike Crocker was a 32-year-old white divorced carpenter referred for a mental health evaluation by the human resources department of a large construction business that had been his employer for 2 weeks.

At his initial job interview, Mr. Crocker presented as very motivated and provided two carpentry school certifications indicating high skill and training. Since starting work, supervisors noted frequent arguments, absenteeism, poor workmanship, and multiple potentially dangerous errors. When confronted, he blamed “cheap wood” and “bad management” and said that if someone got hurt, “it's because of their own stupidity.”

When HR discussed termination, he claimed he had ADHD and bipolar disorder and demanded accommodations or he would sue. He requested a psychiatric evaluation.

During the evaluation, Mr. Crocker focused on unfairness at work and claimed he was “a hell of a better carpenter than anyone there could ever be.” He described both marriages ending due to jealousy and claimed his wives lied to judges and obtained restraining orders against him. He refused to pay child support for his two children, referring to them as “little liars.”

He reported school difficulties, including truancy, juvenile hall at age 14 for theft, and expulsion at 15 for allegedly stealing a car. A probation report confirmed bullying of disabled children, lack of remorse, and conduct disorder beginning in childhood.

Mr. Crocker reported past marijuana use as a teen and regular alcohol use starting at age 22. He denied these were problems. He requested stimulant and benzodiazepine medications, claiming they were the only effective treatments for him.

Mental status examination: casually dressed, reasonable eye contact, normal movements, coherent and goal-directed speech, preoccupied with blaming others, cognitively intact, poor insight. No thought disorder or hallucinations.

Background check revealed falsified carpentry credentials, prior firings after conflicts or abrupt departures, and repeated rule-breaking behavior.

Discussion: Mr. Crocker exhibits a pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others. He demonstrates deceit, aggression, lack of remorse, irresponsibility, and failure to conform to social norms. Evidence of conduct disorder prior to age 15 confirms childhood-onset traits. Substance use and ADHD claims may be comorbid but do not account for pervasive antisocial behavior. Behavior not attributable to bipolar disorder or schizophrenia.

Diagnosis: Antisocial Personality Disorder
