## SUMMARY OF CRITICAL FINDINGS

### Strengths (Limited)
- Therapist demonstrated genuine warmth and maintained supportive tone
- Patient was not invalidated or dismissed
- Therapist was non-hostile and encouraging

### Critical Deficiencies

- **No feedback elicitation:** Minimal checking for understanding or satisfaction
- **Fundamental misunderstanding of psychosis:** Treated hallucinations as equivalent to normal stress
- **No guided discovery:** Relied entirely on direct advice-giving
- **Inappropriate targets:** Addressed generic stress-management rather than psychotic symptoms
- **No coherent strategy:** No treatment plan or CBT-informed conceptualization
- **Zero CBT techniques:** No cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, reality testing, or other evidence-based interventions
- **No homework:** No between-session tasks or measurement
- **Excessive self-focus:** Violated professional boundaries with personal anecdotes
- **Inadequate for presentation:** Generic advice insufficient for active psychosis with neurocognitive deficits

### Competency Assessment
**NOT COMPETENT** to deliver CBT for psychotic disorders. Immediate, intensive training and supervision required.

### Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit
**UNLIKELY.** Without intervention targeting hallucinations directly, patient's symptoms will likely persist or worsen. Generic advice may increase frustration and reduce engagement with treatment.
