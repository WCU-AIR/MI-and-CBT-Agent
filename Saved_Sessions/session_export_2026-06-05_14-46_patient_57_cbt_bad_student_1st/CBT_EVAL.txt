## SUPERVISOR RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Priority Actions:

1. **Obtain Proper Diagnostic Understanding (URGENT):** Review Arthur's diagnostic evaluation and neuropsychological findings. Understand Alzheimer's disease with progressive cognitive decline differs fundamentally from primary depression.

2. **Eliminate Repetitive Ineffective Advice (URGENT):** Stop suggesting generic interventions without understanding feasibility. Replace advice-giving with Socratic questioning.

3. **Establish Collaborative Agenda (URGENT):** At next session, explicitly set agenda with patient. Acknowledge multiple concerns and collaboratively prioritize them.

4. **Implement Guided Discovery (HIGH PRIORITY):** Replace all advice-giving with Socratic questioning. Help patient reach insights through questions rather than directives.

5. **Conduct Functional Analysis (HIGH PRIORITY):** Systematically explore why patient avoids activities. Identify maintaining factors (fear, energy, initiation difficulty, memory problems).

6. **Develop Specific, Feasible Behavioral Interventions (HIGH PRIORITY):** Based on functional analysis, create specific interventions with built-in supports and accommodations for cognitive deficits.

7. **Actively Solicit and Respond to Feedback (HIGH PRIORITY):** Regularly ask if session is helpful. When patient provides feedback that something isn't working, take it seriously and adjust approach.

8. **Involve Family Members (HIGH PRIORITY):** Recognize wife as critical resource. Include her in treatment planning, psychoeducation, and intervention implementation.

9. **Seek Specialized Supervision (MEDIUM PRIORITY):** Identify supervisor with expertise in CBT for older adults and neurocognitive disorders. Meet regularly to review sessions and develop competence.

10. **Provide Psychoeducation and Validation (HIGH PRIORITY):** Explicitly validate that patient's perceptions of cognitive change are accurate. Provide education about Alzheimer's disease, expected changes, and adaptive strategies.
