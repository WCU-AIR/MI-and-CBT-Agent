
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Cognitive Behavioral Therapy Feedback: Therapist Evaluation

### 1. Agenda
### **Score: 0/6**

**Explanation:**

No agenda was established at any point during this session. The therapist opened with a generic greeting ("Hey there! How's it going today?") and immediately asked an open-ended question without collaboratively determining what should be addressed in the session. Across multiple session segments, there is no evidence of the therapist and patient jointly identifying target problems, prioritizing concerns, or establishing time-specific goals. The patient spontaneously disclosed multiple significant issues (social withdrawal, cognitive difficulties, memory problems, financial worries, sleep disturbance, fear of disorientation), yet the therapist never organized these into a coherent session structure or asked which problems the patient most wanted to address.

The repeated, nearly identical therapeutic responses across different patient disclosures suggest the therapist was operating without any strategic plan or session framework. A proper agenda would have included: (1) acknowledgment of the patient's multiple concerns, (2) collaborative prioritization (e.g., "Of all these things you've mentioned—the sleep, the worry about getting lost, the difficulty with activities—what feels most important to work on today?"), (3) time allocation, and (4) explicit agreement on session focus. None of this occurred.

This is particularly critical given Arthur's neurocognitive disorder diagnosis. Patients with cognitive decline require *more* structure, not less. Clear agendas help compensate for memory and attention deficits and provide external scaffolding for the session.

**Summary:**

The therapist completely failed to establish a collaborative agenda. No target problems were identified, prioritized, or time-allocated. This represents a fundamental failure in basic CBT structure.

### 2. Feedback
### **Score: 0/6**

**Explanation:**

The therapist never elicited feedback from the patient about the session, the therapist's understanding, or the helpfulness of interventions. There are no instances of the therapist asking: "Does that make sense?" "How does that land with you?" "Is this helpful?" "Am I understanding you correctly?" or "What are your thoughts on what we've discussed?"

The patient explicitly provided critical feedback multiple times—stating that positive thinking advice felt "empty," that generic reassurance wasn't addressing the core problem, and that repeated identical responses weren't helping. **Summary:**

The therapist made zero attempts to elicit or respond to patient feedback. When the patient provided explicit negative feedback about the intervention approach, it was ignored and the same ineffective strategy was repeated.

### 3. Understanding
### **Score: 1/6**

**Explanation:**

The therapist demonstrated minimal understanding of Arthur's actual clinical presentation and internal experience.
