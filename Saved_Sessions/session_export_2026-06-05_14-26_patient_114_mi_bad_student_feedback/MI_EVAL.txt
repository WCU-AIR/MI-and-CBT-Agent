### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk through argument and pressure, which typically strengthens resistance. Rowan's reasons for not changing ("I got a lot of stuff to do," "It messes up my routine," "It just feels like another hassle") are met with direct confrontation ("Look, you have to take this seriously," "Just do it and stop making excuses"). This classic MI error—arguing against sustain talk—strengthens it. Rowan becomes more entrenched. One brief attempt at acknowledgment ("Alright, I understand you're not ready yet") is immediately undermined by renewed pressure ("but you have to realize the consequences"). Double-sided reflections absent. Sustain talk not skillfully softened.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamentally Non-MI

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Key Findings:**

This session represents a **severe departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates **fundamental misunderstanding of how to work with ambivalence and resistance**. The psychiatrist employs a directive, confrontational, expert-driven approach that is directly contraindicated in MI and actively counterproductive with this client.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

1. **Systematic use of pressure and ultimatums** rather than evocation and collaboration
2. **Dismissal of client autonomy and choice**, framing compliance as mandatory
3. **Failure to explore or validate emotional experience**; vulnerability is dismissed
4. **Direct argument against sustain talk**, which strengthens rather than softens resistance
5. **Missed opportunities for evocation**; clinician tells rather than asks
6. **Absence of genuine collaboration** despite opening statement
7. **Suppression of change talk**; modest expressions met with pressure
8. **Rigid expert stance** treating Rowan's input as obstacles

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to **decrease Rowan's motivation for change** and **damage the therapeutic alliance**. Rowan enters with workable ambivalence. By session end, he is more entrenched in resistance ("Maybe tomorrow or something. For now, just don't expect me to jump on it"). The psychiatrist has inadvertently strengthened sustain talk and weakened change talk through systematic pressure and control.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** **Very Low**

The session does **not embody MI spirit**. Rowan's vulnerability—his fear about "not handling things right"—was met with dismissal rather than validation, teaching him it is unsafe to share deeper concerns. He has been positioned as defiant rather than as someone with legitimate concerns. Without involvement in plan-creation, experience of clinician as controlling, and unaddressed emotional barriers, Rowan is unlikely to follow through on device charging.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

- **Fundamental retraining in MI principles required**
- Abandon directive pressure; adopt genuine evocation
- Validate and explore emotional experience rather than dismiss it
- Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk
- Amplify and reinforce change talk
- Support autonomy by offering genuine choice
- Establish true partnership by seeking Rowan's input
- Develop tolerance for ambivalence and slow change
- Shift from expert-driven to client-centered stance

**Supervision Status:** Immediate MI-specific clinical supervision essential before continuing with this client.
