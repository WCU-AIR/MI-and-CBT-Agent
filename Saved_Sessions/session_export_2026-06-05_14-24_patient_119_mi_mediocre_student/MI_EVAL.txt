## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.4/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles. The psychiatrist operates from a directive, expert-driven stance that prioritizes the provider's agenda (smoking cessation) over the client's explicitly stated preference (dental treatment only). Core MI principles of autonomy support, collaboration, and evocation are systematically violated.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- Repeated violation of explicitly stated client boundaries
- Ineffective use of "but" statements that trigger resistance
- Missed opportunity to amplify spontaneous change talk
- Failure to explore client's perspective with genuine curiosity
- Conditional rather than unconditional acceptance
- Unilateral decision-making without collaboration

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

Client responses become progressively shorter and more dismissive as session continues ("Yeah," "Okay," "Can I go now?"), indicating withdrawal and disengagement. This session is likely to **decrease** motivation for smoking cessation and **increase** resistance. By continuing the pattern of unsolicited advice that client has experienced from multiple providers, psychiatrist confirms client's expectation that healthcare providers do not respect autonomy, triggering defensive responses.

**Therapeutic Relationship Damage:**

Client came with specific, reasonable request (dental treatment). Rather than honoring this, psychiatrist attempted redirection. This teaches client that preferences will not be respected and that defensive posturing is necessary in medical settings. Therapeutic relationship has been compromised, making future conversations about change less likely to be productive.

**Recommendation:**

This clinician requires intensive MI training focused on MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation) before continuing to use MI in clinical practice. Specific focus needed on: (1) respecting client autonomy and stated boundaries, (2) eliminating directive language and "but" statements, (3) developing genuine curiosity about client perspective, and (4) amplifying rather than contradicting client change talk. Supervised practice with feedback is essential before resuming MI-based interventions.
