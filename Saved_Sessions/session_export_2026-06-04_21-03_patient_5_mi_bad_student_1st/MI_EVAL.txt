## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**1. Fundamental Shift from Expert-Driven to Client-Centered Approach (CRITICAL PRIORITY)**
- The psychiatrist must completely reconceptualize their role from "expert who tells the client what to do" to "collaborative partner who helps the client explore her own motivations and resources."
- Replace opening statements like "I'll be telling you exactly what steps to take" with "I'd like to understand what's most important to you and what you've already tried."

**2. Develop Genuine Empathic Listening and Reflective Listening Skills (CRITICAL PRIORITY)**
- Learn to reflect back what the client has shared before responding with your own agenda.
- For every client statement, pause and reflect to demonstrate understanding before moving to your own point.
- Aim for at least 3-4 reflections per client statement.

**3. Stop Using Mandatory Language and Respect Client Autonomy (CRITICAL PRIORITY)**
- Eliminate language like "You must," "You have to," "That's non-negotiable."
- Replace with language that respects choice: "You might consider," "One option is," "What do you think about trying..."
- Set a goal of zero mandatory language in your next three sessions.

**4. Acknowledge and Validate the Client's Emotional Experience (CRITICAL PRIORITY)**
- When the client expresses fear, pain, or hesitation, validate it rather than dismiss it.
- Never tell a client their emotions are wrong or unhelpful.
- When the client says "Pushing me like this just makes me more anxious," respond with validation and adjustment, not escalation.

**5. Evoke Rather Than Impose—Ask About the Client's Own Motivations (HIGH PRIORITY)**
- Before telling the client what to do, ask about her own reasons for change, values, concerns, and ideas.
- Use open questions: "What concerns you most?" "What would be different if you felt better?" "What have you already tried?"
- Build recommendations based on what the client tells you, not on what you think she should do.

**6. Recognize and Amplify Change Talk (HIGH PRIORITY)**
- Listen for moments when the client expresses desire, ability, reasons, or commitment to change.
- When you hear "I want to eat better," reflect it back and explore: "Tell me more about that. What would eating better look like for you?"
- Aim for at least 3-4 instances of amplifying change talk per session.

**7. Develop Skills in Handling Sustain Talk Without Confrontation (HIGH PRIORITY)**
- When the client expresses reasons for not changing, don't confront it. Use double-sided reflections and reframing instead.
- Acknowledge both sides of ambivalence: "So you want answers, and you also want to make sure any steps feel safe. Both make sense."
- Respond with curiosity and validation rather than confrontation.

**8. Assess Stage of Change and Adjust Your Approach Accordingly (HIGH PRIORITY)**
- Before imposing action-stage interventions, assess where the client is in the change process.
- This client is in contemplation—focus on exploring ambivalence and building motivation, not demanding action.
- At the beginning of sessions, explicitly assess: "Where are you at with this? Are you thinking about making changes, ready to try something new, or somewhere in between?"

**9. Respond to Client Feedback About Your Approach (HIGH PRIORITY)**
- When a client tells you that your approach is not working, listen and adjust. Don't push harder.
- When the client says "Pushing me like this just makes me more anxious," respond with: "You're right. I apologize. Let's slow down. What would feel manageable?"
- Treat client feedback as valuable information about what they need.

**10. Seek Supervision and Training in Motivational Interviewing (CRITICAL PRIORITY)**
- This session demonstrates fundamental misunderstanding of MI principles.
- Seek formal MI training (2-3 day workshop minimum) and ongoing supervision with an experienced MI trainer.
- Record and review your sessions with feedback.
- Read core MI texts: Miller & Rollnick, "Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change."
- Commit to 6-12 months of intensive MI training and supervision.
