## CLINICAL NOTES FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

**Patient Diagnosis:** Major Neurocognitive Disorder with Lewy Bodies (progressive neurodegenerative condition)

**Key Clinical Features:**
- Irreversible cognitive decline with fluctuating attention
- Neurodegeneration (Lewy bodies pathology)
- Parkinsonism and motor decline
- Visual hallucinations
- REM sleep behavior disorder (violent dream enactment)
- Neuroleptic sensitivity
- Apathy and reduced social engagement

**Critical Issues Not Addressed:**
- Safety risk from REM sleep behavior disorder to wife
- Medication adherence and management
- Fall risk from parkinsonism
- Caregiver burden and support for Mrs. Estel
- Realistic functional goals given progressive decline
- Coordination with neurology

**Fundamental Problem:**
CBT cognitive restructuring assumes intact cognitive capacity, neuroplasticity, and ability to challenge/modify thoughts through reasoning. **None of these assumptions apply to this patient.** His cognitive decline is neurobiological, not psychological, and cannot be reversed through thought-challenging. Repeated attempts to teach cognitive reframing are not evidence-based for this population and may increase distress by implying patient can "think his way out" of progressive neurological disease.

**Potential Harms:**
1. False hope that psychotherapy can reverse or significantly improve cognitive decline
2. Delayed appropriate treatment (acceptance-based therapy, caregiver support, medical optimization, safety planning)
3. Invalidation of legitimate concerns about progressive decline
4. Missed safety issues
5. Unaddressed caregiver burden

**Supervisor Recommendations:**
- Immediate consultation with specialist in geriatric neuropsychology
- Comprehensive training in psychotherapy for neurocognitive disorders
- Reassessment of treatment approach and realistic goals
- Coordination with neurology for integrated care plan
- Consider whether standard CBT is appropriate or whether acceptance-based therapy, supportive therapy, or caregiver-focused intervention would be more suitable
