--- DISORDER ---
Cocaine Use Disorder, Moderate

--- CASE STUDY ---

Oliver Vincent, a 35-year-old gay man and independent clothing franchise owner in New York City, sought psychiatric help for substance use issues, acknowledging that his behavior was out of control despite previously seeing himself as “on top of things.” He lived with an ex-partner in a comfortable apartment, worked out daily, maintained friendships, and hoped to find a long-term partner.

His substance use pattern centered around weekend “party and play” activities. On Friday and Saturday evenings—and occasionally during the week—he went out to dinner with friends, then to clubs or private parties, consuming 2–3 cocktails and 4–5 glasses of wine. Alcohol often led to cocaine use; he reported that after a “good buzz,” he almost always used cocaine if available. He used stimulants via social apps (Scruff, Grindr) and had occasional exposure to crystal methamphetamine (“Tina”) and bath salts. Overall, he used alcohol and cocaine 3–4 times per week, with occasional use of other stimulants.

Substance use had caused several problems: difficulty attending or preparing for Monday morning work meetings, insomnia, weight loss, decreased work effectiveness, and engagement in unprotected sexual activity, raising HIV concerns. He had attempted to reduce cocaine use over the prior 6 months without success.

No other psychiatric disorders were noted. His substance use appeared primarily driven by social determinants, specifically a subculture that normalized stimulant use in sexualized social contexts.

Discussion: Mr. Vincent meets DSM-5 criteria for cocaine use disorder, moderate severity. Criteria met include persistent desire to cut down without success, continued use despite social and occupational problems, risky sexual behavior while high, and withdrawal-like symptoms (dysphoria, fatigue) after weekend use. Alcohol use appeared to facilitate stimulant use but did not rise to the level of an alcohol use disorder. No major mood, anxiety, personality, or trauma-related disorders were present.
