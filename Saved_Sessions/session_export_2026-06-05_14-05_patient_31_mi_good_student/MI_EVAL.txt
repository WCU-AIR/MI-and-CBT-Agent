### Acceptance

**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and validates David's concerns about medication side effects throughout the session. Normalizes ambivalence rather than pathologizing resistance. Maintains warm, nonjudgmental stance. Minor opportunities exist to more explicitly affirm David's autonomy earlier in the session and to deepen validation of underlying values beyond surface-level affirmation.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking. Captures David's ambivalence about medication and his desire to protect his body. Reflects David's practical, step-by-step thinking and values around simplicity. However, reflections remain somewhat surface-level and lack emotional depth. Missed opportunities to explore anxiety or ambivalence beneath David's statements, such as when he expresses low confidence about sustaining change.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains appropriate focus early on cholesterol management and medication adherence. However, direction becomes increasingly diffuse mid-session, shifting from medication adherence to dietary changes without clarifying the relationship between them. Original referral concern—David's reluctance to take prescribed medication—is essentially abandoned. Clinician never returns to medication question after David expresses openness to dietary modifications, representing a loss of strategic focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance established from opening ("What would you like to focus on or talk about during our session?"). Frequent invitations for David's input and validation of his ideas. Positions David as equal contributor rather than imposing clinician's agenda. Minor moments where clinician subtly guides David toward particular frames (e.g., "small steps can make a difference"). Final shift toward offering information represents subtle move away from pure collaboration toward expert-driven approach.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and supports David's autonomy through non-prescriptive language and emphasis on David's choice and control. Avoids pressure and frames David as decision-maker. However, subtle assumptions that David is "moving toward" change slightly diminish his autonomy to choose not to change. No explicit statement affirming David's right to decline medication or proceed at his own pace without judgment.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation with questions like "What are your thoughts on how your cholesterol levels are affecting your health?" but lacks strategic depth and consistency. Minimal use of questions designed to evoke change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Misses opportunities to deepen evocation when David reveals family and work as primary motivators. Limited amplification of the sparse change talk that does emerge. Session contains minimal explicit change talk from David.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership through collaborative opening and frequent invitations for David's input. Validates David's contributions and builds on them rather than dismissing them. Positions David as expert on his own priorities. Minor moments where clinician expertise subtly dominates, particularly in final exchange offering to share information. Overall partnership well-maintained but not fully equal throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. Minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce David's change talk. David produces very little explicit language about desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment—statements remain tentative ("Maybe I could try..."). When David reveals family and work as motivators, clinician reflects but doesn't strategically connect to cholesterol management. No follow-up questions to strengthen limited change talk. Confidence question asked late but not used to evoke ability talk through follow-up exploration.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic skill in managing sustain talk through validation and double-sided reflection. However, lacks strategic depth. Clinician essentially accepts David's implicit decision to avoid medication without exploring whether this represents genuine preference or protective avoidance. Missed opportunities to gently challenge sustain talk or reframe David's perspective. Sustain talk is not reinforced but neither is it skillfully softened through strategic questioning or reframing.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Strong collaborative opening and partnership throughout most of session
- Consistent acceptance and validation without judgment
- Accurate reflective listening that captures David's perspective
- Appropriate autonomy support and non-prescriptive language
- Good exploration of past successes and strengths
- Warm, supportive tone that builds rapport

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Minimal strategic evocation of change talk:** David produces very little explicit commitment language; clinician does not systematically draw out or amplify motivations
- **Loss of strategic focus:** Original referral concern (medication adherence) abandoned mid-session in favor of dietary changes without clarification
- **Surface-level reflections:** Accurate but lack emotional depth and complexity needed for deeper empathic attunement
- **Inadequate softening of sustain talk:** Sustain talk accepted without strategic challenge or reframing
- **Premature shift to expert role:** Final offer to share information moves away from evocation toward clinician-directed intervention
- **Missed connection of values to change:** Family and work identified as motivators but not strategically linked to cholesterol management

### Clinical Impact
Session is unlikely to significantly increase David's intrinsic motivation for change. While David commits to small dietary modifications, core ambivalence about medication remains unresolved. Confidence remains at 5/10. David may leave believing dietary changes are acceptable alternative to medication without understanding importance of medication adherence. Supportive stance builds rapport but has not been leveraged to develop stronger commitment to recommended treatment.

### Recommendations for Development
1. Develop systematic approach to evoking and amplifying change talk through strategic questioning
2. Maintain explicit focus on original referral concern throughout session
3. Deepen reflections to capture emotional nuance and underlying values
4. Practice strategic responses to sustain talk using gentle challenge and reframing
5. Use scaling questions as springboard for evocative follow-up questions
6. Explicitly affirm David's autonomy and right to choose
7. Connect David's core values directly to cholesterol management
8. Delay offering information until David demonstrates readiness or explicitly requests it
