Major Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Samuel King, a 52-year-old never-married janitor, presented for treatment of chronic depression. He had previously tried fluoxetine, citalopram, and supportive psychotherapy with limited benefit. He works full-time but engages minimally in activities outside of work.

Mr. King reported persistent low mood, anhedonia, insomnia, hopelessness, low energy, and difficulty concentrating and making decisions. He denied current suicidal thoughts but disclosed a prior suicide attempt during a period of alcohol abuse. He reported no current alcohol or illicit substance use.

He also exhibited obsessive-compulsive symptoms, including contamination fears (HIV, cleaning with bleach), aggression-related intrusive images, scrupulosity (fear of offending others), and symmetry/just-right compulsions (e.g., tub water level). Compulsions included excessive hand washing (up to 30 times daily), checking, repeating behaviors, mental reviewing of conversations, and diary-keeping. These behaviors caused significant impairment in social and occupational functioning.

On examination, he was casually dressed, smelled of bleach, and appeared worried and constricted but cooperative, coherent, and goal-directed. He denied hallucinations, delusions, or suicidal intent. Cognition was intact, and he demonstrated insight into the irrationality of his obsessions.

Discussion: Mr. King meets criteria for major depressive disorder based on persistent depressive symptoms, functional impairment, and history of prior suicide attempt. He also meets DSM-5 criteria for obsessive-compulsive disorder, with multiple obsessions and compulsions, causing distress and significant interference with daily life. His insight is good/fair, consistent with the DSM-5 specifier. Differential diagnoses such as substance-induced OCD, medical conditions, or other psychiatric disorders were ruled out. Comorbidities (e.g., body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder) should also be explored.
