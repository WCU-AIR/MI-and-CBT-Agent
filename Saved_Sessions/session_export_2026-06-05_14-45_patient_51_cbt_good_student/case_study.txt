--- DISORDER ---
Major Depressive Disorder
--- CASE STUDY ---
Crystal Smith was a 33-year-old African American woman who presented to an outpatient clinic with the chief complaint:
 “I need someone to talk to.”
 She reported feeling increasingly down and despairing over the past 8–10 months.
 She described waking up before dawn, tearful and fatigued, and having difficulty getting out of bed or completing household responsibilities.
 She felt guilty for not being her “usual self” and irritable with her husband and mother-in-law over minor issues.
 She noted significant weight loss—13 pounds over a few months—despite no changes in diet.
 She expressed passive thoughts of death, saying she sometimes thought she “should just give up” and “would be better off dead.”
 She reported no pleasure at her friend’s recent wedding, which she normally would have enjoyed.
 She had previously tried fluoxetine but discontinued it due to increased fatigue.
 Her husband encouraged her to seek help, citing episodes where she would pace and yell.
 She withdrew from social, familial, and religious activities, feeling her faith had weakened and that her pastor wouldn’t want to hear about her problems.
She had a history of distress in childhood following her parents' divorce and was referred to a school counselor due to irritability and fighting. She had a positive therapeutic experience at that time.
 No illicit substance use, no alcohol since pregnancy, no significant medical history.
 She lives with her husband of 13 years and their two school-aged children.
Mental status:
 Casually groomed, coherent and goal-directed, but guarded and slow to respond.
 Made poor eye contact with her therapist (a white, middle-aged clinician).
 Tearful at times, sad in affect, required encouragement to elaborate.
 Denied psychosis, confusion, current suicidal ideation, or homicidal thoughts.
 Cognition, insight, and judgment were normal.
Discussion:
 Crystal meets DSM-5 criteria for Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) with melancholic features, including depressed mood, anhedonia, insomnia, weight loss, psychomotor agitation, fatigue, and passive thoughts of death.
 Cultural dynamics, prior negative experience with mental health care, and role expectations may be influencing the presentation and treatment engagement.
