## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL FINDINGS

### **Strengths:**
- Maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation as treatment goal
- One moment of basic empathic reflection in fifth exchange
- Opening statement demonstrates awareness that MI principles exist
- Demonstrates genuine care for patient's wellbeing through persistence on serious health concern

### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist demonstrates an **authoritarian, expert-driven, directive approach** that is antithetical to MI spirit.

**Core Problems:**
1. **Expert-driven rather than evocative:** Tells client what to do rather than evoking client's own motivation
2. **Confrontational rather than collaborative:** Dismisses client ideas and pressures for compliance rather than partnering
3. **Judgmental rather than accepting:** Uses language framing struggle as character flaw ("stop making excuses," "lack of willpower")
4. **Controlling rather than autonomy-supporting:** Uses directives and implicit threats rather than affirming client's right to choose
5. **Fails to respond to direct feedback:** When client explicitly states "I need some empathy here, not just tough love," psychiatrist repeats same ineffective pattern

### **Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is **likely to decrease intrinsic motivation for change** rather than increase it. Client's responses show progressive resistance and defensiveness as session continues—a predictable outcome of confrontational approaches. Client moves from openness ("I'm hoping we can talk about how to finally make a change") to explicit pushback ("I need some empathy here") to defensive statements about being "trapped."

### **MI Competency Assessment:**

**Current Level:** Minimal MI competence. While awareness of MI principles exists, actual implementation is inconsistent with MI spirit and principles. Approach is more aligned with confrontational, directive counseling than motivational interviewing.

### **Clinical Recommendation:**

This psychiatrist should not continue using this approach with this client or others until receiving formal training and supervision in Motivational Interviewing principles and techniques. Current approach risks damaging therapeutic alliance and decreasing likelihood of successful behavior change.
