## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- **Clear, consistent focus on target behavior:** Psychiatrist maintains focus on smoking cessation and repeatedly returns to goal
- **Genuine concern about health:** Psychiatrist communicates authentic concern about patient's health and explicitly articulates health risks
- **Persistence:** Does not abandon goal despite patient resistance

### Critical Deficiencies
This session represents a **complete departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates **directive, coercive, shame-based counseling** that violates nearly every core MI principle:

1. **Authoritarian stance replacing partnership:** Expert-driven, hierarchical position; patient input systematically dismissed
2. **Judgment and shame replacing acceptance:** Repeated labels ("weak," "excuses," "nonsense"); explicit judgment perceived by patient
3. **Confrontation replacing empathy:** Arguments against patient perspective; zero reflective listening; dismissal of legitimate concerns
4. **Coercion replacing autonomy support:** Pressure, threats, demands; predictable increase in resistance
5. **Telling replacing evoking:** Prescriptive plan imposed without exploration; all questions closed or leading
6. **Intensified resistance:** Patient's resistance increases throughout session; explicit statement: "pushing me like this just makes me wanna push back"

### Impact on Client Engagement
This approach is **counterproductive to behavior change**. Patient enters with change talk and workable ambivalence; psychiatrist's coercive approach transforms this into explicit refusal. Patient demonstrates increasing resistance from initial ambivalence ("I don't really want to talk about quitting right now") to explicit refusal ("I'm not gonna commit to using patches or gum today").

### Immediate Recommendations
1. **Shift from expert to partner** (eliminate "I'll tell you what you need to do")
2. **Replace judgment with acceptance** (eliminate shame-based language)
3. **Implement reflective listening** (demonstrate genuine understanding before responding)
4. **Respect autonomy** (eliminate pressure; affirm patient's right to choose)
5. **Evoke rather than tell** (ask genuine open questions about patient's motivations)
6. **Complete formal MI training** (certified workshop + ongoing supervision essential)

### Current MI Competency Level
**No competency in Motivational Interviewing.** This is not "MI with rough edges"—this is a fundamental departure from MI principles. Formal training and supervision required before continuing behavior change counseling.
