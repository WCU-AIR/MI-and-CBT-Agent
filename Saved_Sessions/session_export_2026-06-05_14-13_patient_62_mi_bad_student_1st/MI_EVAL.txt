## Overall Assessment

**Current MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Contraindicated Practice

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates almost no competence in MI principles and actively violates core MI spirit—partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. The approach is authoritarian, confrontational, coercive, and systematically invalidates the patient's experience.

**Key Findings:**

- **Strengths:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation as target behavior; demonstrates knowledge of health consequences.
- **Critical Deficiencies:** Complete absence of MI spirit; systematic invalidation of patient experience; confrontational approach to resistance; zero collaborative exploration; coercive pressure; no evocation of change talk; repetitive, inflexible approach; explicit rejection of patient contributions.

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is **highly likely to decrease** patient motivation for change and increase resistance. Patient demonstrates progressive disengagement: initial hesitation → explicit statements of not being ready → frustration with approach → explicit feedback that pressure causes reactance → withdrawal. Patient explicitly states: "Forcing me just makes me want to push back even more."

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:**

**Very Low.** The session activates psychological reactance through perceived threats to autonomy and damages therapeutic alliance. Patient is unlikely to return for follow-up or engage in behavior change based on this interaction.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Urgent formal MI training required** (minimum 2-day workshop)
2. **Ongoing supervision with MI-trained clinician** (minimum 10 hours with recorded session review)
3. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** (foundational change required)
4. **Replace confrontation with validation and reflective listening**
5. **Develop genuine evocative questioning skills**
6. **Respect patient autonomy—eliminate coercive pressure**

**Clinical Recommendation:**

This psychiatrist should not continue conducting MI sessions without formal training and supervision. Current approach is contraindicated and potentially harmful. Urgent engagement with MI training and supervision is essential before continuing this work.
