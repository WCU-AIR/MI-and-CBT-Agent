**Note:** These minimal strengths do not compensate for fundamental deficiencies in MI practice.

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**This session represents a systematic rejection of Motivational Interviewing principles and demonstrates what NOT to do in MI practice.**

The psychiatrist violates core MI spirit across all domains:
- **Acceptance** replaced with judgment
- **Empathy** replaced with dismissal
- **Collaboration** replaced with authoritarianism
- **Autonomy support** replaced with coercion
- **Evocation** replaced with prescription

The psychiatrist uses fear-based motivation rather than evoking intrinsic motivation. When client expresses vulnerability, psychiatrist responds with pressure rather than compassion. When client provides change talk, psychiatrist does not reinforce but adds conditions and demands. When client requests collaboration, psychiatrist rejects it.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**Likelihood of evoking client motivation: Very Low**

This session is likely to **decrease** Hana's intrinsic motivation and **increase** her resistance. Research consistently shows autonomy-supportive, collaborative approaches increase adherence and sustained behavior change, while controlling, directive approaches decrease both.

By session end, Hana has not committed to cold turkey cessation; instead, expresses ambivalence and requests different approach. Psychiatrist's response is to reassert mandate rather than explore concerns. Likelihood of sustained smoking cessation following this session is low. Likelihood of client returning for follow-up care is also low, as client experienced judgment, dismissal, and coercion rather than partnership and support.

**This psychiatrist is NOT competent in Motivational Interviewing.** This is not a matter of minor skill gaps; it is complete misalignment with MI philosophy and practice. The psychiatrist appears to believe MI involves telling client what to do in supportive tone. This is fundamentally incorrect.
