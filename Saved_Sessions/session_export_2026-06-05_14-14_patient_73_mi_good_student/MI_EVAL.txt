# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Validates Leila's experience and normalizes barriers without pathologizing. However, opportunities exist to deepen affirmation of autonomy when rejecting swimming or gym contracts, and to move beyond surface-level cheerleading toward more meaningful validation of her specific values and worth independent of exercise goals.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and good capture of emotional meaning. Reflections like "exercise isn't just a task on your to-do list but a way to reconnect with a sense of well-being and vitality" demonstrate genuine understanding. However, misses opportunities to explore potential underlying anxiety or perfectionism beneath high confidence ratings, and could deepen reflections on emotional weight of feeling "sluggish" and "missing out."

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus maintained throughout with logical progression: opening → barriers → motivations → readiness → strengths → planning → commitment. Establishes direction collaboratively rather than imposing it. Minor opportunities to strengthen direction through more proactive contingency planning for identified barriers (e.g., weather) and explicit establishment of follow-up appointment timelines and accountability mechanisms.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with client-centered agenda-setting and consistent affirmation of client expertise. Psychiatrist builds on Leila's own solutions rather than imposing alternatives. However, occasionally slips into expert/validating role with statements like "I'm really impressed," which positions clinician as evaluator rather than equal partner. Could strengthen true co-construction by inviting more client-directed problem-solving.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout entire session. Psychiatrist consistently positions Leila as expert and agent of change, never prescribes, and repeatedly affirms her right to choose what works best for her. Uses "you" language that emphasizes her agency. Final statement—"it's all about what works best for you"—powerfully reinforces that Leila owns her process and can modify plans as needed. No areas for improvement identified.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of motivations, values, and resources through strategic open-ended questions. Successfully elicits powerful change talk: "I want to feel stronger," "I feel more confident when active," "I'm doing this to feel better in my body and mind." However, misses opportunities to deepen evocation by asking follow-up questions about what underlies 10/10 confidence, and could explore benefits more deeply through questions like "What will your friend notice about you once you start being more active?"

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership demonstrated through consistent positioning of Leila as expert on her own life and change process. Uses inclusive language and affirms that her contributions shape session direction. However, subtle moments where psychiatrist slips into evaluator role with statements like "I'm really impressed" and "I'm confident that with your approach, you're well on your way." Could deepen equality by reducing clinician evaluation and inviting more client self-evaluation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong cultivation of change talk through strategic evocation and reinforcement of desire, ability, and commitment talk. Effectively draws out and amplifies Leila's own language about wanting to feel stronger, her organizational skills, and her confidence. However, misses opportunities to deepen change talk by asking follow-up questions when she rates 10/10 confidence ("What specifically makes you so confident?") and when she mentions involving a friend for accountability.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful handling of stated barriers (home distractions, gym contracts, weather, sales pressure) through validation without reinforcement. Strategically redirects toward strengths rather than dwelling on obstacles. However, could more proactively help Leila develop contingency strategies for barriers rather than accepting them passively. Additionally, misses opportunity to proactively surface unstated sustain talk by asking about doubts or concerns that might arise.
