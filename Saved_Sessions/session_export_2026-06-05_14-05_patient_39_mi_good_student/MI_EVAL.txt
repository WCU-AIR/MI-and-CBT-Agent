### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates some evocation with open-ended questions ("What do you think matters most to you?"), but lacks strategic amplification of Elena's change talk. Strong spontaneous change talk is present ("I really don't wanna ever feel that panic again") but is reflected rather than deepened. Missed opportunities to ask follow-up questions that strengthen motivation (e.g., "Tell me more about that panic" or "What would it mean to never feel that way again?"). Information about contraception methods is clinician-provided rather than evoking Elena's own knowledge and preferences first. Limited exploration of Elena's values, strengths, and self-efficacy. Evocation is present but inconsistently applied and relatively superficial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness identified. Elena provides strong spontaneous change talk early in session ("I really don't wanna ever feel that panic again about maybe being pregnant"), but psychiatrist does not strategically amplify, deepen, or reinforce this motivation. After Elena expresses core motivation, clinician reflects but doesn't ask follow-up questions to strengthen it. When Elena expresses interest in long-acting methods ("sounds kinda nice"), clinician reflects without cultivating further. Information about contraception methods is provided without first evoking Elena's own curiosity, knowledge, and preferences—a critical missed opportunity. Clinician doesn't strategically evoke Elena's self-efficacy or past successes. This represents a critical gap in MI practice that significantly limits session effectiveness in evoking intrinsic motivation for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates adequate but inconsistent handling of Elena's sustain talk. Approach is generally to validate and normalize hesitation ("It makes complete sense that you're feeling cautious") rather than strategically redirect or explore underlying barriers. When Elena expresses fear about bodily concerns, clinician reflects but doesn't strategically reframe or explore what's underneath. Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections that name both Elena's desire to protect herself and her hesitation, then gently explore the tension. Clinician appropriately avoids reinforcing sustain talk or becoming confrontational, maintaining collaborative stance. However, sustain talk is not strategically softened or deeply explored. Indefinite delay ("Maybe I just need more time") is validated but not redirected toward concrete steps.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current MI Competency Level:** Intermediate (Basic to Intermediate)

**Overall Session Score:** 3.6/5

**Key Strengths:**
- Exemplary autonomy support with consistent affirmation of Elena's right to choose and respect for her timeline
- Strong collaborative stance with appropriate agenda-setting and boundary-respecting
- Solid acceptance and empathy with consistent validation and accurate reflective listening
- Warm, supportive demeanor creating safe, non-pressuring environment
- Appropriate direction with clear focus on contraception and progressive deepening

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Minimal strategic cultivation of change talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY): Elena provides strong spontaneous change talk that is not amplified or deepened through strategic questioning
- **Evoke before informing**: Clinician provides information without first evoking Elena's own knowledge, curiosities, and preferences
- **Superficial exploration of barriers**: Hesitations and fears are validated but not strategically explored for deeper understanding
- **Limited evocation of self-efficacy**: Missed opportunities to strengthen Elena's confidence through exploring past successes
- **Limited strategic use of double-sided reflections**: Ambivalence is acknowledged but not explicitly named and explored

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
The session creates a safe, respectful environment where Elena feels heard and supported. However, it does not strategically strengthen her intrinsic motivation for contraception use. Elena leaves with a concrete next step (talking to friends, confidence 7/10) but without having deeply explored or strengthened her own reasons for considering contraception. Her overall importance rating remains at baseline (6/10). The psychiatrist has created necessary conditions for change but has not strategically evoked Elena's own motivation to move forward.

**Recommendations for Development:**
1. Focus on strategic amplification of change talk through deepening questions
2. Practice evoking client's own knowledge and preferences before providing information
3. Develop deeper exploration of barriers with genuine curiosity
4. Increase use of double-sided reflections to name and explore ambivalence
5. Strategically evoke self-efficacy and past successes to build confidence
