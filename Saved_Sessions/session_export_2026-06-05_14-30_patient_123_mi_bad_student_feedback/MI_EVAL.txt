### Acceptance

**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a near-complete absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard. ### Empathy

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains consistent focus on exercise/activity as the target behavior, which shows some directional clarity. However, direction is imposed through mandate rather than collaborative exploration. The clinician insists on "10 minutes a day, no excuses" and "non-negotiable" commitments without exploring Sofia's readiness or capacity. ## Summary of Clinical Findings

### Strengths
- Maintains consistent focus on exercise/activity as the target behavior
- Offers specific, concrete suggestions (daily walks, phone reminders, 10 minutes per day)
- Expresses genuine concern for Sofia's health outcomes

### Critical Deficiencies
- **Fundamental violation of MI spirit:** The session violates virtually every core MI principle
- **Coercive and controlling language:** Repeated use of "must," "have to," "no excuses," "not optional," and explicit rejection of Sofia's input
- **Judgment and dismissal:** Sofia's legitimate concerns are framed as "excuses" rather than valid barriers
- **Complete absence of empathetic engagement:** Zero reflective listening, no exploration of emotional experience, no complex reflections
- **Expert-driven rather than collaborative:** Tells rather than asks, prescribes rather than evokes, explicitly rejects Sofia's participation
- **Undermines autonomy:** Removes Sofia's sense of choice at the exact moment when autonomy support is most critical
- **Fails to cultivate change talk:** Sofia's own motivations are dismissed rather than amplified
- **Strengthens sustain talk:** Confrontational approach triggers psychological reactance and increases Sofia's resistance throughout the session
- **Ignores client's stated needs:** Sofia explicitly states she needs realistic, manageable steps and fears pressure-induced failure. The psychiatrist does the opposite.

### Impact on Client Engagement
This session will almost certainly harm Sofia's motivation for change. She enters with genuine desire to change and realistic self-awareness. By the end, she is expressing stronger resistance and explicitly stating the approach makes her "want to shut down." The psychiatrist's coercive approach triggers psychological reactance—Sofia's natural response to perceived threats to her autonomy—which will decrease her likelihood of following through and increase her defensiveness.

### Primary Recommendation
**The psychiatrist must fundamentally shift from an expert-driven, coercive stance to a collaborative, autonomy-supportive, empathetic stance.** This requires formal Motivational Interviewing training and ongoing supervision to develop genuine understanding of the MI spirit: that wisdom about change resides within the client, that autonomy is essential to lasting change, and that the clinician's role is to facilitate the client's own discovery and motivation—not to impose solutions.
