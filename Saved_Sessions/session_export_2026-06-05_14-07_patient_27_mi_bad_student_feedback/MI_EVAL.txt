## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on two target behaviors (childcare and substance use)
- Underlying motivation to help client and protect child is clear and appropriate
- Brief moment in fifth intervention suggests some awareness that different approach might be needed

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and actively undermines therapeutic alliance. Psychiatrist employs consistently authoritarian, prescriptive approach violating nearly every core MI principle. Client is not refusing change but expressing readiness level and requesting collaborative approach. Psychiatrist's response communicates that client's readiness doesn't matter and compliance with clinician's timeline is required. This approach is likely to decrease intrinsic motivation, increase resistance, and potentially result in treatment dropout.

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Seek MI-focused supervision and training with recorded session feedback
2. Shift from prescription to evocation through open-ended questioning
3. Develop genuine curiosity about client's perspective and constraints
4. Practice reflective listening to demonstrate empathy
5. Respect client autonomy and readiness level
6. Recognize and amplify client's change talk
7. Affirm client strengths and efforts explicitly
8. Use collaborative language ("we," "let's") rather than directive language
9. Handle sustain talk with empathy and strategic reframing, not confrontation
10. Attend formal MI training workshop and seek ongoing supervision

**Conclusion:** While well-intentioned, current approach is counterproductive. MI is a learnable skill; with focused training and deliberate practice, this clinician can develop genuine competence and significantly improve client outcomes.
