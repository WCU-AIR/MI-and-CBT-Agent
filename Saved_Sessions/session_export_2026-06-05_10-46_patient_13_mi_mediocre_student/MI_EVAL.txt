## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths
- Warm, respectful opening that creates safe, non-judgmental atmosphere
- Basic reflective listening ability demonstrated
- Awareness of motivational assessment (importance/confidence scaling)
- Gentle, non-confrontational tone throughout

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**Fundamental Departure from MI Principles:** This session represents a significant departure from evidence-based Motivational Interviewing. The psychiatrist operates almost entirely in an expert-driven, directive mode, repeatedly prescribing solutions rather than evoking Amira's own motivations and wisdom. This violates the core MI principle that expertise and motivation reside within the client.

**Missed Change Talk Opportunities:** When Amira expresses "I really shouldn't have done it" and "I know I should want to get better," these are genuine statements of desire and need for change—yet the psychiatrist never reflects, amplifies, or builds on them. Instead, the psychiatrist pivots to prescriptions, implicitly communicating that Amira's own insights are insufficient.

**Autonomy Undermined:** The psychiatrist repeatedly overrides Amira's stated preferences with alternative suggestions, communicating that the psychiatrist knows better than Amira what she is capable of. This undermines her sense of agency and self-efficacy—particularly damaging for a client already struggling with hopelessness.

**Ambivalence Not Explored:** Rather than using Amira's ambivalence as an opportunity to strengthen change talk, the psychiatrist attempts to resolve it through reassurance and counter-argument, which can inadvertently reinforce resistance.

**Compliance, Not Motivation:** Amira's capitulation to trying a walk ("Yeah, I guess I can try") reflects compliance rather than genuine intrinsic motivation. Her sustain talk remains strong ("I'm still not sure if it's really going to change anything"), suggesting the psychiatrist's approach has not effectively evoked motivation for change.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

This session is likely to have a negative impact on Amira's engagement and motivation. While the gentle tone prevents overt harm, the repeated directives may reinforce her sense of helplessness and external locus of control—particularly damaging for a client already struggling with hopelessness. Amira's capitulation is likely to be short-lived; without genuine intrinsic motivation or belief in her own capacity for change, behavioral compliance is fragile. Research demonstrates that client-generated change talk is a strong predictor of actual behavior change; the absence of this in the session suggests low likelihood of follow-through.

### Highest Priority Recommendations

1. **Shift from Prescriptive to Evocative Stance:** Replace "should" and "might want to" language with genuine open questions designed to evoke Amira's own ideas, values, and motivations.

2. **Amplify and Reinforce Change Talk:** When Amira offers change talk, pause and deepen it through reflection and follow-up questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

3. **Explore and Validate Protective Hopelessness:** When Amira expresses doubt or hopelessness, explore the protective function rather than counter-arguing. Use double-sided reflection to honor both her recognition of harm and her protective stance.

4. **Collaborate on Problem Definition:** Rather than assuming what Amira's problem is, genuinely collaborate to define the problem and goals together.

5. **Support Autonomy Consistently:** Communicate that Amira is the expert on her own life and has the right to choose her own path. Use language emphasizing choice, agency, and her capacity for self-direction.

6. **Seek Formal MI Training:** This session demonstrates significant departures from evidence-based MI practice. Seek formal training, supervision, or consultation with an MI-certified trainer.

---

**Overall Assessment:** The psychiatrist demonstrates basic counseling skills but lacks fundamental Motivational Interviewing competence. A fundamental shift from expert-driven prescription to collaborative evocation is required to practice effective MI with this client.
