## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

**Therapist Strengths:**
- Demonstrated genuine warmth, concern, and non-judgmental acceptance
- Accurately identified key presenting symptoms (depersonalization, derealization, avoidance)
- Attempted multiple intervention modalities (cognitive, behavioral, somatic)
- Assigned homework rather than skipping between-session work
- Maintained patient engagement throughout sessions

**Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Intervention:**
1. **No trauma-focused conceptualization** — Dissociation treated as isolated symptom rather than acute stress response to fiancé's murder and suicide attempt
2. **Absent safety assessment and planning** — No suicide risk evaluation despite recent attempt; critical clinical gap
3. **Non-collaborative approach** — Directive advice-giving rather than shared decision-making; violates CBT collaborative empiricism
4. **Minimal guided discovery** — Reliance on suggestions rather than Socratic questioning
5. **Vague agenda-setting and no feedback elicitation** — Fundamental CBT structure absent
6. **Oversimplified technique application** — Cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, and grounding taught without precision or systematic approach
7. **No session-to-session progression** — Identical second session suggests no learning, no homework review, no treatment advancement

**Recommended Supervision Plan:**
- Immediate: Safety assessment and planning training; trauma-focused CBT consultation
- Short-term: Weekly supervision with session recording review; Socratic questioning practice; collaborative agenda-setting role-plays
- Ongoing: Training in trauma-focused CBT (TF-CBT or Prolonged Exposure); grief and loss work; cultural competence in working with trauma survivors
- Case management: Consider pairing with trauma specialist for consultation on this case; reassess therapist readiness to work with trauma survivors

**Prognosis for This Patient:**
While case notes indicate patient's acute dissociative symptoms improved significantly during hospitalization and largely resolved within one month, this appears to reflect natural recovery trajectory rather than therapeutic intervention. Current therapy approach is unlikely to accelerate recovery, facilitate trauma processing, or prevent future psychiatric sequelae. Therapy misses critical opportunities for meaningful intervention and may inadvertently reinforce avoidance through oversimplified coping suggestions.
