### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1.5
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal change talk cultivation and actively suppresses Andrea's emerging change talk. When Andrea offers desire talk ("Maybe I could think about cutting down first"), clinician dismisses it ("cutting down often doesn't work"). When Andrea shows ability talk ("I'm careful about school"), clinician contradicts it ("you're underestimating the risks"). When Andrea identifies reasons she cares about health ("keeping my grades up"), clinician dismisses as insufficient. When Andrea shows commitment talk ("I'd rather try to cut back"), clinician responds "Half measures won't help." Late-session change talk ("slowing down might be good") is not amplified; session ends without follow-up. Clinician never asks open-ended questions designed to evoke change talk ("What would be benefits?" "How might reducing help you?"). Change talk is suppressed rather than strategically cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates poor handling of sustain talk through direct confrontation rather than skillful softening. When Andrea expresses sustain talk ("I don't think it's that bad," "I'm not sure I really need to quit," "It's hard to take seriously"), clinician confronts directly ("You're underestimating the risks," "You're making excuses," "You really can't ignore the facts"). These confrontations likely strengthen sustain talk rather than soften it (boomerang effect). Clinician never uses double-sided reflection to explore ambivalence or reframing to align with values. Never explores function of sustain talk ("What makes it hard to imagine stopping?") or uses it as information about barriers. When Andrea expresses concern about peer relationships, clinician does not explore this barrier or use it to understand ambivalence. No skillful softening of resistance occurs.

## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

**Clinical Judgment:** This session demonstrates **severely deficient Motivational Interviewing practice** that is fundamentally inconsistent with MI principles and spirit.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- ☐ Repeated directive pressure and demands ("You have to," "You must," "It's the only way")
- ☐ Systematic dismissal of client's perspective and proposed alternatives
- ☐ Character attacks and shame-based coercion ("If you really cared about your health...")
- ☐ Suppression of change talk rather than amplification
- ☐ Violation of autonomy through coercive language and removal of choice
- ☐ Failure to evoke client's own motivations and values
- ☐ Confrontational handling of resistance rather than skillful softening
- ☐ Therapeutic alliance rupture without genuine repair

**Critical Concern:** This approach is likely to **decrease Andrea's intrinsic motivation for change.** Research demonstrates that confrontation and pressure increase resistance. Andrea's responses show increasing defensiveness, and she explicitly communicates the approach is ineffective ("you're not hearing me").

**Positive Element:** Final segment demonstrates awareness that collaborative, exploratory approach may be more effective, but this comes too late and is insufficiently developed.

---

## IMMEDIATE RECOMMENDATIONS

**Priority 1 – Fundamental Retraining Required:**
Clinician must shift from expert-authority stance to collaborative, evocative, client-centered approach. Core competencies requiring development:
- Ask rather than tell
- Evoke rather than impose
- Explore rather than confront
- Support autonomy rather than demand compliance
- Amplify change talk rather than suppress it
- Soften resistance rather than fight it

**Priority 2 – Supervision:**
Recommend immediate clinical supervision with MI-trained supervisor. Record and review sessions focusing on:
- Elimination of directive language ("have to," "must," "need to," "should")
- Development of open-ended questioning skills
- Practice with double-sided reflections
- Alliance repair techniques

**Priority 3 – Training:**
Recommend formal MI training or refresher course with emphasis on:
- MI spirit and principles
- Evocation techniques
- Change talk cultivation
- Resistance as information, not obstacle

**Clinical Impact:** Without these changes, clinician will continue to harm patients by decreasing intrinsic motivation for change and damaging therapeutic relationships. **Immediate intervention is necessary.**
