## SUPERVISOR SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Critical Findings

**Strengths:**
- Warm, non-threatening demeanor; genuine concern for patient wellbeing
- Validation of distress; normalization of some experiences
- Consistency in reassurance; patient felt safe and not judged
- Recognition of small positive moments (song making patient smile)

**Severe Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental misunderstanding of trauma and ASD:** Treated post-accident symptoms as equivalent to exam stress rather than acute trauma requiring specialized intervention
- **Complete absence of CBT techniques:** No psychoeducation, cognitive restructuring, exposure, behavioral experiments, thought records, or emotion regulation skills
- **Reinforcement of avoidance:** Repeated suggestions for distraction and positive thinking implicitly endorsed avoidance—the primary mechanism maintaining ASD
- **No assessment or case conceptualization:** Lacked thorough symptom assessment, trigger identification, or linking of symptoms to trauma processes
- **No treatment plan or strategy:** Interventions were random and disconnected from ASD pathology
- **Excessive self-disclosure:** Repeated personal anecdotes minimized patient's trauma and shifted focus from patient to therapist
- **No homework or between-session structure:** Patient left without concrete tools or clear expectations for independent work
- **Failure to recognize and respond to feedback:** Patient explicitly stated positive thinking wasn't working; therapist continued offering same ineffective advice

### Impact on Patient Care

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit:** Low to harmful. Session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT. By reinforcing avoidance and distraction rather than processing and exposure, therapist likely reinforced Dylan's maladaptive coping and may have worsened his condition. For a 15-year-old in acute phase of trauma (2 weeks post-accident), early intervention is critical. This session has squandered that opportunity.

**Risk of Harm:** Moderate to high. Continued use of avoidance-reinforcing strategies may chronify acute stress disorder into chronic PTSD. Patient may lose faith in treatment, reducing willingness to engage in evidence-based therapy in future.

### Immediate Actions Required

1. **Specialized Training (URGENT):** Enroll in trauma-focused CBT (TF-CBT) certification program or Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT) training. Do not continue treating trauma patients without this training.

2. **Comprehensive Trauma Assessment:** Conduct thorough assessment including trauma history, current symptoms mapped to DSM-5 criteria, triggers, avoidance patterns, catastrophic thoughts, and functional impairment.

3. **Case Conceptualization:** Develop written formulation linking Dylan's symptoms to underlying trauma processes and identifying maintaining factors (avoidance, catastrophic thoughts, hypervigilance).

4. **Psychoeducation:** Provide clear, age-appropriate explanation of how trauma affects the brain, why symptoms occur, and why processing (not avoidance) is necessary for recovery.

5. **Eliminate Self-Disclosure:** Maintain professional boundaries. Self-disclosure should be rare, brief, and clearly in service of patient's treatment.

6. **Cognitive Assessment:** Systematically identify Dylan's automatic thoughts and core beliefs related to trauma (e.g., "I'm not safe in cars," "Something bad will happen again").

7. **Graded Exposure Plan:** Create hierarchy of trauma reminders from least to most anxiety-provoking. Work with Dylan to gradually face reminders in controlled way to reduce hypervigilance and avoidance.

8. **Emotion Regulation Skills:** Teach specific skills for managing anger outbursts and physiological hyperarousal (grounding techniques, progressive muscle relaxation, cognitive strategies).

9. **Behavioral Activation:** Identify valued activities Dylan has withdrawn from and create plan to gradually re-engage despite anxiety.

10. **Structured Homework:** Assign specific, written homework with clear rationale. Examples: thought records, behavioral experiments, graded exposure tasks, emotion logs, psychoeducation reading.

11. **Supervision:** Arrange regular supervision with trauma-specialized clinician. Present case, discuss treatment planning, and develop trauma competence.

12. **Feedback Solicitation:** At end of each session, explicitly ask: "Was this helpful? Did this make sense? What would be most useful to focus on?" Adjust approach based on feedback.

### Competency Assessment

**Current Level:** Basic counseling skills present (warmth, empathy, non-judgment) but lacks specialized knowledge of trauma and CBT. Operating below standard for evidence-based trauma treatment.

**Recommended Competency Development Path:**
- Immediate: Enroll in TF-CBT or CPT training program
- 3 months: Complete foundational trauma training; begin supervised practice
- 6 months: Demonstrate competency in trauma assessment, case conceptualization, and psychoeducation
- 12 months: Demonstrate competency in exposure-based interventions and cognitive restructuring for trauma

**Supervision Frequency:** Weekly supervision with trauma-specialized clinician until competency demonstrated.
