# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and validates Adrian's perspective throughout ("It's really helpful to hear what's on your mind"). Maintains nonjudgmental stance when Adrian expresses reluctance to change. However, could strengthen acceptance by more explicitly affirming Adrian's autonomy as a natural part of the contemplation process and by recognizing his resourcefulness and survival skills that have sustained him thus far.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking. Captures Adrian's ambivalence and emotional experience well, particularly in the reflection: "You're feeling caught between the discomfort and worry from the infection and your current way of using meth." Demonstrates understanding of the emotional weight of his dilemma. Misses some opportunities to explore underlying fears and values—for example, when Adrian expresses concern about fun being taken away, could have asked "Tell me more about what that would mean for you" to deepen empathic understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on the presenting concern (infection and meth use) but lacks strategic clarity and specific behavioral targets. Session drifts toward abstract discussions of "balance" and "control" without explicitly establishing concrete goals early on. Does not clarify whether the focus is harm reduction or abstinence. Concrete behavioral experiment emerges primarily from Adrian's own thinking rather than strategic clinician guidance. Would benefit from earlier, more explicit establishment of specific change targets.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes strong collaborative stance from opening question: "Before we get started, I want to make sure this time is focused on what's most important to you." Consistently seeks Adrian's perspective and incorporates his ideas as his own insights. Uses collaborative language ("What do you think might help...") rather than prescriptive language. Minor opportunities to strengthen collaboration by inviting Adrian's thinking before offering clinician suggestions, and by explicitly confirming accuracy of reflections ("Is that how it feels to you?").

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and supports Adrian's autonomy throughout. Accepts his stated reluctance to change without pushing or challenging. Uses language emphasizing Adrian's choice and control ("What do you think might help you feel more confident..."). Explicitly affirms Adrian's autonomy as a strength when he expresses concern about "giving up control." Could be more explicit by stating: "Whatever you decide to do is completely up to you—my role is to help you think through what makes sense for you, not to convince you to change."

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some open questions but demonstrates limited strategic evocation of Adrian's own motivations, values, and reasons for change. Reflects Adrian's change talk (infection being "uncomfortable," "messing with me more than expected") without amplifying through follow-up questions like "Tell me more about that" or "What was the hardest part?" Misses opportunities to explore deeper values around fun, connection, and relief from boredom. Does not strategically amplify Adrian's change talk or use MI-specific evocative questioning techniques. This represents a significant gap in MI technique.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes and maintains genuine partnership stance throughout, positioning Adrian as the expert on his own experience. Explicitly affirms Adrian's wisdom and agency ("It's great that you're thinking about changes that fit smoothly into your routine"). Treats Adrian's ideas as his own insights rather than clinician suggestions. Avoids expert role by asking questions rather than providing prescriptive advice. Minor opportunities to strengthen partnership by inviting Adrian's thinking before clinician interpretation and by asking "What do you think would help?" before offering suggestions.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Psychiatrist reflects Adrian's change talk but rarely amplifies it or uses strategic questioning to deepen motivation. Adrian offers multiple pieces of change talk (infection being uncomfortable and unexpected, trying to be careful, acknowledging unknown substances in meth, willingness to try safer practices) that pass without amplification or reinforcement. Does not use strategic MI questions ("What concerns do you have?" "What would be the benefits?"). Fails to use readiness ruler strategically to evoke specific change talk. Does not amplify Adrian's final behavioral experiment ("What would it mean to you if that actually worked?"). This represents the most critical gap in MI technique and likely limits session effectiveness in building intrinsic motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent skill in handling Adrian's sustain talk. Skillfully handles "I don't really want to stop using" by accepting and exploring actual concerns rather than confronting. Empathically reflects concern about losing fun and pivots toward finding middle ground. However, misses opportunities to gently explore contradictions—for example, when Adrian says "I've got it under control" but had an infection, could ask "How do you make sense of that?" Does not use double-sided reflections strategically to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence. Could more skillfully reframe sustain talk without confrontation.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current MI Competency Level:** Intermediate (Basic to Intermediate)

**Session Strengths:**
- Strong alliance-building and genuine therapeutic partnership
- Consistent acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- Accurate reflective listening that captures ambivalence
- Excellent autonomy support and respect for Adrian's choice
- Warm, supportive, nonjudgmental tone throughout
- Concrete behavioral outcome (Adrian generates specific experiment)

**Session Weaknesses:**
- Minimal strategic evocation and amplification of change talk (CRITICAL GAP)
- Lack of strategic direction toward specific behavioral targets
- Insufficient exploration of Adrian's values and meaning
- Passive approach to readiness scaling
- Limited use of advanced MI techniques (double-sided reflections, strategic reframing)
- Missed opportunities to explore discrepancies between beliefs and experience

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Adrian feels heard and respected, evidenced by increasing openness and willingness to generate ideas. However, movement toward change appears to emerge from Adrian's own thinking rather than from strategic clinician evocation. Without amplification of change talk and deeper exploration of values, Adrian may approach his behavioral experiment with ambivalence rather than genuine motivation, reducing likelihood of follow-through.

**Primary Recommendation:**
Focus on strategic evocation and amplification of change talk through deliberate use of MI-specific questioning techniques, particularly: (1) following change talk with open questions that deepen it, (2) using strategic readiness ruler follow-ups, (3) exploring and leveraging Adrian's core values, and (4) amplifying his own behavioral experiments with questions that explore meaning and commitment.

**Overall Session Score: 3.4/5** – Competent foundational MI with strong alliance-building but significant gaps in strategic technique for evoking intrinsic motivation.
