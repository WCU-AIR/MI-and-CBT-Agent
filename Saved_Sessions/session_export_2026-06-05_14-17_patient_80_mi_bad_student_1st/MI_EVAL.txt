# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Pervasive judgment and dismissal of client throughout session. Clinician explicitly rejects patient's emotional experience ("I don't want to hear about how overwhelmed you are"), uses derogatory labels ("making excuses," "stubborn," "dragging your feet"), and demonstrates no unconditional positive regard. Opening statement establishes authoritarian stance rather than acceptance. No affirmation of patient's efforts or acknowledgment of his attempts to address the problem. Fundamental violation of MI's core principle of acceptance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of reflective listening and empathic attunement. Clinician never reflects back patient's statements to demonstrate understanding. When patient expresses overwhelm and feeling trapped, clinician responds with dismissal rather than exploration. No complex reflections capturing emotional nuance. Patient explicitly states "You don't get my situation" multiple times—clear indicator that empathy is not being communicated. Clinician misses critical moment when patient reveals fear-based messaging increases stress rather than motivation, and responds by intensifying fear-based pressure.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician maintains narrow focus on smoking cessation and nicotine replacement therapy, preventing complete aimlessness. However, direction is pursued through rigid repetition without strategic adaptation or responsiveness to client feedback. Same directives repeated verbatim multiple times without variation. No exploration of patient's own values, vision for health, or what meaningful change might look like to him. Direction is entirely imposed rather than collaboratively developed. Fails to address stated barrier of overwhelm, which should inform how change goal is framed and sequenced.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Near-complete absence of collaboration. Opening statement explicitly positions clinician as expert who will dictate solutions: "I'm here to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better." Never solicits patient's input on problem-solving. When patient attempts to contribute perspective, clinician dismisses rather than incorporates: "No, you're just making excuses." Unilateral pronouncements throughout ("You need to," "You have to," "This isn't optional"). No genuine power-sharing or co-construction of solutions. Patient's autonomy and self-knowledge explicitly rejected.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy support completely absent and actively undermined. Controlling, prescriptive language throughout: "You need to quit now—no more delays," "This isn't optional," "You have to start immediately." Explicit dismissal of patient's stated readiness: "There's no 'maybe someday' with this." Patient clearly expresses "I need to figure this out on my own terms"—clinician responds by pressuring harder. Use of shame and fear-based tactics ("If you really cared about your health," "Do you want to end up in the hospital?") designed to coerce compliance rather than support autonomy. No affirmation of patient's right to make own choices or respect for his timeline.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Evocation entirely absent. Clinician asks only closed, leading questions designed to elicit compliance ("Are you going to commit to quitting today or not?"—repeated 4+ times), not genuine exploratory questions. No open-ended questions to draw out patient's own motivations, values, or resources. Patient provides change talk and resources that could be evoked (effort toward education, implicit motivation to avoid deterioration, acknowledgment of trying), but clinician dismisses all of it: "No, you're just making excuses." Patient reveals important barriers (patches felt like "more hassle than relief," needs to figure things out on own terms) that could be explored to identify what might work—clinician never explores these.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership—the fundamental relational stance of MI—entirely absent. Opening statement declares unilateral expertise and authority rather than inviting partnership. When patient asserts own expertise about his situation ("You don't get my situation," "I need to figure this out on my own terms"), clinician dismisses this rather than honoring it. Hierarchical, power-imbalanced language throughout ("You need to," "You have to," "You must"). No inclusive language ("we," "together," "let's explore"). Clinician never shares power or positions patient as having valuable knowledge. When patient expresses frustration about being yelled at about hospitals, clinician responds by yelling about hospitals again—demonstrating complete failure to recognize or adjust harmful power dynamic.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Change talk neither evoked nor cultivated; actively suppressed and dismissed. Patient offers multiple instances of change talk: "All I've been doing is reading them" (effort/ability), "I'm trying here in my own way" (commitment/effort), "Maybe when things calm down a bit" (preparatory change talk). All met with dismissal: "No, you're just making excuses," "There's no 'maybe someday' with this." Clinician never asks questions designed to deepen change language. No follow-up exploration of patient's learning, effort, or readiness. Limited change talk is contradicted rather than amplified, which suppresses motivation rather than cultivating it.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk actively reinforced rather than skillfully softened. When patient expresses overwhelm, clinician validates it exists then pressures to ignore it: "I don't want to hear about how overwhelmed you are. You have to push through that." When patient says "I'm not ready to commit today," clinician directly confronts with pressure: "There's no 'maybe someday' with this." When patient expresses fear-based stress, clinician amplifies it: "Do you want to end up in the hospital?" No double-sided reflections acknowledging both sides of ambivalence. No exploration of what's driving resistance. No curiosity about barriers. Predictable result: patient's resistance strengthens throughout session, moving from ambivalent ("Maybe someday") to firmly resistant ("No, I'm not committing").

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Non-MI Approach

**Session Effectiveness:** Actively counterproductive. Patient ends session more firmly entrenched in resistance with damaged therapeutic relationship and decreased intrinsic motivation for change.

**Critical Findings:**
- Clinician operates entirely from expert-driven, authoritarian stance
- Pervasive judgment and dismissal of patient's emotional experience
- Active suppression of change talk and amplification of sustain talk
- Pressure-based approach predictably increases defensiveness
- Therapeutic relationship rupture evident and unaddressed
- Session ends with verbatim repetition of opening question, suggesting either reset or failure to recognize ineffective pattern

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Seek formal MI training through MINT (Motivational Interviewing Network of Trainers)
2. Obtain clinical supervision with MI-trained supervisor
3. Record and review sessions with focus on collaborative stance and reflective listening
4. Discontinue current approach pending competency development
5. Recognize that resistance is information about clinician approach, not client willingness

**Research-Based Outcome Prediction:** This confrontational, expert-driven approach is associated with worse patient outcomes than collaborative, evocative approaches. Patient is unlikely to follow through on recommendations and may avoid future healthcare engagement.
