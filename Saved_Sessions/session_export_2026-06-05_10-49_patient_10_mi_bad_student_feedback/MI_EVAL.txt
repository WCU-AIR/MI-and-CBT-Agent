### **Overall Summary**

#### **Average MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Session Outcome Assessment:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure to practice Motivational Interviewing**. The psychiatrist operates from a traditional medical model of expert authority and patient compliance, directly contradictory to MI's core principles of partnership, collaboration, autonomy support, and evocation.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- **Authoritarian stance:** Explicitly positions self as sole expert; patient as non-compliant subject to be corrected
- **Absence of empathy and acceptance:** No genuine effort to understand patient's perspective; repeated dismissal of concerns as "excuses"
- **Pressure and coercion:** Uses controlling language, threats, and guilt-induction to pressure compliance
- **Failure to evoke:** Imposes solutions rather than drawing out patient's own motivations and resources
- **Ignores context:** Never explores patient's stated presenting concern (co-parenting conflict); guarantees plan failure
- **Confronts ambivalence:** Aggressively confronts legitimate ambivalence rather than skillfully working with it

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to **decrease** intrinsic motivation and **increase** resistance. Patient enters with stated motivation ("I want to be strong for my son") and genuine engagement; she exits defending her position and explicitly refusing commitment. Psychiatrist's approach violates core behavior change principles: intrinsic motivation is more sustainable than external pressure; autonomy support increases commitment; collaborative planning increases adherence; shame-based messaging decreases engagement.

**Recommendation:**

Psychiatrist requires formal MI training, supervised practice, and ongoing consultation before continuing to see this patient using this approach. Current approach is harmful to patient outcomes and violates collaborative, client-centered spirit of MI.
