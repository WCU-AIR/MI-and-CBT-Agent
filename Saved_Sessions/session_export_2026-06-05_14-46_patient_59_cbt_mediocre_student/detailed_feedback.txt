
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. Establish Proper CBT Session Structure (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
- **Action:** Begin every session with a collaborative agenda. Explicitly identify 2-3 target problems, prioritize them with the patient, and allocate time. End every session with a summary and homework assignment.
- **Example:** "Robert, I want to make sure we use our time well today. Last week we talked about your anxiety and some strategies to manage it. Today, I'd like to focus on three things: (1) reviewing how the walks and thought monitoring went, (2) developing a relapse prevention plan, and (3) setting up a more structured thought record. Does that sound good? We have 45 minutes, so we'll spend about 15 minutes on each. Does that work for you?"
- **Practice:** Before each session, write out a 3-item agenda with time allocation. Review it with the patient at the start. At the end, summarize what was covered and what homework is assigned. This should become automatic.

### 2. Implement Mandatory Homework Review (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
- **Action:** Begin every session (after Session 1) by reviewing the previous week's homework. Ask: "What did you complete?" "What worked?" "What got in the way?" "What did you learn?" Adjust the strategy based on results.
- **Example:** "Robert, last week I asked you to try some walks and to notice your thoughts. Let's start by reviewing that. Did you get out for any walks? How many? What did you notice about your anxiety before and after? Did the thought monitoring help? What got in the way?"
- **Practice:** Create a homework review checklist: (1) Ask what was completed, (2) Ask what worked/didn't work, (3) Ask about barriers, (4) Adjust strategy based on results, (5) Reinforce completion. Use this checklist at the start of every session.

### 3. Develop a Coherent Case Formulation and Treatment Strategy (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
- **Action:** Explicitly articulate a formulation of Robert's anxiety that accounts for medical factors, psychological factors, and relapse vulnerability. Use this formulation to guide all interventions.
- **Example:** "Robert, I want to make sure I understand what's going on. It sounds like your anxiety has several causes: (1) The steroids you're taking can cause anxiety and irritability—this is a medication effect, not a personal failing. (2) The uncertainty about the transplant and your health is genuinely stressful. (3) You're in early recovery from alcohol, and that takes a lot of mental energy. (4) You've lost your job as a delivery driver, which was part of your identity. All of these together are making you feel on edge. Does that match your experience? I think our plan should focus on: first, understanding the steroid effects and talking to your doctor about that; second, developing a relapse prevention plan so you feel more secure in your recovery; and third, managing the transplant-related anxiety. Does that make sense?"
- **Practice:** For each patient, write out a one-paragraph formulation that includes: (1) Biological factors, (2) Psychological factors, (3) Social/contextual factors, (4) Maintaining factors, (5) Treatment priorities. Share this formulation with the patient and refine it based on feedback.

### 4. Create Structured, Specific Homework Assignments with Written Instructions (HIGH PRIORITY)
- **Action:** Replace vague homework suggestions with specific, structured assignments. Provide written instructions, establish baseline/measurement, and check for understanding.
- **Example:** Instead of "Try to do a little distraction activity," assign: "Behavioral Activation Log: This week, schedule 3 specific activities (walks, puzzles, music, etc.). For each activity, record: (1) Activity name, (2) Day/time, (3) Duration, (4) Anxiety before (0-10 scale), (5) Anxiety after (0-10 scale). Bring the log to our next session so we can review what helped."
- **Practice:** Create homework templates for common assignments: (1) Thought Record form, (2) Behavioral Activation Log, (3) Relapse Prevention Worksheet, (4) Worry Monitoring form. Use these templates consistently. Always provide written instructions. Always establish baseline and measurement. Always check: "Does this make sense? What might get in the way? How will you remember to do this?"

### 5. Implement Systematic Cognitive Restructuring, Not Thought Replacement (HIGH PRIORITY)
- **Action:** Move beyond suggesting replacement thoughts. Use structured thought records that include: (1) Situation, (2) Automatic thought, (3) Emotion/physical sensation, (4) Evidence for the thought, (5) Evidence against the thought, (6) Alternative thought, (7) Outcome/belief in alternative.
- **Example:** Instead of "You could tell yourself 'I'm doing my best,'" use Socratic questioning: "When you think 'What if the transplant doesn't happen in time?', what evidence do you have that this will happen? What evidence do you have against it? What would someone who cares about you say about this thought? What's a more balanced way to think about it?" Then complete a thought record together.
- **Practice:** For each catastrophic thought Robert identifies, complete a full thought record. Do not skip steps. Help Robert examine evidence systematically. Help him generate alternatives through questioning, not suggestion. Have him rate belief in the alternative thought (0-100%). Track changes in belief over time.
