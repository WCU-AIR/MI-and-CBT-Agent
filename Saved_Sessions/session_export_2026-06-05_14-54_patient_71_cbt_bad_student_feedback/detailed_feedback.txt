
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. **URGENT: Implement Systematic CBT Technique Application**
- **Action:** In the next session, conduct a formal cognitive restructuring exercise using a thought record. Do not simply explain the technique; actually practice it with the patient in session.
- **Example:** 
  - Therapist: "You mentioned the thought 'I'm not doing enough.' Let's write this down and examine it together. First, when did you have this thought? What was happening?"
  - [Patient describes situation]
  - Therapist: "Okay, so in that situation, you thought 'I'm not doing enough.' What evidence do you have that this thought is true? What did you actually do that day?"
  - [Patient lists activities]
  - Therapist: "I see. So you did [specific activities]. How does that fit with 'I'm not doing enough'? What would 'doing enough' look like?"
  - [Continue examining evidence, developing alternative thought]
- **Practice:** Review the Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale (CTRS) section on "Application of Cognitive-Behavioral Techniques" and practice conducting thought records with colleagues or in supervision. Record yourself and review for skill development.

### 2. **URGENT: Eliminate Excessive Self-Disclosure**
- **Action:** In future sessions, minimize personal anecdotes. If you share anything, make it brief (one sentence), clearly in service of the patient's needs, and immediately redirect focus to the patient.
- **Example (Problematic):** "When I was feeling down last year, I just reminded myself that things will get better."
- **Example (Improved):** "Many people find it helpful to track what they're doing, even on difficult days. Have you noticed anything you're doing despite the fatigue?"
- **Practice:** Before each session, review your notes and identify any planned self-disclosures. Ask yourself: "Is this about me or about the patient? Does this serve the patient's treatment goals?" If the answer is unclear, remove it.

### 3. **URGENT: Establish Formal Agenda-Setting in Every Session**
- **Action:** Begin each session with explicit agenda-setting. Ask the patient what she wants to work on, identify 2-3 priorities, allocate time, and check in at the end about whether you covered what was important.
- **Example:**
  - Therapist: "Today we have about 50 minutes. Last time we talked about your thought 'I'm not doing enough' and how it affects your mood. I'd like to continue exploring that. Is that still what you want to focus on? Are there other things you want to discuss today?"
  - [Patient responds]
  - Therapist: "Okay, so we'll spend about 30 minutes on [Topic 1] and 15 minutes on [Topic 2]. Does that work for you?"
  - [At end of session] "We talked about [topics]. Did we cover what was most important to you today?"
- **Practice:** Use the CTRS "Agenda" section as a checklist. Every session should include: (1) collaborative identification of topics, (2) prioritization, (3) time allocation, (4) adherence to agenda, (5) end-of-session review.

### 4. **URGENT: Assign Formal Homework with Clear Rationale and Follow-Up**
- **Action:** At the end of each session, assign 1-2 specific, written homework tasks. Explain the rationale, demonstrate if possible, ensure understanding, and plan to review next session.
- **Example:**
  - Therapist: "This week, I'd like you to keep a thought record. Here's how it works: [show form]. When you notice the thought 'I'm not doing enough,' write down what happened, what you thought, how you felt, and what evidence you have for and against the thought. This will help us understand your thoughts better. Can you do this? Are there any barriers?"
  - [Patient agrees]
  - Therapist: "Great. Let's do one example together right now. [Complete one example]. Next session, we'll review what you wrote and see what you learned."
- **Practice:** Review the CTRS "Homework" section. Every session should end with: (1) review of prior homework, (2) assignment of new homework, (3) clear rationale, (4) demonstration, (5) commitment check, (6) plan for follow-up.

### 5. **HIGH PRIORITY: Implement Systematic Feedback Elicitation**
- **Action:** Throughout each session, regularly ask for the patient's reactions, understanding, and satisfaction. Do not assume; ask explicitly.
- **Example:**
  - After explaining CBT model: "Does this make sense? Does this fit with your experience?"
  - After suggesting an intervention: "What do you think about this approach? Does it feel doable?"
  - Mid-session: "How are you feeling about our conversation so far? Is this helpful?"
  - End of session: "What was most useful about today's session? Is there anything you wish we'd talked about?"
- **Practice:** Set a goal to ask for feedback at least 3 times per session. Use the CTRS "Feedback" section as a guide. Record yourself and count how many times you elicit feedback.
