## **OVERALL SESSION SCORE: 1.7/5**

---

### **SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION**

#### **Strengths:**
- Warm, welcoming opening that explicitly invites Marco to discuss health and lifestyle changes
- Use of at least one open question that allows Marco to articulate core motivation (being present for children)
- Strategic use of scaling questions (importance and confidence) to assess readiness
- Identification of Marco's powerful intrinsic motivation rooted in family values
- Concrete planning with specific goal (snack swapping) and follow-up structure
- Acknowledgment that Marco's situation is challenging

#### **Critical Deficiencies:**

**1. Expert-Driven, Directive Stance (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
Psychiatrist consistently tells Marco what he "should" do rather than collaboratively exploring options. This undermines autonomy, partnership, and evocation. Session reflects traditional medical model (doctor prescribes, patient follows) rather than MI's collaborative, client-centered approach.

**2. Failure to Develop Change Talk**
Marco articulates profound intrinsic motivation (wanting to be present and healthy for his children), but psychiatrist does not deepen or reinforce this. Represents significant missed opportunity to strengthen most important driver of change.

**3. Minimal Empathic Exploration**
Psychiatrist uses basic reflections but does not explore Marco's emotional experience, ambivalence, or deeper meaning of statements. When Marco expresses worry or concern, these are dismissed or minimized rather than explored with genuine curiosity.

**4. Dismissal of Marco's Autonomy and Concerns**
When Marco expresses hesitation about family involvement, psychiatrist recommends family involvement anyway. This fails to respect Marco's autonomy and his knowledge of his own family dynamics.

**5. Inadequate Handling of Sustain Talk**
Rather than skillfully softening Marco's concerns about consistency, fatigue, and temptation, psychiatrist offers reassurance and advice. Does not address underlying ambivalence or evoke Marco's own problem-solving.

**6. Premature Problem-Solving**
Psychiatrist moves quickly to solutions without fully exploring Marco's barriers, resources, and preferences. Reflects problem-focused rather than client-centered approach.

**7. Lack of Affirmation**
Psychiatrist does not explicitly affirm Marco's strengths (motivation for children, self-awareness, willingness to try, insight about barriers). Affirmation is core MI technique for building self-efficacy and alliance.

---

### **IMPACT ON CLIENT ENGAGEMENT AND CHANGE LIKELIHOOD**

This session is likely to **undermine Marco's intrinsic motivation** and increase risk of non-adherence. By positioning psychiatrist as expert with solutions and Marco as passive recipient, session reduces Marco's sense of ownership and agency. Marco's stated concern about family involvement is overridden, which may increase resistance. Most importantly, Marco's powerful intrinsic motivation is not developed or reinforced, leaving him with generic plan lacking deep emotional resonance.

Marco's ambivalence is not explored or normalized; instead treated as something to overcome through reassurance and advice. This may increase defensiveness or activate reactance (natural resistance to being told what to do), which could undermine follow-through. While Marco agrees to try snack swapping, his commitment appears tentative ("I'm willing to give it a try") rather than enthusiastic. Without deeper exploration of motivation, affirmation of strengths, and genuine collaboration, Marco's likelihood of sustained change is **low**.

---

### **KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT**

**1. Shift from Expert-Driven to Collaborative Stance (CRITICAL)**
Replace directive language ("You should," "Maybe you should") with genuine curiosity and partnership language ("What do you think?" "How do you see this?" "What feels doable to you?"). Before offering any suggestion, ask an open question first.

**2. Deepen and Reinforce Change Talk (CRITICAL)**
When Marco articulates change talk, especially his powerful motivation about being present for his children, pause and explore it further. Use open questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation: "Tell me more about that," "What does that look like to you?" "How would your kids notice the difference?"

**3. Develop Genuine Empathic Exploration of Ambivalence**
When Marco expresses concern or doubt, respond with curiosity and double-sided reflections rather than reassurance or advice. Explore ambivalence as legitimate psychological state: "You really want to make these changes for your kids, and at the same time, you're worried that when you're tired and tempted, you might fall back into old patterns."

**4. Respect and Explore Marco's Stated Concerns About Family Dynamics**
Treat hesitation about family involvement as important information about his values and relationships, not as obstacle to overcome. Explore collaboratively: "Help me understand—what do you think would happen if you talked to them? What would feel comfortable to you?"

**5. Use Affirmation Strategically to Build Self-Efficacy**
Explicitly recognize Marco's strengths, self-awareness, motivation, and willingness to try. Connect affirmations to change: "That kind of self-awareness is really important and could help you succeed with this."

**6. Ask Open Questions Before Offering Any Suggestions**
Make it a rule: always ask an open question first to explore client's own ideas before offering suggestions. This honors autonomy, evokes resources, and increases buy-in.

**7. Use Scaling Questions to Explore Ambivalence, Not Prescribe Solutions**
Follow up scaling questions with open exploration: "What would it take to move from a 6 to a 7?" "What would help you feel more confident?" Listen to client's own ideas about what would help.

**8. Develop Skill in Softening Sustain Talk Without Confrontation**
Use double-sided reflections and strategic redirection: "On one hand... on the other hand..." Avoid arguing against sustain talk; instead acknowledge it and redirect: "I hear that concern. And I'm wondering..."

**9. Explicitly Invite Marco's Input into the Plan**
Co-construct plan with Marco rather than presenting it. Ask what feels doable, what he wants to prioritize, and what would be most helpful: "What feels like the most important thing to focus on first?"

**10. Pursue Formal MI Training**
Attend 2-3 day MI training workshop, seek supervision from MI-trained clinician, record and review sessions to identify directive moments and missed opportunities for evocation. This session reflects gaps requiring structured learning in MI principles and techniques.
