# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist uses judgmental language ("you're just making excuses" repeated 4+ times), dismisses patient perspective, and explicitly rejects autonomy ("You don't get to decide how serious this is"). No affirmation of patient's significant progress in reducing smoking. Tone is corrective and disapproving throughout, communicating blame rather than respect for patient's worth and autonomy.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero reflective listening statements. Psychiatrist talks *at* patient rather than *with* him. No genuine effort to understand patient's perspective, values, or readiness concerns. When patient expresses "I'm not ready," psychiatrist does not explore this. Single instance of "I hear you" is immediately negated with "but you have to understand," representing pseudo-empathy. Patient explicitly states: "it feels like you're not really hearing me."

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear focus on smoking cessation maintained throughout, preventing session drift. However, direction is imposed rather than collaborative. Psychiatrist prescribes specific interventions (nicotine patches, throwing away cigarettes) without exploring patient's preferences or readiness. Rigid, controlling approach actively increases resistance; patient ends session with explicit refusals to all suggestions, moving further from stated goal rather than toward it.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely absent. Psychiatrist explicitly positions himself as sole expert: "I'm the expert here, and I know what's best for your health." Makes unilateral decisions throughout with no joint problem-solving. Never asks "What do you think might help?" or seeks patient input. Dismisses patient's perspective as obstacles to overcome. Patient directly requests collaboration: "If you want to help, maybe try understanding where I'm coming from instead of pushing me"—request is ignored.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematically undermined through controlling language ("You have to," "You must," "You need to" appears 15+ times). Psychiatrist explicitly denies patient's right to choose: "You don't get to decide that." Responds to patient's declining interventions with pressure ("Why won't you take this seriously?") rather than respect. Patient's final statement—"I want to feel ready and do it in my own way"—represents autonomy needs that psychiatrist fails to honor throughout.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely absent. No open-ended questions designed to elicit patient's motivations, values, or reasons for change. Psychiatrist *tells* patient why to quit rather than drawing out patient's own perspective. Patient's initial change talk ("I pretty much stopped already," "I don't smoke every day now") is dismissed rather than explored and amplified. No questions about patient's values, health concerns, or what quitting might mean. Session produces only sustain talk and resistance; no genuine change talk developed.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Completely absent. Psychiatrist explicitly assumes expert authority and rejects shared power: "I'm the expert here, and I know what's best." Language is consistently hierarchical ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you should do," "You have to," "You must"). No inclusive language ("we," "together," "let's explore"). No invitation for patient to shape session direction. Psychiatrist positions himself as sole decision-maker; patient as passive recipient of directives.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Change talk is actively suppressed rather than cultivated. Patient's initial progress statements ("I pretty much stopped already") are dismissed with "but you really need to quit completely." No follow-up questions to deepen or amplify change talk. Psychiatrist's confrontational approach elicits and reinforces *sustain talk* instead. By session end, patient produces zero change talk and multiple explicit refusals: "So no, I won't put on the patch today," "So no, I'm not really willing," "So no, I'm not going to throw them all away today."

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk is actively amplified and reinforced rather than softened. Psychiatrist uses confrontational language that strengthens resistance: "You're just making excuses," "you're only hurting yourself," "Why won't you take this seriously?" These responses invite defensiveness and articulation of more reasons against change. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic redirection attempted. Patient's sustain talk escalates throughout session, culminating in explicit refusals to change.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Competency Level:** Pre-MI. This session demonstrates the *absence* of motivational interviewing rather than MI performed poorly. The psychiatrist employs directive, expert-driven, coercive counseling that violates core MI principles at every level.

**Key Findings:**
- **Therapeutic Relationship:** Damaged. Patient feels unheard, blamed, and controlled.
- **Patient Resistance:** Increased. Patient entered with low concern (precontemplation) and exits with explicit refusals and resentment.
- **Change Likelihood:** Reduced. Psychiatrist's approach has foreclosed possibility of near-term engagement on smoking cessation.
- **MI Principle Violations:** Systematic across all nine domains evaluated.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- Zero empathic attunement or reflective listening
- Authoritarian expert positioning with explicit denial of patient autonomy
- Systematic suppression of change talk and amplification of sustain talk
- Controlling language and blame throughout
- No collaborative problem-solving or joint decision-making

**Patient Impact Statement:**
The patient explicitly communicated the harm: "it feels like you're not really hearing me," "this kind of push just makes me dig in my heels," and "I want to feel ready and do it in my own way." These statements indicate the psychiatrist's approach is counterproductive and damaging.

**Recommendation:**
This psychiatrist requires immediate formal MI training (MINT-certified program) and ongoing clinical supervision before continuing behavioral health counseling. Current practice is harmful to patient outcomes and violates MI principles fundamentally.
