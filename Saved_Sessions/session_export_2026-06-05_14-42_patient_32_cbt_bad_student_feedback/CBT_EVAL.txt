## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR CLINICAL IMPROVEMENT

1. **Understand Delusional Disorder:** Obtain training in phenomenology and treatment of psychotic symptoms. Recognize delusions are fixed false beliefs requiring reality-testing and medication, not equivalent to anxiety.

2. **Establish Clear Agenda and Session Structure:** Collaboratively set agenda at session start with explicit priorities, target problems, and time allocation.

3. **Implement Systematic Feedback Elicitation:** Ask for feedback throughout session, not just at end. Check understanding after each intervention.

4. **Use Guided Discovery Instead of Advice-Giving:** Replace directive suggestions with Socratic questions to help patient reach own insights.

5. **Focus on Core Cognitions and Behaviors:** Identify and systematically explore central delusional beliefs and associated avoidance behaviors.

6. **Develop Coherent Treatment Strategy:** Before each session, establish clear conceptualization, goals, interventions, sequencing, and rationale.

7. **Apply CBT Techniques Appropriately:** Use evidence-based techniques appropriate for delusional disorder (reality-testing, behavioral experiments, psychoeducation), not generic stress-management.

8. **Assign Formal, Specific Homework:** Provide written instructions with clear rationale, commitment check, and systematic review at next session.

9. **Reduce Self-Disclosure:** Minimize personal sharing; maintain focus on patient's experience.

10. **Recognize When Psychiatric Intervention Required:** Understand delusional disorder typically requires antipsychotic medication; make referral promptly with clear explanation.

11. **Develop Psychosis-Specific Competence:** Obtain specialized training in cognitive therapy for psychosis (CTP) including reality-testing, belief modification, and coping strategies.

12. **Improve Collaboration:** Position patient as active partner; solicit input on problems, goals, and interventions; make decisions collaboratively.
