## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Strengths:**
- Maintained non-hostile, non-demeaning tone
- Did not overtly criticize or demean patient
- Made some attempt to engage patient in conversation
- Asked some questions about patient's experience

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental failures in acute psychiatric assessment:** No systematic assessment of manic symptoms, safety assessment, substance use inquiry, medication review, or functional impairment evaluation
- **Complete absence of CBT framework:** No agenda-setting, problem formulation, guided discovery, cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, or coherent treatment strategy
- **Inappropriate therapeutic approach:** Excessive self-disclosure, validation of grandiose ideation without reality-testing, generic wellness advice disconnected from clinical formulation, cheerleading that reinforced problematic beliefs
- **Missed opportunities for intervention:** No exploration of discrepancy between patient's self-perception and observable reality, no gentle challenging of grandiose beliefs, no discussion of medication, no safety planning

**Recommendations:**
1. Conduct systematic psychiatric assessment in future sessions using structured protocols and standardized rating scales
2. Establish clear agenda and session structure collaboratively with patient
3. Eliminate excessive self-disclosure; maintain professional boundaries
4. Use guided discovery and Socratic questioning to address poor insight
5. Develop coherent CBT formulation and treatment strategy before each session
6. Apply specific CBT techniques systematically (thought records, behavioral experiments, sleep tracking, activity scheduling)
7. Address medication management explicitly as core component of treatment
8. Conduct explicit safety assessment and planning
9. Assign specific, structured homework with clear rationale and monitoring mechanisms
10. Elicit and respond to patient feedback throughout session
11. Recognize and gently challenge grandiose ideation and poor insight without direct confrontation
12. Pursue specialized training in CBT for bipolar disorder with intensive supervision

**Overall Assessment:** Psychiatrist requires immediate, intensive training and supervision before continuing to treat patients with bipolar disorder. Current session quality is insufficient for evidence-based psychiatric care.
