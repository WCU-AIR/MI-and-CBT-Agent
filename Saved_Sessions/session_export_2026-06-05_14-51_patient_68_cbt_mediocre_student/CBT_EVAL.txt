## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Quality Assessment:**

This session represents **inadequate, non-evidence-based treatment** that fails to meet standards for CBT for opioid use disorder. While the therapist demonstrates basic interpersonal warmth and non-judgment, critical clinical competencies are absent.

**Most Concerning Gaps:**

1. **No Medical-Psychological Integration**: The therapist does not discuss, coordinate, or initiate medication-assisted treatment (Suboxone), which is the gold standard for severe opioid use disorder. This is a fundamental clinical error.

2. **No Coherent Treatment Strategy**: The therapist has no clear plan for treating this patient. Interventions appear ad hoc rather than part of an integrated, evidence-based approach.

3. **Inadequate Assessment of Risk**: The patient expresses profound hopelessness ("what's the point?") but no suicide risk assessment occurs. No safety planning. This is a critical omission.

4. **Poor Homework Design and No Follow-Up**: Homework is vague and not reviewed in Session 2. This undermines treatment effectiveness.

5. **Superficial CBT Application**: Cognitive and behavioral techniques are applied with significant flaws and lack the depth, precision, and integration necessary for effective treatment.

**Immediate Supervisor Actions:**

- Schedule intensive supervision with this therapist focusing on addiction-specific CBT
- Require training in medication-assisted treatment and addiction medicine
- Require training in suicide risk assessment and safety planning
- Require training in functional analysis and relapse prevention
- Require training in proper cognitive restructuring and behavioral activation
- Recommend referral of patient to addiction medicine specialist immediately
- Consider whether this therapist is appropriate for treating opioid use disorder without significant additional training

**Patient Safety Concern:**

This patient is at high risk for relapse, overdose, and suicide. Current treatment is inadequate and potentially harmful. Immediate intervention required.
