## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- **Strong collaborative agenda-setting** with explicit invitation for patient input on priorities
- **Excellent empathic listening** with accurate reflection of emotional experience, physical sensations, and defensive patterns
- **Skillful guided discovery** using Socratic questioning to help patient examine evidence and generate own balanced thought
- **Warm, genuine interpersonal presence** creating safe, non-judgmental environment
- **Coherent cognitive restructuring** with appropriate pacing and depth
- **Specific, achievable behavioral planning** with attention to confidence level and barriers

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Failure to assess or address core sexual dysfunction** (Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder diagnosis not addressed)
- **No comprehensive trauma assessment** despite case summary indicating possible trauma history
- **No assessment of alcohol use** in sexual contexts despite patient's disclosure of using alcohol to tolerate intercourse
- **No identification or targeting of avoidant coping pattern** (avoidance of intimacy, disclosure, discussing sexual history)
- **Limited range of CBT techniques** (only cognitive restructuring and behavioral planning; no thought records, experiments, exposure, or functional analysis)
- **No articulated treatment conceptualization or strategy** for sexual dysfunction
- **Homework does not address core clinical issues** (focuses on communication, not sexual dysfunction)
- **Inadequate time allocation** to comprehensive assessment; 60-70% of session on single relationship conflict

### Clinical Impact:
This session demonstrates competent foundational CBT skills applied to a peripheral presenting complaint (relationship arguments) while failing to address the primary diagnosis (Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder). The therapist created good alliance and helped patient develop communication skills, but the likelihood of meaningful therapeutic change for the sexual dysfunction is low. The session represents a missed opportunity for early intervention on a time-sensitive issue (patient is 38 and wants child before age 40). Substantial revision of treatment approach is needed in next session to focus on core sexual dysfunction, trauma assessment, and avoidance reduction.

### Immediate Action Items for Supervisor Review:
1. Conduct comprehensive sexual dysfunction assessment in next session
2. Assess trauma history and sexual abuse with trauma-informed approach
3. Assess and address alcohol use in sexual contexts
4. Develop clear treatment conceptualization integrating all relevant factors
5. Identify and target avoidant coping pattern as central treatment goal
6. Revise homework to address sexual dysfunction, not just communication
7. Establish clear treatment plan with timeline and expected outcomes
