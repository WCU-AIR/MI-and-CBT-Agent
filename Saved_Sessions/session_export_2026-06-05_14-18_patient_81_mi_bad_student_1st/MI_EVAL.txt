### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1

Uses power-over language throughout: "you have to," "you must," "you don't get to negotiate," "No, there's no working with that." These statements assert clinician authority and minimize client expertise about own life, values, and readiness. True partnership would recognize client has expertise about own situation—capacity, barriers, priorities, readiness. Client demonstrates this when stating: "I've made some changes already by cutting down on drinking, and that took time and effort. I need to build on that step by step." ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Neither evoked nor cultivated; actively suppressed. Client offers change talk early on ("I do want to be healthier and do better," "I do want to feel better," "I want to get better, really"). Skilled practitioner would amplify with reflections; instead psychiatrist responds with pressure and dismissal: "but you really should," "you need to," "you're just making excuses." ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Neither softened nor skillfully managed; intensified. Client's sustain talk includes: "feels like another thing to deal with," "feels a bit overwhelming," "I'm just not sure I'm ready," "adding more pressure feels like too much," "jumping all in feels like it could backfire." Skilled practitioner would use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence while gently shifting toward change. Instead psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk with blame and pressure: "You're just making excuses," "you can't keep putting this off," "you're not listening," "stop being so hesitant." This approach typically strengthens sustain talk by triggering psychological reactance—when people feel pressured, they defend position more strongly. Client's sustain talk intensifies as session progresses. By end, client states: "being told 'no more excuses' and having to say yes right this minute just makes me feel like you're not hearing where I'm at" and "it just makes me want to resist more." Psychiatrist has inadvertently amplified resistance. Skillful approach would involve reframing concerns as understandable, exploring discrepancy between values and behavior, collaboratively identifying small steps. Instead psychiatrist creates more reasons for client to resist.

- Confrontational, blame-based communication triggering defensiveness
- Failure to evoke or amplify intrinsic motivation

**Clinical Recommendation:**
