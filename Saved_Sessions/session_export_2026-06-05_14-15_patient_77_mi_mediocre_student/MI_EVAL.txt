## Overall Session Summary

### **Overall MI Competency Score: 1.4/5**

---

### Strengths
- **Non-confrontational tone:** Clinician avoids arguing with or directly challenging client's sustain talk, preventing defensiveness.
- **Warm opening:** Initial greeting is genuine and welcoming.
- **Appropriate target behavior identification:** Clinician eventually identifies self-harm as focus, which is clinically appropriate.
- **Acknowledgment of difficulty:** Statement "I understand it feels overwhelming" shows some recognition of client's struggle.
- **Attempted autonomy support:** Closing statement attempts to reinforce client choice, though undermined by session tone.

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**1. Fundamental Departure from MI Spirit (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
This session is not motivational interviewing—it is directive, expert-driven supportive counseling. Clinician prescribes solutions rather than evoking motivation. Shift from expert stance to collaborative partnership. Replace "should" and "probably" language with open-ended questions that invite client thinking.

**2. Absence of Genuine Evocation**
Clinician never draws out client's own reasons for change, values, or resources. Instead, tells her why she should change. Prepare open-ended evocation questions before sessions. Ask about client's intrinsic motivation rather than imposing reasons for change.

**3. Minimal Empathic Depth**
Reflections are factual and surface-level. Critical clinical details (somatic pain, hopelessness, ambivalence) never explored. Spend 10-15 minutes in next session exploring function of self-harm without suggesting alternatives. Simply understand and reflect.

**4. Sustain Talk Unaddressed**
Client's hopelessness and belief that "nothing will help" are acknowledged but not skillfully softened. Use double-sided reflections to name ambivalence. Explore sustain talk collaboratively rather than overriding with suggestions.

**5. Prescriptive Goal-Setting**
Goals suggested by clinician, not co-created with client. Ask client what she thinks might be manageable. Let her lead; offer options and let her choose rather than prescribing.

**6. Exploration of Somatic Experience**
Client mentioned "pain in my body that I can't explain"—critical clinical information suggesting dissociation or trauma. Ask client to describe embodied experience in detail. Reflect back what you hear.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**Likelihood of sustained behavior change: LOW**

Client's minimal engagement ("Mm-hmm," "Yeah," "I guess") and persistent hopelessness suggest compliance rather than commitment. While she agrees to try small steps, this is likely compliance to satisfy clinician, not genuine motivation. Prescriptive approach may reinforce her sense of helplessness and belief that "nothing will help." Without genuine evocation of intrinsic motivation, client will likely try suggested strategies, find them insufficient (as predicted), return to cutting, and conclude "nothing works"—further entrenching hopelessness and potentially damaging trust in professional support.

---

### Recommendations for Next Session

1. **Reframe your role:** Ask questions to understand client's perspective rather than telling her what to do.
2. **Evoke motivation:** Use open-ended questions about values, reasons for change, and resources.
3. **Develop empathic understanding:** Explore what cutting does for her without immediately suggesting alternatives.
4. **Name ambivalence:** Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge conflicting feelings.
5. **Soften sustain talk:** Explore hopelessness collaboratively rather than prescribing solutions.
6. **Explore somatic pain:** Ask about embodied experience and what it means.
7. **Validate difficulty:** Acknowledge how hard stopping is and normalize the struggle.
8. **Co-create goals:** Ask client what she thinks might help; let her lead.
9. **Amplify change talk:** When client expresses willingness, explore and reinforce it.
10. **Trust client wisdom:** Believe in her capacity to find her own path forward.

---

### Critical Reflection

**This session is not motivational interviewing.** To practice true MI, you must fundamentally shift from telling Liora what to do to asking questions that help her discover her own reasons for change. This requires genuine curiosity about her experience, empathic understanding of self-harm's function, and trust in her capacity to find her own path. The current approach—while well-intentioned—is unlikely to evoke intrinsic motivation necessary for sustained change and may inadvertently deepen her sense of hopelessness and alienation from professional support.
