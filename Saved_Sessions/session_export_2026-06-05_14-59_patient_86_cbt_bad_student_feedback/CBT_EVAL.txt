## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Session Classification:** NOT evidence-based CBT. Not evidence-based treatment for any psychiatric condition.

**Patient Presentation:** First-episode psychosis with acute symptoms including:
- Paranoid delusions ("people are watching me," "people are out to get me")
- Grandiose/identity delusions ("I am the Joker")
- Erotomania (preoccupation with Anne)
- Identity disturbance ("I don't feel like me," "other part trying to take over")
- Disorganized behavior (roof-standing, gesture-conducting, classroom outburst)
- Negative symptoms (social withdrawal, anhedonia, avolition)
- Possible hallucinations ("strange thoughts or voices")

**Therapist's Response:** Offered positive thinking, hydration, fresh air, music, and personal anecdotes—none of which are evidence-based for schizophrenia.

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. **URGENT:** Refer for psychiatric evaluation and medication assessment
2. Conduct structured safety assessment (suicidality, homicidality, command hallucinations)
3. Clarify hospitalization status and treatment plan
4. Eliminate inappropriate self-disclosure and false reassurance
5. Seek specialized training in psychosis assessment and CBT for psychosis
6. Implement proper follow-up and safety planning

**Prognosis Impact:** Early intervention with antipsychotic medication improves outcomes in first-episode psychosis. This session, by suggesting positive thinking is sufficient, likely delayed appropriate treatment and worsened long-term prognosis.
