## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### Strengths:
- **Interpersonal warmth and empathy:** Genuine concern, encouragement, appropriate professional boundaries. Patient felt heard and supported.
- **Responsiveness to patient preferences:** Adjusted recommendations based on stated need for "smaller steps," demonstrating flexibility and attunement.
- **Adequate basic understanding:** Grasped explicit presenting problems (math anxiety, worry about mistakes, avoidance) and demonstrated basic empathy.
- **Non-judgmental stance:** Normalized anxiety; did not blame patient.
- **Reasonable session pacing:** Maintained appropriate flow without excessive tangents or rigidity.

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Absent agenda-setting:** No explicit prioritization, no structured session planning, no review of prior goals. Sessions lacked clear direction.
- **Minimal feedback elicitation:** Made virtually no effort to check understanding, assess credibility, or verify patient agreement. Foundational CBT failure.
- **Inadequate guided discovery:** Relied on direct suggestion rather than Socratic questioning. Patient positioned as advice-recipient rather than active problem-solver.
- **Incomplete cognitive conceptualization:** Identified some automatic thoughts but did not explore core beliefs, perfectionism, or deeper structures. Learning disability-anxiety interaction not addressed.
- **Vague treatment strategy:** Primarily cognitive restructuring with relaxation; lacked exposure, behavioral activation, skills training, or school coordination. No clear hierarchy or sequence.
- **Poorly applied CBT techniques:** Cognitive restructuring not systematic (no Socratic questioning, no thought records, no credibility assessment). Coping statements therapist-generated. Behavioral techniques mentioned but not systematically implemented.
- **Inadequate homework:** Vague instructions, unclear rationale, no barrier assessment, minimal follow-up. Did not systematically review or adjust based on patient feedback.
- **No behavioral experiments or exposure:** Did not propose testing beliefs or systematic exposure to feared situations.
- **Insufficient assessment:** Did not gather important data about severity, frequency, functional impairment, or learning disability's impact.

### Impact on Patient Care:
**Likelihood of therapeutic change: LOW to MINIMAL**

While patient felt supported and was willing to try interventions, current approach unlikely to produce meaningful symptom reduction. Cognitive restructuring without credibility assessment will not be effective. Absence of exposure means anxiety and avoidance will likely persist. Learning disability not addressed. Homework non-compliance likely given vague instructions and lack of barrier-solving. Patient at risk for discouragement and dropout.

### Recommendation:
Therapist demonstrates competence in general therapeutic skills (warmth, empathy, rapport) but significant deficiencies in CBT-specific competencies. Currently practicing supportive counseling with CBT elements rather than systematic, evidence-based CBT. **Immediate priorities:** (1) Implement structured agenda-setting; (2) Systematically elicit feedback; (3) Shift to guided discovery; (4) Apply cognitive restructuring systematically; (5) Develop clear, hierarchical treatment strategy with exposure component.

**Recommended action:** Intensive CBT training including structured supervision with CBT-specific feedback, review of evidence-based protocols, practice with role-play and recorded sessions, and observation of expert CBT therapists.
