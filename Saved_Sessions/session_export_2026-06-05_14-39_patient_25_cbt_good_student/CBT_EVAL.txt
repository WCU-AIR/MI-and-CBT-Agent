## CLINICAL SUMMARY

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- **Excellent agenda-setting and collaboration:** Explicit patient input, confirmation of priorities, maintained focus
- **Strong empathic presence and interpersonal effectiveness:** Warm, genuine, validating, non-judgmental tone throughout
- **Skillful guided discovery:** Effective use of Socratic questioning; patient generated own solutions
- **Appropriate cognitive restructuring:** Identified relevant trigger thought; helped develop balanced alternative
- **Well-designed homework:** Specific, collaborative, achievable with implementation planning and obstacle anticipation
- **Good session structure and pacing:** Logical flow from agenda to exploration to intervention to closure

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Failure to assess and address suicidal ideation:** Case formulation notes "thoughts of death"; patient reports mood 4/10; no suicide risk assessment conducted (URGENT CLINICAL CONCERN)
- **Inadequate assessment of depression severity:** Despite depression being primary presenting problem, no exploration of hopelessness, anhedonia, sleep, concentration, or depressive cognitions
- **Incomplete eating disorder assessment:** No evaluation of binge-purge frequency, medical complications, nutritional status, body image, or overvaluation of weight/shape
- **Narrow treatment strategy:** Single cognitive intervention without addressing behavioral structure essential for bulimia (regular eating, stimulus control, exposure) or behavioral activation for depression
- **Limited technique application:** Only cognitive restructuring and brief coping skill used; no behavioral monitoring, thought records, behavioral experiments, or activity scheduling
- **Missed core beliefs and maintaining factors:** Did not explore body image beliefs, perfectionism, control, or historical context (onset age 13, progression to bulimia in college)
- **Insufficient feedback-seeking:** No systematic mid-session understanding checks; episodic rather than consistent feedback elicitation
- **No explicit comorbidity treatment plan:** Did not discuss which disorder to prioritize, how they interact, or how treatment addresses both conditions

### Impact on Patient Care:
This session, while therapeutically warm and collaborative, is **unlikely to produce significant, sustained therapeutic change** due to:
1. Intervention addresses only moment of urge, not underlying behavioral patterns maintaining bulimia
2. Depression symptoms—likely primary drivers of eating disorder—remain untreated
3. Cognitive work is superficial; alternative thought may lack believability
4. Patient's 5/10 confidence suggests ambivalence not adequately addressed
5. Session does not meet evidence-based CBT standards for bulimia nervosa or comorbid depression

### Immediate Actions Required:
1. **URGENT:** Conduct comprehensive suicide risk assessment at next session
2. Develop explicit comorbidity treatment plan addressing both depression and eating disorder
3. Implement behavioral monitoring (food and mood diary) as foundation for understanding patterns
4. Introduce evidence-based behavioral interventions for bulimia nervosa
5. Implement behavioral activation for depression
6. Deepen cognitive work on core beliefs about body image and self-worth
7. Systematically check understanding throughout sessions
8. Explore and address ambivalence about change using motivational interviewing

**Supervisor Recommendation:** This therapist demonstrates solid foundational therapeutic skills and basic CBT competence but requires targeted training in: (1) comprehensive assessment of comorbid disorders, (2) evidence-based treatment for eating disorders, (3) behavioral activation for depression, (4) suicide risk assessment, and (5) systematic application of CBT techniques across multiple clinical domains. Recommend clinical supervision with focus on case conceptualization, treatment planning for comorbidity, and evidence-based protocol implementation.
