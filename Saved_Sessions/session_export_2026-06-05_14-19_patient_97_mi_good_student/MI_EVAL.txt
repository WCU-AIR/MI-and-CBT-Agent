### Autonomy Support

**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support throughout. Psychiatrist consistently affirms client's right to choose, avoids pressure or prescription, and respects decision-making authority. Opening question centers client choice. Clinician does not challenge commitment or introduce doubt. Skillfully avoids siding with external authorities while validating client's autonomy. However, when taken to this extreme without balancing clinical direction, autonomy support can become clinical abandonment of professional role.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Critical deficiency in core MI competency. Psychiatrist fails to draw out client's own motivations, values, and concerns regarding health. When client states "If something actually went wrong...then maybe I'd think twice," clinician does not deepen this potential opening with curiosity. No exploration of client's values around health, self-care, or long-term wellbeing. Does not ask what client knows about actual health risks or what concerns her. Fails to evoke any exploration of clinical concern itself.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Partnership undermined by clinician's overly passive stance. Psychiatrist does not bring clinical expertise into conversation as shared resource. True partner would say: "Your dentist raised specific concerns—let's explore that together." Instead, clinician implicitly accepts client's framing that dentist's concerns are external noise. Does not introduce new perspective, ask challenging questions, or invite different thinking. Session becomes one-directional validation rather than true co-exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Most significant clinical failure. Psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no skill in evoking change talk. Client presents with strong sustain talk ("I don't plan on changing anything," "I'm not buying into warnings," "I don't think there's any next step"). When client offers conditional statement about reconsidering if problems arose, clinician does not amplify. No strategic questions designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment regarding health concern. Session ends with client at same place—no movement, no exploration, no change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Critical failure. Clinician reinforces sustain talk rather than softening it. When client says "I don't plan on changing anything," psychiatrist validates without gentle exploration. When client dismisses warnings, clinician implicitly agrees by focusing only on autonomy. No use of double-sided reflections that might soften resistance. Does not reframe or explore client's dismissal of concerns. Clinician accepts "no next steps needed" completely without exploring differently. Validates only one side of ambivalence.

## Overall Session Summary

### **Overall Session Score: 2.4/5**

**Calculation:** (4 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1) ÷ 9 = **2.4/5**

---

### Strengths
- Consistent autonomy support and nonjudgmental stance
- Skillful affirmation of client's right to choose without pressure
- Effective use of open-ended questions inviting client input
- Validation of identity and self-expression
- Acknowledgment of client's responsible piercing care approach
- Warm, collaborative tone creating safe space for client sharing

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**1. Failure to Maintain Clinical Direction**
Psychiatrist does not clarify or explore dentist's specific health concerns. Session drifts into abstract autonomy discussion without anchoring to clinical reality. This represents abandonment of the referral concern.

**2. Minimal Evocation of Client Values and Concerns**
Clinician does not draw out client's own motivations regarding health. Misses opportunity to deepen conditional statement ("If something went wrong...then maybe I'd think twice"). No exploration of what client values about oral health or long-term wellbeing.

**3. Reinforcement Rather Than Softening of Sustain Talk**
Psychiatrist validates client's resistance to considering health concerns rather than skillfully softening it. No double-sided reflections acknowledging both autonomy and clinical concern. Clinician implicitly sides with client against external concerns.

**4. Absence of Change Talk Cultivation**
No strategic questions designed to evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment regarding health concern. Client leaves with no movement toward even contemplating clinical issue.

**5. Passive Partnership and Clinical Abandonment**
Clinician does not bring clinical expertise into conversation. Does not help bridge gap between client's minimization and clinical reality. True partnership requires sharing clinical knowledge collaboratively while respecting autonomy.

---

### Clinical Impact

This session is unlikely to evoke meaningful change or exploration of health concern. While client feels respected and validated (positive for alliance), she leaves with no new information, no deepened understanding of dentist's concerns, and no movement toward considering clinical issue. Client's stage of change remains firmly in pre-contemplation with no movement toward contemplation. Psychiatrist has achieved acceptance and autonomy support at expense of direction and evocation—creating supportive but clinically ineffective interaction.

---

### Key Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Establish clear clinical direction** by explicitly naming the health concern and inviting collaborative exploration
2. **Evoke and amplify change talk** by pausing when client expresses openness and deepening with curiosity
3. **Use double-sided reflections** to soften sustain talk while honoring both autonomy and clinical concern
4. **Strategically evoke client's values** regarding health through targeted questioning
5. **Bridge gap between minimization and clinical reality** by gently introducing clinical information as shared resource
6. **Bring clinical expertise into conversation** as collaborative partner, not passive observer
7. **Cultivate change talk systematically** using DARN-CAT framework (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Commitment, Taking steps, Activation, Taking steps)
8. **Recognize that autonomy support without clinical direction becomes clinical abandonment** of professional responsibility

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Clinician demonstrates foundational MI skills in autonomy support and acceptance but requires focused training in direction, evocation, and sustain talk management. With targeted practice on these competencies, clinician can develop into effective MI practitioner who balances respect for client autonomy with strategic clinical engagement.
