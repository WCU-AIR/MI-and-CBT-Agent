## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.0/5 (Novice/Pre-competent)**

### Critical Findings:

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement motivational interviewing** and demonstrates practices directly contradictory to MI principles and spirit. The psychiatrist operates from an expert-driven, confrontational model characterized by:

- Systematic use of confrontation and argument rather than collaboration and evocation
- Complete absence of acceptance, empathy, and unconditional positive regard
- Coercive pressure and systematic violation of patient autonomy
- Failure to evoke or amplify patient's own change talk
- Escalating pressure in response to patient resistance
- Dismissal of patient's collaborative suggestions
- Mechanical repetition without reflection or adjustment

### Impact on Patient Engagement:

The session has moved the patient **away from change** rather than toward it. Patient's change talk ("I know I probably should quit") has been replaced with entrenched resistance ("Today feels like too much," "I'm not ready to just start today like you want"). Patient explicitly provided feedback that pressure makes him feel "more stuck," yet psychiatrist ignored this crucial clinical information and intensified the same approach.

### Likelihood of Positive Outcome:

**Very Low.** Therapeutic alliance is damaged. Patient engagement and dropout risk are significantly increased. Research on MI consistently demonstrates that confrontation, pressure, and dismissal of client perspective increase defensiveness and sustain talk while decreasing change talk and commitment to behavior change.

### Recommendation:

**This practitioner should not continue using MI language or claiming to practice MI without formal training, supervision, and demonstrated competency.** Current approach is harmful and contradicts evidence-based practice. Immediate intervention and retraining required.
