### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and avoids judgmental language throughout. Validates Thomas's position on smoking and affirms his honesty about low readiness ("Thanks for being honest about that 2"). However, opportunities to more warmly affirm Thomas's autonomy and strengths are missed. Warmth is present but measured—professional rather than deeply personal. Could strengthen by explicitly celebrating his right to define his own experience and reinforcing capacity for change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated throughout. Accurate reflections capture both Thomas's stated position and underlying ambivalence (e.g., "It sounds like your family is really important to you, and being there for them is a strong value that you hold. At the same time, you're balancing that with a sense that there's still time..."). However, reflections remain somewhat surface-level and lack emotional depth. Could deepen by exploring what makes smoking feel different from gym commitment or what emotions underlie his resistance. Complex reflections highlighting internal conflict would strengthen insight.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on smoking and health behavior maintained throughout. Opening question establishes direction without imposing it. Strategic progression toward exploring values, readiness, past successes, and concrete next steps. However, notable gaps exist: alcohol use (mentioned in referral) is never addressed, representing incomplete agenda-setting. Minor topic drift occurs when exploring gym example without explicitly bridging back to smoking. Tighter focus and complete agenda coverage would strengthen this domain.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated through invitational language ("I'm curious," "Can you tell me," "What do you think"). Opening question positions Thomas as co-creator of agenda. Validates low readiness without challenging. However, true co-construction of solutions could be deeper. Psychiatrist does not explicitly invite Thomas to problem-solve barriers or identify his own solutions. Power-sharing present but not fully activated—clinician maintains slightly expert stance by guiding direction rather than truly sharing authority. More explicit invitation for Thomas's expertise would strengthen collaboration.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Thomas's right to choose and avoids directive pressure. Uses conditional language throughout ("could take, if any"). Does not prescribe change or use "should/must" language. Validates readiness level and explores what might naturally shift perspective. However, autonomy support could strengthen by explicitly naming and celebrating Thomas's ownership of the change process. Does not explore what would make change feel self-directed versus externally imposed (referral from "the doc"). Deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation would enhance this domain.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate evocation with notable gaps in strategic amplification of change talk. Successfully draws out important statements about family values ("I want to be around, you know, for them") and past success with gym. However, does not strategically amplify or deepen these statements. Misses opportunities to connect gym success to smoking motivation or to evoke commitment and ability talk. Does not strategically ask about confidence in identified steps or reasons for choosing them. Readiness and confidence scales introduced but not used strategically to evoke deeper change talk (e.g., "What would move you from 2 to 3?"). Limited use of open-ended questions focused on evoking change talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative tone present with invitational language, but true partnership limited by structure and power-sharing. Psychiatrist asks most questions and guides exploration direction, maintaining subtle expert role. While accurate reflections are offered, they are presented as clinician's observations rather than invitations for Thomas's input. Does not explicitly invite Thomas to co-construct solutions or identify his own barriers. Does not share observations as hunches open to Thomas's correction. Session follows clinician's agenda without explicitly checking whether this is Thomas's priority. More explicit invitation for Thomas's expertise and direction-setting would activate true partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Important change talk evoked (family values, past success, ability) but not consistently cultivated or amplified. When Thomas identifies family as central, clinician reflects accurately but does not deepen exploration or explicitly connect to smoking. Gym success discussed but not used to build momentum toward smoking change. Readiness scale (2/10) introduced but not used strategically—could ask "What would move you from 2 to 3?" Confidence scale (6-7/10) introduced near end but not amplified. Sustain talk handled with basic competence but lacks strategic reframing. Missed opportunities to amplify commitment, ability, and reasons talk limit session's motivational impact.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled with basic competence—accurately reflected and generally pivoted toward exploration without confrontation. However, strategic softening through double-sided reflection and reframing is inconsistent. Statements like "It's not like I'm addicted" and "It's not really the same kind of challenge" could be reframed as strengths or explored more deeply. When Thomas minimizes smoking frequency, reflection is accurate but does not strategically soften resistance. Clinician avoids confrontation skillfully but misses opportunities to use double-sided reflection highlighting ambivalence. More skillful pivoting that reframes sustain talk as evidence of capability would strengthen this domain.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Consistent acceptance and nonjudgmental stance throughout
- Accurate reflective listening capturing Thomas's perspective
- Strong autonomy support with nonprescriptive language
- Clear direction-setting maintaining focus on health behavior
- Collaborative tone with frequent invitational language
- Skillful handling of low readiness without pressure
- Effective exploration of family values evoking change talk
- Strategic use of past successes building self-efficacy
- Concrete, manageable next steps feeling client-owned

### Areas for Improvement
- Limited amplification of evoked change talk
- Incomplete agenda-setting (alcohol never addressed)
- Inconsistent cultivation of commitment and ability talk
- Subtle expert stance despite collaborative language
- Missed opportunities to reframe sustain talk as strengths
- Limited exploration of intrinsic versus extrinsic motivation
- Superficial emotional attunement in reflections
- Insufficient use of double-sided reflection to highlight ambivalence

### Clinical Impact
Session successfully maintains engagement and avoids defensiveness typical of directive approaches. Thomas identifies a small step and expresses moderate confidence (6-7/10). However, readiness remains unchanged at 2/10, and change talk remains underdeveloped. The session represents competent engagement but suboptimal motivational impact. Strategic amplification of change talk and deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation could have shifted Thomas's trajectory toward greater readiness for change.
