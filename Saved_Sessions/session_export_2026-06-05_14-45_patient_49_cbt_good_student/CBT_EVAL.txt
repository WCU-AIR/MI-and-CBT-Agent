## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

### Strengths Demonstrated
- **Exceptional feedback-seeking and collaborative stance**: Therapist consistently elicited patient input, checked understanding multiple times, and explicitly solicited session evaluation.
- **Exemplary guided discovery**: Skillful Socratic questioning facilitated patient self-discovery; patient generated insights independently.
- **Accurate empathic understanding**: Therapist demonstrated thorough comprehension of patient's cognitive, emotional, behavioral, and physical experience.
- **Strong interpersonal effectiveness**: Warm, genuine, confident, and professional; established safe, respectful therapeutic environment.
- **Skillful cognitive restructuring**: Guided patient through identifying, examining, and restructuring central automatic thought with appropriate depth and collaboration.
- **Exemplary homework design**: Specific, rationale-based, collaborative, feasible, and clearly explained with confirmed commitment.

### Critical Deficiencies
- **Incomplete CBT-I approach**: Narrowly focused on cognitive restructuring; omitted first-line CBT-I techniques (sleep restriction, stimulus control, worry postponement, formal relaxation training).
- **Failure to address behavioral perpetuators**: Patient's nightly alcohol consumption (1-2 glasses) causes early morning awakenings and fragmented sleep architecture—not explored or addressed. This is a serious clinical oversight.
- **Limited scope of behavioral analysis**: Did not address other maintaining behaviors (trying hard to sleep, using bed for work) or anticipatory anxiety about sleep itself.
- **No explicit treatment rationale or plan**: Did not explain why proposed intervention would work or establish broader multi-component treatment strategy.
- **No sleep restriction or stimulus control**: Given that patient gets up to work at 3 AM, stimulus control is particularly relevant and was not addressed.

### Recommendation
Therapist requires formal training in evidence-based CBT-I protocols. Current approach is appropriate for anxiety/depression but insufficient for insomnia disorder. Immediate priority: address alcohol use, introduce stimulus control and sleep restriction, and establish comprehensive treatment plan. Without these additions, treatment effectiveness will be significantly compromised.
