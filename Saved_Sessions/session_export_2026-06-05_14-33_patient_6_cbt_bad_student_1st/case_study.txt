--- DISORDER ---
Alcohol Use Disorder; Alcohol-Induced Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Matthew Tucker, a 45-year-old European American plumber, was referred for psychiatric evaluation after his family held an intervention expressing concern that his alcohol use was out of control. Mr. Tucker had abstained for 3 days prior to the evaluation.

For 20 years post–high school, he typically drank 3–5 beers per evening, five times per week. Over the last 7 years, he consumed alcohol almost daily: 6 beers on weeknights and 12 beers on weekends/holidays. Despite efforts to limit intake, he spent much of weekends drinking, sometimes missing family events, and frequently passed out watching TV, though he remained productive at work. He had achieved two 30-day periods of abstinence in the past 4 years without withdrawal symptoms.

In the 6 months prior, Mr. Tucker became irritable, fatigued, dysphoric, and worried. He lost interest in usual activities including food and sex, had difficulty concentrating, reacted more emotionally to stress, and expressed unsubstantiated concern about his business. He often awoke at 2:00 A.M. and struggled to return to sleep. He and his wife reported several similar episodes over the past 5 years, lasting 4–6 weeks each.

He had been married 18 years with one 17-year-old daughter. Education: high school graduate with 2 years of community college. Occupation: owner of a successful plumbing company. No prior psychiatric or medical problems, no history of mania or suicide attempts, and had never seen a psychiatrist.

Recent labs: mildly elevated blood pressure (135/92), GGT 47 IU/L, MCV 92.5 μm3, all other labs normal. Physical exam: neatly dressed, good eye contact, no confusion or psychotic symptoms, eyes teary when discussing future, cognition within normal limits, insight into alcohol’s effects.

Discussion: Mr. Tucker meets criteria for moderate alcohol use disorder (unsuccessful attempts to cut down, excessive time intoxicated/recovering, missed social activities, continued use despite problems). No clinically significant withdrawal symptoms. His depressive episode meets criteria for alcohol-induced depressive disorder: persistent depressed mood, reduced interest, insomnia, diminished concentration, fatigue. Past episodes resolved spontaneously after 4–6 weeks abstinence. Depressive symptoms are expected to diminish with ongoing sobriety.
