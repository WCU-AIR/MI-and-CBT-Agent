### Summary of Key Findings

#### **Strengths:**
- Clear identification of target behaviors (medication adherence and lifestyle compliance)
- Explicit communication of treatment expectations

#### **Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental violation of MI spirit:** Every core MI principle—acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support, and evocation—is systematically violated
- **Mechanical, non-responsive approach:** Identical monologue repeated six times demonstrates psychiatrist is not listening to or responding to client
- **Dismissal of legitimate safety concerns:** Valid clinical concerns about citalopram-phenelzine interactions are dismissed as "overthinking" rather than addressed appropriately
- **Mischaracterization of client position:** Alessia's reasonable requests for information are repeatedly framed as "making excuses" and "resistance" when she is actually expressing willingness to comply once informed
- **Controlling language and coercion:** Repeated use of "you have to," "you must," and threats about condition deterioration functions as coercion rather than motivation
- **Active suppression of change talk:** Rather than amplifying Alessia's substantial spontaneous change talk, psychiatrist ignores it and responds with dismissal
- **Zero adaptation or flexibility:** Despite six opportunities to adjust approach based on client response, psychiatrist repeats identical opening without modification

#### **Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is likely to **decrease rather than increase** Alessia's motivation for change. She enters with substantial change talk and willingness to comply once informed. The psychiatrist's response—dismissal, controlling language, mechanical repetition, and refusal to address legitimate concerns—communicates that her perspective is invalid and her autonomy is not respected. This is likely to increase resistance, decrease trust, and reduce likelihood of medication adherence.

#### **Clinical Recommendation:**
This psychiatrist requires **formal MI training and supervised practice** before continuing to use MI-based approaches with clients. The current approach is antithetical to motivational interviewing and potentially harmful to client engagement and treatment outcomes. Immediate reorientation to MI spirit and philosophy is necessary.
