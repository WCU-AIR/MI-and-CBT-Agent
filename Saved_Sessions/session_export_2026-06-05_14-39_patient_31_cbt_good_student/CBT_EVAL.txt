### PART II. CONCEPTUALIZATION, STRATEGY, AND TECHNIQUE

**Summary of Conceptualization Issues:**

The therapist's conceptualization fundamentally misunderstood the patient's presentation. The therapist formulated the case as an anxiety disorder with automatic thoughts about danger, suitable for cognitive restructuring. However, the patient presents with acute delirium—a medical emergency characterized by:

- Acute onset (2 weeks) fluctuating consciousness and attention
- Fever (39.9°C), tachycardia (115 bpm), hypertension (172/74)
- Anticholinergic toxidrome (mydriasis, dry mucosa, urinary retention, depressed reflexes)
- Medication-induced etiology (cyclobenzaprine + benzodiazepines + opioids)
- Dangerous behavior (running with knives, displaying guns)
- Severe cognitive impairment (MMSE 16/30)

**CBT is contraindicated for delirium.** The correct conceptualization would recognize delirium pathophysiology and guide medical intervention (medication review, treatment of underlying cause, environmental management) rather than cognitive restructuring.

**Expected Outcome:** If this approach continues, the patient is unlikely to experience meaningful symptom improvement. His paranoia, perceptual disturbances, and confusion will persist because the underlying medical cause is not being addressed. He may become frustrated with psychotherapy, disengage from treatment, and remain at risk for dangerous behavior. ## IMMEDIATE RECOMMENDATIONS

### 1. **URGENT: Recognize Delirium and Refer for Medical Intervention**
Immediately discontinue CBT and refer the patient for comprehensive medical evaluation. Delirium is a medical emergency requiring medical, not psychological, intervention.

### 2. **Conduct Comprehensive Medical Assessment Before Any Psychotherapy**
Assess vital signs, medications (including doses, duration, interactions), recent medical events, substance use, and cognitive status (MMSE or MoCA) before engaging in any psychotherapy.

### 3. **Assess Safety Comprehensively and Develop Safety Plan**
Conduct thorough safety assessment including current suicidal/homicidal ideation, access to means, recent dangerous behavior, and protective factors. Develop explicit safety plan with patient and family.

### 4. **Recognize Cognitive Impairment and Adjust Treatment Accordingly**
Assess cognitive status before initiating any psychotherapy. If cognitive impairment is present, adjust treatment to match cognitive capacity or defer psychotherapy until cognitive function improves.

### 5. **Understand Delirium Phenomenology and Distinguish from Primary Psychiatric Disorders**
Develop expertise in recognizing delirium and understanding how it differs from anxiety disorders, psychotic disorders, and mood disorders. Learn that delirium requires medical intervention, not psychotherapy.

### 6. **Develop Medication Literacy and Recognize Medication-Induced Psychiatric Symptoms**
Learn common medications that cause psychiatric symptoms, especially anticholinergic agents, benzodiazepines, and opioids. Always inquire about medications and consider medication-induced etiology.

### 7. **Establish Clear Diagnostic Formulation Before Selecting Treatment**
Develop a clear diagnostic formulation (diagnosis, etiology, maintaining factors) before selecting treatment. Ensure the formulation is accurate and guides appropriate treatment selection.

### 8. **Collaborate with Medical Providers and Coordinate Care**
Establish communication with the patient's medical providers. Share clinical observations and coordinate treatment planning. Understand the limits of psychotherapy and when medical intervention is primary.

### 9. **Develop Competence in Recognizing When CBT Is and Is Not Appropriate**
Learn the indications and contraindications for CBT. Develop clinical judgment about when CBT is appropriate and when other interventions (medical, psychiatric, environmental) are primary.

### 10. **Seek Supervision and Consultation on Complex Cases**
When encountering complex presentations (acute medical illness, severe psychiatric symptoms, safety concerns), seek supervision or consultation from experienced clinicians. Do not proceed with treatment without guidance.
