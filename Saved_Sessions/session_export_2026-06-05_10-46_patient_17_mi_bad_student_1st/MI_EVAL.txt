### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk through direct confrontation rather than skillful softening. When client expresses sustain talk ("I don't think it's going to hurt me," "It makes me relax and it's fun"), psychiatrist attacks it directly: "No, no, vaping is just as bad as smoking," "You're not thinking straight." This confrontational approach is counterproductive; research demonstrates direct confrontation strengthens sustain talk through psychological reactance. Client responds by reasserting sustain talk: "I don't think vaping a few times a month is going to wreck my lungs right away." More skillful approach would use double-sided reflection: "So vaping helps you relax and have fun, and you're also worried about basketball eligibility." Psychiatrist maintains some focus away from sustain talk through repeated redirection, but this is through coercion rather than skillful softening.

## Overall Summary

### Strengths
- Maintains consistent topical focus on vaping cessation, preventing topic drift
- Communicates genuine concern about health risks, demonstrating some investment in client wellbeing

### Critical Deficiencies
This session represents a fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles across nearly every dimension. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, authoritarian, coercive approach that directly contradicts MI's evidence-based foundation. Rather than evoking intrinsic motivation, the psychiatrist attempts to impose change through threats, shame, and elimination of autonomy. Rather than partnering with the client, the psychiatrist asserts unilateral expert authority. Rather than amplifying substantial change talk, the psychiatrist dismisses and contradicts it.

The most critical deficiency is the complete absence of MI spirit—partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. The psychiatrist treats this as a compliance problem to be solved through force rather than a motivation problem to be solved through collaboration.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change
**Highly Counterproductive.** Anika enters with genuine ambivalence and emerging readiness (7/10 confidence, concrete goal, identified triggers). The psychiatrist's confrontational approach triggers psychological reactance, causing Anika to defend her current behavior and assert autonomy by refusing compliance. By session end, Anika explicitly states she will not quit "just because I'm told" and needs "support, not pressure." The psychiatrist has transformed a contemplation-stage client with change talk into a resistant, defensive client less likely to change. This is the opposite of intended outcome.

### Overall MI Competency Assessment
**Minimal to None.** The psychiatrist demonstrates no evidence of understanding MI principles, spirit, or techniques. The approach is explicitly contrary to evidence-based MI practice.

### Recommendation
**Do not continue behavior change counseling without formal MI training.** The psychiatrist should complete MINT-certified Motivational Interviewing training and demonstrate competency through supervised practice before resuming this type of clinical work. Current approach causes harm by increasing client resistance and decreasing motivation for change.
