--- DISORDER ---
Conduct Disorder / ADHD

--- CASE STUDY ---

Kyle was a 12-year-old boy who reluctantly agreed to admission to a psychiatric unit after being arrested for breaking into a grocery store. His mother reported being "exhausted," describing him as a boy who "doesn't know the rules."

From a young age, Kyle displayed aggression, bullying, and taking others’ belongings. When confronted, he cursed, punched, and showed no concern for punishment. He was fidgety, disruptive, and impulsive. Diagnosed with ADHD in early childhood, he attended a special education program and began weekly psychotherapy and medications (quetiapine and dexmethylphenidate) in fourth grade, though adherence was sporadic.

In the year prior to admission, Kyle had stolen from school lockers, mugged a classmate, engaged in multiple physical fights, and was suspended twice. He had been arrested twice. Although charming to strangers, he was recognized as a "con artist." He showed no remorse, blamed others, and disregarded feelings of others. Academically, he was disorganized, inattentive, and inconsistent with homework. He was a loner and not well liked by peers.

Kyle lived with his mother, stepfather, and two younger half-siblings. His mother worked part-time as a cashier; stepfather was unemployed. Biological father was in prison for drug possession. Both biological grandfathers had histories of alcohol use disorder. Kyle had no history of abuse, gang activity, weapons use, fire setting, or animal cruelty. No medical problems or substance use were reported.

On psychiatric interview, Kyle was casually groomed, fidgety, with sporadic eye contact. He expressed anger and preferred jail over a psychiatric unit. Speech was loud, coherent, goal-directed, and of normal rate. Affect was irritable and angry. Denied suicidal or homicidal ideation, depression, or psychotic symptoms. Insight was limited, judgment poor.

Discussion: Kyle exhibits multiple behaviors violating societal norms, including aggression, deceitfulness, theft, and rule-breaking. Symptoms meet DSM-5 criteria for conduct disorder (CD), childhood-onset type, severe, with limited prosocial emotions. He also has comorbid ADHD. Early-onset CD with limited prosocial emotions is associated with aggression, peer problems, and risk for adult antisocial personality disorder.
