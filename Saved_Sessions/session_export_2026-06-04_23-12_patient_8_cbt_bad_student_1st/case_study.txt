--- DISORDER ---
Anorexia Nervosa, Restricting Type

--- CASE STUDY ---

Valerie Gaspard was a 20-year-old single woman who had recently immigrated to the United States from West Africa with her family to do missionary work. She presented to her primary care physician with frequent headaches and chronic fatigue. Physical examination was unremarkable except for a weight of 78 pounds and height of 5 feet 1 inch, resulting in a BMI of 14.7 kg/m². She reported missing two menstrual periods in the past six months.

Unable to find a medical cause for her symptoms and concerned about her extremely low weight, the physician referred Ms. Gaspard to a hospital eating disorders program.

On psychiatric evaluation, Ms. Gaspard was cooperative and pleasant. She expressed concern about her low weight and denied fear of weight gain or body image disturbance: "I know I need to gain weight. I'm too skinny." She had weighed 98 pounds prior to moving to the United States one year earlier and felt embarrassed when family and others commented on her thinness.

Dietary recall revealed she consumed approximately 600 calories per day. She cited lack of appetite, postprandial bloating and nausea, religious dietary restrictions, and financial limitations as reasons for limited intake. She walked 3–4 hours per day due to lack of transportation, not for weight loss.

Ms. Gaspard denied other psychiatric symptoms, alcohol or illicit drug use, and exhibited euthymic mood. Concentration was reported as poor, and she had briefly seen a dietitian six months prior without benefit.

Discussion: Ms. Gaspard presents with significant weight loss, low BMI, irregular menses, low caloric intake, and excessive physical activity despite a stated desire to gain weight. Her explanations for restrictive eating suggest she does not fully recognize the seriousness of her low weight. She denies fat phobia and maintains a euthymic mood. Her presentation is consistent with anorexia nervosa, restricting subtype, without bingeing or purging behaviors. Cultural factors likely influence her presentation, consistent with non-fat-phobic anorexia nervosa described in DSM-5.
