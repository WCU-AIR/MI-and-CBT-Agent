## Overall Session Score: **2.9/5**

### Summary Assessment

**Competence Level:** Below Average

This session demonstrates **below-average MI competence** with significant deficiencies in core MI principles. While the psychiatrist maintains a respectful tone and demonstrates basic reflective listening, the session is fundamentally **expert-driven rather than client-centered**.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Expert-driven stance dominates; psychiatrist functions as solution-provider rather than collaborative partner
- Prescriptive language and subtle pressure undermine autonomy support
- Limited open-ended exploration; closed/leading questions predominate
- Advice-giving crowds out evocation of patient's own ideas and resources
- Patient must repeatedly educate psychiatrist about what works for her
- Core ambivalence inadequately explored
- Change talk cultivation inconsistent and largely patient-initiated

**Strengths:**
- Basic respect and warm tone maintained
- Accurate paraphrasing of factual content
- Adaptive pivoting when patient provides feedback
- Clear agenda-setting and reasonable direction
- Affirmation of patient's family-based motivation
- Appropriate use of scaling questions

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
While patient expresses willingness to try changes, this appears driven primarily by intrinsic motivation (family) rather than clinician's skillful evocation. Moderate confidence (5/10) and expressed doubt suggest potential difficulty sustaining changes. Session does not fully embody MI spirit of partnership and autonomy support necessary to maximize likelihood of sustained behavior change.

## Key Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from Expert to Partner Stance** – Fundamentally reframe role; ask open-ended questions before offering suggestions
2. **Eliminate Prescriptive Language** – Replace "should," "might want to," and pressure language with autonomy-supportive alternatives
3. **Deepen Empathy** – Move beyond paraphrasing to complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and ambivalence
4. **Use Scaling Questions as Springboards** – Follow scales with open-ended questions that evoke patient's own thinking
5. **Strategically Soften Sustain Talk** – Use double-sided reflections and reframing to address core ambivalences
6. **Actively Evoke Change Talk** – Ask strategic questions designed to draw out patient's reasons, desires, abilities, and commitment
7. **Explore Patient's Experience First** – Ask about past attempts and barriers before offering solutions
8. **Connect Goals to Core Values** – Explicitly weave patient's family values into direction-setting throughout session
9. **Develop True Partnership** – Invite patient to co-create solutions; use collaborative language
10. **Recover Skillfully** – When missteps occur, acknowledge and pivot back to collaborative stance
