### Critical Areas for Improvement

**Fundamental Misalignment with MI Principles:** This session demonstrates a **directive, expert-driven approach that is fundamentally inconsistent with Motivational Interviewing.** The psychiatrist functions as an authority figure who tells Ahmed what to do rather than as a collaborative partner who helps Ahmed explore his own motivations and resources.

**Core Deficiency—Absence of MI Spirit:** Rather than communicating acceptance, empathy, partnership, and evocation, the psychiatrist communicates:
- Conditional regard based on behavioral compliance
- Minimal understanding of Ahmed's perspective
- Hierarchical power dynamics
- Prescriptive advice and pressure

**Counterproductive Patterns:**
- Unilateral agenda-setting without asking Ahmed's priorities
- Token reflections immediately followed by contradiction
- Dismissal of Ahmed's contributions and expertise
- Prescriptive language minimizing choice and autonomy
- Continued pressure despite Ahmed's stated low confidence (2-3/10)
- No exploration of Ahmed's values, strengths, or past experiences
- Direct confrontation of sustain talk, which strengthens resistance
- Elicitation of sustain talk rather than change talk throughout

**Impact on Client:** Ahmed's language becomes increasingly resistant throughout the session. His confidence to change has likely decreased further. The therapeutic relationship has been strained rather than strengthened. Likelihood of Ahmed making recommended changes is very low.

---

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Adopt Collaborative Stance:** Shift from expert role to asking Ahmed's perspective. Use language positioning Ahmed as expert on his own life.

2. **Demonstrate Genuine Empathy:** Move beyond surface acknowledgments to complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and underlying perspective.

3. **Respect Autonomy:** Explicitly acknowledge Ahmed's right to decide. Replace "should" with "could" and "what feels right to you?"

4. **Evoke Rather Than Educate:** Replace lectures with strategic questions drawing out Ahmed's own reasons, values, and concerns.

5. **Explore Ambivalence:** Use double-sided reflections to explore both sides of Ahmed's ambivalence rather than arguing for one side.

6. **Address Low Confidence Collaboratively:** Treat Ahmed's 2-3/10 confidence as critical information to explore, not ignore.

7. **Soften Sustain Talk Through Exploration:** Respond to resistance with curiosity and reflection, not contradiction.

8. **Establish Collaborative Agenda-Setting:** Ask Ahmed what he wants to focus on; build session around his priorities.

9. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:** Listen for any language suggesting openness to change; reflect it back and explore it.

10. **Seek MI Training and Supervision:** Attend formal MI training (MINT-certified) and engage in ongoing supervision with MI expert. Record sessions and use MITI scale for self-assessment.

---

### Conclusion

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5 — Minimal Competence in MI**

This session demonstrates **fundamental misalignment with Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit.** The psychiatrist needs to shift from a directive, expert-driven approach to a collaborative, evocative, client-centered approach. This requires philosophical reorientation, not just tactical changes. Ahmed is the expert on his own life; the psychiatrist's role is to help Ahmed explore his own motivations and resources, not prescribe solutions.

**Immediate Priority:** Seek formal MI training and clinical supervision before continuing with this patient population. The current approach, while well-intentioned, is counterproductive to evoking motivation for change and risks damaging the therapeutic relationship.
