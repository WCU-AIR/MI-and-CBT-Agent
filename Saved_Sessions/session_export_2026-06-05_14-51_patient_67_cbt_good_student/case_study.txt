--- DISORDER ---
Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea (OSAH)

--- CASE STUDY ---

César Linares was a 57-year-old married Hispanic man who presented for reevaluation of his antidepressant medication. He described several years of worsening fatigue, daytime sleepiness, and generally "not feeling good." He lacked energy for usual activities but still enjoyed them when participating. He experienced difficulty focusing at work as an information technology consultant and worried about job loss. He had been on an SSRI antidepressant for 6 months with some symptom improvement and reported adherence.

He denied recent stressors. His medical history included depression, hypertension, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. He complained of heartburn and erectile dysfunction, which had not been evaluated. He was born in Venezuela, had two grown children, did not smoke or drink alcohol, but consumed several servings of coffee daily for alertness.

On physical examination, he was 5'10", weighed 235 lbs (BMI 34), neck circumference 20 inches, respiratory rate 15, blood pressure 155/90. No other abnormalities were noted.

Mental status: cooperative, appeared tired, no depression, anxiety, psychosis, or cognitive decline. Focused questioning revealed trouble staying awake at work and occasional nodding off while driving. Slept 8–10 hours nightly with frequent awakenings, nocturia, waking with choking sensation or headaches. Snored loudly since childhood; wife reported intermittent breathing pauses.

Sleep studies (polysomnography) results: apnea hypopnea index 25 events/hour, oxygen desaturation index 20 events/hour, nadir O₂ saturation 82%, 10% of time <90%, arousal index 35 events/hour. Sleep stages: N1 20%, N2 65%, N3 5%, REM 10%. Findings consistent with obstructive sleep apnea hypopnea.
