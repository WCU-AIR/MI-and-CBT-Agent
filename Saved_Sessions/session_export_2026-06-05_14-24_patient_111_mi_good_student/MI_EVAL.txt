### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some cultivation of change talk but misses numerous opportunities to systematically evoke and amplify Richard's own language about change. Richard produces several instances of change talk ("I'm willing to try," "I'd say I'm pretty confident—maybe a 7 out of 10," "I think those changes aren't too big"). Psychiatrist generally affirms these but doesn't strategically amplify or deepen them with follow-up questions. Doesn't systematically ask targeted questions to elicit specific types of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Confidence scaling questions are appropriate but psychiatrist doesn't explore what's driving increases or what would move Richard higher. Represents missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good skill in addressing Richard's sustain talk without confrontation or reinforcement. Skillfully reframes concerns rather than arguing against them. Uses double-sided reflections effectively (e.g., acknowledging concern about burden while redirecting toward quality of life values). When Richard expresses worry about side effects, psychiatrist validates and pivots toward deeper motivation. Strategically shifts focus from barriers to resources and past competence. However, could be more proactive in softening sustain talk. When Richard expresses 5/10 confidence level, psychiatrist could have explored specific worries driving hesitation before offering reassurance.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level: 3.7/5 (Adequate to Good)**

**Session Strengths:**
- Strong opening and collaborative stance with genuine invitation for client agenda-setting
- Accurate reflective listening capturing both content and emotional tone
- Consistent validation and normalization of ambivalence without judgment
- Values-centered approach connecting health management to deeper life goals
- Skillful handling of sustain talk with strategic redirection toward resources and values
- Affirmation of client competence and past successes
- Practical focus moving toward concrete, manageable first steps

**Primary Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Insufficient evocation:** Provides information and reassurance too readily rather than asking questions that draw out client's own reasoning and motivations
2. **Expert-driven medical information:** Presents medical information as expert knowledge to be accepted rather than co-constructing understanding with client
3. **Insufficient amplification of change talk:** Change talk present but not systematically evoked or strategically amplified through targeted questioning
4. **Limited barrier exploration:** Doesn't deeply explore specific concerns about side effects or medication complexity
5. **Passive confidence scaling:** Doesn't explore what's driving confidence levels or what would increase them
6. **Incomplete autonomy support:** Could be more explicit about client's right to accept, reject, or question recommendations

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Richard's language becomes progressively more change-oriented throughout session (moving from ambivalence to 7/10 confidence and concrete action planning), indicating psychiatrist's approach is having positive effect. However, remaining 7/10 confidence level suggests significant ambivalence persists. Deeper exploration of concerns and more systematic evocation of intrinsic motivation might have increased confidence and commitment more substantially.

**Recommendations for Development:**
Prioritize evocation over information-giving; systematically cultivate and amplify change talk; deepen exploration of ambivalence before action planning; co-construct medical information collaboratively; explore confidence levels more thoroughly; strengthen partnership through explicit acknowledgment of client expertise; use strategic double-sided reflections; develop detailed action plans collaboratively; explicitly support autonomy; slow pace to allow more exploration time.
