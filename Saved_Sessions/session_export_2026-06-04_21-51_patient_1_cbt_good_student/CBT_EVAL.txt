## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL PERFORMANCE

### Strengths
- **Strong agenda-setting and collaboration:** Explicit, mutually satisfactory agenda established and maintained throughout session
- **Accurate empathic understanding:** Therapist accurately reflected Dylan's trigger-thought-feeling-behavior chain and demonstrated solid listening skills
- **Effective cognitive restructuring:** Evidence examination and alternative thought development were well-executed using guided discovery
- **Good therapeutic alliance:** Warm, professional, validating tone with appropriate boundaries
- **Practical, specific homework:** Homework directly connected to session work with implementation supports (sticky note, reminders)
- **Appropriate pacing:** Logical flow without tangents; efficient use of time
- **Systematic feedback-seeking at key moments:** Checked understanding after cognitive chain summary, alternative thought, and homework assignment

### Critical Deficiencies
- **Dangerously narrow symptom focus:** Addressed only startle response to loud noises; ignored sleep disturbance, nightmares, avoidance of driving, avoidance of loud environments, irritability, concentration difficulties, and trauma discussion avoidance
- **Incomplete case formulation:** Did not identify or target key maintaining cognitions (helplessness, shame, unfairness) or avoidance behaviors
- **Insufficient trauma-focused intervention:** For ASD, evidence-based treatments (CPT, PE) involve intensive trauma processing; this session used standard cognitive restructuring without explicit trauma narrative or imaginal exposure work
- **Limited CBT technique application:** Relied heavily on cognitive restructuring; did not apply behavioral techniques (exposure, activation, safety behavior analysis), relaxation/grounding, imagery work, or trauma-focused interventions
- **No behavioral exposure or graded hierarchy:** Did not develop exposure hierarchy or discuss graded approach to feared situations (loud noises, driving, discussing trauma)
- **Incomplete homework design:** No structured monitoring form, limited rationale, no behavioral experimentation component, no discussion of barriers or contingency planning
- **Insufficient depth in thought work:** Did not explore core beliefs, function of catastrophic thoughts, or cost-benefit analysis

### Impact on Patient Care
By focusing narrowly on one symptom (startle response), therapist risks leaving Dylan's most impairing symptoms unaddressed. Driving refusal is critical developmental concern at age 15. Sleep disturbance likely contributes to school concentration difficulties. Irritability damages family relationships. Resistance to discussing trauma may reflect avoidance maintaining emotional processing difficulties. Reliance on cognitive restructuring alone, without behavioral exposure or trauma processing, is less likely to produce robust symptom reduction. Research shows exposure-based interventions produce larger effect sizes than cognitive interventions alone for trauma. If alternative thought does not significantly reduce anxiety in real situations (likely without exposure work), Dylan may become discouraged and lose confidence in treatment.

### Recommendations for Next Session
1. Develop comprehensive multi-symptom treatment plan addressing all ASD symptoms
2. Incorporate trauma-focused interventions (trauma narrative, imaginal exposure)
3. Create graded exposure hierarchy for avoidance behaviors, prioritizing driving
4. Teach grounding and relaxation techniques for hyperarousal
5. Explicitly address safety behaviors and avoidance patterns
6. Assign structured monitoring forms and more active behavioral experiments
7. Explore anger, unfairness beliefs, and shame cognitions
8. Develop sleep hygiene and nightmare management plan
9. Increase systematic feedback-seeking throughout sessions
10. Establish clear session-to-session goals and objective progress monitoring

### Overall Clinical Impression
This therapist demonstrates solid foundational CBT skills (4.7/6) with particular strength in agenda-setting, collaboration, and empathic understanding. However, the session does not fully qualify as evidence-based CBT for Acute Stress Disorder due to narrow symptom focus, incomplete case formulation, and insufficient trauma-focused and behavioral interventions. With recommended additions, treatment would align with evidence-based protocols and substantially increase likelihood of meaningful symptom reduction and functional improvement for Dylan.
