## SUMMARY

**This session represents a fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles.** The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, confrontational counseling that directly contradicts MI spirit across all nine competency domains.

**Critical Findings:**

- **Acceptance (1/5):** No unconditional positive regard; judgment and disapproval communicated throughout.
- **Empathy (1/5):** No reflective listening; dismissal of Adrian's perspective and emotional experience.
- **Direction (1/5):** Rigid, imposed direction without collaboration or exploration of readiness.
- **Collaboration (1/5):** Unilateral expert-authority stance; explicit rejection of negotiation and partnership.
- **Autonomy Support (1/5):** Systematic suppression of Adrian's autonomy through directive language and coercion.
- **Evocation (1/5):** Imposition of reasons for change rather than evoking Adrian's own motivations and values.
- **Partnership (1/5):** Hierarchical relationship explicitly rejecting true partnership and shared decision-making.
- **Cultivating Change Talk (1/5):** Change talk ignored; confrontation strengthens resistance rather than cultivating motivation.
- **Softening Sustain Talk (1/5):** Direct confrontation escalates sustain talk; no skillful management of resistance.

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

Adrian's resistance escalates throughout the session in direct response to the psychiatrist's confrontational approach. He moves from "I'm not ready" to "I'm not going to just follow that." This is a predictable response to authoritarian pressure and indicates the approach is counterproductive.

**Research Implications:**

Confrontational, directive approaches are consistently associated with increased resistance and reduced intrinsic motivation for change. This session exemplifies that pattern. Adrian is unlikely to leave feeling understood, respected, or motivated to change. Instead, he is likely to feel judged, pressured, and defensive—emotions that predict treatment dropout and continued substance use.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Comprehensive MI training** with certified MI trainer (minimum 2-3 day workshop)
2. **Ongoing clinical supervision** with MI-certified supervisor; record and review sessions
3. **Eliminate directive language** ("have to," "must," "should"); replace with collaborative inquiry
4. **Implement reflective listening** as primary intervention; aim for 2:1 reflection-to-question ratio
5. **Explore and validate ambivalence** rather than arguing against sustain talk
6. **Establish true partnership** by seeking Adrian's input on goals and plans
7. **Respect autonomy explicitly** through autonomy-supportive language
8. **Evoke Adrian's own motivations** through strategic open-ended questioning
9. **Amplify change talk** when it emerges; explore and reinforce it
10. **Soften sustain talk** through validation and strategic pivoting, not confrontation

**Critical Priority:**

This is not a matter of adding reflections or asking open-ended questions. The psychiatrist requires **fundamental reorientation to MI principles and spirit**—a shift from authoritarian, expert-driven practice to collaborative, empathic, evocative partnership. Without this shift, Adrian is unlikely to engage in genuine change work, and the psychiatrist's approach risks causing iatrogenic harm.
