### Autonomy Support

Clinician does not pressure toward particular change and validates Marcus's desire to keep his dog as legitimate. Permission-seeking before information-sharing ("Would it be okay if I shared...?") respects autonomy. Language is permissive rather than directive. Limitations: subtle implications that Marcus should be able to manage change again. Insufficient explicit statements that all choices are acceptable or that clinician will support whatever Marcus decides.

Critical limitation: clinician does not adequately amplify or build on Marcus's change talk about routine helping him succeed. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This represents a significant weakness. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce Marcus's change talk. Marcus produces spontaneous change talk ("my asthma is out of control and that scares me," "I want to breathe easy like before," "I was taking my medication regularly and felt better"), but clinician does not strategically respond to deepen it. When Marcus identifies that routine helped him succeed, clinician doesn't ask follow-up questions to explore this further. Session lacks targeted questions designed to evoke specific types of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). No strategic summaries selectively highlight and reinforce change talk. Significant missed opportunity to strengthen Marcus's motivation for change.

When Marcus says "I'm not ready to make big changes yet," clinician accepts but doesn't explore what "big changes" means or whether smaller steps are possible. ## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative opening that explicitly invites Marcus to shape agenda
- Excellent complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and competing values
- Consistent validation of both health concerns and attachment to dog
- Good exploration of past success demonstrating capability
- Appropriate autonomy support and respect for Marcus's choices
- Respectful handling of resistance without confrontation
- Permission-seeking before information-giving

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient cultivation of change talk** – minimal strategic evocation, amplification, or reinforcement of Marcus's motivation statements
- **Premature shift to information-giving** – occurs before adequate exploration of Marcus's own ideas, past attempts, and specific triggers
- **Limited strategic questioning** – lacks targeted questions designed to evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment for change
- **Vague goal-setting** – maintains general focus on asthma without narrowing to specific, concrete, measurable change targets
- **Subtle expert positioning** – shift toward expert-driven problem-solving rather than true co-construction during information phase
- **Minimal reframing** – limited strategic reframing of concerns or sustain talk to shift perspective toward possibility

**Clinical Impact:**

The psychiatrist creates a safe, validating therapeutic environment that likely increases Marcus's engagement and willingness to return. However, the session does not maximize opportunities to significantly strengthen intrinsic motivation for change. Marcus leaves with vague commitment to "talk to my doctor about adjusting medication" rather than deeply explored, personally meaningful reasons for change. The session demonstrates **intermediate MI competence** with solid foundational skills but notable gaps in strategic depth, particularly in the core MI technique of evoking and amplifying change talk.

**Recommendations Priority:**
1. Strategically evoke and amplify change talk through targeted follow-up questions
2. Use strategic summaries to selectively highlight and reinforce change talk
3. Maintain evocative stance longer before offering information
4. Ask targeted questions to elicit specific types of change talk (DARN-CAT framework)
5. Collaboratively develop specific, concrete, measurable change goals
