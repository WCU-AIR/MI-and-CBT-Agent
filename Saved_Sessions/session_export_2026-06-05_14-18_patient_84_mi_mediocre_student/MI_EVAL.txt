## Summary Assessment

**Clinical Competence Level:** Supportive Counseling with Limited MI Elements

This psychiatrist demonstrates basic clinical competence in providing information and maintaining respectful tone. However, the session is fundamentally **directive and expert-driven** rather than collaborative and evocative, failing to constitute genuine MI practice.

### Key Strengths:
- Basic warmth and courtesy in opening
- Provides necessary medication information when client asks
- Attempts confidence scaling (appropriate MI technique)
- Avoids confrontation and maintains respectful tone
- Provides clear closing summary

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- **Excessive prescriptive language** undermines collaboration and client autonomy
- **Minimal reflective listening** despite many questions
- **Failure to evoke change talk** systematically
- **Lack of explicit autonomy support**
- **Agenda-driven rather than client-centered** approach
- **Missed opportunities for collaboration** when client identifies own ideas or barriers
- **Insufficient exploration of ambivalence**

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change:
Client demonstrates **external compliance rather than intrinsic commitment**. Moderate confidence (5/10) is not explored to strengthen commitment. Three behavioral targets may overwhelm client. Medication adherence likely; snack swap possible; morning walks unlikely given low confidence, identified barriers, and lack of intrinsic motivation. Psychiatrist's directive approach has reduced likelihood of sustained behavior change.

### Recommendation:
Psychiatrist requires focused training in MI fundamentals, particularly: shifting from prescriptive to evocative language, developing complex reflective listening, systematically evoking and amplifying change talk, explicitly supporting autonomy, and collaborating on agenda-setting and problem-solving.
