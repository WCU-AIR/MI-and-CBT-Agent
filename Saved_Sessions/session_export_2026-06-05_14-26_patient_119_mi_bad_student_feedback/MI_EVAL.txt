## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.3/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles. The psychiatrist operates from a confrontational, expert-driven stance that directly contradicts MI spirit and actively reduces client motivation for change. Across all nine MI competency domains, the psychiatrist demonstrates minimal skill and frequent violations of core MI principles.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- Fundamental violation of MI spirit through confrontational, authoritarian language
- Active undermining of change talk when it emerges
- Dismissal of client expertise regarding own barriers and experience
- Repeated reversion to directive approach despite brief collaborative moment
- Failure to explore client ambivalence, which is key to evoking change talk
- Confrontation of sustain talk rather than softening through validation and reflection

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

Client enters with some ambivalence and openness ("I guess I got to really want to"). By session end, client is more entrenched in resistance ("I need to figure it out my own way, on my own time"). Psychiatrist's confrontational approach triggers defensiveness and strengthens commitment to status quo. Therapeutic alliance is damaged; client explicitly states feeling "judged and cornered" rather than helped.

**Recommendation:**

This psychiatrist requires intensive MI training with supervised practice and feedback. Fundamental MI spirit—partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation—must be understood and internalized before advanced techniques can be applied. Recommend formal MI training program (2-3 days minimum), supervised practice with feedback on recorded sessions, and regular consultation with MI expert.
