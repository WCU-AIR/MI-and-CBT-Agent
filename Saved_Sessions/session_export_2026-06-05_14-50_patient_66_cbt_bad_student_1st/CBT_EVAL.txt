## SUMMARY OF CRITICAL FINDINGS

**Strengths:**
- Basic warmth and non-judgmental stance
- Cooperative demeanor; no hostility or demeaning behavior
- Attempted to normalize patient experience (though often inappropriately)
- Offered encouragement and support

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- ☐ No agenda-setting or session structure
- ☐ No CBT techniques applied
- ☐ No treatment conceptualization or strategy for change
- ☐ Missed core OCPD pathology (catastrophic thinking, control-seeking, anxiety avoidance)
- ☐ Excessive self-disclosure; therapist-centered rather than patient-centered
- ☐ Minimal collaboration; patient remained passive
- ☐ No guided discovery; directives and reassurance instead of Socratic questioning
- ☐ Vague, unspecific homework without rationale or follow-up
- ☐ No feedback-seeking; no understanding checks
- ☐ Premature reassurance without exploring underlying beliefs

**Clinical Impact:**
Session unlikely to produce therapeutic change. Patient will leave feeling heard but without meaningful tools for addressing core OCPD difficulties. Approach may reinforce maladaptive patterns. Continued use of this approach risks patient discouragement and treatment failure.

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. Establish clear CBT conceptualization and present to patient
2. Implement structured agenda-setting at start of each session
3. Replace personal anecdotes with Socratic questioning
4. Identify and target catastrophic thoughts using cognitive restructuring
5. Design behavioral experiments to test beliefs about control
6. Address anxiety avoidance through graded exposure to relaxation
7. Systematically elicit feedback throughout sessions
8. Establish clear, specific, rationale-based homework
9. Involve Peter in treatment; address relationship impact
10. Provide psychoeducation about OCPD
11. Maintain professional boundaries; reduce self-disclosure
12. Seek intensive CBT training and supervision
