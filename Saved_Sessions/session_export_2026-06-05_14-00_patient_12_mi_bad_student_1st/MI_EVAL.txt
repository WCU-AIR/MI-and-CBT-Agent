Client provides substantial change talk: "I'm about an 8 out of 10 ready to get moving," "I want to feel stronger, more confident, and have better energy," "I definitely want to get moving more." Clinician ignores all of it entirely. Never asks: "What makes you want to feel stronger?" or "Tell me more about how improved energy would change your life?" or "What would it mean to feel more confident?" Fails to ask any open-ended questions designed to elicit client's thinking about barriers, solutions, or success. **Comments:** Client expresses minimal sustain talk; primary dynamic is not resistance but thoughtful prioritization. Client's statements ("Changing everything all at once feels like too much," "Cutting it out completely feels tough") are legitimate concerns about sustainability, not primarily sustain talk. Clinician's response is confrontational rather than skillfully softening. Uses pressure and accusation ("No, you're just making excuses," "You have to stop thinking about barriers and just do it") rather than reflections or reframing. The psychiatrist operates from an authoritarian, expert-driven model that is antithetical to MI spirit and research on effective behavior change.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- Systematic elimination of client autonomy and collaboration
- Complete failure to evoke client's own motivation despite clear change talk
- Confrontational responses to client concerns that increase defensiveness
- Coercive language and pressure tactics throughout
- Misframing of thoughtful prioritization as "making excuses"

**Likely Outcome:** Despite entering with 8/10 readiness and clear change talk, client is now in defensive position, repeatedly asserting own perspective and resisting clinician's demands. Therapeutic relationship damaged. Likelihood of follow-through on health recommendations significantly reduced. Session likely *decreased* intrinsic motivation rather than supporting it.

**Immediate Action Required:** Formal Motivational Interviewing training and supervised practice essential to realign this clinician's approach with evidence-based principles.
