--- DISORDER ---
Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ken Waldron, a 20-year-old white male college sophomore, was referred to the university mental health clinic by his resident advisor (RA) after appearing depressed following a breakup with his girlfriend. Initially hesitant, Ken reported feeling crushed by the breakup, declining academically, and withdrawing from friends and family. He described feeling detached from his body and emotions, experiencing a sense of “no self,” going through daily activities “like a robot,” and wondering if he were dead or living in a dream.

He reported transient episodes of depersonalization and derealization in the past: once in elementary school after his parents’ divorce, and once during a 2-month period of panic attacks in tenth grade triggered by his mother’s psychiatric hospitalization. Neither prior episode met full criteria for depersonalization/derealization disorder.

Ken denied substance use, physical or sexual abuse, and psychiatric history aside from prior brief anxiety and depression. Laboratory testing, brain MRI, EEG, and medical examinations were normal. He maintained reality testing despite persistent detachment, and his social and academic functioning was impaired.

Discussion: Ken’s symptoms are persistent and not explained by medical conditions, substances, or other psychiatric disorders. History and clinical presentation support a diagnosis of depersonalization/derealization disorder, with recurrent and persistent depersonalization symptoms outweighing derealization during the current episode.
