## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

This session demonstrates solid, competent motivational interviewing with particular excellence in **empathic listening** and **collaborative stance**. The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, nonjudgmental acceptance and uses sophisticated reflective listening that captures both content and emotional nuance of Erik's experience. The clinician skillfully evokes Erik's own motivations and past successes rather than imposing solutions, and maintains genuine partnership throughout by asking rather than telling. The progression from exploration of barriers and strengths toward concrete action planning is strategic and client-centered. Affirmations are genuine and build Erik's confidence in his ability to change. Overall, the session embodies the spirit of MI through emphasis on collaboration, evocation, and respect for autonomy.

### Areas for Improvement

**Primary deficiency: Inconsistent cultivation and reinforcement of change talk.** While the clinician identifies and amplifies some change talk (gym attendance progress, back pain improvement, clothes fitting better), there are significant missed opportunities to actively elicit and strengthen importance, confidence, ability, and commitment talk. When Erik rates motivation at 7/10, the clinician doesn't explore what would increase this or what Erik's own confidence level is. Strategic questioning like "What makes it a 7 rather than a 4?" or "How confident do you feel about sticking with three gym days?" would strengthen change talk.

**Secondary deficiency: Inadequate softening of sustain talk.** The clinician validates Erik's barriers but doesn't use strategic MI techniques like reframing, double-sided reflections, or exploration of past successes to pivot sustain talk toward change talk. When Erik mentions difficulty with motivation or self-comparison, the clinician could ask: "What keeps you going even when discouraged?" or "How have you managed similar challenges before?"

**Tertiary opportunity: Deepen exploration of values and meaning.** While the session focuses effectively on practical goals and strategies, there's limited exploration of what health and self-care mean to Erik personally or what becoming healthier would make possible in his relationships and self-image.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

Erik remains engaged and collaborative throughout, suggesting the clinician's strong empathy and partnership are effective. However, inconsistent cultivation of change talk may result in **underutilization of Erik's own motivation for change**. Without explicit elicitation and reinforcement of his confidence, commitment, and reasons for change, opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation are missed. The session ends with Erik proposing concrete next steps, suggesting momentum, but without explicit exploration of his confidence in following through, there's uncertainty about sustained behavior change when barriers arise.

---

## Recommendations for Skill Development

1. **Actively elicit confidence and commitment talk** through strategic questioning after any plan proposal (e.g., "On a scale of 0-10, how confident do you feel about sticking with this?")

2. **Strategically redirect sustain talk** using reframing and double-sided reflections that acknowledge barriers while pivoting toward change talk and past successes

3. **Deepen values exploration** by asking what health means personally and what becoming healthier would make possible in Erik's life

4. **Elicit explicit commitment statements** by asking clients to articulate their commitment in their own words rather than assuming agreement

5. **Explore importance ratings more deeply** by asking "What would increase it?" and "Is this enough for sustainable change?"

6. **Use double-sided reflections** that honor both sustain talk and change talk, then pivot toward change-oriented questions

7. **Ask about self-talk and internal dialogue** to elicit ability talk and help clients recognize their own capacity to manage barriers

8. **Strengthen links between values and concrete behaviors** by explicitly connecting stated values to plan components
