## Summary Assessment

### Critical Findings

**This session demonstrates a fundamental misalignment with Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit.** The psychiatrist exhibits:

- **Zero acceptance or unconditional positive regard** – Pervasive judgment, dismissal, and contempt
- **Complete absence of empathy** – No reflective listening; repeated failure to understand Sofia's perspective
- **Authoritarian expert-driven stance** – Sofia treated as object to be directed, not partner in care
- **Systematic undermining of autonomy** – Coercion, demands, and shame used to pressure compliance
- **Suppression of change talk** – Sofia's emerging motivation is dismissed and attacked
- **Confrontational handling of resistance** – Judgment and threat intensify defensiveness
- **Failure to address actual presenting concern** – Fixation on irrelevant smoking topic; peer conflict never explored

### Impact on Client Engagement

**This session is actively harmful to Sofia's motivation and therapeutic engagement.**

Sofia enters with early contemplation and hints of openness ("Maybe I'd go more if things weren't so messed up," "I want things to get better"). By session end, she has moved into defensive resistance: "It just makes me wanna push back more," "Don't expect me to just jump in like nothing's wrong."

The psychiatrist's approach has:
- Increased Sofia's defensiveness and resistance
- Damaged the therapeutic relationship (Sofia: "It just feels like you're not really listening")
- Reinforced her sense of being blamed and misunderstood (mirroring peer rejection)
- Decreased intrinsic motivation for change
- Failed to address underlying peer conflict and social isolation

**Likelihood of Sofia following through on recommendations or continuing treatment is extremely low.** She will likely disengage from mental health care, viewing it as another authority figure blaming and shaming her rather than understanding her.

## Critical Recommendations for Training

**This session should serve as a teaching example of what NOT to do in MI.** The psychiatrist requires:

1. **Comprehensive MI training** – Complete formal MI training program (MINT-certified)
2. **Ongoing clinical supervision** – Weekly or bi-weekly review of recorded sessions with MI supervisor
3. **Fundamental reorientation** – Shift from expert-driven to collaborative, evocative approach
4. **Development of core MI skills** – Reflective listening, acceptance, empathy, autonomy support, evocation
5. **Self-reflection and accountability** – Regular review of sessions to identify judgmental language and authoritarian patterns
6. **Understanding of adolescent development** – Recognition that peer relationships are developmentally significant; social exclusion is profoundly threatening

**Without significant intervention, this psychiatrist will continue to harm clients through this approach.**
