No explicit affirmation of client's right to decide or language emphasizing choice ("It's entirely up to you," "You get to decide," "What feels right to you?"). No strategic use of open-ended questions designed to evoke change talk (e.g., "Tell me more about what being strong for him means to you?"). Closed or leading questions used instead of evocative open-ended questions. Premature action planning occurs before adequate evoking of motivation, readiness, or client-generated ideas. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.6/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Warm, welcoming opening greeting establishes basic rapport
- Acknowledgment that Amina is experiencing stress and validation that this is understandable
- Attempt to consolidate plan with recap at end
- Recognition of client's core motivation (her son)

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- **Fundamental misalignment with client priorities:** Psychiatrist imposes health habits agenda despite Amina's explicit statement that co-parenting and her son's wellbeing are "what's really important"—critical failure to honor client autonomy
- **Expert-driven, prescriptive stance:** Assumes expert role, generates all solutions, uses prescriptive language throughout—inconsistent with MI's collaborative, client-centered approach
- **Minimal evocation:** While Amina articulates powerful change talk and values, psychiatrist does not draw these out, deepen them, or amplify them
- **Failure to explore ambivalence:** Significant ambivalence and concerns about ability to sustain changes not explored; instead dismissed with quick solutions
- **Lack of genuine collaboration:** Client not invited to contribute ideas or problem-solving; all suggestions from clinician; client positioned as passive recipient
- **Minimal autonomy support:** Client's right to choose not affirmed; plan presented as logical path forward rather than client's decision
- **Weak empathy:** Surface-level reflections do not capture emotional nuance or explore internal experience

### Impact on Client Engagement:
This session is likely to have **negative impact on Amina's engagement and motivation for change**. Misalignment with values, undermined autonomy, unmagnified motivation, dismissed concerns, and ambitious plan without genuine buy-in create high likelihood of non-adherence and potential internalization of failure.

### Recommendation:
Psychiatrist requires training in core MI principles, particularly **evocation, collaboration, and autonomy support**. Session does not embody MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation) and is unlikely to effectively evoke or sustain client's motivation for change.
