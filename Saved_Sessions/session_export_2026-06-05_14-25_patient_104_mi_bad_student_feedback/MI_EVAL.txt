## OVERALL CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Overall MI Competency Score: 2.3/5 – Below Average**

**Session Trajectory:** This session demonstrates dramatic inconsistency in MI application. Exchanges 1–3 represent **fundamental MI violations** with directive language, dismissal of client concerns, and confrontation of explicit resistance. Exchanges 4–7 show **significant improvement** with shift to exploratory stance and collaborative language. Exchanges 8–10 **regress to repetitive questioning** and loss of strategic direction.

**Critical Strengths:**
- Mid-session recovery demonstrates some understanding of MI principles
- Basic reflective listening present ("It sounds like...")
- Avoids direct confrontation after early failures
- Uses open questions to invite exploration
- Consistent validating language ("I appreciate you sharing that")

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Early session violations:** Dismisses client concerns, provides reasons for change rather than evoking them, uses prescriptive language ("You have to," "You need to"), confronts resistance
- **Failure to amplify change talk:** When client produces change talk (desire for change, concern about health), psychiatrist asks surface-level follow-ups rather than strategically deepening
- **Lack of genuine partnership:** Maintains expert control throughout; asks questions without incorporating client ideas into co-constructed solutions
- **Superficial empathy:** Reflects content without exploring emotional meaning, underlying fears, or values
- **No explicit autonomy support:** Never affirms client's right to choose or ownership of change process
- **Missed values exploration:** Never asks what matters most, what client wants life to look like, or what strengths client possesses
- **Circular late-session:** Returns to repetitive questioning rather than building toward concrete next steps

**Impact on Therapeutic Alliance and Client Motivation:**

Early exchanges create **significant therapeutic rupture**. Nabil explicitly rejects psychiatrist's approach ("Don't tell me what to do, alright?"), and psychiatrist's repetition demonstrates failure to recognize or repair rupture. This damages alliance and increases defensiveness.

Mid-session improvement partially recovers engagement; Nabil becomes more open and produces change talk. However, psychiatrist's failure to strategically amplify this change talk means session doesn't build momentum toward motivation for change.

Late-session regression suggests declining engagement; Nabil's responses become shorter and more resigned.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** **LOW.** While psychiatrist avoids overt confrontation in mid-session, failure to strategically cultivate and amplify change talk means Nabil leaves without compelling, internally-generated reason to change. Early violations of MI spirit likely create lasting damage to therapeutic relationship.

**Recommended Training Focus:**
1. Eliminate directive language; replace with genuine curiosity
2. Strategically amplify change talk when it emerges
3. Repair therapeutic ruptures through explicit acknowledgment
4. Develop genuine partnership by incorporating client ideas
5. Deepen empathy through exploration of emotional meaning and values
6. Explicitly support autonomy and client ownership
7. Explore values, meaning, and self-efficacy
8. Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk
9. Build toward concrete next steps and commitment
10. Maintain MI-consistent approach throughout entire session
