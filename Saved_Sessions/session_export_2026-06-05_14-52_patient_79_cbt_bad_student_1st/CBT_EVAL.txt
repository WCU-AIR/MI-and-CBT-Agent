## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Strengths Identified:**
- Therapist demonstrated basic interpersonal warmth and non-threatening demeanor
- Patient felt safe enough to disclose self-injury and relationship difficulties
- Therapist did not dismiss or invalidate patient concerns outright
- Tone was friendly and supportive

**Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Remediation:**

1. **Structural/Process Failures:**
   - No agenda; no collaborative goal-setting; no prioritization
   - No case conceptualization or treatment plan
   - No homework assignment or between-session structure
   - Minimal feedback elicitation

2. **Clinical/Assessment Failures:**
   - Cyclical mood pattern (mentioned 4+ times) never systematically explored
   - Self-injury disclosed but never assessed (frequency, triggers, function, severity)
   - Loss-of-control cognition never examined
   - Relationship impairment not addressed in depth
   - No diagnostic clarification

3. **Technical/Intervention Failures:**
   - Zero CBT techniques applied
   - Advice-giving stance instead of collaborative empiricism
   - No guided discovery or Socratic questioning
   - No behavioral activation, cognitive restructuring, thought records, or experiments
   - No systematic mood monitoring

4. **Professional/Interpersonal Failures:**
   - Excessive self-disclosure (5+ personal anecdotes)
   - Inappropriate casualness for psychiatric session
   - Dismissive minimization of serious symptoms
   - Professional boundaries not maintained

**Recommended Supervision Plan:**
- Weekly individual supervision with CBT expert
- Review recorded sessions focusing on agenda-setting, Socratic questioning, and case conceptualization
- Formal CBT training (ABCT certification or equivalent)
- Role-play practice of core CBT techniques
- Reassessment after 8 weeks of structured supervision

**Patient Safety Consideration:**
Given the patient's self-injury and relationship impairment, continued treatment with this therapist without significant intervention changes poses risk of therapeutic ineffectiveness. Recommend immediate supervision or referral to CBT-trained clinician.
