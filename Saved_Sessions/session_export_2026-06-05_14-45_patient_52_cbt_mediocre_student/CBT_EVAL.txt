## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

**Patient:** Isaac (pseudonym), 42-year-old Black male surgeon  
**Presenting Problems:** Major depressive disorder, prolonged grief disorder (grandmother's death 18 months ago), guilt, exhaustion, concentration difficulties, work impairment, social withdrawal  
**Sessions Evaluated:** Two consecutive sessions

### Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Therapist maintained warm, non-judgmental tone
- Patient felt safe enough to disclose sensitive information
- No hostile or demeaning language
- Appropriate professional boundaries

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **No collaborative agenda:** Generic openings without prioritization or time allocation
- **Minimal feedback elicitation:** Only two superficial check-ins; no homework review in Session 2
- **Superficial understanding:** Grasped explicit content but missed critical cognitive, behavioral, and emotional patterns
- **Absence of guided discovery:** Relied on direct advice and reassurance rather than Socratic questioning
- **No case conceptualization:** Did not articulate coherent understanding of maintaining factors or treatment strategy
- **Poor technique application:** CBT techniques mentioned but applied with significant flaws; no in-session practice
- **Contradictory interventions:** Suggested both distraction and acceptance without recognizing contradiction
- **Critical homework failures:** Vague assignments with weak rationale; no review in Session 2 despite worsening symptoms

### Impact on Patient Care

The patient is experiencing serious mental health conditions requiring evidence-based treatment. Current approach is unlikely to produce meaningful improvement because:

1. **Reinforcement of avoidance:** Distraction suggestions reinforce existing avoidance coping, maintaining depression and preventing grief processing
2. **Inadequate behavioral activation:** No structured, values-based approach to re-engagement
3. **No grief processing:** Missing exposure to grief-related memories and emotions
4. **Weak homework:** Vague assignments unlikely to produce change; no follow-up
5. **Risk of dropout:** Patient already reluctant to engage; if therapy feels ineffective, may disengage further

### Recommendations

**Immediate Priority:**
1. Establish collaborative, structured agenda with clear prioritization
2. Develop explicit case conceptualization and communicate to patient
3. Implement systematic homework review at start of each session
4. Shift from direct advice to guided discovery using Socratic questioning

**Clinical Priority:**
5. Identify and address core beliefs about responsibility driving guilt
6. Recognize and address avoidance as maintaining factor
7. Implement values-based behavioral activation (not just distraction)
8. Facilitate grief processing through exposure and memory work

**Ongoing:**
9. Explore and validate anger as grief response
10. Address stigma concerns affecting engagement
11. Establish clear, measurable treatment goals
12. Provide psychoeducation about depression and prolonged grief

**Supervision Recommendation:** Therapist should seek consultation before continuing with this patient. Without significant improvements in CBT competence and technique, therapeutic outcomes are unlikely and patient may deteriorate.
