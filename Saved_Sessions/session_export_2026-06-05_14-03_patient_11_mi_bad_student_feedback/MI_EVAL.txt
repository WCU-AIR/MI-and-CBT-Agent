### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Relies on prescription and imposition of motivation rather than drawing out client's own reasons for change. Tells rather than asks throughout session. When client offers own motivation ("I want to avoid health problems," "I want to do better, I really do"), psychiatrist does not amplify or develop this change talk. Instead pivots back to pressure and prescription. Never asks evocative questions such as: "What would it mean for your health if you made some changes?" or "What matters most to you about being healthy?" Only final question demonstrates genuine evocation: "What's one small step you think you could try this week?" Client responds with own realistic idea.

## Summary of Findings

### Strengths
- **Maintains focus on target behavior:** Psychiatrist consistently addresses dietary change throughout session, demonstrating clear direction.
- **Demonstrates capacity for change:** Final exchange represents significant shift toward MI principles. When psychiatrist asks "What's one small step you think you could try this week?" and genuinely listens, client immediately generates realistic, sustainable goal (swapping one fast food meal weekly).
- **Shows adaptability:** Shift from demanding complete elimination to supporting incremental change demonstrates capacity to adjust when initial approach is not working.

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Violates acceptance and empathy:** Labels legitimate barriers as "excuses," uses demeaning language ("It's not rocket science"), fails to communicate unconditional positive regard or genuine understanding.
- **Imposes direction and undermines autonomy:** Prescribes specific behaviors, uses fear-based messaging, explicitly states compliance is "not optional."
- **Assumes expert role:** Positions self as sole authority; dismisses client's expertise about her own life and constraints.
- **Relies on prescription rather than evocation:** Tells client what to do rather than asking questions to draw out own motivations, values, and ideas.
- **Fails to amplify change talk:** Does not strategically respond to client's expressed motivations; redirects to pressure when client offers change talk.
- **Uses confrontation for sustain talk:** Rather than skillfully softening resistance through double-sided reflections and reframing, psychiatrist argues against client's concerns.

### Impact on Client Engagement
Session is likely to decrease intrinsic motivation and increase resistance through confrontational, authoritarian approach. However, final exchange demonstrates different trajectory: when psychiatrist shifts to MI stance, client immediately engages authentically and generates own realistic goal. This demonstrates client is capable of self-directed change when given autonomy and genuine collaboration.

---

## Key Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Adopt collaborative partnership stance from outset** – Begin with genuine curiosity about client's perspective rather than prescription
2. **Demonstrate genuine empathy through reflective listening** – Respond with reflections demonstrating understanding before offering suggestions
3. **Validate legitimate barriers** – Recognize contextual obstacles (childcare, time, fatigue) as real constraints, not character flaws
4. **Evoke rather than prescribe** – Ask questions designed to draw out client's own motivations, values, and resources
5. **Support autonomy explicitly** – Communicate that change is client's choice; avoid pressuring language
6. **Amplify change talk strategically** – Deepen and strengthen client's expressions of desire, ability, and commitment to change
7. **Use double-sided reflections** – Soften sustain talk without confrontation by validating both sides of ambivalence
8. **Shift to incremental change** – Reframe large, overwhelming goals toward small, manageable steps (as successfully demonstrated at session end)
9. **Eliminate fear-based messaging** – Replace consequences-focused language with values-based exploration
10. **Model the final exchange throughout** – The successful end-of-session approach ("What's one small step?") should be the template for entire session from beginning

---

**Overall Assessment:** Session demonstrates fundamental misalignment with MI principles but shows critical learning potential. Psychiatrist's capacity to shift at session end suggests these improvements are achievable. Key is adopting collaborative, evocative stance demonstrated in final exchange and maintaining it throughout entire session from beginning.
