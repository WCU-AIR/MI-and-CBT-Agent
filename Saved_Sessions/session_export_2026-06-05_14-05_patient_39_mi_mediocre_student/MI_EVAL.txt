## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5**

**Session Assessment:** This session demonstrates fundamental deficiencies in Motivational Interviewing practice. The psychiatrist assumes an expert, directive stance throughout, making unilateral decisions about problems, goals, and solutions. Rather than evoking Elena's intrinsic motivation through collaborative exploration and strategic questioning, the clinician prescribes change and assesses compliance. Elena's legitimate concerns about bodily autonomy, relational power, and overwhelm are treated as obstacles to overcome rather than valid perspectives to understand and explore.

**Strengths Identified:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (contraceptive consistency)
- Avoids overt judgment or shaming language
- Offers concrete, actionable suggestions (carrying condoms, setting reminders)
- Acknowledges Elena's ambivalence without aggressive pushing
- Establishes follow-up mechanism for continued support

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Shift from expert to collaborative stance** (HIGHEST PRIORITY): Recognize Elena as expert on her own life; reframe clinician role from problem-solver to collaborative partner
2. **Evoke rather than tell**: Deepen Elena's spontaneous change talk through strategic questioning; develop her own reasons for change
3. **Validate and explore concerns**: Treat Elena's fears about bodily autonomy, relational awkwardness, and overwhelm as legitimate values to understand, not obstacles to overcome
4. **Replace prescriptive language**: Eliminate "should" language; use autonomy-supportive language emphasizing choice
5. **Normalize and explore ambivalence**: Help Elena develop her own resolution of ambivalence rather than rushing past it
6. **Develop genuine empathy**: Deepen understanding of Elena's relational and bodily autonomy concerns
7. **Use strategic questioning**: Evoke Elena's own commitment, ability, reasons, and meaning around contraceptive use
8. **Establish collaborative goals**: Genuinely invite Elena to set agenda; build session around her priorities, not clinician's predetermined goals
9. **Develop reflective listening**: Move beyond factual acknowledgment to emotional empathy capturing feeling and values
10. **Assess confidence collaboratively**: Explore barriers and solutions with Elena rather than offering suggestions

**Likelihood of Sustained Behavior Change:** Low. Elena has agreed to carry condoms and set reminders, but this represents compliance with clinician's suggestions, not autonomous decision-making. Elena's confidence is only 5/10; underlying concerns remain unaddressed; ambivalence unexplored. Directive stance risks strengthening resistance through perceived lack of understanding and respect for autonomy.

**Whether Session Embodies MI Spirit:** No. This session violates fundamental MI spirit. Clinician assumes expert role, makes unilateral decisions, and prescribes change rather than evoking it. True MI requires genuine partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation—none consistently demonstrated. Session would benefit from fundamental reorientation toward collaborative, client-centered practice respecting Elena's autonomy and drawing out intrinsic motivation for change.
