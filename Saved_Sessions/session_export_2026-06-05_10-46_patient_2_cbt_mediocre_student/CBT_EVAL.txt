## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### Strengths:
- **Interpersonal Effectiveness (5/6):** Warm, genuine, professional tone with appropriate boundaries and accessible language. Good rapport established.
- **Understanding (4/6):** Adequate basic listening, empathy, and reflection of emotional complexity.
- **Pacing (4/6):** Reasonable session flow and time management without excessive tangents.
- **Patient Engagement:** Franklin is cooperative and motivated, suggesting adequate therapeutic alliance.

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Agenda-Setting (2/6):** Vague, non-specific openings with no explicit prioritization, time allocation, or collaborative goal-setting. Identical opening in both sessions indicates no planning.
- **Feedback Elicitation (1/6):** Almost no systematic checking for understanding or patient satisfaction. **Critical failure:** No homework review in second session.
- **Collaboration (3/6):** Pseudo-collaboration with therapist-directed advice-giving rather than true collaborative empiricism. Franklin positioned as passive recipient.
- **Guided Discovery (3/6):** Asks some Socratic questions but frequently abandons them in favor of prescriptive advice. Reduces patient agency.
- **Focusing on Key Cognitions/Behaviors (3/6):** Identifies some relevant thoughts/behaviors but misses core issues (breakup, cultural context, sleep deprivation). Scattered rather than strategic focus.
- **Strategy for Change (2/6):** No explicit, coherent, individualized strategy. Reactive rather than strategic. Ignores maintaining factors and cultural context.
- **Application of Techniques (3/6):** Techniques applied with moderate skill but lack depth, precision, and systematic implementation. Many important techniques absent.
- **Homework (2/6):** Vague assignments without clear rationale or monitoring. **Critical failure:** No homework review in second session.

### Clinical Impact:
**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change: MODERATE**

Franklin has good prognostic factors (young, motivated, good insight, supportive network) but CBT delivery quality is compromised. With current approach, improvement will be slower and less robust. With recommended changes, significant improvement could be achieved in 6-8 weeks.

### Competency Classification:
**Does NOT meet evidence-based CBT competency threshold.** Sessions are better characterized as "supportive counseling with CBT elements" rather than systematic CBT. Therapist has foundational skills but requires focused training on CBT-specific competencies.

### Immediate Priority Actions:
1. **Establish structured, collaborative agenda-setting** at session start with explicit prioritization and time allocation
2. **Implement systematic homework review** at beginning of every session (after first)
3. **Develop explicit, collaboratively-agreed treatment strategy** addressing core stressors (breakup, financial stress, cultural context, sleep deprivation)
4. **Move from advice-giving to guided discovery** through Socratic questioning
5. **Replace vague homework with specific, structured assignments** including rationale, monitoring forms, and troubleshooting

---

**Evaluator Recommendation:** Therapist demonstrates foundational counseling skills and good rapport-building ability. With targeted supervision focused on CBT-specific competencies (agenda-setting, homework review, guided discovery, strategy development, technique application), this therapist has strong potential to become an effective evidence-based CBT practitioner. Recommend: (1) weekly supervision with CBT focus, (2) review of recorded sessions, (3) deliberate practice on Socratic questioning and guided discovery, (4) structured homework protocol development, (5) treatment planning template implementation.
