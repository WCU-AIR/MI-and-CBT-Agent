### Empathy

Identifies functional aspects of smoking ("serves as a meaningful way for you to relax and unwind"). Sophisticated understanding of tension between family influence and autonomy. Areas for deepening: Could explore emotional experience of cravings and stress more deeply; could more directly validate frustration about external workplace policy constraint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on smoking but lacks strategic clarity about specific change goal (cessation vs. harm reduction vs. exploration). Session meanders through multiple topics without explicit clarification of what "bigger changes" means. External motivator (workplace policy) is mentioned early but largely abandoned as a focus. Direction would benefit from strategic linking of workplace constraint to internal motivation and clearer goal definition.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

Explicitly asks permission before offering information. Respects client's preferences and conditional openness. One opportunity: Resource exploration presented somewhat didactically as clinician-generated menu rather than co-constructed with client's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout session. Consistently reinforces client's right to choose and self-direction. Language is non-prescriptive; explicitly affirms that change must be authentic and self-driven. Accepts conditional openness without pressure ("I'm not promising I'll use everything"). Frames patches as client's emerging preference rather than clinician recommendation. Communicates respect for client's timeline and choice consistently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. While some change talk emerges spontaneously ("Sure, I can quit," "I've got some willpower"), it is rarely strategically amplified or deepened. Minimal systematic evocation of DARN-CAT (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Commitment, Taking steps, Activation). Missed opportunities to explore: intrinsic reasons for change, values around family and health, past success in detail, self-efficacy, and what could shift readiness. Strategic questioning designed to elicit change talk is largely absent.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership demonstrated with consistent power-sharing and collaborative language. Seeks permission, values client's perspective, and positions client as authority on own experience. Respects client's role as decision-maker. One opportunity: Could more fully partner on resource exploration by eliciting client's own ideas first rather than presenting clinician-generated menu. Could collaborate more explicitly on problem-solving around implementation barriers.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Critical gap in MI practice. Minimal strategic cultivation despite multiple opportunities. When change talk emerges (e.g., "Sure, I can quit"), clinician acknowledges but doesn't follow up to deepen. Past success mentioned but not systematically explored to build confidence. Lacks targeted open questions designed to evoke specific change talk types. No systematic exploration of: benefits of quitting, personal reasons for change, concerns about smoking, or what would increase readiness. This significantly limits motivation-building effectiveness.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent skill. Handles some sustain talk skillfully through reflective listening and validation (e.g., "You enjoy smoking...makes the idea of giving it up feel pretty distant"). Prevents reactance by validating preferences. Missed opportunities: Could use strategic double-sided reflections to highlight tension between status quo and reasons for change. Could leverage workplace policy and family values more strategically to redirect toward change without confrontation. Could ask "What would need to shift?" rather than simply normalizing middle ground.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.6/5 – Adequate but Inconsistent MI Practice**

### Strengths:
- ✓ Exemplary autonomy support with consistent reinforcement of client choice
- ✓ Strong acceptance and unconditional positive regard throughout
- ✓ Good collaborative stance with permission-seeking and respect for preferences
- ✓ Solid reflective listening capturing ambivalence and competing values
- ✓ Appropriate pacing respecting client's readiness level
- ✓ Warm, non-judgmental therapeutic environment

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ **Minimal strategic evocation of change talk** – spontaneous change talk rarely amplified or deepened
- ✗ **Insufficient exploration of intrinsic motivation** – limited inquiry into client's own reasons for change and core values
- ✗ **Underutilization of external motivators** – workplace policy mentioned but not strategically linked to internal motivation
- ✗ **Lack of systematic self-efficacy building** – past success acknowledged but not deeply explored
- ✗ **Diffuse direction** – no clear clarification of whether goal is cessation, harm reduction, or exploration
- ✗ **Limited deepening of ambivalence** – normalization without exploration of what could shift balance toward change

### Clinical Impact:
This session represents **supportive counseling with MI elements** rather than **strategic motivational interviewing**. While the psychiatrist creates a respectful, safe environment where the client feels heard, the session falls short of exemplary MI practice by failing to systematically draw out and amplify intrinsic motivation. Client leaves with tentative plan to try patches but without deep exploration of *why* he wants to change or connection to core values. Readiness remains at 3-4 with minimal exploration of what could shift it upward. Without stronger internal motivation, adherence to patches is at moderate risk.

### Recommendation for Development:
Clinician demonstrates strong foundational MI skills. Next level of development: **Add strategic questioning and systematic change talk cultivation** to existing foundation. Focus on recognizing change talk when it emerges and consistently following up with open questions that deepen and amplify it. This single skill—strategic amplification of change talk—would significantly increase MI intervention effectiveness.
