## **OVERALL CLINICAL SUMMARY**

### **Overall Session Score: 1.0/5**

**Strengths:**
- Maintains topical focus on smoking and nicotine replacement therapy without drift
- Gathers basic factual information about smoking frequency and past quit attempts

**Critical Deficiencies:**
This session represents a **systematic failure across all nine MI domains**. The psychiatrist operates from a medical model of authority and prescription rather than an MI model of partnership and evocation.

**Key Problems:**
1. **Authoritarian stance:** Opens with "I'm going to tell you exactly what you should do"—opposite of MI's collaborative approach
2. **Confrontation and judgment:** Uses shaming language ("Are you just making excuses?"), moral judgment ("You have to take this seriously"), triggering defensiveness and psychological reactance
3. **Violation of autonomy:** Patient explicitly states need for autonomy; psychiatrist responds by removing choice entirely through unilateral prescription
4. **Failure to evoke:** Tells rather than asks; no open questions to explore patient's motivations, values, concerns, or resources
5. **Missed opportunities:** Patient acknowledges marital conflict, shows insight, expresses openness—none explored or amplified
6. **Argument against sustain talk:** Directly argues against patient's reasons for not changing, strengthening resistance
7. **No reflective listening:** Virtually absent—fundamental MI skill completely missing
8. **Ignores psychological reactance:** Patient explicitly states pressure makes him more resistant; psychiatrist responds by increasing pressure

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is **likely to be counterproductive**. Patient enters with precontemplation-stage ambivalence—an opening for MI to help develop intrinsic motivation. Instead, psychiatrist's approach triggers psychological reactance. Patient becomes more defensive, more entrenched, more resistant. By session end, he explicitly refuses treatment: "I'm not ready to start those therapies today. Maybe down the road, but not now."

Patient explicitly states coercive approach is counterproductive: "I don't like feeling rushed or pushed around... forcing me into patches or gum today just makes me more resistant." Psychiatrist's response is to increase pressure—exact opposite of what would help.

Unilateral prescription at end will likely result in poor adherence and poor outcomes. Patient will likely disengage from future treatment and tell wife: "The psychiatrist just told me what to do and didn't listen to me."

**Recommendation:**
**Formal MI training is essential.** This clinician must fundamentally shift approach from authoritarian prescription to collaborative partnership. Without this change, this clinician will continue to harm patients by decreasing intrinsic motivation for change and damaging therapeutic relationships.
