### Evocation

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Multiple instances of client change talk completely missed or minimized. Client offers: "I do want to feel better and live longer," "I want to get healthier," "I want to do better," "I'm thinking hard about making changes soon," "I know I need to do something eventually." Skilled MI practitioner would recognize these as precious moments of intrinsic motivation and amplify through reflections and follow-up questions. Instead, psychiatrist ignores or minimizes these statements and continues with directives. When client says "I want to get there, I do," psychiatrist responds with demand for commitment rather than exploring what "getting there" means. No open-ended questions designed to evoke additional change talk. Critical failure in MI technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Poor handling of sustain talk through confrontation rather than skillful softening. Client expresses sustain talk: "It's part of my social life," "It's like instant friends," "It feels like losing part of my social life." Psychiatrist responds with direct confrontation ("You're just making excesses," "Socializing is no reason to keep smoking," "It's just a bad habit you're clinging to") rather than skillful redirection. No double-sided reflections acknowledging both client's concerns about change and status quo. No reframing of client's concerns. Repeated pressure likely triggers psychological reactance, strengthening rather than softening client's resistance. Client's responses become increasingly defensive as session progresses, suggesting confrontational approach is backfiring.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 1.1/5**

### Summary of Findings

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, confrontational practice that is antithetical to MI spirit and research-based principles of behavior change.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Pervasive judgment and rejection of client's perspective
- Complete absence of empathic listening (zero reflective statements)
- Suppression of client autonomy through controlling language
- Missed opportunities to amplify client change talk
- Confrontational handling of sustain talk triggering defensive reactance
- Absence of genuine collaboration and partnership

**Strengths (Limited):**
- Clear focus on target behaviors (smoking cessation, exercise)
- Explicit communication of health risks

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is likely to be **counterproductive**. Client enters with appropriate ambivalence and genuine consideration of change. Psychiatrist's confrontational, judgmental approach triggers defensive reactance, entrenches resistance, and reduces intrinsic motivation. By final exchange, client has not moved closer to commitment; she has become more entrenched in ambivalence and more defensive about autonomy. Likelihood of sustainable behavior change is low.

**Critical Recommendation:**
This clinician requires **comprehensive retraining in MI principles and practice**. Current approach is not only ineffective but potentially harmful to therapeutic relationship and client motivation. Immediate, intensive training and supervision in MI is essential. Without intervention, this clinician's practice will continue to produce poor outcomes and may actively harm clients by triggering defensive reactance and reducing intrinsic motivation.

**This session does NOT embody MI spirit.**
