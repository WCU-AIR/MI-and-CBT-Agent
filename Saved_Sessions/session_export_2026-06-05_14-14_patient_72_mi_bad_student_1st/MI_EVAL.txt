## Overall Session Score: 1.1/5

**Summary Assessment:**

This session represents a near-complete failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, judgmental, and coercive clinical practice fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit and evidence-based approaches to behavior change. Rather than evoking Laura's intrinsic motivation, the psychiatrist suppresses it through dismissal, shame, and threat. Rather than supporting autonomy, the psychiatrist undermines it through directives and coercion. Rather than building partnership, the psychiatrist establishes hierarchy and control.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- No acceptance or unconditional positive regard
- No empathic understanding of client perspective
- No collaboration or partnership
- No autonomy support
- No evocation of client's own motivations
- Escalating confrontation when resistance emerges
- Suppression of change talk
- Intensification of sustain talk

**Likely Outcomes:**
- Decreased intrinsic motivation
- Increased resistance and refusal
- Damaged therapeutic relationship
- Reduced likelihood of behavior change
- Potential disengagement from care

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist requires fundamental retraining in MI principles through formal training (MINT) and regular clinical supervision with MI-focused review of session recordings. Without retraining, psychiatrist is likely to harm rather than help clients struggling with behavior change.
