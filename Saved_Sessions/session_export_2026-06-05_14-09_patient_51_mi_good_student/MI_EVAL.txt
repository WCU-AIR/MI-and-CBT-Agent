### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing and client-centered agenda-setting. The opening question immediately establishes partnership and invites Hana to set the agenda. Consistent use of open questions ("What do you think...?" "What feels doable for you?") invites co-construction of understanding and solutions. However, there are subtle moments where the clinician edges toward expert stance. ### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects and affirms Hana's autonomy throughout the session, avoiding prescriptive language and emphasizing choice and client ownership. Key examples include inviting choice about session focus, asking what would make the biggest difference for her, and explicitly offering choice about whether to involve support ("with or without support"). The clinician frames the plan as Hana's creation, emphasizing her ownership. However, autonomy support could be more explicitly stated. When asking "How confident do you feel about following through with this plan?" the psychiatrist subtly assumes she will follow through. A more autonomy-supportive framing would explicitly acknowledge her right to choose differently. The clinician could more directly state: "This is your decision, and whatever you choose is okay."

### Evocation

**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong evocation, consistently drawing out Hana's own motivations, values, and resources rather than imposing them. Hana's responses reveal rich change talk successfully elicited: "weight lifted off my shoulders," "Maybe I really can do things I'm scared of," "I'm ready to try that." ### Partnership

**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a genuinely collaborative, equal partnership stance with active power-sharing and positioning of Hana as the expert on her own experience. The clinician consistently asks "What do you think...?" rather than telling, acknowledges Hana's insights ("You're recognizing how complex this is"), and frames the plan as her creation. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid skill in evoking and reinforcing Hana's change talk. Strategic questions elicit desire talk ("weight lifted off my shoulders," "relief is possible"), ability talk ("Maybe I really can do things I'm scared of"), reasons talk ("constant worry drains me"), and commitment talk ("I'm ready to try that"). The clinician reinforces change talk through reflections that amplify and strengthen it. However, there are missed opportunities to more strategically cultivate change talk. When Hana expresses doubt ("I'm not sure if I can be that kind to myself yet"), the psychiatrist reflects but doesn't strategically redirect toward her ability to develop self-compassion. Additionally, when confidence moves from 0 to 3, the psychiatrist doesn't explicitly reinforce the significance of this progress, which would strengthen commitment talk. The cultivation is consistent but could be more strategically amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** This is a notable weakness in the session. Sustain talk emerges multiple times ("I still have a ton of anxiety," "I'm not sure if I can be that kind to myself yet," "It's hard because there's so much pressure to get it perfect," "I'm still nervous"), and the psychiatrist's handling is inconsistent. When Hana expresses sustain talk, the clinician validates without strategically redirecting. For example, when she says "I still have a ton of anxiety," the psychiatrist reflects "It's understandable that the weight of anxiety still feels overwhelming," which essentially agrees with and reinforces the sustain talk rather than softening it. When perfectionism emerges, the clinician doesn't reframe it as a strength that could be redirected. Most critically, the core fear of failure and embarrassment is never directly addressed or explored—the psychiatrist could have asked deeper questions about what failure would mean and how avoidance guarantees the feared outcome. Double-sided reflections and strategic reframing are underutilized. Sustain talk is validated but not strategically softened.

## Summary of Strengths and Areas for Improvement

### **Strengths:**
- ✓ **Exceptional empathy** with consistently accurate, complex reflections capturing emotional nuance
- ✓ **Strong collaboration** with genuine power-sharing and client-centered agenda-setting
- ✓ **Solid evocation** of client's own motivations, resources, and wisdom
- ✓ **Consistent acceptance** with nonjudgmental stance and validation
- ✓ **Good autonomy support** emphasizing client choice and ownership
- ✓ **Effective change talk cultivation** with multiple instances of evoking desire, ability, reasons, and commitment
- ✓ **Clear direction** with progressive sequencing from exploration to planning
- ✓ **No confrontation** or pressure; clinician maintains respectful stance throughout

### **Areas for Improvement:**
- ⚠ **Sustain talk management** – Sustain talk is validated but not strategically softened; missed opportunities to reframe barriers or amplify discrepancies
- ⚠ **Core fear exploration** – The fundamental fear of failure and embarrassment is never directly addressed or explored
- ⚠ **Self-compassion building** – When perfectionism and self-criticism emerge, clinician reflects but doesn't actively challenge or build alternative narratives
- ⚠ **Strategic amplification of progress** – Clinician doesn't explicitly acknowledge movement from 0 to 3 confidence or other incremental progress
- ⚠ **Explicit autonomy affirmation** – While respected, client's right to choose (including choosing not to proceed) could be more explicitly stated
- ⚠ **Depth of evocation** – Could more strategically evoke client's own beliefs about resilience, capability, and learning from mistakes
- ⚠ **Follow-up planning** – Session ends without explicit discussion of how to handle setbacks or when to reconnect

---

## Clinical Recommendations

1. **Strategically soften sustain talk** by using double-sided reflections and reframing barriers as redirectable strengths
2. **Directly explore core fears** about failure and embarrassment; help client see the paradox that avoidance guarantees the feared outcome
3. **Build self-compassion as an explicit change goal** by linking it to her past success (shifting from "perfect" to "good enough")
4. **Amplify progress explicitly** by acknowledging movement from 0 to 3 confidence and asking what helped her get there
5. **Deepen evocation of resilience** by asking what she learned from past successes and how that applies now
6. **Use explicit autonomy statements** such as "This is your decision, and whatever you choose is okay"
7. **Establish clear follow-up planning** including when to reconnect, what to review, and what to do if anxiety overwhelms her

---

**Overall Competency Level:** Solid, competent MI practice with particular strength in empathy and collaboration. To move from "good" (4/5) to "excellent" (5/5), focus on developing more sophisticated skills in managing sustain talk, deepening exploration of core fears, and building concrete change goals around self-compassion and anxiety management.
