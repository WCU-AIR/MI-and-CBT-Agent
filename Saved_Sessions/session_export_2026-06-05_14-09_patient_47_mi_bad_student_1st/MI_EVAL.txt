## Overall Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.2/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on target behaviors (alcohol use and exercise)
- Correctly identifies relevant health risks (cardiovascular disease, diabetes)
- Does not actively reinforce sustain talk

### Critical Deficiencies:
This session represents a **fundamental failure in Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no MI competence across all nine domains. The session is characterized by:

- **Authoritarian stance** violating collaborative MI spirit
- **Complete absence of reflective listening** (zero reflective statements)
- **Dismissal of genuine barriers** labeled as "excuses"
- **Failure to evoke or amplify change talk** despite multiple opportunities
- **Coercive pressure tactics** using fear and ultimatums
- **Active rejection of collaboration** when explicitly requested by patient
- **Elimination of patient autonomy** through controlling language

### Impact on Client Engagement:
This session is likely to **decrease** motivation for change and **increase resistance**. Patient's responses show increasing defensiveness and explicit rejection of psychiatrist's approach ("just telling me to quit right now like that doesn't really work for me"). Research demonstrates confrontational, expert-driven approaches trigger psychological reactance and reduce intrinsic motivation. This session demonstrates how **not** to conduct MI and will likely harm therapeutic relationship and reduce likelihood of behavior change.

### Competency Assessment:
**This psychiatrist is NOT competent in Motivational Interviewing.** The approach is more consistent with traditional directive medical counseling than evidence-based MI. Comprehensive MI training and supervised practice required before continuing MI-based sessions.
