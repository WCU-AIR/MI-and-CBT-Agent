## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Session Embodies MI Spirit:** ☐ Yes  ☒ No

**Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist operates from a confrontational, expert-driven, authoritarian model that is **directly contraindicated in MI** and likely to **harm the therapeutic relationship and reduce intrinsic motivation**.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance, empathy, and unconditional positive regard
- Systematic undermining of patient autonomy through coercion and control
- Failure to evoke and amplify substantial intrinsic motivation present at session onset
- Confrontational stance creating defensiveness and psychological reactance
- Escalating demands that undermine patient's concrete commitments
- Lack of collaborative problem-solving and exploration of barriers
- Dismissal of patient's self-knowledge and emotional experience

**Likelihood of Evoking Patient Motivation for Change:** Very Low

Patient enters with clear intrinsic motivation ("I really want to start working out again," "I'm sick of feeling the way I do"). Psychiatrist's confrontational, controlling approach is likely to:
- Trigger psychological reactance (evident in patient's repeated defensive statements)
- Convert intrinsic motivation into external pressure, reducing long-term adherence
- Damage therapeutic alliance (patient's language becomes increasingly withdrawn)
- Reduce likelihood of behavior change (research demonstrates confrontational approaches are less effective)
- Increase dropout risk

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Fundamental shift from expert-driven to collaborative, evocative stance
2. Develop genuine curiosity about patient's perspective without judgment
3. Amplify patient's intrinsic motivation through reflective listening
4. Explore barriers collaboratively rather than confronting
5. Support autonomy by respecting patient's readiness and pace
6. Seek formal MI training and clinical supervision
7. Examine personal beliefs about motivation and behavior change

**Critical Note:** This session demonstrates why MI training is essential. While psychiatrist's intentions are positive, approach is counterproductive and potentially harmful. Immediate training in MI principles and spirit is **strongly recommended**.
