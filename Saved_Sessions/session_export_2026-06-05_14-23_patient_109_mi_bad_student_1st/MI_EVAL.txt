# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist uses explicit character attacks ("weak," "quit feeling sorry for yourself") and maintains punitive, shaming tone throughout. No affirmation of client strengths or acknowledgment of normal human struggle with behavior change. Communicates contempt rather than respect for client's inherent worth and dignity.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening demonstrated. When client explains barriers ("life gets busy," need for collaborative approach), psychiatrist responds with dismissal ("No, it doesn't make sense to me") rather than reflection. No attempt to understand client's emotional experience, ambivalence, or underlying values. Zero complex reflections; no exploration of what health means to client or what has prevented sustained change in past.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on health behavior change (alcohol reduction, exercise three times weekly), but targets are imposed rather than negotiated. No exploration of why these specific targets or whether they align with client's values, schedule, or readiness. Direction achieved through coercion rather than collaborative guidance, reducing likelihood of genuine intrinsic commitment to change.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Explicitly hierarchical, expert-driven stance from opening ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do"). When client attempts collaboration ("Maybe we can talk about what actually fits into my life"), psychiatrist responds: "No, you don't get to set the terms here." No shared decision-making; psychiatrist makes all decisions unilaterally and demands compliance. Client's self-knowledge is ignored entirely.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematic elimination of client choice through coercive language ("This isn't optional," "That's non-negotiable," "you don't get to set the terms here"). Client's attempts to assert autonomy are overridden and framed as weakness. Fear and threat used to coerce compliance ("your life depends on it," "If you slip up, that's just you being weak"). No affirmation of client's freedom to choose differently.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation present. Psychiatrist does not ask about client's values, reasons for wanting health, past successes, or ideas about what might work. Client's change talk ("I want to get healthier," "My health is worth the effort") is ignored rather than amplified. Client's self-awareness and insights are dismissed. No open-ended questions designed to elicit intrinsic motivation; reliance entirely on external pressure and threat.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively assumes sole expert role and explicitly denies client's expertise about himself. When client explains that rigid approaches make him defensive, psychiatrist responds: "No, it doesn't make sense to me." Unilateral decision-making throughout; no "we" language or collaborative problem-solving. Client's direct request for partnership ("Maybe instead of telling me what I'm supposed to do, we could talk about what's actually doable for me?") is explicitly rejected.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Client produces genuine change talk ("My health is worth the effort," "I want to live longer and feel better"), but psychiatrist does not cultivate, amplify, or reinforce it. No strategic questioning to evoke more change talk. Client's compliance-based statements at end ("Yeah, I'm gonna commit") appear to result from pressure and shame rather than intrinsic motivation. No evidence of genuine confidence in ability to change or deep commitment to health goals.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist does redirect client away from sustain talk through sheer force and coercion rather than skillful MI techniques. Client eventually stops arguing for status quo and agrees to plan, but through confrontation ("you really need to stop making excuses") rather than double-sided reflection or strategic reframing. Redirection achieved at cost of client autonomy and intrinsic motivation. No exploration of legitimate function of sustain talk.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.1/5**

### Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Clear, specific behavioral targets established (no drinking weekdays, two drinks max weekends, gym three times weekly)
- Persistent focus on health behavior change maintained throughout session
- Nominal compliance achieved by session end

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Complete absence of MI spirit:** Directive, coercive, shame-based approach directly contradicts MI principles
- **No genuine acceptance or empathy:** Client judged as weak and irresponsible; attempts to explain experience met with dismissal
- **Systematic elimination of autonomy:** Client explicitly denied right to participate in own care decisions
- **No collaboration or partnership:** Unilateral expert stance; client's self-knowledge dismissed as irrelevant
- **Failure to evoke intrinsic motivation:** External pressure and threat used instead of drawing out client's own reasons for change
- **Confrontational handling of ambivalence:** Normal ambivalence met with judgment and pressure rather than exploration and validation

### Likely Clinical Outcomes

**Short-term:** Nominal compliance with treatment plan; client appears to have agreed primarily to end session and escape psychiatrist's judgment.

**Long-term:** High probability of treatment failure and relapse. Research demonstrates externally imposed goals without intrinsic motivation produce lower adherence and higher relapse rates. Client has explicitly stated that rigid, pressuring approaches make him "pull back" and that he needs collaborative support—psychiatrist has done the opposite. Shame and judgment may paradoxically reduce motivation to change.

### Critical Recommendation

**Fundamental shift required:** Psychiatrist must transition from expert-directive to collaborative partnership stance. This requires formal MI training, development of genuine acceptance and empathy, mastery of reflective listening, evocation of client's own motivation, support for autonomy, and validation of ambivalence. Regular clinical supervision focused on MI skill development is essential. Without this shift, psychiatrist's approach will continue to undermine client motivation and reduce likelihood of sustained behavior change.
