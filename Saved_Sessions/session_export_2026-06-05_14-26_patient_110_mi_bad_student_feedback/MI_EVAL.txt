Psychiatrist positions himself as sole authority who knows what Rafael "must" do, not as partner in exploration. ### Summary of Key Findings

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Fundamental failure in acceptance and autonomy support throughout first 80% of session; uses blame, judgment, and coercive language
- Absence of reflective listening in early exchanges; psychiatrist tells rather than listens
- Dismissal of client's legitimate concerns about overcommitment and self-sabotage; frames these as excuses rather than valid insights
- Failure to evoke change talk in early exchanges; imposes external motivation rather than drawing out internal motivation
- Confrontational approach to sustain talk that reinforces resistance rather than softening it
- Dramatic inconsistency between early and late session approach; suggests psychiatrist recognized early approach was problematic but did not explain shift
- No strategic deepening of change talk even in final exchanges; affirmation present but not sophisticated cultivation
- Lack of exploration of ambivalence throughout; psychiatrist treats ambivalence as defiance rather than as normal and workable

**Strengths:**
- Maintains focus on target behaviors (exercise and diet) throughout session
- Offers practical suggestions (short home workouts, twice-weekly gym attendance) that are realistic
- Demonstrates dramatic improvement in final exchanges, showing capacity for MI-consistent practice
- Explicitly acknowledges client expertise in final exchanges ("You know your life best")
- Successfully collaborates on goal-setting in final portion of session
- Affirms client suggestions rather than overriding them in final exchanges

---

### Clinical Impact

This session demonstrates critical failure in MI practice that directly undermines client motivation and likelihood of sustained change. First six exchanges employ coercive, judgmental language that violates MI spirit and likely increases Rafael's defensiveness and resistance. Rafael's responses show increasing defensiveness: he moves from "I'm willing to try" to "I'm not ready to say yes to daily exercise starting tomorrow"—clear indicator that psychiatrist's approach is backfiring.

However, final exchanges demonstrate that when psychiatrist shifts to collaborative, autonomy-supportive approach, Rafael immediately becomes more engaged and offers genuine change talk. His final responses ("Yeah, going twice a week sounds doable," "I think I could try that") suggest authentic commitment rather than defensive compliance.

**Critical Concern:** Inconsistency between early and late session approaches is clinically problematic. Rafael may perceive shift as manipulative rather than as genuine collaboration. Mixed messaging could undermine positive momentum from final exchanges and reduce long-term adherence to agreed-upon goals.

---

### Recommendations for Training

1. **Eliminate coercive language and blame** – Replace all "you must," "you have to," "you need to," and "you can't" statements with collaborative language respecting autonomy
2. **Develop consistent reflective listening skills** – Use reflective listening in every exchange, not just at end of session
3. **Validate client concerns rather than dismissing them as excuses** – Treat concerns as valuable self-knowledge, not resistance
4. **Evoke rather than impose motivation** – Ask open questions inviting Rafael to articulate his own reasons for change
5. **Use strategic questioning to deepen change talk** – Follow up with questions that strengthen and explore change talk further
6. **Handle sustain talk with soft redirects and double-sided reflections** – Acknowledge both sides of ambivalence rather than confronting sustain talk directly
7. **Establish consistent collaborative partnership from start** – Begin by explicitly acknowledging Rafael as expert on his own life
8. **Maintain consistency throughout session** – Apply MI principles consistently from beginning to end, not shifting midway through

**Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires intensive MI training focused on understanding MI spirit, developing consistent reflective listening skills, learning to evoke rather than impose motivation, and practicing MI-consistent responses across all client presentations. Supervision with MI-consistent feedback is essential.
