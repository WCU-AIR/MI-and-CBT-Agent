# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist uses judgmental language ("you're just making excuses again," "No, that's just the kind of thinking that holds you back") and shame-inducing statements throughout. Conditional regard demonstrated—acceptance only if patient complies immediately. No affirmation of patient's genuine strengths (deep love for son, willingness to engage, partial self-care efforts). Repeated use of "No more excuses" invalidates lived experience of complexity and overwhelm. Patient's worth is tied entirely to behavioral compliance rather than accepted unconditionally.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1

When patient states "It's not as easy as just stopping or ignoring the tension," psychiatrist responds with dismissal rather than empathic reflection. No clarifying questions to understand patient's specific experience, barriers, or values. Session is entirely clinician-driven monologue with no curiosity about patient's world. Fundamental failure to demonstrate understanding of patient's emotional reality and dilemma.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2

However, direction is imposed rather than collaboratively developed. Psychiatrist never explores patient's actual goals or what direction patient wants to move toward. Ignores patient's repeatedly stated primary concern: son's wellbeing and co-parenting conflict. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely authoritarian approach. Opening statement—"I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do"—explicitly rejects collaboration and establishes expert-driven hierarchy. Commanding language throughout: "You have to," "You must," "You need to," "Are you going to commit or not?" No joint problem-solving. When patient expresses concern about time management, psychiatrist dismisses rather than problem-solves collaboratively. Plan is entirely clinician-generated and clinician-imposed. Patient's contributions do not substantially influence session direction. Zero power-sharing or genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Active undermining of autonomy through coercion and threat language. Explicit removal of choice: "You have to make that appointment this week—no excuses," "The walks? They're not optional—they're necessary," "This is not optional." Threat-based pressure: "If you don't make that appointment this week, you're just putting yourself and your son at risk." When patient expresses ambivalence, psychiatrist attacks it rather than respecting it as legitimate. Message communicated: compliance is mandatory or consequences will follow. Fundamental violation of MI principles likely to trigger reactance and decrease intrinsic motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation of patient's own motivations, values, ideas, or resources. Session is entirely clinician-driven prescription with no genuine curiosity. Patient offers multiple opportunities for evocation ("I love my son dearly," "I want to be strong for my son," "I want to be better for my son and for myself"), but psychiatrist ignores them entirely. No open-ended questions designed to elicit patient's own reasons for change. Psychiatrist provides externally imposed reasons rather than evoking patient's intrinsic motivation. No exploration of patient's values, strengths, past successes, or what she sees as possible. Complete failure of core MI competency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role throughout and explicitly rejects partnership. Opening statement establishes clinician as authority and patient as deficient. Language positions clinician as knowing better than patient: "You have to stop making excuses," "No, that's just the kind of thinking that holds you back," "You'll feel better once you start, trust me." No recognition of patient's expertise about her own life, constraints, barriers, or what might work for her. Message conveyed: "I know what you need; you don't." Power dynamic entirely clinician-centered. Fundamental violation of MI spirit and collaborative practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient spontaneously offers substantial change talk ("I do want to commit to making that appointment this week," "I'm going to find a way to make it happen," "I want to be better for my son and for myself"), but psychiatrist fails to cultivate, amplify, or reinforce it. No reflections that strengthen patient's commitment language. No strategic use of open-ended questions to elicit more change talk. Psychiatrist responds to change talk with demands for more certainty and threats about consequences. Approach is entirely prescriptive rather than evocative. Psychiatrist maintains some focus on behavioral change goals, preventing score of 1, but does not strategically strengthen patient's own motivation language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Direct confrontation of sustain talk rather than skillful softening. Patient expresses legitimate ambivalence and barriers ("It's not as easy as just stopping," "I'm not sure the stress will ever fully go away," "I'm not convinced yet that it'll make a big difference until things change with his father"), but psychiatrist attacks thinking rather than exploring it. Uses confrontational language: "Look, you've got to stop making excuses," "No, that's just the kind of thinking that holds you back," "You're just making excuses again." No MI techniques employed for handling ambivalence. No double-sided reflections, no reframing of barriers as opportunities, no validation of difficulty while moving toward change. Approach is entirely adversarial and likely to increase defensiveness.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Session Assessment:** This session represents a **severe failure of motivational interviewing practice** and a fundamental violation of MI spirit. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no competency across all nine MI domains. The session is characterized by authoritarianism, coercion, shame-induction, and complete absence of empathic listening or genuine collaboration.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Zero reflective statements in entire transcript
- Explicit rejection of collaboration from opening statement
- Coercive language and threat-based motivation throughout
- Ignoring patient's stated primary concern (son's wellbeing and co-parenting conflict)
- Active undermining of patient autonomy
- No genuine curiosity or evocation of patient's own motivation
- Direct confrontation of ambivalence rather than skillful exploration

**Impact on Patient Engagement:** While patient shows some compliance by end of session ("I'll do it"), this represents external pressure-based compliance, not genuine intrinsic motivation rooted in patient's own values and goals. Research consistently demonstrates that behavior change achieved through coercion is less likely to be sustained. This approach risks damaging therapeutic relationship and decreasing likelihood of future engagement.

**Urgent Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should not continue conducting MI sessions without formal training and supervision. Current approach contradicts evidence-based practice. Immediate enrollment in formal MI training program (e.g., MINT-affiliated workshop) with ongoing supervision is essential. Session demonstrates pre-MI level functioning requiring foundational skill development across all domains.
