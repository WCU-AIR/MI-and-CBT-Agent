
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

### 3. Understanding
### **Score: 2/6**

**Explanation:**

The therapist demonstrated surface-level understanding of the patient's explicit statements but missed critical nuances and the patient's underlying experience. The therapist never acknowledged the medical urgency or validated how terrifying these symptoms are. 2. **The patient's ambivalence and fear**: The patient expressed fear ("That scares me"), hopelessness ("It feels like I'm trapped"), and doubt ("It's hard to believe that when I'm in the thick of it"). ## Recommendations

### 1. **URGENT: Ensure Medical Safety and Coordination**
- **Action**: Immediately coordinate with medical team. Ensure patient has been medically evaluated, is medically cleared for outpatient treatment, and is on appropriate medications (benzodiazepines for withdrawal management, thiamine, magnesium, folic acid).
- **Example**: "Nicholas, before we continue with therapy, I need to make sure you're medically safe. Have you seen our doctor? Are you on any medications for the withdrawal? Let's review your medical plan together."
- **Practice**: Develop a protocol for all substance use cases: (1) Medical evaluation checklist, (2) Medication review, (3) Coordination with medical team, (4) Safety assessment (suicide, self-harm, overdose risk).

### 2. **Establish Explicit, Collaborative Treatment Strategy**
- **Action**: In the next session, clearly articulate the treatment strategy. Explain the rationale for each component. Involve the patient in planning.
- **Example**: "Nicholas, here's my understanding of what's happening: You have severe alcohol dependence, which means your body has become dependent on alcohol. When you try to stop, you experience withdrawal symptoms—the shaking, sweating, anxiety. Your brain has also learned that drinking relieves these symptoms, so the craving is very strong. Our strategy has three parts: (1) Medical management to safely manage withdrawal, (2) Building skills to manage cravings and resist urges, (3) Addressing the life problems that contribute to drinking—your marriage, work stress, etc. We'll work on this over [X] weeks/months. Here's what success looks like: [specific goals]. Does this make sense? What questions do you have?"
- **Practice**: For every patient, develop a written treatment plan that includes: (a) Problem formulation, (b) Treatment goals, (c) Specific interventions, (d) Timeline, (e) How progress will be measured.

### 3. **Establish Structured Agenda with Time Allocation**
- **Action**: At the beginning of each session, collaboratively set a specific agenda with time allocation. Prioritize topics. Follow the agenda.
- **Example**: "Nicholas, we have 50 minutes today. I want to check in on how you've been since we last met, review your homework, and then work on identifying your triggers and developing a plan for managing them. Does that sound good? Is there anything else you want to make sure we cover?"
- **Practice**: Use a written agenda. Show it to the patient. Adjust based on patient input. Refer back to it during the session.

### 4. **Implement Systematic Homework Review**
- **Action**: Begin every session (after the first) with homework review. Ask what was tried, what worked, what didn't, and what barriers emerged. Adjust homework based on outcome.
- **Example**: "Let's start by reviewing your homework from last week. You were going to try deep breathing when you felt cravings. Did you try it? What happened? How many times did you use it? What made it hard or easy?"
- **Practice**: Create a homework tracking sheet. Review it at the start of each session. Document what was completed and what wasn't. Use this information to adjust interventions.

### 5. **Conduct Functional Analysis of Drinking Behavior**
- **Action**: Systematically analyze the patient's drinking patterns. Identify triggers, antecedents, consequences, and reinforcers. Use this to develop targeted interventions.
- **Example**: "Let's map out your drinking. Walk me through a typical day. When do you first feel the urge to drink? What's happening? What are you thinking and feeling? What happens when you drink? How long does the relief last? What happens after? Let's identify the patterns."
- **Practice**: Use a functional analysis worksheet. Ask detailed questions about: (a) When drinking occurs, (b) What triggers it, (c) What the patient is thinking/feeling, (d) What happens after drinking, (e) What the consequences are.

### 6. **Develop Specific, Tailored Relapse Prevention Plan**
- **Action**: Identify high-risk situations. Develop specific coping plans for each. Practice these plans during session.
- **Example**: "Based on what we've discussed, your highest-risk times seem to be: (1) Morning when you wake up with tremor and anxiety, (2) After work when you're stressed, (3) Weekends when you have free time. For each of these, let's develop a specific plan. For mornings, here's what we'll do: [specific steps]. Let's practice this now."
- **Practice**: Create a written relapse prevention plan. Include: (a) High-risk situations, (b) Specific coping strategies for each, (c) Emergency contacts, (d) What to do if a lapse occurs.

### 7. **Use Guided Discovery and Socratic Questioning Systematically**
- **Action**: Replace advice-giving with genuine Socratic questioning. Help the patient discover insights and solutions independently.
- **Example**: Instead of "You could try breathing exercises," ask: "When you feel a craving coming on, what have you tried? What's worked, even a little? What do you think might help? Let's test that idea and see what actually happens."
- **Practice**: For every intervention, ask questions first: "What do you think?" "What have you noticed?" "What would happen if...?" "How could we test that?" Only provide suggestions after exploring the patient's ideas.

### 8. **Implement Behavioral Experiments to Test Beliefs**
- **Action**: Rather than just suggesting thought changes, design behavioral experiments to test beliefs in real-world situations.
- **Example**: "You believe that if you don't drink, the anxiety will keep building until you can't handle it. Let's test that. Next time you feel the urge, try using the breathing technique we practiced. Notice what actually happens to the anxiety. Does it keep building, or does it change? Let's see what the evidence shows."
