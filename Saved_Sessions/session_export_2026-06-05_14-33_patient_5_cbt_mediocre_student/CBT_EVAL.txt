## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Session Quality Summary:**

This clinical session represents a significant departure from evidence-based CBT standards, particularly concerning given the patient's acute medical presentation and severe clinical condition. The therapist demonstrated adequate interpersonal warmth but failed to deliver competent, structured cognitive-behavioral treatment.

**Most Critical Safety Concern:**

The patient presents with **acute alcohol withdrawal** (tremor, diaphoresis, autonomic hyperactivity, anxiety) and **severe alcohol use disorder**. The therapist made no attempt to:
- Assess medical safety or clearance for outpatient treatment
- Discuss medication management (benzodiazepines, thiamine, magnesium)
- Evaluate need for inpatient detoxification
- Coordinate with medical team
- Assess suicide/self-harm risk
- Provide safety planning

**Alcohol withdrawal can be life-threatening (seizures, delirium tremens, cardiac arrhythmias).** Treating this with breathing exercises alone is medically inadequate and potentially dangerous.

**Most Critical Clinical Failure:**

**No homework review occurred in Session 2.** This is a fundamental CBT component. The therapist began Session 2 with the identical opening as Session 1, suggesting no learning or continuity. This signals to the patient that accountability is not important and undermines the therapeutic structure.

**Recommended Supervision Plan:**

1. **Immediate:** Review medical coordination protocols. Ensure this patient is medically evaluated and on appropriate medications.

2. **This week:** Conduct clinical supervision focused on: (a) Treatment planning for severe substance use disorders, (b) Medical-psychological integration, (c) Homework assignment and review procedures, (d) Agenda-setting and collaboration.

3. **Ongoing:** Require therapist to: (a) Develop written treatment plans for all substance use cases, (b) Implement systematic homework review at start of each session, (c) Use structured agenda-setting, (d) Receive weekly supervision until competency improves.

4. **Training needed:** (a) CBT for substance use disorders (functional analysis, relapse prevention), (b) Motivational interviewing, (c) Medical aspects of alcohol withdrawal, (d) Suicide risk assessment and safety planning, (e) Socratic questioning and guided discovery.

**Prognosis for This Patient Without Intervention Changes: POOR**

This patient requires intensive, multimodal treatment. Current approach is insufficient and potentially harmful.
