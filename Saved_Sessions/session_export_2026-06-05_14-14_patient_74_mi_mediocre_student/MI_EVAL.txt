# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal unconditional positive regard and frequently adopts a prescriptive stance. While opening is warm, the shift into directive mode communicates implicit judgment that the client needs to be "fixed." Repeated prescriptions ("Maybe you should consider," "You should also try") undermine genuine acceptance. No explicit affirmation of Linda's worth, past efforts, or identified strengths (insight, self-awareness, openness). Tone is expert-driven rather than genuinely warm and supportive.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level reflective listening present but lacks emotional depth and attunement. Reflections are factually accurate but emotionally shallow (e.g., "It sounds like you've faced a lot of challenges"). Critical misses: fails to reflect Linda's fear of isolation when she expresses concern about losing friendships; dismisses low confidence (4 or 5) with "which is okay" rather than exploring underlying discouragement; does not deepen the insight that "talking about this stuff makes me feel better already." Reflections restate content without capturing emotional experience or identity conflict.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains adequate but inconsistent focus on tobacco cessation as target behavior. Session does not wander; psychiatrist consistently returns to smoking. However, direction is imposed rather than collaboratively established. Opens with broad invitation but quickly narrows focus and steers toward specific interventions (patches, support groups, gradual cutback). Lacks collaborative exploration of Linda's own goals and priorities before suggesting direction. Focus is psychiatrist-determined rather than negotiated.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal true collaboration; adopts expert-driven stance throughout. Prescriptive language frames suggestions as directives ("Maybe you should consider," "You should also try," "It would probably help if"). Unilateral problem-solving when Linda expresses uncertainty—psychiatrist proposes solutions rather than inviting Linda to explore her own ideas. Limited solicitation of client input; questions are closed and leading rather than genuinely collaborative. Power imbalance evident: psychiatrist positioned as expert with knowledge; Linda's expertise about her own life underutilized. One partial moment when Linda suggests ongoing support could help, but psychiatrist does not deeply explore or co-construct based on her insight.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently undermines autonomy support through directive language and prescriptions. Final statement ("you're the one in charge here, and you can change the plan anytime") comes after extensive direction-setting and feels like a disclaimer rather than genuine autonomy support. Prescriptive recommendations carry weight of expert authority, subtly pressuring compliance. Assumes readiness for specific plan without exploring whether this is Linda's chosen approach. Minimizes Linda's expressed concern about all-or-nothing thinking by proposing a plan requiring consistent adherence to patches—potentially triggering the same pattern she identified as problematic. Lacks exploration of Linda's preferences and self-knowledge.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal evocation—core MI skill of drawing out client's own motivations, values, ideas, and resources. Primarily tells rather than asks. When Linda spontaneously offers change talk ("It's for me, for my health, and for the money too"), psychiatrist does not amplify or deepen; moves on to problem-solving. Fails to explore values: never asks what health means to her, what relationships mean, what she wants her life to look like. Superficial exploration of ambivalence between wanting to quit and maintaining social connections; does not explore this tension deeply. Failure to explore self-efficacy: when Linda expresses low confidence, psychiatrist prescribes strategies rather than asking Linda to explore what would increase confidence or past successes. One partial moment when psychiatrist asks about confidence level (1-10), but does not skillfully follow up to deepen change talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Fails to establish genuine partnership; assumes expert role throughout most of session. Repeatedly positions self as authority who knows what works ("Maybe those could help," "It would probably help if," "many people do it by trying different methods"). Unilateral plan-setting: proposes specific plan without genuine collaboration; language "How about we" suggests collaboration but plan is psychiatrist-determined. Fails to honor Linda's insights: when she states "talking about this stuff makes me feel better already," psychiatrist does not deeply explore or build on this; pivots to external interventions, communicating psychiatrist's ideas are more valuable. Dismisses Linda's self-knowledge about all-or-nothing thinking without adjusting plan accordingly. Expert language throughout positions psychiatrist as knowledgeable guide and Linda as novice needing instruction.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal skill in evoking, amplifying, and reinforcing change talk—strongest predictor of actual behavior change. Fails to amplify spontaneous change talk ("It's for me, for my health, and for the money too"); does not ask follow-up questions to deepen. Rarely asks open-ended questions designed to elicit change talk; questions about barriers and past failures can inadvertently evoke sustain talk instead. Insufficient exploration of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment. When Linda expresses low confidence, psychiatrist does not help her explore own ability or past successes. Does not deeply explore Linda's reasons for change beyond surface-level health and money concerns. Fails to explore whether Linda feels genuine need to change or is complying with external pressure. When Linda says "I'm willing to give it a shot," psychiatrist does not deepen commitment language. One partial moment: psychiatrist asks about confidence level (1-10), but does not skillfully follow up.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates inconsistent skill in handling sustain talk. Adequately avoids reinforcing sustain talk; does not argue for change or confront directly, maintaining supportive tone. Adequate reflection without reinforcement when Linda says "It's just part of my life." However, misses opportunities for strategic redirection. No double-sided reflections that acknowledge both sides while subtly emphasizing change side. Inadequate exploration of "six months, six months and a day" timeline—this is delay tactic/sustain talk that psychiatrist does not gently explore. Prescriptive response to ambivalence rather than exploring doubt or using strategic reflection. Approach is passive rather than actively cultivating change talk while softening resistance.

---

### **Overall Session Score: 2.3/5**

**Summary Assessment:**

**Strengths:**
- Maintains supportive, non-confrontational tone; warm and does not argue or express judgment
- Demonstrates basic reflective listening with factually accurate reflections
- Maintains focus on tobacco cessation as target behavior
- Acknowledges Linda's autonomy in final summary
- Responds supportively to Linda's willingness to try

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- Excessive directiveness and prescriptive language undermines MI spirit
- Minimal evocation of change talk; spontaneous change talk not amplified
- Failure to explore ambivalence deeply despite clear evidence of internal conflict
- Expert-driven stance rather than partnership; positions self as authority
- Insufficient emotional attunement; reflections factually accurate but emotionally shallow
- Missed opportunity to explore therapeutic relationship as change mechanism
- Inadequate handling of sustain talk; opportunities to strategically redirect missed

**Overall MI Competency:** Below-average. While basic supportive counseling skills present, core MI techniques—evocation, partnership, autonomy support, systematic cultivation of change talk—are largely absent or poorly executed. Session does not embody MI spirit of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation.

**Impact on Client Motivation:** Moderate to low likelihood of evoking sustained intrinsic motivation. Linda remains engaged and cooperative, but agreement to plan appears to be compliance rather than genuine commitment. Prescriptive approach may undermine autonomy and self-efficacy. Research demonstrates client-generated change talk far more predictive of behavior change than clinician-prescribed plans; failure to cultivate change talk significantly reduces likelihood of sustained motivation and follow-through.

**Recommendation:** Intensive MI training focused on core techniques of evocation, partnership, and autonomy support. Specific practice in open-ended questioning, amplifying change talk, and complex reflections essential. Recording and reviewing sessions with MI-trained supervisor recommended to accelerate skill development.
