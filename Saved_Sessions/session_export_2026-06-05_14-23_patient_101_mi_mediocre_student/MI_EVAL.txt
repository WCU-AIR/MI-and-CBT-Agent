## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.4/5 (Poor)**

**Session Embodies MI Spirit:** No. This session is fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit. The psychiatrist operates from expert-driven, prescriptive stance rather than collaborative, evocative approach. While not hostile, the approach actively undermines client autonomy and intrinsic motivation.

**Key Strengths:**
- Warm opening establishes basic rapport
- Open-ended question ("What feels most important to you right now?") demonstrates some evocative intent
- Attempt to explore barriers ("What do you think is stopping you?")
- Basic session structure and organizational coherence maintained
- Closing offers accessibility and support

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Non-collaborative expert-driven stance contradicts MI foundation
- Autonomy actively undermined through contradiction of priorities and prescription
- Minimal evocation; solutions imposed rather than drawn from client
- Significant ambivalence (4/10 importance, doubts, fears) explored but not addressed
- Weak change talk produced and not amplified
- Sustain talk minimized or contradicted rather than softly handled
- Acceptance and empathy gaps throughout; coping mechanisms invalidated

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
Maya leaves with weak commitment ("I'll try to keep that in mind"), unaddressed ambivalence, and no genuine ownership of change process. Polite compliance rather than intrinsic motivation evident. Research indicates this approach is associated with poor adherence and sustained change. Maya likely to abandon prescribed goals within days/weeks and may interpret as personal failure rather than recognizing approach undermined motivation.

**Recommendations for Improvement (Priority Order):**
1. **Shift from expert to collaborative partner** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)—Reframe role fundamentally; practice genuine power-sharing and curiosity about client perspective
2. **Evoke rather than impose motivation**—Ask "What do you think?" before prescribing; explore client's own reasons for change
3. **Validate and explore ambivalence**—Treat doubts and barriers as important information, not obstacles to overcome
4. **Honor client priorities and autonomy**—Never contradict client assessment; use autonomy-supportive language
5. **Develop complex, emotion-focused reflections**—Move beyond factual restatements to capture emotional nuance
6. **Strategically amplify change talk**—Reinforce and deepen when client produces desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment
7. **Softly handle sustain talk**—Explore collaboratively using double-sided reflections and reframing
8. **Establish true partnership through shared decision-making**—Involve client in problem definition and goal-setting
9. **Strengthen acceptance and unconditional positive regard**—Consistently communicate value and non-judgment
10. **Seek formal MI training and supervision**—Enroll in evidence-based MI training; record and review sessions with supervisor

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should seek formal MI training before continuing to use MI as stated approach. Focus immediately on shifting from expert-driven to collaborative stance and on evoking rather than imposing motivation.
