## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 2.1/5**

**Calculation:** (2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 3) ÷ 9 = **2.1/5**

---

### Key Strengths

- **Mid-session course correction:** Demonstrates self-awareness and flexibility by recognizing that directive approach is ineffective and pivoting to collaborative, exploratory stance
- **Improved reflective listening:** Later exchanges contain accurate empathic reflections capturing emotional nuance ("It sounds like smoking is more than just a habit for you—it's a way to find a moment of calm")
- **Open-ended questioning:** Final portion uses genuine exploratory questions ("Can you tell me more about how smoking fits into your daily life?") that invite client perspective
- **Validation and normalization:** Later portion includes statements validating client's struggle and normalizing her experience
- **Collaborative language shift:** Use of "I wonder" and invitations for client input represent meaningful movement toward partnership

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

- **Catastrophic early session approach:** First half fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit. Judgmental language, shame-based pressure, and directive confrontation directly activate client defensiveness and undermine therapeutic alliance. This damage is difficult to repair.

- **Suppression of change talk:** Throughout much of session, clinician fails to recognize and amplify client's change talk. Instead pivots to pressure and advice-giving, which suppresses intrinsic motivation.

- **Missed evocation opportunities:** Multiple instances where client expresses values, concerns, or openness are met with directive responses rather than curious exploration.

- **Inconsistent autonomy support:** Early session actively undermines client autonomy through coercive language. While later portion improves, never explicitly affirms client's right to choose or ownership of change process.

- **Lack of strategic direction:** Session oscillates between rigid directive pressure and exploratory diffuseness without clear, collaboratively-developed focus on smoking cessation while respecting autonomy.

- **Insufficient amplification of emerging change talk:** Even in later portion, when client expresses willingness ("I'm willing to try if it means maybe I don't have to quit cold turkey"), clinician doesn't deeply explore or strengthen this motivation.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

The early session approach—characterized by pressure, judgment, and shame—would likely increase client defensiveness and entrench sustain talk. By end of early exchanges, client explicitly rejects clinician's pressure and requests collaborative exploration instead ("Can we maybe talk about how I could start thinking about making changes, instead of jumping to quitting cold turkey?"). Later portion shows improvement, and client begins expressing some openness to alternatives, but this appears to be response to clinician's changed approach rather than genuine intrinsic motivation for change. For lasting change, client needs to develop own reasons for quitting and confidence that she can do so—neither adequately cultivated in this session.

---

### Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Eliminate shame-based language and judgmental stance** – Remove all directive, coercive language; replace with collaborative, respectful language honoring autonomy
2. **Develop consistent reflective listening** – Make reflective listening primary response mode; aim for 70%+ reflective listening vs. directive statements
3. **Master evocative questioning** – Learn to ask open-ended questions that invite client to generate own reasons, values, and motivations for change
4. **Amplify change talk** – When client expresses change talk, pause and explore deeply to strengthen and develop this language
5. **Develop autonomy support as core stance** – Explicitly affirm client's right to choose; emphasize ownership of change process
6. **Use double-sided reflections** – Acknowledge both sides of ambivalence to soften sustain talk without confrontation
7. **Establish clear, collaborative direction** – Collaboratively establish focus at beginning; maintain throughout while remaining flexible
8. **Develop partnership consistently** – Communicate that client is expert on own life; invite input into problem-solving throughout
9. **Implement self-monitoring** – Track reflection-to-question ratio, change talk amplification, autonomy-supporting language after each session
10. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** – Enroll in MINT-accredited training program and commit to ongoing supervision with MI-trained supervisor
