### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Multiple instances of client change talk completely ignored and actively suppressed. Client explicitly expresses desire for change ("I really do want to feel better," "I want to get better," "I hope things get better") and ability language ("Maybe if I had some help figuring out other ways to cope first, then I could think about it"). Psychiatrist responds with pressure and criticism rather than amplification and support. No questions designed to evoke change talk. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist systematically reinforces sustain talk through confrontation and pressure rather than softening it through empathic exploration. Client's normal expressions of ambivalence ("I don't know if I'm ready," "I'm not sure if I can do that right now," "I can't just quit drinking today") met with direct confrontation ("No, you really have to do it now," "No, you have to stop making excuses"). Confrontational approach strengthens sustain talk. Client responds with increased resistance: "I don't like feeling pressured like this," "I need more support and understanding first." No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic responses.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.1/5**

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, confrontational practice that actively undermines therapeutic alliance and client motivation for change.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Coercive language throughout ("you have to," "you must," "you need to")
- Systematic dismissal of client experience and self-knowledge
- Explicit judgment and criticism ("weakness," "excuses," "no excuse")
- Complete failure to listen to client's stated priorities (anxiety)
- Suppression of client change talk through pressure and criticism
- Reinforcement of client resistance through confrontation
- Damaged therapeutic alliance evidenced by client feedback: "I don't like feeling pressured like this" and "It feels like you're not listening to how scared or stuck I am"

### Impact Assessment

This session is likely to result in:
- Decreased client engagement and treatment adherence
- Increased client resistance and sustain talk
- Reduced intrinsic motivation for change
- Damaged therapeutic relationship
- Reduced likelihood of meaningful behavior change

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Seek formal MI training** through MINT-certified trainers
2. **Engage in regular clinical supervision** with MI-focused review
3. **Adopt collaborative stance** beginning with client priorities
4. **Replace directives with reflective listening**
5. **Validate client experience** rather than dismissing it
6. **Explore behavior function** before prescribing change
7. **Recognize and amplify change talk** when present
8. **Soften resistance** through empathic reflection, not confrontation
9. **Explicitly respect client autonomy** in all interactions
10. **Attend to feedback** about therapeutic relationship with genuine curiosity

**Critical Priority:** This clinician requires immediate, intensive MI training and supervision. Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based practice and violates core principles of therapeutic alliance and client-centered care.
