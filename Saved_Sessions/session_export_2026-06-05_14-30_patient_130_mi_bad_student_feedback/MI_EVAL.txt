# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Pervasive judgment throughout session with repeated labeling of Tyler as "stubborn," "making excuses," "avoiding responsibility," and "selfish." Zero affirmation of strengths or values. No normalization of adolescent ambivalence. Tone consistently punitive and controlling. Tyler explicitly states he feels unheard and pressured—direct result of lack of acceptance. Psychiatrist responds to feedback by increasing control rather than adjusting approach.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or attempt to understand Tyler's perspective. Psychiatrist speaks *at* rather than *with* client. Multiple opportunities for empathic connection dismissed as "excuses" (e.g., smoking serves social and coping functions, autonomy concerns, ambivalence about readiness). No exploration of why Tyler smokes or what needs it meets. Repeated cycling through identical monologues demonstrates no genuine effort to understand client's emotional experience or worldview.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear focus on smoking cessation maintained (prevents score of 1), but method is fundamentally incompatible with MI. Approach is authoritarian rather than collaborative. Specific plan imposed without exploring Tyler's preferences or barriers. Uses threats and demands without exploring ambivalence. Repeats same directive multiple times without adaptation. Tyler's responses become progressively more entrenched in resistance, indicating directional approach is counterproductive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of collaboration. Psychiatrist assumes expert-authority role; Tyler treated as passive recipient of instructions. Monologues delivered without genuine dialogue. Tyler's input dismissed as "excuses" rather than incorporated. Unilateral decisions made about treatment without client input. When Tyler expresses concerns, they are not explored but dismissed. Language throughout ("you have to," "you must," "no exceptions," "follow this plan exactly") explicitly denies partnership and imposes hierarchy.

#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Active undermining of autonomy at every turn. Controlling language pervasive: "Quitting is non-negotiable," "You don't get to pick and choose," "No exceptions," "Follow this plan exactly." Threat-based control used repeatedly ("If you keep smoking, you'll face severe consequences"). Tyler explicitly identifies problem: "It feels like people are just trying to control me" and "I don't wanna just stop because someone's telling me to." Psychiatrist's response is to increase control rather than adjust approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation present. Closed yes/no questions used that don't invite exploration. Questions are rhetorical and controlling, not genuinely curious. Rich material from Tyler (smoking serves social/coping functions, autonomy concerns, need for own timeline) never explored or developed. Dismissal of client input as "excuses" replaces genuine curiosity. No attempt to understand Tyler's perspective, values, or internal motivation. Didactic monologues replace collaborative exploration.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Fundamental violation of partnership principle. Psychiatrist assumes complete expertise; Tyler positioned as passive, resistant problem to be fixed. Opening statement establishes unilateral authority: "I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do." Tyler's experience dismissed as irrelevant ("This isn't about feelings; it's about your health"). Complex behavior reduced to simple willpower, implying failure is character weakness. Tyler positioned as problem; psychiatrist as solution-holder. Power entirely one-directional.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No strategic evocation of change talk. Approach actively suppresses potential change language through dismissal and threat. When Tyler expresses minimal change talk ("Maybe one day I'll decide to quit"), it is immediately shut down with "Quitting is non-negotiable." Few moments of openness dismissed rather than developed. Tyler's acknowledgment of concern ("I know it's bad for me") met with "You need to stop making excuses." Psychiatrist's controlling approach generates sustain talk rather than change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Direct confrontation of resistance used repeatedly, which intensifies rather than softens sustain talk. Tyler's resistance language becomes progressively stronger throughout session, indicating approach is counterproductive. No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic non-response. Each instance of sustain talk met with argument and blame ("You're just making excuses," "You're just avoiding the truth," "You're being stubborn"). Research demonstrates this confrontational approach increases resistance; session exemplifies this pattern.

---

## Overall Assessment Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI level competence. This is not MI with deficiencies; this represents the opposite of MI principles. Psychiatrist has not internalized fundamental MI spirit of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation.

**Key Violations:**
- Authoritarian expert stance replacing collaborative partnership
- Pervasive judgment and blame damaging therapeutic alliance
- Complete absence of empathy and perspective-taking
- Elimination of client autonomy through controlling language and threats
- No evocation of client motivation; questions are rhetorical and controlling
- Intensification of resistance through confrontational approach
- Repetitive ineffective approach with no adaptation to client responses

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Session is **actively harmful** to client engagement and motivation for change. Tyler explicitly states he feels unheard, controlled, and pressured. His resistance language intensifies throughout. Likelihood of future engagement in smoking cessation has **decreased**, not increased. Tyler will likely leave feeling blamed, misunderstood, and more entrenched in resistance.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. Complete formal MI retraining (MINT-certified training or equivalent)
2. Develop genuine curiosity and reflective listening skills
3. Eliminate all judgmental language; develop acceptance
4. Explicitly support and respect client autonomy
5. Replace prescriptive advice with evocative questions
6. Understand and address functional aspects of behavior
7. Develop skill in handling resistance without confrontation
8. Engage in regular MI-focused clinical supervision with fidelity assessment

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should not continue MI-focused sessions until demonstrating competence through formal training and supervised practice. Current approach undermines client motivation and violates MI principles.
