### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session, with genuine and specific affirmations grounded in observable client behavior ("Thanks for sharing that openness with me," "I'm really impressed by your self-awareness and planning"). However, opportunities exist to deepen acceptance by more fully exploring and normalizing client ambivalence. When Anya expresses nervousness about talking to adults, the response is adequate but somewhat surface-level, lacking deeper exploration of what underlies that nervousness or explicit normalization as developmentally appropriate for a 17-year-old.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking with several complex reflections that effectively capture both content and emotional nuance. Metaphor-rich reflections such as "carrying both hope and pressure at the same time" and "building a bridge step by step" add meaningful depth. However, some reflections remain somewhat surface-level. When Anya mentions fearing to "waste time" and have "regrets," the psychiatrist reflects this but doesn't deeply explore what these concepts mean to her. Additionally, when Anya's confidence is rated "4 or 5," the psychiatrist validates but doesn't explore the emotional experience beneath—anxiety, overwhelm, or discouragement—that might deepen affective understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains general direction toward exploring Anya's future planning and identifying concrete next steps; however, this represents a critical clinical failure. The psychiatrist never explicitly mentions alcohol, substance use, or the screening that prompted the referral. The session drifts toward career exploration without addressing the original clinical reason for the referral—early alcohol exposure and related risk factors. The psychiatrist should have either explicitly explored whether substance use is relevant, clarified the referral purpose, or collaboratively decided with Anya whether to focus on a different concern. Direction is reactive rather than strategically maintained around the clinical target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaborative stance with open-ended questions that position Anya as an active agent ("What would you like to focus on during our time together?"). The clinician consistently seeks Anya's perspective and builds on her ideas rather than prescribing solutions. However, collaboration could be strengthened by inviting Anya to self-reflect rather than offering interpretations ("That shows a strong sense of self-awareness"). Additionally, the final plan summary is presented as the psychiatrist's synthesis rather than collaboratively co-constructed with explicit invitation for Anya's input and modification.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects and supports Anya's autonomy throughout the session, avoiding directive language and emphasizing choice and client ownership ("What do you think might be a manageable first step..."). The clinician explicitly affirms Anya's right to choose whether to continue ("It's nice to know I can check back in if I want to") and respects her pace. However, autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging that Anya could choose not to reach out to her teacher/counselor or take a different approach entirely. The plan is presented as the natural next step rather than one option among several possibilities.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocation is inconsistently demonstrated. The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions ("What do you think makes that future feel important or meaningful to you?" and "What have you done in the past when you've faced something new or uncertain...?") that invite Anya to access her own wisdom. However, the session is heavily weighted toward the psychiatrist's reflections and interpretations rather than sustained evocation of Anya's own change talk and motivations. When Anya expresses "I want to feel like I'm making a choice that really works for me," the psychiatrist responds with affirmation rather than deepening questions. Most critically, there is no evocation of Anya's own perspective on substance use, health values, or protective factors related to the referral concern.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership is demonstrated inconsistently. While the psychiatrist invites Anya's input in some moments, several statements subtly position the psychiatrist as the expert evaluator of Anya's qualities: "That shows a strong sense of self-awareness," "That shows good judgment," "I'm really impressed by your self-awareness and planning." True partnership would invite Anya to reflect on her own strengths. The session structure maintains a subtle expert-client hierarchy rather than true co-expertise. Most significantly, the psychiatrist never addresses the referral concern explicitly, suggesting the clinician is directing the session rather than truly partnering with Anya to determine focus. A partnership-oriented approach would explicitly discuss: "The reason you were referred here was because of a school screening about alcohol. Is that something you'd like to talk about?"

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Cultivating change talk is minimally demonstrated, representing a critical deficiency in MI practice. While Anya expresses some change talk ("I want to feel like I'm making a choice that really works for me," "I think I could probably do that this week"), the psychiatrist's responses are primarily affirmations and reflections rather than strategic amplification designed to strengthen and deepen this language. When Anya commits to reaching out this week, the psychiatrist affirms but doesn't ask follow-up questions to reinforce or explore what this step means to her. Most critically, there is virtually no change talk evoked regarding the referral concern (alcohol/substance use). The psychiatrist never explores Anya's own reasons for avoiding or moderating substance use, her values around health and safety, or her commitment to protective factors. This represents a fundamental gap in MI application.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully avoids dwelling on barriers and sustain talk. When Anya expresses uncertainty about where to start and time constraints, the psychiatrist validates without reinforcing these barriers, then pivots toward exploring past successes and resources: "What have you done in the past when you've faced something new or uncertain...?" When Anya mentions nervousness about talking to adults, the psychiatrist normalizes this and helps her reframe the interaction as "just a conversation" rather than a big commitment. This strategic redirection moves away from sustain talk toward building confidence. However, the psychiatrist could have been more proactive by explicitly evoking change talk: "What would help move that from a 4 or 5 to a 6 or 7?" to strengthen motivation while softening barriers.

## Summary of Key Findings

### Critical Clinical Failure
The session contains a **fundamental breach of clinical responsibility**: the complete absence of any discussion of the referral concern. Anya was referred for a school-based alcohol screening, yet the psychiatrist never mentions substance use, alcohol, or the reason for the referral. This represents a critical failure of MI principle #3 (Direction) and basic clinical duty.

### Strengths
- Consistent warmth, genuine affirmation, and nonjudgmental stance
- Solid reflective listening with metaphor-rich reflections demonstrating accurate understanding
- Skillful normalization and reframing of barriers without reinforcing sustain talk
- Open-ended questions that position Anya as an active agent
- Consistent autonomy support emphasizing client choice and pace
- Concrete, manageable plan resulting in reasonable confidence (7-8/10)

### Areas for Improvement
- **CRITICAL:** Address referral concern explicitly and collaboratively
- Insufficient evocation; relies heavily on reflections rather than sustained questioning
- Subtle expert-evaluator positioning undermines true partnership
- Minimal cultivation of change talk; insufficient amplification of client's own language
- Missed opportunities to explore underlying emotions and values
- Failure to co-construct plans collaboratively

### Clinical Impact
While Anya appears engaged and leaves with a concrete plan around career exploration, the session has not fulfilled its clinical purpose. The complete failure to address the referral concern means potential substance use issues remain unexamined. The psychiatrist has missed critical opportunities to evoke Anya's intrinsic motivation around health, safety, and protective factors.
