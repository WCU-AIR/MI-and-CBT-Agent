## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

This session demonstrates exemplary motivational interviewing practice across multiple domains. The psychiatrist's empathic accuracy is exceptional—reflections consistently capture emotional nuance, isolation, and ambivalence with sophistication that goes beyond simple parroting. The clinician's partnership stance is genuine and pervasive; Luis is positioned as the expert on his own life from the opening question through final planning, and the clinician actively avoids the expert role despite clinical authority. Autonomy support is consistently strong, with repeated affirmation of Luis's right to choose and respect for his self-knowledge. The clinician skillfully handles Luis's ambivalence and sustain talk through validation and strategic pivoting rather than confrontation or dismissal. The session structure is logical and progressive, moving from exploration through identification of past successes to concrete, manageable first steps. Most importantly, the clinician embodies the MI spirit throughout—genuine curiosity about Luis's perspective, unconditional positive regard, and collaborative problem-solving.

### Areas for Improvement

While this is strong MI practice, several opportunities exist for deeper skill application. The clinician could more strategically amplify Luis's change talk through deeper questioning. When Luis expresses desire ("I don't wanna be hooked on all this stuff forever"), the clinician affirms but doesn't ask follow-up questions that would deepen and expand this motivation. Similarly, when Luis demonstrates ability ("I could probably manage it"), the clinician could ask "What makes you think you could manage it?" to strengthen his confidence. The clinician also misses an opportunity to reframe Luis's competing priorities (work, family, bills) as potentially aligned with rather than opposed to asthma management—exploring how better asthma control might actually support his ability to handle these other demands. When Luis expresses doubt or skepticism, the clinician sometimes moves to scaling questions rather than exploring the specific sustain talk underlying his concern. Additionally, while the clinician identifies some of Luis's values (autonomy, freedom), deeper exploration of other values (family, work performance, longevity, modeling health) could provide additional fuel for motivation. Finally, the clinician doesn't explicitly highlight Luis's own change talk progression (from minimization to willingness to try), which could reinforce his movement toward change.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

This session is highly likely to increase Luis's engagement and intrinsic motivation for change. The psychiatrist's genuine partnership stance directly addresses Luis's stated need for "someone who's not just another doctor telling me what to do but actually helps me figure out what works for me." By positioning Luis as the expert and drawing out his own ideas, the clinician avoids the resistance that typically emerges when authority figures impose solutions. Luis's willingness to try tracking (confidence rating of 6/10) and his identification of concrete, manageable first steps suggest genuine movement toward change. The clinician's validation of his overwhelm and competing priorities likely reduces defensiveness and increases openness. However, the session could have generated even stronger intrinsic motivation through deeper amplification of Luis's change talk and more explicit connection between his competing priorities and asthma management benefits. Luis's importance rating remains at 4/10, which suggests moderate rather than strong motivation—deeper exploration of values and reframing of competing priorities could have moved this higher. Overall, this session successfully establishes a collaborative foundation for change and generates concrete behavioral commitment, though there's room to deepen Luis's intrinsic motivation.

1. **Strategically amplify change talk** through deeper questioning when Luis expresses desire, ability, reasons, or commitment
2. **Reframe competing priorities** as aligned with rather than opposed to asthma management goals
3. **Explore underlying sustain talk** more thoroughly rather than moving quickly past doubt and skepticism
4. **Deepen values exploration** to connect asthma management to what truly matters to Luis
5. **Explicitly highlight change talk progression** to reinforce Luis's movement from minimization to willingness
6. **Use scaling questions more strategically** to explore both what's keeping importance at 4/10 and what would move it higher
