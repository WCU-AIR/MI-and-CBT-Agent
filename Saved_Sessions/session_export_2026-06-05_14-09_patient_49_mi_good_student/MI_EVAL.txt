## Critical Recommendations for Improvement

**1. Prioritize Evocation Over Education**
Before offering any strategies or psychoeducation, exhaust evocative questions to draw out client's own ideas first. Ask at least 3–5 open questions before suggesting anything. Example: Instead of providing strategies, ask: "You mentioned that small goals worked before and that walking during breaks could fit your schedule. What other ways might you weave movement into things you're already doing?"

**2. Deepen Cultivation of Change Talk**
When client produces change talk, follow up with questions that strengthen motivation language. Example: When client says "I want to feel better, have more energy," ask: "What would having more energy allow you to do? How would that change your life?" Then reflect the deepened response.

**3. Maintain True Partnership Throughout**
Avoid shifting into expert mode. Position client as the expert on their own life. Use language like "What do you think?" "You know yourself best—what feels right?" Avoid phrases like "I'd suggest" or "Research shows."

**4. Explore Values and Intrinsic Motivation**
Move beyond behavioral strategies to explore what truly matters to the client. Ask: "Beyond just having more energy, what does being healthy mean to you? What's important to you about that?" Connect exercise to deeper values.

**5. Explicitly Affirm Autonomy, Including Right to Fail**
When client expresses uncertainty, validate their right to choose differently or fail. Example: "That's real—you might try this and find it doesn't work, and that's completely okay. You get to decide what's right for you."
