## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.9/5 – POOR**

**Key Strengths:**
- Clear opening with open question inviting client to identify own goal
- Maintains focus on target behavior (exercise) without significant drift
- Uses importance/confidence ruler (evocative MI tool)
- Does not reinforce or argue with sustain talk
- Provides concrete, evidence-based suggestions

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Severely directive and prescriptive stance** fundamentally inconsistent with MI principles
- **Pressuring language** ("you might want to," "you should," "push through") undermines autonomy and collaboration
- **Minimal empathic exploration** of ambivalence, fears, and emotional experience
- **No genuine partnership** – clinician as unilateral expert, client as passive recipient
- **Missed opportunities** to evoke and amplify strong intrinsic change talk
- **Fear-based motivation** ("if you don't, you might never get started") inconsistent with MI spirit
- **Unsolicited advice** about nutrition further positions clinician as expert

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This directive, pressuring approach likely undermines client's intrinsic motivation despite strong initial desire for change. Client's low confidence (5/10) is met with pressure rather than exploration, potentially increasing self-doubt. By removing client agency and providing complete plan without input, psychiatrist reduces sense of ownership and self-efficacy. Research demonstrates directive advice-giving often triggers reactance and reduces intrinsic motivation, particularly in clients with ambivalence or low confidence.

**Session does not embody MI spirit** of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. Reflects traditional medical model with clinician as expert and client as passive recipient.

---

### Recommendations for Improvement (Priority Order):

1. **Shift from expert to collaborative stance** – Reframe role from providing solutions to exploring client's own ideas and resources
2. **Develop deeper empathic reflections** – Explore ambivalence with complex, double-sided reflections capturing internal conflict
3. **Amplify and reinforce change talk** – Identify and strategically deepen client's own reasons for change through follow-up questions
4. **Support autonomy** – Replace prescriptive language with autonomy-supportive choices; emphasize client control
5. **Explore low confidence through evocation, not pressure** – Use curiosity to build confidence; ask about strengths and past successes
6. **Avoid unsolicited advice** – Invite client's ideas first; only offer suggestions after exploring client's own thinking
7. **Use reflective listening to validate concerns** – Reflect fears and doubts with empathy before problem-solving
8. **Co-construct plans collaboratively** – Build plan together based on client input, increasing ownership and autonomy
9. **Explore values and meaning** – Connect exercise goal to deeper values (comfort, confidence, self-care) to strengthen intrinsic motivation
10. **Eliminate fear-based motivation** – Replace pressure with collaborative exploration of client's own reasons for change

---

**Recommendation:** Focused supervision and practice on core MI competencies essential. Clinician must fundamentally shift from expert-driven to client-centered collaborative approach to effectively support client's intrinsic motivation and sustainable behavior change.
