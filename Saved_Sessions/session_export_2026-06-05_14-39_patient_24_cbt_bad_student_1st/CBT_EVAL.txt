## SUPERVISOR SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- **Warmth and Safety:** Therapist created non-threatening environment; patient felt comfortable disclosing symptoms and reported feeling heard.
- **Engagement:** Patient remained engaged throughout; responded positively to therapist's friendliness.
- **Non-Judgmental Stance:** Therapist did not pathologize or shame patient for experiences.
- **Availability:** Therapist was present and attentive during session.

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Complete Absence of Agenda-Setting:** No session structure, goals, or priorities established.
- **No CBT Conceptualization:** Therapist never articulated understanding of brief psychotic disorder or treatment strategy.
- **Failure to Address Delusional Content:** Core pathology (parasitosis delusions, visual hallucinations) never directly addressed or reality-tested.
- **Missed Maintaining Factors:** Sleep deprivation, compulsive behaviors, stressors, and isolation not systematically explored or targeted.
- **Excessive Self-Disclosure:** Personal anecdotes consumed 40-50% of session time; undermined professional authority.
- **Minimal CBT Technique Application:** Generic reassurance and cheerleading replaced systematic cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, exposure, or behavioral experiments.
- **No Homework:** No structured between-session tasks assigned.
- **Lack of Collaboration:** Patient positioned as passive recipient; no shared problem-solving or collaborative empiricism.
- **No Feedback Elicitation:** Therapist never checked whether suggestions were understood, credible, or helpful.
- **Poor Time Management:** Critical clinical issues not prioritized; time wasted on tangential content.

### Immediate Recommendations:

1. **STOP excessive self-disclosure immediately.** This undermines professional authority and wastes session time.
2. **Establish clear agenda and treatment strategy for every session.** Session must have structure and purpose.
3. **Directly address delusional content through gentle reality-testing.** This is core of clinical work.
4. **Apply CBT techniques systematically.** Generic reassurance is insufficient.
5. **Assign specific, rationale-based homework.** This extends learning and tests beliefs between sessions.
6. **Pursue specialized training in CBT for Psychosis (CBTp).** This therapist requires intensive training before continuing to treat psychotic disorders.
7. **Arrange weekly supervision with CBTp expert.** Case consultation essential.
8. **Pause new psychosis cases** until training and supervision established.

### Competency Assessment:
**Current Level:** Supportive counseling/peer support. **Required Level:** Evidence-based CBT with specialization in psychotic disorders. **Gap:** Significant. **Timeline for Competency:** 6-12 months with intensive training and supervision.

**CRITICAL NOTE:** This patient's safety and recovery depend on receiving competent, evidence-based treatment. Current therapy does not meet that standard. Immediate intervention required.
