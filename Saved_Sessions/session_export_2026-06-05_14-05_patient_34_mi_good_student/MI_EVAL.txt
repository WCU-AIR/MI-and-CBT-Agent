### Empathy

Complex early reflection about relief and competing safety concerns shows sophisticated understanding. ### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear, appropriate focus on harm reduction throughout session. Strategic questioning progressively clarifies direction (what tried, what information needed, what resources helpful, what concrete steps possible). However, session lacks explicit agenda-setting or collaborative agreement about session goals. Psychiatrist does not proactively explore potential barriers or obstacles to stated goals. More strategic direction would include anticipatory problem-solving about what might prevent follow-through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaborative stance with opening question positioning Diego as expert on priorities. Consistent invitation of Diego's input and building on his ideas rather than imposing direction. However, psychiatrist occasionally slips into subtle expert-driven positioning with evaluative language ("powerful place to start," "solid foundation"). Does not explicitly invite Diego into co-creating plan for accessing resources. True partnership could be strengthened by reducing implicit expert judgment about Diego's progress.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates exemplary autonomy support—a significant strength. Explicitly respects Diego's choice to continue using while pursuing harm reduction without subtle pressure toward abstinence. Consistently emphasizes Diego's freedom to choose and ownership of change process. Language throughout affirms Diego's agency ("you're taking control," "you're showing thoughtfulness"). Does not attempt to convince Diego to change faster than ready. Avoids prescriptive language, instead asking what Diego thinks would be helpful. This is exceptional MI practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates basic evocation through open questions but lacks depth and consistency. Does not deeply explore Diego's intrinsic motivations, underlying values, or what he hopes for his life. Limited exploration of broader coping resources or resilience beyond harm reduction logistics. When Diego identifies strengths, psychiatrist does not deeply explore or amplify them through follow-up questions. Does not evoke Diego's own problem-solving abilities regarding how to approach conversation with housemate. Significant opportunity to draw out Diego's own wisdom and capacity for change remains underutilized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong partnership positioning Diego as expert on his own situation. Consistent validation of Diego's contributions and building on his ideas. Explicitly affirms Diego's understanding of what he needs. However, partnership could be strengthened by reducing subtle expert positioning in evaluative statements. Does not explicitly invite collaborative problem-solving about potential barriers or obstacles. Could more actively position psychiatrist and Diego as true partners in planning rather than psychiatrist affirming Diego's plan from slightly elevated position.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates inconsistent and somewhat passive cultivation of change talk. Diego spontaneously offers substantial change talk (10/10 readiness, commitment to steps), but psychiatrist does not strategically amplify or reinforce through targeted questioning. When Diego expresses 10/10 readiness, psychiatrist affirms but does not deepen with exploratory follow-up. Scaling questions underutilized—does not ask "what would move you from 8 to 9?" or "what's helping you feel as confident as 8?" Limited exploration of Diego's values or reasons for change. Significant opportunity to strengthen motivation through strategic change talk cultivation remains missed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates generally skillful handling of Diego's sustain talk through double-sided reflections acknowledging both positive function of heroin (relief, escape) and competing safety concerns. Does not reinforce or amplify sustain talk; instead gently pivots toward change talk while holding both sides. However, could be more strategic by exploring limitations of heroin as coping strategy ("at least for a while"—what happens after?). Does not proactively explore potential barriers or sustain talk that might emerge as obstacles. Could offer alternative coping strategies for difficult thoughts Diego is escaping from. Overall approach avoids direct confrontation while maintaining focus.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.9/5**

### Key Strengths
- **Exemplary autonomy support:** Consistently respects Diego's choice to continue using while pursuing harm reduction; explicitly affirms his right to make own decisions
- **Strong collaborative stance:** Positions Diego as expert; invites input throughout; builds on his ideas rather than imposing direction
- **Solid empathy and acceptance:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with accurate reflections capturing ambivalence and validating existing safety efforts
- **Skillful sustain talk handling:** Uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge relief while highlighting safety concerns without direct confrontation
- **Clear harm-reduction focus:** Maintains consistent, appropriate direction through strategic questioning

### Areas for Improvement
- **Insufficient evocation of intrinsic motivation:** Lacks depth in drawing out underlying values, reasons for change, broader coping resources, and problem-solving abilities
- **Passive change talk cultivation:** Does not strategically amplify, elaborate, or deepen Diego's spontaneous change talk through targeted questioning
- **Limited exploration of underlying pain:** Does not deeply explore "difficult thoughts" Diego escapes from or offer alternative coping strategies
- **Subtle expert positioning:** Occasionally uses evaluative language that positions psychiatrist as evaluator of progress rather than maintaining true partnership
- **Insufficient barrier anticipation:** Does not proactively explore obstacles to stated goals or invite collaborative problem-solving about potential challenges
- **Lack of explicit agenda-setting:** No collaborative agreement about what will be accomplished by session end

### Clinical Impact
The psychiatrist successfully engaged Diego and maintained his openness, with sustained high confidence ratings (9-10/10) and specific commitment to concrete steps. Strong autonomy support and collaborative stance likely contributed to Diego feeling respected and heard—critical for engagement with client expressing fear of judgment. However, session's impact on deeper, more sustained change motivation may be limited. Focus on harm-reduction logistics without exploring values, hopes, and underlying pain may have missed opportunity to strengthen intrinsic motivation. Without proactive barrier exploration, Diego may encounter obstacles he has not mentally rehearsed, potentially reducing likelihood that stated intentions translate to action.

---

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist demonstrates solid MI competence with particular strengths in autonomy support and collaboration. To advance to exemplary practice, focus on: (1) deepening evocation of values and intrinsic motivation, (2) strategically amplifying change talk rather than passively affirming, (3) proactively exploring barriers and sustain talk, and (4) reducing subtle expert positioning while strengthening true partnership. With these refinements, psychiatrist would deliver MI that strengthens both immediate harm-reduction goals and Diego's deeper motivation for change and recognition of his own capacity for growth.
