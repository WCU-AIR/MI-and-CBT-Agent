## Overall Session Assessment

### **Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

---

### Strengths
- Maintains topical focus on health behavior change
- Persistent about the change goal despite patient resistance

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**Fundamental Deficiencies:**

1. **Complete absence of MI spirit:** No partnership, acceptance, compassion, or evocation. Psychiatrist is adversarial rather than collaborative.

2. **Systematic rejection of client autonomy:** Patient repeatedly told they "don't get to negotiate." Coercive practice throughout.

3. **Ignoring critical clinical information:** Patient explicitly states pressure triggers avoidance and anxiety. This crucial information is treated as irrelevant rather than as vital clinical data about what will and won't work.

4. **No reflective listening:** Psychiatrist never reflects back what patient has said or demonstrates empathic understanding.

5. **Escalating pressure and threats:** Language designed to manipulate through fear and shame rather than support intrinsic motivation.

6. **Negation of spontaneous change talk:** Patient's expressions of desire to change are met with dismissal rather than amplification.

7. **Rejection of collaborative problem-solving:** When patient suggests smaller steps, psychiatrist responds "No, no, no" rather than exploring viability.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement

This session is likely to produce the opposite of intended effect. Patient has explicitly stated that pressure triggers avoidance and anxiety escalation. Psychiatrist's response is to escalate pressure and coercion—a direct mismatch between client's stated needs and clinician's approach. Patient is likely to leave feeling unheard, disrespected, and more anxious. Likelihood of behavior change is substantially reduced. Client will likely disengage from treatment.

---

### Clinical Recommendation

**Immediate Action Required:** This psychiatrist does not demonstrate competence in motivational interviewing. The approach is antithetical to MI principles and contraindicated for this client. Recommend:

- Comprehensive MI training or retraining (MINT-certified program)
- Supervised practice with MI-trained supervisor
- Regular session recording and review
- Reassignment of this client to clinician trained in evidence-based, collaborative approaches

**Clinical Concern:** The psychiatrist's failure to adjust approach when client explicitly stated that pressure triggers avoidance raises serious concerns about clinical judgment and adherence to evidence-based practice.
