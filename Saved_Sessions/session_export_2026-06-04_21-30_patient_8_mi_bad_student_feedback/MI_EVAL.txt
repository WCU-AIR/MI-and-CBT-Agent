### **SUMMARY ASSESSMENT**

**Severity Level:** CRITICAL DEFICIENCIES

This session demonstrates **severe failures across all motivational interviewing domains** and does not embody the spirit of MI. The psychiatrist operates from a traditional medical model (expert-driven, agenda-focused) rather than an MI model (client-centered, evocative, collaborative).

**Most Critical Issue:** The psychiatrist violates Amina's autonomy by continuing to probe after she explicitly sets a boundary twice ("It's not really any of your business"). This is not a minor technical error—it is a fundamental breach of the therapeutic alliance and MI spirit. For a resistant, precontemplative adolescent who likely already distrusts authority figures, this approach will deepen defensiveness and decrease likelihood of engagement or change.

**Key Failures:**
- ☐ Boundary violation and autonomy undermining
- ☐ Absence of genuine empathy and reflective listening
- ☐ Hierarchical, expert-driven stance with no partnership
- ☐ Premature focus on change without rapport-building
- ☐ Scripted, repetitive approach lacking dynamic responsiveness
- ☐ No evocation of client's own motivations or values

**Strengths (Limited):**
- ☐ Warm, non-hostile tone maintained throughout
- ☐ Persistence in offering therapeutic relationship despite minimal response
- ☐ Explicit reassurance attempts to normalize discomfort

**Clinical Impact:** Amina's minimal responses ("Mm-hmm," "Okay," "Fine") suggest compliance with emotional withdrawal. Continued probing after boundary-setting will likely increase resistance and entrench distrust. **Likelihood of evoking intrinsic motivation for change: Very Low.**

**Recommendation:** This clinician requires **immediate, intensive training in motivational interviewing**, including formal MI certification, weekly supervision with MI-trained clinician, and deliberate practice with recorded feedback. Without significant skill development, risk of iatrogenic harm is substantial.
