### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with frequent invitations for client input ("What do you think?" "What would you want?"). Positions client as expert on own experience. Uses inclusive language suggesting partnership. However, collaboration could strengthen by: (1) following up on 0-10 importance scale with "What would move that number higher?" to co-construct goals, and (2) inviting collaborative problem-solving when client expresses doubt about intervention efficacy rather than accepting skepticism passively.

### Autonomy Support

**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout session. Consistently emphasizes client choice and self-direction from opening question onward. Explicitly affirms client's right to own timeline ("It's okay that the motivation feels different; sometimes it takes time for that feeling to build"). Respects client's pace by offering small, manageable steps rather than pressuring major change. Uses language emphasizing client ownership ("if you decided," "if any, do you think you might be willing"). No pressure or persuasion detected. Clear strength of session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic evocation through open-ended questions but lacks depth and strategic consistency. Asks about past successes with smoking cessation but does not adequately deepen responses. Critical gap: does not evoke change talk around alcohol use—misses opportunities to ask "What do you already know about alcohol and blood pressure?" or "What would be benefits of reducing your drinking?" When client spontaneously suggests reducing alcohol, psychiatrist does not amplify this change talk or explore underlying motivation. Does not systematically explore client values, which are central to MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership through power-sharing and client-centered language. Positions client as equal contributor rather than assuming expert role. Validates client experience even when it differs from clinical expectations. However, partnership could strengthen by: (1) more collaborative exploration of client skepticism rather than passive acceptance, and (2) reducing reliance on clinician-directed assessment tools (0-10 scale) without collaborative follow-up. Could invite more explicit co-investigation of client doubts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Client produces change talk statements ("I want to understand," "I'd want it to get back to normal," "Maybe cutting back on the drinks a little") but psychiatrist does not strategically amplify or reinforce these. When client spontaneously suggests reducing alcohol—key change talk—psychiatrist does not recognize significance or deepen it. Does not ask "Tell me more about that idea" or explore underlying motivation. Reflections validate but do not invite elaboration. Does not strategically evoke specific types of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Minimal reinforcement of client's own motivations represents critical gap limiting session effectiveness.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent skill. Successfully validates some sustain talk through double-sided reflections (e.g., "It sounds like even though readings are higher...you're feeling pretty well"). However, critical gaps exist: when client expresses strong skepticism ("I don't think it's going to change much for my blood pressure"), psychiatrist does not address this sustain talk or strategically redirect—simply moves to problem-solving. Does not explore whether "just a little less" represents genuine commitment or defensive minimization. Allows client's doubt to remain unexamined, potentially undermining future change efforts. Inconsistency in managing resistance represents moderate gap in MI technique.

## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- ✓ Exemplary autonomy support with consistent emphasis on client choice and respect for timeline
- ✓ Strong acceptance demonstrated through unconditional positive regard and affirmation of strengths
- ✓ Good collaborative stance with frequent invitations for client input
- ✓ Solid reflective listening capturing content and emotional nuance
- ✓ Clear session direction with progressive structure
- ✓ Appropriate pacing respecting client readiness

**Areas for Improvement:**
- ✗ **Minimal cultivation of change talk:** Client statements favoring change not strategically amplified or reinforced
- ✗ **Inconsistent management of sustain talk:** Critical skepticism not adequately addressed or redirected
- ✗ **Limited exploration of values:** Questions remain surface-level; deeper motivation not systematically explored
- ✗ **Missed proactive linking of alcohol to blood pressure:** Connection introduced late rather than strategically early
- ✗ **Insufficient deepening of change talk:** Validation present but elaboration and exploration limited
- ✗ **Limited exploration of behavior function:** Emotional functions of current behaviors (alcohol for relaxation) not explored

---

## Clinical Impact

This session demonstrates solid foundational MI competence, particularly in autonomy support and acceptance, creating a safe, non-judgmental environment. However, minimal cultivation of change talk and inconsistent management of sustain talk significantly limit potential to build intrinsic motivation.

Client leaves with fragile, tentative commitment to reduce alcohol by one beer daily, accompanied by explicit skepticism ("I don't think it's going to change much for my blood pressure"). Psychiatrist has not adequately addressed doubt or helped client develop compelling personal rationale for change. Without stronger evocation of motivation and strategic management of skepticism, likelihood of sustained behavior change is reduced.

Session succeeds in establishing rapport and avoiding confrontation but falls short of MI goal of evoking and strengthening intrinsic motivation. Client's ambivalence remains largely unresolved.

---

## Recommendations for Development

1. **Strategically amplify change talk** through follow-up questions deepening client statements favoring change
2. **Address sustain talk skillfully** using double-sided reflections and strategic reframing rather than simple validation
3. **Systematically explore values** connecting them to potential change for compelling personal rationale
4. **Proactively explore alcohol-blood pressure link** through evocative questions early in session
5. **Explore function of current behaviors** to understand ambivalence and identify replacement needs
6. **Use scaling questions strategically** with follow-up exploration of what would move numbers higher
7. **Verify goals are client-generated** by clarifying source of suggestions
8. **Develop deeper reflections** capturing emotional and relational nuance beyond surface content
9. **Strengthen commitment language** as sessions progress while maintaining autonomy support
10. **Integrate psychoeducation skillfully** within MI framework by asking permission and maintaining collaborative tone
