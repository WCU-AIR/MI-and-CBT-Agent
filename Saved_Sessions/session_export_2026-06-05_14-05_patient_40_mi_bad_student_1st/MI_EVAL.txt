## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Clinical Summary:**

This session represents a fundamental departure from evidence-based Motivational Interviewing practice. The psychiatrist demonstrates a confrontational, authoritarian clinical approach that violates core MI principles across nearly all domains. While the clinician maintains clear focus on health behaviors, the manner of maintaining that focus is fundamentally incompatible with MI and is contraindicated for patients with ambivalence about behavior change.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- **Complete absence of MI spirit:** Violates all four pillars of MI (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation)
- **Systematic undermining of autonomy:** Explicitly denies patient's right to participate in treatment planning
- **Suppression of change talk:** Dismisses patient's genuine motivation as "excuses" and "doubting yourself"
- **Confrontational approach:** Uses confrontation which research shows increases resistance rather than softening it
- **Failure to explore patient perspective:** Never asks open-ended questions to understand patient's experience, values, or concerns
- **Dismissal of legitimate limitations:** Frames realistic concerns as character flaws rather than valid information

**Impact on Patient Engagement:**

This session is likely to produce the opposite of intended effect. Patient enters with genuine motivation ("I want to get better," "It was very, very beautiful") and realistic self-awareness. By session end, psychiatrist has invalidated concerns, labeled realistic self-assessment as "excuses," eliminated sense of agency, suppressed change talk, and increased resistance through confrontation. Patient's final response ("I'm just not sure I can") suggests entrenchment rather than movement toward change.

**Likelihood of Adherence:** Low. Research demonstrates patients who experience clinician as controlling and dismissive of autonomy are significantly less likely to adhere to recommendations. Patient is likely to leave feeling misunderstood, invalidated, and defensive—conditions that predict non-adherence.

**Recommendation:** This clinician requires formal Motivational Interviewing training and supervised practice before continuing to use MI-based interventions. Current approach, while well-intentioned, is likely to harm patient outcomes. Recommend enrollment in MINT-certified MI training program and ongoing supervision from MI-trained clinician.
