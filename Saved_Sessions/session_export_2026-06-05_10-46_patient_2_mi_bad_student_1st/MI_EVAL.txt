### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk, which strengthens it rather than softens it. Each confrontation causes patient to defend position more firmly and elaborate reasoning. Creates debate dynamic that patient must win by defending smoking. ## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.0/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **complete failure of Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist operates from a directive, confrontational, expert-driven model that is explicitly contraindicated for behavior change work and violates evidence-based practice standards for smoking cessation counseling.

**Session Embodies MI Spirit:** No. The spirit of MI is partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. This session demonstrates authoritarianism, judgment, contempt, and imposition—the antithesis of MI spirit.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** Very low. Patient explicitly refuses to quit ("no, I won't quit right now"), rejects the approach ("this kind of 'end of discussion' approach doesn't really work for me"), and indicates he will disengage from this provider ("I need to figure this out on my own terms"). Research consistently demonstrates that confrontational, directive approaches are less effective than collaborative, evocative approaches for behavior change. This session likely decreased patient motivation and damaged the therapeutic alliance.

**Clinical Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires immediate, intensive training in Motivational Interviewing before continuing to provide smoking cessation counseling. A fundamental shift in clinical philosophy and technique—from expert/authority to collaborative partnership—is necessary. Current approach is likely to harm patient outcomes.
