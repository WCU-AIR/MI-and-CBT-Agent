## IMPACT ON CLIENT ENGAGEMENT AND CHANGE

Session is unlikely to increase Luis's intrinsic motivation for change. While Luis agrees to small step (carrying inhaler by keys), this agreement appears to reflect compliance with authority figure rather than genuine commitment rooted in his own values and reasons. Psychiatrist's directive approach may actually reinforce Luis's resistance and defensiveness. Luis has already stated he does not feel understood and that being told what to do reduces his willingness to listen. By continuing this pattern, psychiatrist risks deepening Luis's alienation from healthcare system and his resistance to asthma management.

Session misses opportunity to connect asthma management to Luis's actual priorities (family time, avoiding medical burden). If psychiatrist had explored how better asthma control might protect Luis's ability to spend time with family without frequent medical appointments, this could have generated intrinsic motivation. Instead, psychiatrist focuses narrowly on inhaler use and reminders, which Luis experiences as burdensome and disconnected from his life.

Likelihood of sustained behavior change is low. While Luis may carry inhaler for short period, absence of genuine motivation rooted in his own values and continued expert-driven dynamic suggest this change will not be sustained. Luis will likely return to previous patterns once external pressure (psychiatrist's recommendations) is removed.

## KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

**1. Shift from Expert-Driven to Collaborative Stance (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
- Fundamentally reframe role from "expert who tells client what to do" to "collaborative partner who helps client explore his own motivations and solutions"
- Replace: "Maybe you should try to use your inhaler more regularly" with "What's your sense of what might help you manage your asthma in a way that actually fits your life?"
- Practice: Record sessions and count prescriptions vs. evocations; aim for 1:3 ratio

**2. Evoke and Amplify Change Talk Systematically**
- When Luis produces change talk, pause and deepen it rather than moving on
- Use follow-up questions to strengthen his own language about change
- Identify every instance of change talk and ask at least one follow-up question to deepen it

**3. Connect Asthma Management to Luis's Own Values and Priorities**
- Explore what matters most to Luis (family, work, independence)
- Help him see how asthma management serves those values
- Ask: "How might managing your asthma help you protect those things that matter?"

**4. Explicitly Affirm Autonomy and Client Ownership**
- Use language emphasizing Luis's right to choose: "It's your decision," "You get to decide," "You're the expert on what you can handle"
- When Luis sets boundary, explicitly respect it
- Use phrase "It's your choice" at least three times per session

**5. Use Double-Sided Reflection to Address Sustain Talk**
- When Luis minimizes risk, acknowledge both sides of ambivalence without arguing
- Example: "On one hand, you've managed this for years without major problems. On the other hand, you mentioned sometimes feeling tight in your chest—I wonder if there's a way to prevent those uncomfortable moments while keeping things simple"

**6. Ask Open Questions and Wait for Luis's Ideas Before Offering Suggestions**
- Replace closed questions with open questions
- Ask Luis for his ideas first; only offer suggestions if he asks or exhausts his own ideas
- Aim for at least 70% open questions

**7. Recognize and Affirm Luis's Efforts and Strengths**
- When Luis mentions efforts (reading about asthma, trying to manage symptoms), explicitly recognize and affirm as evidence of commitment and capability
- Identify at least three instances of Luis's effort or strength per session and explicitly affirm each one

**8. Explore Luis's Concerns About Identity and Burden**
- When Luis expresses that inhaler feels like "reminder that I'm sick," explore this identity concern rather than dismissing it
- Help him see asthma management as compatible with his identity as capable, independent person
- Ask: "How do you see yourself? I wonder if there's a way to manage your asthma that doesn't feel like it defines you"

**9. Establish Collaborative Goals at Start of Session**
- Begin by asking Luis what he wants to work on, rather than assuming asthma management is target
- Spend first 5-10 minutes collaboratively establishing agenda
- Write down what Luis says he wants to work on and use as focus for session

**10. Develop Genuine Curiosity About Luis's Perspective**
- Approach Luis with authentic curiosity about his life, priorities, concerns, and ideas
- Ask questions you genuinely want to know answers to, not questions designed to confirm your hypothesis
- Before session, remind yourself: "I don't know Luis's life. I need to understand it from his perspective. I'm genuinely curious about how he sees things"
