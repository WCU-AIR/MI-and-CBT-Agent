### Acceptance

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated throughout. Accurate early reflections capture emotional nuance ("That pain feels like an unwelcome guest that's interfering"). Effective use of metaphorical language ("persistent, unwelcome shadow") deepens Adrian's self-understanding. Clinician accurately identifies competing motivations and tracks Adrian's values. However, opportunities for deeper empathic understanding are missed—when Adrian expresses difficulty feeling urgency ("It's hard to feel like it's serious enough all the time"), the psychiatrist reflects accurately but doesn't explore the underlying tension between intellectual recognition and emotional felt sense. More complex reflections addressing this ambivalence would demonstrate enhanced empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriate strategic focus maintained on pain management and underlying substance use. Session progresses logically: pain → frustration → importance → confidence → past successes → concrete plan. Collaborative goal-setting established through opening question. However, significant gap exists: the psychiatrist never explicitly names methamphetamine use as the target behavior. Conversation circles around it using indirect language ("your thing on weekends," "doing it one night instead of both," "the usual"), creating ambiguity about what Adrian is actually committing to change. Direct clarification would strengthen direction and accountability. Connection between pain and substance use acknowledged but not deeply explored as motivational lever.

### Autonomy Support

Ambivalence validated without pressure ("It's like the challenge is finding that motivation that feels strong enough on the tough days"). Clinician affirms Adrian's choice to cut back rather than pushing for abstinence, demonstrating respect for his pace and approach. ### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate evocation demonstrated with notable gaps in strategic amplification. Foundational evocation present: clinician asks what matters most and invites identification of concerns. Excellent question about past successes ("Can you tell me about a time when you were able to make a change?") allows Adrian to generate own evidence of capability. However, multiple missed opportunities for deeper evocation: when Adrian rates confidence at 5, clinician reflects but doesn't ask what would move him higher—classic evocation technique. Adrian's reasons for change (enjoy weekends, avoid complications, return to normal) identified but not deeply explored through follow-up questions ("What else matters to you about your health?" "Who would notice if things got better?"). Adrian's own ideas about barriers and solutions not thoroughly evoked. Change talk present but not strategically amplified through follow-up questions about importance, confidence, or commitment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate cultivation of change talk with Adrian generating several statements favoring change ("I don't want this infection or whatever to mess me up more down the line," "Maybe I could try not to use as much on the weekends"). However, clinician's responses primarily reflective rather than strategically amplifying. When Adrian expresses change talk, follow-up questions that would deepen motivation are absent. Clinician validates but doesn't ask "Tell me more about that concern" or "How worried are you about that happening?" When Adrian proposes cutting back to one night, psychiatrist validates but doesn't strengthen commitment through targeted questions about benefits or confidence. Open questions that would evoke additional change talk underutilized ("What concerns you most about continuing at current level?"). Confidence scale not used strategically—when Adrian rates at 5, clinician reflects but doesn't ask "What would help you move from 5 to 6?" Adrian later spontaneously increases to 6-7, but clinician didn't strategically evoke this shift. Affirmations of change talk present but amplification techniques limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate management of sustain talk without confrontation, which is appropriate for MI, but strategic redirection and reframing opportunities missed. Adrian produces sustain talk throughout ("It's not like a big deal," "I just do my thing on weekends," "worried I might slip up or weekends might get boring"). When Adrian minimizes use, clinician doesn't directly address minimization; instead reflects pain experience, subtly shifting focus without explicit challenge. When Adrian expresses worry about slipping up or boredom, psychiatrist validates but doesn't strategically reframe or explore deeply. Clinician could ask "What do you think you could do if you start feeling bored?" or "What's worked for you in the past when tempted?" to shift focus to Adrian's problem-solving. Double-sided reflections not used strategically to acknowledge sustain talk without amplifying it. Underlying functions of weekend use (boredom relief, social connection, fun) not explored, missing opportunity to identify alternative need-meeting strategies. Clinician avoids direct confrontation appropriately but management remains passive rather than actively strategic.

## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative stance with consistent invitation of Adrian's input and respect for his agenda
- Consistent acceptance and warmth with genuine, behavior-specific affirmations
- Solid reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and effective metaphorical language
- Appropriate autonomy support respecting Adrian's pace and choices without pressure
- Logical session structure progressing from pain identification through motivation to concrete planning
- Excellent opening question establishing Adrian as expert on his priorities
- Effective validation of ambivalence without judgment
- Respect for Adrian's proposed plan without pushing for abstinence

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Target behavior never explicitly named:** Lack of direct statement about methamphetamine use reduction creates ambiguity about change commitment
- **Insufficient strategic evocation:** Multiple missed opportunities to ask follow-up questions deepening Adrian's motivations, values, and problem-solving abilities
- **Weak change talk cultivation:** Primarily reflective rather than strategically amplifying through targeted questions about importance and confidence
- **Passive sustain talk management:** Avoids confrontation appropriately but misses opportunities for strategic reframing and barrier exploration
- **Limited exploration of underlying motivations:** Functions of weekend use (boredom relief, social connection) not explored; alternative coping strategies not developed
- **Confidence scale underutilized:** When Adrian rates at 5, clinician doesn't ask what would increase confidence—missed evocation opportunity
- **Subtle clinician approval language:** Phrases like "I really admire..." could subtly pressure Adrian toward compliance rather than intrinsic motivation
- **Limited active co-construction:** Clinician reflects Adrian's ideas rather than actively problem-solving collaboratively

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
Session demonstrates solid foundational MI skills with successful collaborative engagement. Adrian moves from confidence level 5 to 6-7 and generates own change plan, indicating positive short-term impact. However, moderate rather than optimal likelihood of sustained behavior change due to: ambiguity about target behavior, insufficient depth of change talk cultivation, unexplored underlying functions of substance use, and unaddressed ambivalence about losing "fun" of weekend use. Adrian more likely to attempt plan but may struggle maintaining it without deeper exploration of values and alternative coping strategies.
