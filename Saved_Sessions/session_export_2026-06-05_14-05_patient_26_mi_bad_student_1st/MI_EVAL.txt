Psychiatrist uses threat-based pressure ("otherwise you're just setting yourself up for failure"), binary ultimatums ("are you going to do it or not?"), and urgency-based coercion ("before it's too late"). ## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **critical failure in motivational interviewing implementation**. The psychiatrist operates from a fundamentally non-MI stance characterized by authoritarianism, judgment, pressure, and systematic dismissal of client input. Rather than evoking intrinsic motivation, the approach suppresses it and generates resistance. The session violates core MI principles across all nine domains.

**Current MI Competency:** Minimal (1.1/5). While the psychiatrist maintains focus on health behaviors, this does not constitute MI practice. The session lacks the fundamental spirit of MI—partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation.

**Impact on Client Engagement:** The client entered with genuine openness to change, clear intrinsic values (family), and demonstrated capability (smoking cessation). The psychiatrist's approach has converted this into explicit resistance and defensive positioning. Client is likely to disengage from treatment or comply superficially without genuine behavior change.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. **Develop genuine reflective listening and empathy skills**
3. **Eliminate confrontational language and pressure**
4. **Learn to evoke rather than impose motivation**
5. **Seek formal MI training and clinical supervision immediately**

**Immediate Action Required:** The psychiatrist should not continue this approach without significant retraining. Formal MI training and supervision are essential before continuing to deliver MI-based counseling.
