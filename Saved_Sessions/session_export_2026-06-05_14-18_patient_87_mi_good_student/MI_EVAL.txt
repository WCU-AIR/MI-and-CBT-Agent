# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard and validates Marco's desire to avoid pressure throughout the session. Explicitly affirms autonomy and normalizes ambivalence as "holding two truths at once." However, acceptance could be deepened by sitting longer with Marco's initial resistance before exploring readiness, and by including more explicit affirmation of his inherent worth beyond change potential. Subtle moments exist where the clinician moves relatively quickly past Marco's doubt rather than fully validating its legitimacy.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary empathic accuracy demonstrated throughout. Psychiatrist uses sophisticated, nuanced reflective listening that captures both surface content and deeper emotional meaning. Complex reflections such as "smoking holds a lot of comfort and routine for you—it's almost like a steady companion" and "holding two truths at once" demonstrate genuine understanding of Marco's ambivalence and identity-level concerns. Clinician accurately tracks emotional nuance, incorporates Marco's metaphors ("little pebble in the way"), and consistently reflects back in ways that deepen his self-understanding rather than simply parroting content. This is the psychiatrist's strongest domain.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear, strategic focus on smoking cessation with appropriate, client-centered direction. Session progresses logically: exploring ambivalence → assessing readiness → accessing past resources → identifying behavioral steps → building confidence. Direction remains responsive to Marco's pace and resistance. However, direction could be strengthened by exploring more deeply what would shift Marco's readiness from 2-3 rather than moving directly to past successes. Additionally, emerging change talk ("I'm open to listen," "I'm willing to give it a try") could be amplified more strategically before moving forward.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated throughout most of session. Opening question immediately establishes partnership by inviting Marco's agenda. Clinician validates Marco's preferred approach (cutting back rather than quitting) rather than pushing for abstinence, demonstrating genuine collaboration. Frequently uses collaborative language ("Can you tell me," "What do you think") and positions Marco as expert on his own experience. However, collaboration could be strengthened through more balanced turn-taking and co-construction. Some reflections are quite lengthy and directive in tone, subtly shifting power toward clinician's interpretation rather than true partnership.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout session. Psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Marco's right to choose and reinforces his ownership of the change process. Validates desire to avoid pressure early on and uses language that positions change as Marco's choice ("if you were to," "if you were going to think about it"). When Marco identifies cutting back as his preferred approach, clinician affirms this without suggesting it's insufficient. Explicitly states the plan "respects your pace and autonomy." Final exchange demonstrates clinician will honor Marco's stated need for "no lectures, no nagging" and "on my terms." This is model-level autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong evocation of Marco's own motivations and resources. Skillfully asks Marco to recall past challenge (open-heart surgery) and what helped him move forward, drawing out his own resilience and coping strategies. Reflects back Marco's strengths ("taking things one day at a time seems like a strength you've already drawn on"). However, evocation could be deepened by spending more time evoking Marco's own reasons for change when he expresses sustain talk. When Marco identifies readiness as 2-3, clinician could ask more evocative questions: "What's keeping you at a 2 or 3 rather than a 1?" or "What would it take to move higher?" These would evoke Marco's own thinking rather than clinician providing direction.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong partnership with genuine respect for Marco's expertise about his own life and choices. Opening establishes equality by inviting Marco's agenda and priorities. Clinician consistently invites Marco's perspective and allows his contributions to shape session. Validates his preferred approach rather than redirecting toward abstinence. However, partnership could be strengthened by reducing length and interpretive nature of some reflections. Several reflections present clinician's interpretation as fact rather than collaboratively exploring Marco's experience. The 0-10 readiness and confidence scales, while useful, are introduced as clinical tools rather than collaboratively developed, subtly shifting power toward clinician expertise.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates adequate but inconsistent cultivation of change talk. Multiple instances of change talk emerge ("I'm open to listen," "I could go for that," "I'm willing to give it a try," "I can see how the plan might work") but clinician does not consistently amplify or reinforce them strategically. When Marco identifies readiness as 2-3, he states "I'm not completely closed off" and "I'm willing to hear"—subtle change talk not explicitly recognized or explored. When Marco identifies confidence as 5/10, psychiatrist accepts this without asking what the "5" represents or what would strengthen it. Significant missed opportunity—in MI, change talk is the mechanism of change, and strategic amplification is essential to building intrinsic motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates adequate but inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Marco expresses significant sustain talk ("I can't see myself changing now," "I know I'm setting myself up for failure," "I'm still skeptical"), and clinician validates these feelings appropriately, consistent with MI spirit. However, strategic softening techniques are underutilized. When Marco says "I can't see myself changing now," clinician could use double-sided reflection: "You can't see it now, and that makes sense given 40 years. What would need to be different for you to imagine it?" Clinician does skillfully soften skepticism by reframing it as evidence of autonomy and self-awareness. However, more active reframing and deliberate redirection toward change talk would strengthen balance between sustain and change talk.

### Summary of Session Performance

**Strengths:**
- Exemplary empathic accuracy with sophisticated reflective listening that captures emotional nuance and deepens Marco's self-understanding
- Exceptional autonomy support with consistent emphasis on Marco's right to choose and ownership of change process
- Strong collaborative stance with genuine partnership and validation of Marco's preferred approach
- Skillful evocation of past resources and resilience to build confidence
- Appropriate strategic direction with logical session progression responsive to Marco's pace
- Validation of ambivalence rather than pathologizing resistance
- Concrete, manageable action planning that respects Marco's autonomy

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Inconsistent amplification and reinforcement of change talk—missed opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation
- Limited strategic softening of sustain talk through double-sided reflections and reframing
- Readiness exploration could be deeper before moving to other topics
- Some clinician-directed interpretation presented as fact rather than collaborative exploration
- Limited explicit affirmation of Marco's inherent worth beyond change potential
- Scales introduced as clinical tools rather than collaboratively developed

**Clinical Impact:**
Marco moves from initial resistance (2-3 readiness) to identifying a concrete step and moderate confidence (5/10), representing positive progress. However, inadequate amplification of change talk means intrinsic motivation is not maximally strengthened. Marco's change talk remains somewhat tentative and sustain talk retains significant weight. Session is effective at building rapport and avoiding confrontation but less effective at maximizing evocation of intrinsic motivation for change.
