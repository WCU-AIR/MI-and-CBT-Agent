This psychiatrist does opposite—positions himself as expert who knows what Andrei should do. Repeated "Maybe you should," "You might want to," "It would probably help if" establishes psychiatrist as authority and Andrei as passive recipient of advice. Most critically, completely ignores Andrei's opening statement about 45-minute wait—fundamental partnership failure signaling that Andrei's concerns and experience are not valued or important. When Andrei expresses skepticism about fitting changes into schedule, psychiatrist does not treat as valuable expertise about Andrei's own life; instead persists with prescriptions, implying Andrei's assessment is wrong or insufficient. ## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.6/5**

---

### Summary of Findings

#### **Strengths:**
- Maintains clear focus on target behavior (weight, diet, exercise, blood pressure)
- Asks some evocative questions (importance and confidence ratings)
- Eventually helps Andrei identify specific, manageable goal (snack swap)
- Demonstrates some basic reflective listening ("Yeah, I hear you about the time being tight")
- Shows persistence in engaging resistant client

#### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**

**1. Fundamental Failure to Address Opening Concern**
Completely ignores Andrei's 45-minute wait complaint—a critical failure that establishes disrespect and damages rapport from the start. This should have been the first priority.

**2. Pervasive Prescriptive Language**
Repeatedly uses "should," "might want to," "would probably help if," which undermines autonomy and positions psychiatrist as expert rather than collaborative partner.

**3. Minimal Acceptance and Empathy**
Fails to validate Andrei's frustration, constraints, and emotional experience. No warmth or genuine curiosity. Does not acknowledge how difficult it is to balance health goals with genuine life pressures.

**4. No Genuine Collaboration or Partnership**
Imposes solutions rather than co-creating them. Andrei's expertise about his own life is not sought or valued. Andrei's contributions do not substantially influence session direction.

**5. Inadequate Autonomy Support**
No explicit affirmation of Andrei's right to choose. Language creates external pressure rather than internal motivation.

**6. Failure to Evoke and Amplify Change Talk**
Spontaneous change talk is not strategically followed up or reinforced. Evocative questions asked but not genuinely explored.

**7. Inadequate Softening of Sustain Talk**
Sustain talk is ignored or met with more prescriptions rather than skillful reframing and validation.

**8. Mechanical Application of MI Questions**
Importance and confidence questions asked but not genuinely explored or followed up with curiosity.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Likelihood of Change

This session is likely to **decrease Andrei's intrinsic motivation** for change and **increase defensiveness and resistance**. By ignoring his opening concern, psychiatrist signals that Andrei's experience is not important. By repeatedly prescribing solutions without genuine collaboration, psychiatrist undermines Andrei's autonomy and self-efficacy.

Andrei's moderate ratings (importance: 7, confidence: 5) indicate delicate motivational state—aware of problem and somewhat willing to change, but uncertain about capacity given constraints. This is precisely when skillful MI is most critical. Instead of evoking Andrei's own reasons for change and building confidence, psychiatrist adds external pressure and prescriptions, likely pushing Andrei further into resistance.

Andrei's eventual agreement to try snack swap appears to be **compliance rather than genuine internal motivation** ("I guess we can start there," "I'm willing to try this for a week"). Without genuine internal motivation, likelihood of sustained behavior change is low.

---

### Recommendations for Improvement

**Priority 1: Address Opening Concern Immediately**
- Explicitly acknowledge and validate frustration about 45-minute wait
- Apologize sincerely and explore client's experience
- Establish respect and rapport before moving to health goals

**Priority 2: Eliminate Prescriptive Language**
- Remove "should," "might want to," "would probably help if" from vocabulary
- Replace with genuine evocative questions that invite client to explore own thinking
- Practice recording and reviewing sessions to identify and eliminate prescriptive patterns

**Priority 3: Develop Genuine Empathic Reflections**
- Move beyond surface-level reflections to complex reflections capturing emotional nuance
- Pause after client statements to identify deeper emotion or meaning
- Use reflections to deepen understanding rather than move conversation forward

**Priority 4: Strategically Evoke and Amplify Change Talk**
- When client spontaneously offers change talk, pause and follow up with genuine curiosity
- Ask open-ended questions inviting elaboration and strengthening of motivation
- Practice identifying all instances of change talk and writing follow-up questions

**Priority 5: Validate and Explore Sustain Talk**
- Validate concerns rather than ignoring or prescribing over them
- Explore collaboratively using double-sided reflections
- Practice softening sustain talk through validation and reframing

**Priority 6: Establish Genuine Collaboration and Partnership**
- Explicitly invite client into problem-solving process
- Seek client's expertise about their own life
- Use inclusive language ("we," "together") and genuinely incorporate client's ideas

**Priority 7: Explicitly Support Autonomy**
- Regularly affirm client's right to choose
- Emphasize that any change is entirely client's decision
- Use autonomy-supporting statements regularly

**Priority 8: Follow Up on Evocative Questions with Genuine Curiosity**
- When asking about importance or confidence, genuinely explore response
- Use follow-up questions to deepen understanding and evoke more change talk
- Resist urge to move on quickly; sit with client's answer and explore thoroughly

**Priority 9: Co-Create Plans Rather Than Prescribing**
- When client shows openness to change, explore what would work for them
- Build plan together with client's input shaping final agreement
- Genuinely incorporate client's preferences and constraints

**Priority 10: Develop Awareness of MI Spirit**
- Regularly reflect on whether sessions embody MI spirit: partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation
- Use self-assessment tools to identify patterns of prescriptive or directive behavior
- Record and review sessions regularly with supervisor or MI trainer

---

### Conclusion

This session represents a **significant departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates **poor MI competence**. While psychiatrist maintains focus on health behavior change goal and eventually helps Andrei identify concrete first step, overall approach is fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit.

**This psychiatrist requires substantial training and practice in MI principles and techniques.** Regular supervision, recording review, and practice with feedback are essential for developing true MI competence. Without significant changes to approach—particularly in eliminating prescriptive language, developing genuine empathic reflections, and establishing true collaboration—likelihood of sustained behavior change for clients is low.
