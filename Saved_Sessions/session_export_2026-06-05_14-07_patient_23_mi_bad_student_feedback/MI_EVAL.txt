**Fundamental Violation of MI Spirit:** This session is characterized by judgment, coercion, and rejection of client autonomy. This is not motivational interviewing; it is confrontational, directive counseling that actively harms the therapeutic relationship and violates MI principles.

## Immediate Recommendations

### **HIGHEST PRIORITY: Fundamental Reorientation to MI Spirit**
Shift from confrontational, expert-driven model to collaborative, client-centered approach grounded in MI principles. This requires fundamental mindset change: from "I know what's best and need to convince client to comply" to "Client has expertise about their own life; my role is to help them explore their own motivations for change."

### **CRITICAL: Develop Genuine Empathic Understanding**
Before attempting to change behavior, develop accurate understanding of client's perspective, fears, and values. Use reflective listening to demonstrate understanding, not to judge or dismiss.

### **CRITICAL: Evoke Rather Than Impose Change Talk**
Stop telling client what they need to do. Ask questions that help them discover their own reasons for change. Amplify and reinforce their own change talk rather than dismissing it.

### **CRITICAL: Respect Client Autonomy and Collaborate on Goals**
Recognize client's right to choose their own goals, even if you disagree. Work collaboratively to find goals meaningful to client and aligned with their values.

### **CRITICAL: Eliminate Coercive and Shaming Language**
Remove all judgmental, shaming, and coercive language. Replace with language communicating respect and understanding.

### **IMPORTANT: Develop Skill in Handling Ambivalence**
Learn to skillfully handle client ambivalence using MI techniques (double-sided reflection, exploring discrepancy, strategic responding) rather than confrontation.

### **IMPORTANT: Shift from Expert Role to Collaborative Partnership**
Position yourself as collaborator and partner rather than expert who knows what's best. Seek client's input and allow their contributions to shape session.

### **IMPORTANT: Develop Responsiveness to Client Feedback**
When client explicitly tells you approach is not working, listen and adjust. Client's perception of therapeutic relationship is more important than your intentions.

### **FOUNDATIONAL: Understand Evidence on Confrontation and Behavior Change**
Study research on why confrontation increases resistance and why MI-consistent approaches are more effective. This understanding will motivate your own change in clinical practice.

### **FOUNDATIONAL: Seek Formal MI Training and Supervision**
This session demonstrates fundamental misalignment with MI principles. Enroll in formal MI training program (MINT-certified trainers available) and seek regular clinical supervision focused on MI skill development. Record and review sessions with supervisor.
