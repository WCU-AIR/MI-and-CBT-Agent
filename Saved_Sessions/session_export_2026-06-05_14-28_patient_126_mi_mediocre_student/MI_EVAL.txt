### Acceptance

**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates basic courtesy but fails to communicate genuine unconditional positive regard. Tariq's frustration about losing phone privileges is acknowledged factually but not validated emotionally. ### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician uses basic reflective listening but fails to capture emotional nuance and meaning. Reflections are factual restatements ("So you're feeling stuck and frustrated...") rather than empathic understanding of the deeper emotional layers—Tariq's anxiety about separation from family, vulnerability masked as frustration about fairness, and the profound significance of his father's birthday. When Tariq mentions his mother and father's birthday, the clinician does not pause to explore these values or reflect their emotional importance. Family connection is reduced to a means of stress reduction rather than understood as a core value and source of meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician maintains loose focus on the presenting concern (phone privileges and family communication) but fails to establish clear, collaborative direction. Multiple implicit goals compete without clarity: getting phone privileges back, improving mood/behavior, talking to staff or counselors, and following rules. The psychiatrist never clarifies the actual conditions for phone privileges restoration or explores what the facility requires. Direction is imposed rather than collaboratively developed. The final plan ("try talking to the counselor and keeping your area clean") is vague and generic rather than strategic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates minimal collaboration and adopts an expert-driven, directive stance throughout. All problem-solving is clinician-generated; Tariq is positioned as a passive recipient of expert advice rather than as a collaborator with valuable insight into his own situation. When Tariq expresses doubt ("I'm not sure they'd do anything different"), the psychiatrist does not explore this concern but suggests the action anyway, communicating: *Your doubt doesn't matter; try my suggestion anyway.* No co-construction of plan occurs. Power imbalance is maintained throughout, with the clinician telling rather than asking, directing rather than exploring.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Clinician actively undermines autonomy through prescriptive language ("you might want to," "you should," "maybe you should just try"), conditional framing ("keep your area clean and follow the rules closely to show them you're serious"), and subtle pressure. The confidence scaling is used manipulatively to pressure Tariq toward action rather than to explore readiness. Tariq is positioned as needing to comply with clinician recommendations rather than as an autonomous agent making his own choices. No genuine exploration of Tariq's own ideas or preferences occurs. Final statement ("Remember, it's important to keep trying even if it feels tough") emphasizes obligation rather than choice.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates virtually no evocation and instead relies entirely on advice-giving and clinician-generated solutions. Tariq's own problem-solving, values, strengths, and understanding are never explored. Clinician never asks "What do you think might help?" or explores why his father's birthday matters or what staying connected to family means to him. Tariq's identity as a "model resident" is not explored as a source of strength and competence. Instead, the psychiatrist **installs** motivation through advice ("It would probably help if...," "Maybe you should...") rather than drawing out Tariq's own thinking, values, and intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Clinician maintains a hierarchical, expert-driven stance throughout with no genuine power-sharing or recognition that Tariq possesses wisdom about his own situation. The implicit message is: *I am the expert. You are the problem. Follow my recommendations.* Tariq is evaluated rather than partnered with ("Have you tried talking to the staff?" implies insufficient effort). All suggestions are clinician-generated unilaterally. There is no moment where Tariq's input substantially shapes the session direction. The clinician's agenda dominates entirely. This is the antithesis of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates minimal skill in evoking and reinforcing change talk. While Tariq does offer some change talk ("I usually do try to keep things straight," "I've been good most of the time," "I'll try and talk to the counselor"), the clinician fails to strategically amplify it. No open questions are asked to elicit Tariq's own reasons for change or values. When Tariq mentions his mother and father's birthday—profound sources of intrinsic motivation—the clinician does not explore these. Tariq's strengths as a "model resident" are not reinforced or explored. Instead, the psychiatrist reinforces extrinsic motivation (compliance and rule-following) rather than evoking intrinsic change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates minimal skill in addressing sustain talk and sometimes inadvertently reinforces it. Tariq's sustain talk includes external blame ("one of the officers, it's like he has it out for me"), minimization ("I don't think it's that big of a deal"), hopelessness ("every time I bring it up, it just feels like they don't wanna hear me"), and doubt ("I'm not really sure they'd do anything different"). Rather than exploring these concerns with empathy or using double-sided reflection, the clinician dismisses them and prescribes action anyway. When the psychiatrist says "you might want to keep your area clean and follow the rules closely," this implicitly validates sustain talk by suggesting the problem is Tariq's behavior, potentially deepening defensiveness. No strategic reframing occurs.

---

### Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Basic interpersonal warmth and courtesy in opening
- Some use of reflective listening to restate content
- Recognition that family connection is important to Tariq

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental MI spirit violation:** Expert-driven, directive counseling rather than motivational interviewing
- **Minimal acceptance and empathy:** Tariq's frustration acknowledged factually but not validated emotionally
- **No genuine collaboration:** All problem-solving clinician-generated; Tariq positioned as passive recipient
- **Autonomy undermined:** Prescriptive language and pressure throughout
- **Evocation absent:** Motivation installed through advice rather than evoked from client
- **Change talk not cultivated:** Opportunities to explore intrinsic motivations missed
- **Sustain talk not skillfully addressed:** Doubts and external blame not strategically softened

---

### Clinical Impact

This session is likely to **decrease** Tariq's intrinsic motivation and **increase** defensiveness. By adopting an expert-driven stance, the clinician communicates that Tariq's perspective doesn't matter and compliance is expected. This is precisely the opposite of what Tariq needs—he is already feeling unfairly targeted and powerless. The psychiatrist misses the opportunity to strengthen Tariq's intrinsic motivation around family connection, which is a powerful source of motivation for maintaining compliance and preparing for successful reentry.

---

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Adopt collaborative, partnership-based stance** – Shift from expert-driven advice to genuine collaboration
2. **Evoke rather than install motivation** – Ask strategic questions about Tariq's values, strengths, and intrinsic motivations
3. **Validate and accept without condition** – Reflect frustration with genuine empathy regardless of agreement
4. **Use complex, emotion-capturing reflections** – Move beyond factual restatement to reflections that deepen insight
5. **Support autonomy and client choice** – Replace prescriptive language with language emphasizing Tariq's agency
6. **Explore sustain talk with empathy** – Use curiosity rather than dismissal when addressing doubts and resistance
7. **Clarify actual pathway to change** – Collaborate to understand specific conditions for phone privileges restoration
8. **Strengthen recognition of strengths** – Explicitly affirm Tariq's positive history and capacity for change
9. **Use confidence scaling as exploration** – Not as pressure tool, but to understand what would increase readiness
10. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** – This is essential for providing competent, evidence-based care

---

**Conclusion:** This clinician requires immediate formal training in motivational interviewing fundamentals and ongoing supervision. Current practice is inconsistent with MI principles and likely to undermine client engagement and treatment outcomes.
