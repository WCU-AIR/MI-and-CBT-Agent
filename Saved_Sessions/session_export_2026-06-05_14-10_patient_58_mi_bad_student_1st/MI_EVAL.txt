Client's repeated attempts to collaborate met with dismissal ("No, no, you don't get it"). No joint problem-solving. ### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session actively undermines autonomy through coercive language, threats, and removal of choice. Directive commands: "You have to stop immediately," "You need to be stronger," "You have to commit to quitting today." Threat-based messaging: "If you don't follow this, you'll face serious health consequences." Removal of choice: "No exceptions," "There's no 'kind of' or 'sometimes' about it." Shame and guilt used as pressure tactics. Client's statement "I don't have a plan to quit today" met with demand rather than respect for readiness. Zero recognition of client's autonomy to make own decisions about timing or approach.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session entirely about telling, not evoking. Psychiatrist provides reasons for change without asking client about his own reasons, values, or concerns. Client's existing change talk ("I know that it's bad for me," "I'm aware of the risks," "I want to do right by the team") ignored or undermined. No open questions designed to evoke motivation: "What concerns you most?" "What matters to you about your health?" "What would be different if you weren't using alcohol?" "What strengths do you have?" Psychiatrist tells rather than asks, imposes rather than explores, prescribes rather than collaborates.

Response "You can't keep blaming the team or social stuff. You have to take responsibility" invalidates client's stated barrier and frames it as excuse. ## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** No demonstrated MI competency. This represents non-MI practice that actively violates core MI principles.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance, empathy, and collaboration
- Authoritarian, expert-driven approach antithetical to MI spirit
- Coercive pressure tactics (threats, shame, demands) undermine autonomy
- Failure to evoke or amplify client's own motivations
- Dismissive language ("No, no, you don't get it" repeated five times) damages therapeutic relationship
- Fundamental misunderstanding or rejection of MI principles

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is likely to **decrease** Jamal's motivation for change and damage the therapeutic relationship. Repeated dismissal communicates that his experience is invalid. Shame-based messaging and coercive pressure create defensiveness rather than openness. Client's responses become increasingly defensive and withdrawn, suggesting disengagement from conversation. By final exchange, client states "I'm not ready" and "I need help with the team pressure first"—valid statements about readiness that psychiatrist continues to ignore.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. **Fundamental reorientation to MI spirit** (highest priority)
2. Eliminate dismissive language and develop genuine acceptance
3. Develop reflective listening and empathy skills
4. Shift from telling to asking (evocation)
5. Replace unilateral planning with collaborative problem-solving
6. Amplify and reinforce client's change talk
7. Develop autonomy support; eliminate coercive language
8. Handle sustain talk with validation and double-sided reflection
9. Develop genuine curiosity about client's experience
10. **Seek formal MI training and ongoing supervision** (essential)

**Likelihood of Evoking Change:** Research demonstrates this directive, confrontational approach **increases resistance and decreases change likelihood**. Session is counterproductive to stated goal of reducing client's alcohol use.
