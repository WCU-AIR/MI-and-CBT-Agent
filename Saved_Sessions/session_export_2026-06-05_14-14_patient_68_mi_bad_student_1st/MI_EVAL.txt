## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.1/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol consumption)
- Gathers factual information about drinking frequency and quantity
- Expresses concern about health consequences

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Complete absence of MI spirit**: No partnership, acceptance, compassion, or evocation
- **Systematic undermining of autonomy**: Repeated denials of client's right to choose own pace
- **Failure to evoke intrinsic motivation**: Imposes external reasons through fear and demands compliance
- **Dismissal of change talk**: Ignores or shuts down emerging awareness and desire for change
- **Confrontational approach**: Sustain talk met with judgment and ultimatums, increasing resistance
- **Absence of reflective listening**: Not a single reflective statement in entire session
- **Violation of collaborative stance**: Maintains unilateral control and rejects client input

### Competency Assessment:
The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal MI competency. This session represents fundamental departure from MI principles and demonstrates confrontational, directive, expert-driven approach explicitly contraindicated in MI training and research. Immediate formal MI training and ongoing supervision with MI-trained clinician are essential before continuing work with clients on behavior change.

### Likelihood of Evoking Motivation:
**Low.** This session is unlikely to increase Kenji's intrinsic motivation for change. The confrontational approach and repeated ultimatums increase resistance and defensiveness. Kenji's escalating sustain talk throughout demonstrates this pattern. For genuine motivation development, Kenji needs a clinician who understands his perspective, respects his autonomy, and helps him discover his own reasons for change—the opposite of what occurred in this session.

---

**Recommendation:** Formal MI training (MINT-certified workshop) and monthly supervision with MI-trained clinician required. Use MITI scale to rate future sessions. Focus on developing reflective listening, recognizing change talk, and adopting collaborative stance as immediate priorities.
