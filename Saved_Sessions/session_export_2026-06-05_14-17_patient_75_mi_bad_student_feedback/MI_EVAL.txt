## Overall Clinical Assessment

**Average MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **severe departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates fundamental misunderstanding of evidence-based behavior change counseling. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, coercive, expert-driven approach that directly contradicts MI spirit and research on effective counseling.

**Session Impact:** This session is **actively harmful to Linda's motivation for change**. Research demonstrates that pressure and confrontation increase psychological reactance—Linda's own words confirm this: "being pushed like this kinda makes me want to hold on to my cigarettes even more." The psychiatrist has communicated that the clinician does not understand her, does not respect her autonomy, and views her as a moral failure. Linda is likely to leave this session more entrenched in her smoking behavior and may avoid future counseling encounters.

**Critical Improvements Needed:**
1. Eliminate coercive language; replace with autonomy-supporting language
2. Develop genuine reflective listening and empathy
3. Evoke rather than impose change motivation
4. Recognize that pressure increases resistance; shift to curiosity and exploration
5. Establish true partnership rather than expert-driven stance
6. Seek formal MI training and clinical supervision

**Recommendation:** Formal Motivational Interviewing training and supervision are essential. Without fundamental shift in approach, psychiatrist will continue to harm clients' motivation for change despite good intentions.
