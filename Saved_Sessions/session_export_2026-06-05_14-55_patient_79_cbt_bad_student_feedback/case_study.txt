--- DISORDER ---
Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD), provisional

--- CASE STUDY ---

Emma Wang, a 26-year-old investment banker, self-referred to outpatient psychiatry due to recurrent mood swings that disrupted her romantic relationship. She described monthly episodes occurring 7–10 days before menses, characterized by tension, irritability, argumentativeness, anxiety, sadness, regret, social withdrawal, diminished energy, poor concentration, and interpersonal conflict. During these episodes, she occasionally engaged in superficial self-injury (cutting) to reduce anxiety.

These symptoms remitted completely with the onset of menses, after which she returned to her baseline upbeat, energetic, and optimistic state. She denied suicidal or homicidal thoughts, manic or hypomanic episodes, psychotic symptoms, and substance use. She reported regular menstrual cycles with typical premenstrual physical symptoms (breast tenderness, bloating, increased appetite, mild weight gain).

Ms. Wang was born in Taiwan, moved to the U.S. at age 14, graduated from an elite business school, and lives with her older sister. Her family history is notable for a mother with possible depression.

On mental status examination, Ms. Wang appeared well-groomed, cooperative, and fashionably dressed. Her mood was “generally good,” with full, reactive, mildly irritable affect. Thought processes were linear with no evidence of delusions, obsessions, or hallucinations. Insight, judgment, and impulse control were intact aside from perimenstrual impairment.

Discussion: Emma’s cyclical affective and physical symptoms, coupled with remission after menses, are consistent with DSM-5 criteria for PMDD. Her self-injurious behavior is atypical for PMDD and may suggest underlying borderline personality traits. A prospective daily symptom rating over at least two cycles is required to confirm the diagnosis.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---

Diminished concentration and energy

Physical premenstrual symptoms (breast tenderness, bloating, appetite changes)

Symptoms remit with onset of menses

Impairment in social and occupational functioning
