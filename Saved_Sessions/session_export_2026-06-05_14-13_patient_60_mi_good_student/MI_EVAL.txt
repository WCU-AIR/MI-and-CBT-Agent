### Empathy

Captures both Jamal's stated priorities and emotional nuance ("It sounds like you're feeling pretty neutral or even a bit detached from this process"). Reflections are accurate but somewhat surface-level. Missed opportunities to explore underlying emotions beneath defensive presentation—such as anxiety, frustration, or discomfort with vulnerability. Could deepen empathic exploration of what drives Jamal's impatience and minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on marijuana use but lacks strategic coherence and progression. Session moves between multiple directions (establishing usefulness, exploring reasons to change, assessing scales, pivoting to past success) without building clear strategic sequence. Does not systematically develop discrepancy between Jamal's stated values (not wanting to mess up his future) and current behavior (marijuana possession in legal context). Feels exploratory rather than purposefully guided toward building motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine collaborative stance with strong use of open questions and client-centered language ("I'm curious," "What do you think," "Can you tell me about"). Opening question effectively positions Jamal as partner in determining session direction. Validates his preference for efficiency rather than imposing agenda. Minor shift toward subtle expert positioning in latter portion when connecting past success to potential future change, but overall collaboration remains solid and power-sharing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and supports Jamal's autonomy throughout session. Does not pressure toward change; explicitly affirms his independence and right to choose ("It sounds like you value your independence and want to make your own decisions about what matters"). Avoids arguing when Jamal resists external pressure. Could strengthen autonomy support by more explicitly emphasizing Jamal's ownership of the assessment process and by asking directly about his internal experience rather than interpreting it.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant area of weakness. Makes minimal effort to strategically draw out Jamal's own motivations, reasons, or concerns about marijuana use. Successfully elicits past success example but does not deeply explore underlying values or intrinsic motivation. When Jamal offers conditional concern ("If school or something important started getting messed up 'cause of it, then maybe"), psychiatrist does not follow up to deepen this opening. Does not persistently ask about reasons for change across multiple domains. Change talk remains minimal and largely unelicited throughout session.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains strong partnership stance with genuine power-sharing and inclusive language. Positions Jamal as expert on his own situation rather than assuming clinician expertise. Avoids lecturing; instead invites Jamal's thinking through collaborative questioning. Subtle shift toward expert positioning when guiding Jamal to recognize connections between past success and potential future change. Could strengthen partnership by more explicitly inviting Jamal to identify connections himself rather than suggesting them.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Critical deficiency. Change talk is minimal throughout session and largely unelicited. Rare instances include: "I can get stuff done when I need to" (ability talk, not related to marijuana), "I could try" (minimal ability talk), and conditional concern about school. Psychiatrist does not strategically amplify these statements when they emerge. Does not use targeted open questions designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, or commitment to change. Asks about reasons to change but does not persist or vary approach when Jamal resists. This represents a fundamental MI mechanism gap that significantly limits session effectiveness.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist appropriately avoids reinforcing sustain talk but does not strategically soften or reframe it. Sustain talk present throughout ("It's not a lot," "It's not like I was gonna sell it," "No one cares," "It's not messing up my school"). Does not use double-sided reflections or discrepancy development to address these statements. Attempts to develop discrepancy in isolated moments but does not consistently apply this technique to sustain talk. More strategic softening through reframing and gentle introduction of alternative perspectives would strengthen this area.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 3.5/5**

**Strengths:**
- Strong acceptance and unconditional positive regard maintained throughout
- Solid empathic listening with generally accurate reflections
- Genuine collaborative stance with effective use of open questions
- Consistent autonomy support without pressure or prescription
- Skillful avoidance of confrontation and argumentative stance
- Effective elicitation of past success example demonstrating client capability

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient evocation of change talk:** Minimal strategic questioning designed to elicit client's own reasons, concerns, or motivations for change
- **Failure to amplify emerging change talk:** When Jamal offers statements suggesting concern or ability, psychiatrist does not follow up to deepen or strengthen them
- **Inadequate softening of sustain talk:** Present throughout session but largely unaddressed through double-sided reflections or reframing
- **Lack of strategic direction:** Session lacks coherent progression toward developing discrepancy between values and behavior
- **Limited exploration of underlying values:** Does not deeply explore what matters to Jamal or what he cares about for his future
- **Passive rather than strategic approach:** Respects autonomy but does not actively draw out motivation through persistent, skillful evocation

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Jamal leaves the session with minimal intrinsic motivation for change and confirmation that the psychiatrist is "just going through the motions"—exactly his initial expectation. The session has not created sufficient discrepancy, evoked meaningful change talk, or built motivation for reconsideration of marijuana use. For a client in precontemplation, this passive approach is insufficient to move toward change.

**Competency Assessment:**
Psychiatrist demonstrates intermediate MI competence with strong relational and collaborative skills but significant gaps in the core mechanism of evoking and amplifying change talk. This is a common pattern in clinicians who have mastered MI's respectful stance but have not fully developed strategic evocation skills necessary to drive motivation for change.

**Primary Recommendations:**
1. Develop systematic approach to evoking change talk across multiple domains (health, relationships, future, legal, family)
2. Immediately amplify and reinforce all emerging change talk through targeted follow-up questions
3. Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk without confrontation
4. Strategically develop discrepancy between Jamal's values and current behavior
5. Persist in evocation rather than accepting initial resistance
6. Explore underlying emotions and concerns beneath defensive presentation
7. Use scaling questions more strategically to evoke change talk and explore reasoning
8. Build stronger connection to Jamal's future vision and identity as foundation for motivation
