--- DISORDER ---
Dissociative Amnesia / Major Depressive Disorder / Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Judith Vaughan was a 29-year-old white woman, a special education teacher, who sought psychiatric consultation because: “I'm tired of always being sad and alone.”
She reported chronic, severe depression unresponsive to multiple antidepressant and mood stabilizer trials. Psychotherapy (CBT, DBT) had helped more. Electroconvulsive therapy was suggested but refused. She had two prior hospitalizations for suicidal ideation and severe self-cutting requiring stitches.

Her younger sister reported past sexual abuse by their father when Judith was 13, though Judith denied being abused. She had minimal memory of ages 7–13 and attributed it to “nothing happened.” Visits from her family often triggered self-injurious and suicidal behavior.

Ms. Vaughan was socially withdrawn in childhood but academically successful in high school and college. She excelled at work, forming long-term friendships, but had difficulty with intimacy, experiencing intense fear and disgust toward sexual advances. She often felt worthless or “bad.” Sleep was poor, and fatigue was common. She denied alcohol and drug use.

Mental status examination: well-groomed, cooperative, coherent and goal-directed responses, emotionally constricted, appeared sad and “numb.” Denied hallucinations, confusion, or current suicidal intent, though suicidal thoughts were “always around.” She had nightmares of being chased by a dangerous man, difficulty concentrating (but hyperfocused at work), intense startle reaction, and engaged in compulsive behaviors (counting, singing, checking, arranging furniture) to reduce anxiety.

Discussion: Ms. Vaughan exhibits persistent depressed mood, insomnia, fatigue, worthlessness, suicidality, trauma-related memory loss, avoidance of intimacy, nightmares, hyperarousal, and compulsive behaviors. The history and presentation support diagnoses of dissociative amnesia, chronic major depressive disorder with suicidal ideation, and posttraumatic stress disorder. She denies symptoms of dissociative identity disorder.
