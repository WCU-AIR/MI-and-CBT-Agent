# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist opens with controlling directive ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do") and repeatedly labels Diego's behavior as "excuses" (5+ instances). Pervasive judgment throughout session with shame-inducing statements ("Stop feeling sorry for yourself," "You're just trying to justify your addiction"). No affirmation of Diego's existing harm-reduction efforts (Narcan at home, involving housemate, learning safer practices). Dismissive of all client input and perspective. Zero warmth, support, or genuine care communicated.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No reflective listening demonstrated throughout entire session. Closed yes/no questions ("Do you use heroin daily? Yes or no?") shut down exploration rather than deepen understanding. When Diego expresses emotional reality ("It helps me get away from stuff I don't want to face," "I'm scared of failing again"), psychiatrist responds with dismissal and invalidation rather than reflection. No attempt to understand previous treatment failure or explore Diego's internal experience. Psychiatrist never asks about feelings, barriers, or motivations. Client's emotional data points completely ignored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear focus on heroin cessation and treatment entry, providing minimal structural clarity. However, direction is imposed rather than collaboratively developed. Unilateral goal-setting ("You must get into a treatment program this week") without exploring Diego's goals, readiness, or preferences. Ignores Diego's stated readiness for harm reduction and articulated intermediate goals. Repeated conversation restarts suggest loss of direction or abandonment of therapeutic process. Direction achieved through coercion, not collaboration.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero collaboration demonstrated. Psychiatrist explicitly positions himself as sole authority: "I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do" and "I'm setting this plan for you." Unilateral decision-making language throughout ("You have to," "You must," "No more half-measures"). When Diego offers perspective or proposes alternatives, psychiatrist dismisses rather than explores. No shared decision-making or joint problem-solving. Diego explicitly requests collaboration ("I'm asking you to work with me on what I can do now"), and psychiatrist responds by doubling down on demands. Power dynamic entirely one-sided.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematic violation of autonomy through coercion, pressure, and removal of choice. Opening statement signals Diego's choices do not matter. Psychiatrist imposes plan: "I'm setting this plan for you: get into a program by tomorrow, no ifs or buts. Understood? Good." Repeated ultimatums ("You have to quit immediately," "You must commit fully," "No more delays") are pressure tactics that violate autonomy. When Diego clearly states inability to commit to treatment tomorrow, psychiatrist escalates pressure with moral judgment ("You're putting your health, your family, and your future in jeopardy"). No acknowledgment of Diego's right to self-determination. No autonomy-supportive language present.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist imposes motivation rather than evoking it. Closed yes/no questions elicit minimal information and shut down exploration. When Diego spontaneously offers change talk ("I'm very ready to take some steps. I'd say 10 out of 10"), psychiatrist dismisses rather than amplifies. No open-ended questions designed to explore Diego's reasons for change, values, strengths, or resources. Psychiatrist tells Diego what he should think rather than asking what he thinks. Diego's stated readiness for harm reduction completely ignored. No attempt to draw out internal resources or build upon existing motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role and communicates Diego's expertise about his own life is irrelevant. Opening establishes psychiatrist as sole authority: "I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do to improve." Positions himself as expert who knows Diego's needs better than Diego does ("You need to have more willpower," "You must get into a treatment program"). When Diego offers self-knowledge about readiness and capacity, psychiatrist dismisses as invalid. Makes assumptions about motivations without exploration ("You're just making excuses," "You're just trying to justify your addiction"). No language positioning Diego as partner with valuable knowledge. Repeated conversation restart suggests partnership has completely broken down.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist suppresses and dismisses Diego's change talk rather than cultivating it. Diego spontaneously offers multiple instances: "I'm very ready to take some steps. I'd say 10 out of 10," "I think learning safer injecting would help," "I'll tell my housemate about the Narcan," "I want to eventually do more." Psychiatrist responds by dismissing as insufficient ("Those 'small steps' won't save you," "Harm reduction is just delaying the inevitable"). No questions designed to evoke more change talk or deepen existing motivation. Uses language reinforcing sustain talk ("Fear of failure won't save you," "You're just making excuses," "You're just setting yourself up for failure"). Fails to reinforce Diego's confidence when expressed. Change talk actively discouraged rather than amplified.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist handles sustain talk through confrontation and pressure rather than skillful softening. When Diego expresses fear of failure, psychiatrist responds with direct confrontation ("Fear of failure won't save you") that invalidates concern rather than exploring it. When Diego expresses ambivalence, psychiatrist responds with pressure and ultimatums rather than exploring underlying concerns. No double-sided reflections acknowledging both appeal of heroin use and reasons to change. Sustain talk dismissed as invalid ("You're just making excuses," "You're just trying to justify your addiction"). Minimal positive element: psychiatrist maintains focus on change goal rather than getting lost in exploration, but this achieved through confrontation. Sustain talk likely reinforced rather than softened.
