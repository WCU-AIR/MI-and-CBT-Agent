## DETAILED COMMENTS BY DOMAIN

### **Medical Safety and Clinical Urgency (Critical Concern)**

The therapist failed to recognize or appropriately respond to the patient's acute medical presentation. The patient presents with signs of moderate-to-severe alcohol withdrawal: tremor, diaphoresis, tachycardia (HR 104), hypertension (BP 155/95), nausea, insomnia, and anxiety. The suggestion to use "deep breathing or going for a walk" is clinically inadequate and potentially dangerous. A competent therapist would have: (1) assessed withdrawal severity using a validated scale (CIWA-Ar); (2) coordinated with a physician for medical evaluation; (3) discussed pharmacotherapy (benzodiazepines); (4) established safety planning and crisis protocols; (5) explained when to seek emergency care.

### **Treatment Strategy and Conceptualization (Critical Concern)**

No evidence-based strategy for treating severe AUD with acute withdrawal was evident. The therapist did not articulate a conceptualization of the disorder or explain how interventions connect to recovery goals. For this patient, an evidence-based approach would include: (1) medical management of withdrawal; (2) cognitive-behavioral coping skills for cravings and withdrawal; (3) functional analysis and relapse prevention; (4) addressing maintaining factors (marital stress, occupational problems); (5) long-term recovery planning (medication, peer support, lifestyle change). The current approach is generic and unfocused.

### **Guided Discovery and Socratic Method (Critical Concern)**

The therapist abandoned guided discovery in favor of direct suggestion and persuasion. This is a fundamental CBT failure. Rather than asking questions to help the patient discover insights ("What do you think is driving the cycle of drinking and withdrawal?" "What evidence do you have for the thought 'I can't handle this'?"), the therapist told the patient what to think and do. This reduces patient autonomy, engagement, and effectiveness.

### **Homework Quality and Follow-Up (Critical Concern)**

Homework was assigned but with critical deficiencies: vague instructions, no rationale, no feasibility assessment, no written materials, no practice, and no follow-up. The second session did not review first session homework—a fundamental failure in CBT structure. For a patient with reduced cognitive capacity due to withdrawal, clear, specific, written homework with follow-up is essential.

### **Strengths (Limited)**

- Warm, non-judgmental tone
- Genuine effort to validate patient experience
- Professional demeanor
- Willingness to engage with patient

### **Recommendations for Supervision and Training**

1. **Immediate:** Coordinate with addiction medicine specialist for medical assessment and management
2. **Urgent:** Intensive supervision from expert in AUD treatment
3. **Essential:** Training in evidence-based CBT for substance use disorders (CBCST, motivational interviewing)
4. **Important:** Development of competence in guided discovery, functional analysis, and relapse prevention planning
5. **Ongoing:** Case consultation and review of treatment protocols
