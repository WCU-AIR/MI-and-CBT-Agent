## Summary Assessment

**Significant MI Deficiencies Identified:**

This session demonstrates **critical violations of core MI principles**, particularly in early and middle portions. The psychiatrist enters with a predetermined agenda (rapid, complete smoking cessation via cold turkey) and pursues this agenda through pressure, prescription, and expert-driven direction, despite clear patient feedback indicating misalignment with her readiness and capacity.

**Primary Concerns:**
- Autonomy undermined through prescriptive language and repeated pressure
- Agenda imposed rather than collaboratively established
- Clinician-provided reasons for change replace genuine evocation of patient's motivations
- Sustain talk met with confrontation rather than skillful softening
- Patient's exhaustion and stress dismissed as obstacles rather than legitimate factors affecting readiness

**Strengths Identified:**
- Significant improvement demonstrated in final third of session
- Capacity for collaborative, empathic MI practice evident in later exchanges
- Genuine validation of patient's fatigue and stress eventually demonstrated
- Appropriate identification of patient's need for support
- Avoidance of harsh judgment or shame throughout

**Critical Issue:**

The early pressure-based approach risks **decreasing intrinsic motivation and increasing sustain talk**, despite patient's genuine motivation to address smoking. Patient's continued engagement despite pressure suggests resilience and commitment, but collaborative approach from opening would likely have been more effective in evoking and strengthening motivation.

**Recommendation for Development:**

Priority is developing **consistency in applying collaborative, empathic, evocative principles throughout entire session**, not just final third. Clinician demonstrates capacity for excellent MI practice; work involves applying these skills from opening and maintaining them throughout. Focus should be on eliminating prescriptive language, developing genuine curiosity about patient readiness, amplifying patient's own change talk, and establishing true partnership from session start.
