### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** General focus on engagement and motivation is maintained, but direction lacks strategic clarity. No specific change target is clearly established or progressively clarified throughout the session. Conversation meanders through: compliance frustration → desire for meaningful activity → uncertainty about follow-through → support needs → stability as goal → defensive coping. Psychiatrist follows Kieran's lead appropriately but doesn't strategically narrow focus or clarify which topics are primary. Missing periodic check-ins like "Of all we've discussed, what feels most important to focus on?"

Despite this, autonomy support is strong and consistent overall.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocation is present but inconsistently developed. Psychiatrist asks some evocative questions ("What would make this feel meaningful?") but doesn't strategically amplify or deepen Kieran's change talk. When Kieran expresses desire for "something active" and "training," psychiatrist reflects accurately but doesn't ask follow-up questions like "Tell me more about that" or "Why is that important to you?" Critical gap: psychiatrist doesn't strategically evoke Kieran's reasons for change, values, or resources. Misses opportunity to ask "What would change make possible for you?" or "What's at stake if things stay the same?" when Kieran expresses minimal caring.

### Partnership

Clinician avoids expert role and invites Kieran's wisdom throughout. However, subtle moments where clinician interpretation slightly overrides collaborative exploration—for example, reframing defensive coping as "self-care" rather than asking Kieran's own interpretation. Affirming Kieran's showing up as evidence of "strength" is clinician's interpretation rather than inviting Kieran's understanding. Overall partnership is well-maintained despite these minor moments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Kieran produces meaningful change talk (desire for active engagement, need for patient support, goal of stability) but psychiatrist doesn't strategically amplify or reinforce it. Critical MI error: when Kieran expresses desire for meaningful activity, psychiatrist immediately introduces doubt rather than first fully developing the change talk. Sequence should be: (1) Evoke and amplify change talk, (2) Then acknowledge ambivalence. Instead, psychiatrist does: (1) Reflect, (2) Immediately introduce doubt. Minimal use of MI-specific evocative techniques (elaboration, exploring values, exploring ability, exploring commitment). Missed opportunity to deeply evoke change talk when Kieran expresses minimal motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled with appropriate softness and without confrontation, which is appropriate. Psychiatrist avoids arguing against Kieran's pessimism. However, approach is primarily reflective rather than strategically redirecting. Misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections, reframing, and exploration of ambivalence. For example, when Kieran says "I don't really see how that's gonna happen here," psychiatrist could use: "On one hand, you can't see how this could work. On the other hand, you mentioned wanting something active and meaningful. What would need to happen for you to believe change is possible?" One effective redirect: reframing showing up as "meaningful step" rather than "pointless." Overall, sustain talk is not reinforced but also not strategically softened or redirected toward possibility.

## Summary of Clinical Observations

### Strengths
- **Consistent acceptance and unconditional positive regard** throughout; Kieran is never judged or pathologized
- **Strong empathy with sophisticated, complex reflections** that capture emotional nuance and ambivalence
- **Solid partnership and collaboration** with Kieran positioned as expert on his own experience
- **Excellent autonomy support** with explicit affirmation of Kieran's right to choose and pace
- **Warm, genuine affirmation** of Kieran's worth and effort, even when minimal
- **Appropriate softness with sustain talk**; no confrontation or argument against pessimism
- **Open, invitational stance** from beginning; clinician frames session as Kieran's to shape

### Areas for Improvement
- **Underdeveloped change talk cultivation**: Kieran produces change talk but psychiatrist doesn't strategically amplify through follow-up questions
- **Lack of strategic direction**: No clear target behavior or change goal established; session meanders through related topics
- **Minimal use of evocative questioning**: Relies on reflection rather than strategic questions to draw out intrinsic motivations and values
- **Insufficient exploration of ambivalence**: While acknowledged, ambivalence isn't deeply explored; missing "What's the doubt about?" questions
- **Sustain talk not strategically redirected**: Soft reflection present but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections and reframing
- **Limited MI-specific techniques**: Elaboration, exploring values, exploring ability, and exploring commitment rarely used
- **Change talk immediately followed by doubt**: When Kieran expresses desire for meaningful activity, psychiatrist immediately introduces doubt rather than first fully developing change talk

---

## Key Recommendations

1. **Strategically amplify change talk** through follow-up evocative questions before addressing doubt or ambivalence
2. **Establish clear, specific change targets early** in the session using collaborative language
3. **Use strategic questioning to evoke intrinsic motivation** beyond reflection alone (elaboration, values exploration, ability exploration, commitment exploration)
4. **Develop ambivalence through double-sided reflections** rather than immediately introducing doubt after change talk
5. **Strategically redirect sustain talk** using reframing and exploration to soften hopelessness and redirect toward possibility
6. **Explore Kieran's values and what he cares about** more deeply, even when he expresses minimal caring
7. **Explicitly explore protective coping and its cost** while acknowledging its protective function
8. **Use affirmations strategically** to reinforce change talk and strengths related to change
9. **Clarify and deepen Kieran's stated need for "patient support"** through specific follow-up questions
10. **Establish a concrete, collaborative plan by session end** with at least one small next step Kieran has chosen

---

## Clinical Impact

The psychiatrist's strong acceptance, empathy, and partnership create psychological safety essential for engaging resistant clients. Kieran is more elaborative than his initial presentation suggests. However, the session is unlikely to significantly strengthen Kieran's intrinsic motivation for change. Change talk remains underdeveloped and unstrengthened; sustain talk is not strategically redirected. Kieran leaves with ambivalence intact and motivation unstrengthened—at risk of continuing behavioral compliance while remaining emotionally disengaged.

**The relational foundation is strong; strategic use of that foundation to evoke and strengthen client motivation for change is the critical next step.**
