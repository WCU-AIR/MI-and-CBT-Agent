--- DISORDER ---
Adjustment Disorder with Anxiety, Alcohol Use Disorder (mild), Cannabis Use Disorder (mild)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Shaun Yates, a 32-year-old married African American IT security analyst, presented for psychiatric evaluation because he felt "stressed." He and his wife have two young children and a stable social life with a large extended family. This was his first psychiatric evaluation. He reported no prior psychiatric history.

Mr. Yates traced his stress to a death in his city 4 months earlier: an unarmed Black man was murdered by two off-duty white policemen. He first saw the video on social media, which was frequently reposted by friends. His social media timeline then filled with other videos of police brutality toward Black men. Despite trying to avoid such content, he found news, images, and community memorials unavoidable.

He became preoccupied with fears for the safety of his wife, children, extended family, and himself. Normally private about these issues, his wife encouraged treatment because he "didn't seem himself." He emphasized that he was not paranoid or overly sensitive about racism but admitted difficulty stopping thoughts about violence against Black men. He disabled his social media accounts and avoided news and TV coverage.

He reported edginess, nervousness, hypervigilance, and decreased exploration of his city. He stopped jogging in his local park to avoid potential danger. Sleep was disrupted for 3 months with vivid nightmares of being chased by police officers.

To "unwind," he increased his use of alcohol and cannabis: drinking six beers nightly after dinner and smoking cannabis before bedtime, which helped sleep and reduced nightmares. He had been late to work and reprimanded twice by his supervisor, though he did not inform his wife.

Substance use history: began alcohol and cannabis at 19 in college. After college, use was occasional and minimal. Use only escalated after the traumatic exposure 3 months ago. No tobacco or other substances.

He denied feelings of guilt, worthlessness, or hopelessness, as well as prior panic attacks, mania, psychosis, or obsessive-compulsive symptoms. No psychiatric hospitalizations or outpatient treatment. No suicidality. Family history: father with PTSD following Vietnam military service.

Discussion: Mr. Yates’s stress is related to repeated exposure to violent videos of police killings of Black men. He exhibits intrusion symptoms (preoccupation, nightmares), avoidance (stopped jogging, social media avoidance), and hyperarousal (edginess, hypervigilance, sleep difficulties). DSM-5 criteria for PTSD are likely not met due to electronic media exposure not being counted as trauma. His stress and functional impairment align with adjustment disorder with anxiety.

His increased alcohol and cannabis use has led to marital conflict and workplace reprimands, meeting criteria for mild alcohol use disorder and mild cannabis use disorder.
