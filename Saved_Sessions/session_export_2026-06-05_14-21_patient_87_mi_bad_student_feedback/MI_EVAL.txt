### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No cultivation of change talk demonstrated. Marco's explicit change talk ("I'm always open to listen") is completely ignored and not developed. No questions asked to evoke change talk (e.g., "What would be the benefits?" "What are you capable of?" "What matters most to you?"). Psychiatrist focuses exclusively on reasons against change and Marco's failures, reinforcing sustain talk rather than cultivating change talk. Repeated directives ("You have to quit right now") suppress rather than cultivate change talk. Pressure actively moves Marco away from change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk is actively reinforced and amplified through confrontation rather than softened. Marco's legitimate expressions of ambivalence ("I can't see myself changing now," "I've tried before, didn't work," "Why would now be different?") are met with direct confrontation ("Stop making excuses," "You just need more willpower"). ## **OVERALL SESSION SCORE: 1.0/5**

### **Summary of Findings**

#### **Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on smoking cessation as target behavior throughout session

#### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine competency domains. The psychiatrist demonstrates:

- **Authoritarian stance**: Session built on prescription, pressure, and control rather than collaboration
- **Confrontational approach**: Direct challenges to Marco's perspective; dismissal of concerns as "excuses"; questioning of integrity
- **Failure to recognize change talk**: Marco's stated openness completely ignored and not developed
- **Amplification of sustain talk**: Confrontational approach actively reinforces resistance and entrenchment
- **Absence of evocation**: Reasons for change provided rather than drawn from client
- **Undermining of autonomy**: Choice explicitly removed; compliance pressured
- **Lack of genuine empathy**: No reflective statements; pseudo-empathy contradicted by pressure
- **Damaged therapeutic relationship**: Marco's initial openness likely closed by psychiatrist's approach

#### **Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is **actively counterproductive** to evoking client motivation for change. Research consistently demonstrates that external pressure and confrontation are ineffective for sustained behavior change. The psychiatrist has moved Marco *away* from change, not toward it. Marco's final statement—*"That just makes me want to dig in my heels harder"*—is a direct result of the psychiatrist's approach, not a reflection of Marco's character or capacity for change.

#### **Recommendation:**

**Immediate formal Motivational Interviewing training and ongoing clinical supervision are essential.** This approach violates fundamental MI principles and is likely to harm patients and undermine their capacity for sustained behavior change.
