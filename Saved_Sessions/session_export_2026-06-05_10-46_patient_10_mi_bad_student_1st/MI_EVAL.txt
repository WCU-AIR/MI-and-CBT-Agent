## Summary of Findings

### Strengths
- **Clear focus on target behaviors:** Psychiatrist maintains consistent direction toward specific health goals (exercise, alcohol reduction).
- **Explicit expectations:** Goals clearly communicated with no ambiguity about what behavior change is expected.

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**Fundamental MI Violations:**

1. **Rejection of collaboration and partnership:** Explicit statements: "I'm not here to negotiate or find middle ground" and "this isn't about hearing you or supporting you" directly violate core MI principles.

2. **Systematic dismissal of client experience:** Legitimate concerns (overwhelm, exhaustion, co-parenting conflict) repeatedly labeled as "excuses," communicating that client's experience is invalid.

3. **Absence of empathy and reflective listening:** Zero reflective statements in entire session. Psychiatrist never demonstrates understanding of client's perspective or validates emotional experience.

4. **Suppression of intrinsic motivation:** Client's powerful change talk rooted in core values (love for son) ignored; external pressure imposed instead.

5. **Coercive language and pressure:** "Have to," "must," "no exceptions," and fear-based threats used to force compliance, violating autonomy support and triggering reactance.

6. **Failure to evoke:** No open-ended questions designed to elicit client's own reasons, ideas, or resources. Psychiatrist tells rather than asks.

### Impact on Client Engagement

**This session is actively counterproductive.** By session end, client expresses increased ambivalence and resistance: "I'm scared I'll end up shutting down instead of improving," "it feels like I'm being forced to choose between my feelings and my son's future—and that's tearing me apart."

The approach has:
- Intensified sustain talk rather than softening it
- Suppressed intrinsic motivation rather than evoking it
- Damaged therapeutic alliance through dismissal and coercion
- Increased client resistance through pressure and control

**Likelihood of client follow-through:** Low. Client unlikely to adhere to recommendations and likely to disengage from treatment.

---

## Critical Recommendations

### 1. **Fundamental Reorientation to MI Spirit**
Complete formal MI training (MINT-certified). Current approach is antithetical to MI principles and requires comprehensive retraining. This is not optional.

### 2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills**
Every client statement should be met with a reflection demonstrating understanding. Goal: 2-3 reflections for every directive. Practice with recorded feedback.

### 3. **Shift from Imposing to Evoking Motivation**
Ask questions that help clients discover their own reasons for change. Build on intrinsic motivation rather than imposing external pressure.

### 4. **Eliminate Coercive Language**
Replace "have to," "must," "no exceptions" with autonomy-supportive language respecting client choice and ownership of change process.

### 5. **Build Genuine Partnership**
Explicitly invite client input and ideas. Make space for genuine dialogue rather than one-way directives. Value client's knowledge about their own life.

### 6. **Develop Skill in Handling Ambivalence**
Learn double-sided reflections and reframing to acknowledge both sustain talk and change talk without reinforcing resistance.

### 7. **Recognize and Build on Client Strengths**
Actively identify and affirm strengths (resilience, commitment, clear values). Use these as building blocks for change.

### 8. **Eliminate Fear-Based Pressure**
Stop using threats ("your son's future depends on it") to force compliance. This violates MI principles and triggers reactance.

### 9. **Develop Strategic Questioning Skills**
Learn to ask open-ended questions that evoke change talk and help clients discover solutions. Goal: 70% open-ended questions.

### 10. **Seek Formal MI Supervision**
Enroll in 2-day MI training workshop followed by 6-12 months of ongoing clinical supervision with MI feedback on recorded sessions before continuing MI work with clients.

---

## Final Assessment

**Current MI Competency Level:** Minimal to no competence in motivational interviewing. This is not "MI with rough edges"—this is a fundamental misunderstanding of MI principles and practice.

**Recommendation:** This clinician should not continue conducting MI sessions until formal training and supervised practice with demonstrated competence is completed. Current approach is harmful and violates evidence-based practice standards for behavior change counseling.
