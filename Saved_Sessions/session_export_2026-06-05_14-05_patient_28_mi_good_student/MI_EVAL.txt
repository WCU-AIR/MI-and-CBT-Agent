### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary empathic attunement throughout. Psychiatrist consistently offers complex, emotionally accurate reflections capturing both content and emotional depth (e.g., "That mix of wanting to be open but also feeling scared about their reactions must feel pretty heavy"). Sophisticated use of metaphor and double-sided reflections validates competing emotions without collapsing them. No instances of misunderstanding or surface-level reflections. This represents MI practice at its best.

#### **Score (1–5):** 4

Logical progression from understanding ambivalence → exploring reasons for change → assessing importance/confidence → identifying strengths → envisioning concrete steps → planning preparation. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance established from opening invitation ("what would you most like to focus on?"). Psychiatrist consistently seeks client input rather than prescribing solutions. Client-generated strategies are affirmed as "really practical and thoughtful" rather than clinician-generated. Language is inclusive and positions client as expert. Minor refinement needed: scaling questions are somewhat directive; could more explicitly invite co-creation of session structure and next steps.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect for client's autonomy and right to choose throughout. Psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence without pressure ("A 6 shows you're definitely leaning toward sharing, even if there's still some hesitation"). Language emphasizes choice: "when you're ready," "feels manageable for you," "your approach." Could be strengthened by explicitly affirming client's right to *not* disclose or to disclose on her own timeline indefinitely, and by avoiding subtle directiveness in scaling language ("boost your confidence").

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes client's own motivations, values, and resources rather than imposing reasons for change. Client generates intrinsic motivations ("it's part of me," "keeping it secret feels like hiding something important"). Strengths are self-identified and then reflected back with amplification. Opportunities exist to deepen evocation by more explicitly exploring underlying values (authenticity, trust, health) and by drawing out health-related intrinsic motivations alongside social ones.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within client. Session structure reflects partnership through open invitation and client-led agenda. Client-generated solutions are affirmed; language centers client agency ("You've clearly thought about," "You seem to have a thoughtful way"). Could be deepened by more explicitly inviting co-creation of session structure and by balancing clinician reflections with more space for client self-articulation rather than offering clinician's interpretation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategically evokes and reinforces change talk throughout. Client's commitment language ("I think maybe it's time to figure out how to tell them") is reflected and amplified. Reasons for change, ability talk, and commitment language are skillfully drawn out. However, change talk cultivation could be more intensive: sustain talk is not strategically redirected; explicit commitment elicitation in client's own words is limited (e.g., "So what are you planning to do?" not asked).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** **AREA FOR GREATEST IMPROVEMENT.** Sustain talk (fear, worry, hesitation) is reflected accurately but not strategically softened or redirected. Psychiatrist takes passive stance toward client's concerns rather than using strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, exploratory questioning, productive tension). When client expresses "I'm still a little scared," response is empathic but doesn't pivot toward change talk or explore fear more deeply. Missed opportunities to ask: "What's the scariest part?" or "What would it take for you to feel ready, even if you're still nervous?"

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 4.0/5**

**Strengths:**
- Exemplary empathy and reflective listening with sophisticated capture of ambivalence and emotional nuance
- Strong collaborative stance positioning client as decision-maker and expert
- Skillful evocation of client's own reasons for change, strengths, and resources
- Consistent acceptance and genuine, specific affirmations
- Clear, appropriate direction with logical session progression
- Effective autonomy support respecting client's pace and choice

**Primary Areas for Development:**
1. **Strategic softening of sustain talk** – Most critical gap. Develop use of double-sided reflections, exploratory questioning, and productive tension to gently challenge fear while maintaining empathy
2. **Deeper exploration of health-related values and motivations** – Session focuses on social/relational motivations but misses opportunity to connect disclosure to diabetes self-management and health outcomes
3. **Explicit commitment elicitation** – Add direct requests for client to articulate plan and commitment in own words
4. **Reduced subtle directiveness** – Refine scaling language to avoid implying upward movement is expected
5. **More explicit autonomy affirmation** – State clearly that client has right to non-disclosure

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Session successfully engages client and builds intrinsic motivation for disclosure. Change talk increases; concrete steps identified; psychological safety established. However, passive handling of sustain talk represents missed opportunity to substantially increase confidence and readiness. With strategic softening of fears and deeper exploration of health-related values, client's confidence could exceed current 5/10 rating and commitment could be more explicit.

**Competency Assessment:**
Psychiatrist demonstrates solid foundational MI competence with particular strength in relationship-building and basic evocation. To reach exemplary practice (5/5), clinician needs focused development in strategic sustain talk management and deeper value exploration. These are advanced MI skills requiring deliberate practice and feedback.
