### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** **Critical failure.** Change talk is entirely absent from session. Sofia never expresses desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change anything. Psychiatrist makes no strategic effort to evoke change talk through targeted questions. Sofia expresses only sustain talk throughout ("Nothing too serious," "I'm not really thinking about big changes," "I don't really feel like I need to change much," "I just want to keep things steady"). No DARN-CAT questions asked. No exploration of what would increase Sofia's confidence or motivation. No questions about what matters to her or why change might be important. Change talk is the mechanism through which intrinsic motivation develops; its complete absence significantly reduces likelihood of Sofia engaging with clinical concern or making any behavior change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** **Critical failure.** Sustain talk is present throughout session and is reinforced rather than softened. Sofia expresses multiple instances: "Nothing too serious," "Nothing really big or stressful," "It's just normal," "I'm not really thinking about big changes," "I don't really feel like I need to change much," "I just want to keep things steady." Rather than softening this, psychiatrist reinforces it: "A 6 shows you have some confidence in keeping things steady...That middle ground where you feel capable and cautious can be a thoughtful place to be" and "Keeping that up in a way that feels manageable seems important to you." No double-sided reflections, amplified reflections, or strategic reframing used. No techniques to gently introduce alternative perspective without confrontation. By validating and reinforcing sustain talk, psychiatrist has actually reduced Sofia's likelihood of engaging with clinical concern. Sofia leaves more entrenched in belief that nothing needs to change.

## SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

### Strengths
- Genuine warmth, acceptance, and nonjudgmental stance create safe therapeutic environment
- Basic reflective listening accurately captures Sofia's surface statements
- Collaborative language and permission-seeking demonstrate respect for client autonomy
- Consistent affirmation of Sofia's strengths and organizational skills
- Appropriate pacing; no rushing or pressure

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Clinical mandate abandoned:** Session never addresses alcohol risk screening that prompted referral—fundamental clinical failure
- **Referral reason never clarified:** Sofia is never asked why she was referred or what she understands about the screening
- **Sustain talk reinforced:** Sofia's language favoring status quo is validated rather than softened, reducing engagement likelihood
- **No change talk evoked:** No strategic effort to elicit Sofia's own motivations, values, or reasons for change
- **Ambivalence unexplored:** Sofia's potential mixed feelings about alcohol never explored
- **Deflection enabled:** Psychiatrist follows Sofia's deflection to homework/friends without gently clarifying session purpose
- **Empathy lacks depth:** No exploration of emotional experience, underlying concerns, or meaning behind statements
- **Partnership absent on clinical issue:** True partnership requires positioning client as expert on own experience; this does not occur regarding alcohol

### Impact on Client Engagement
**Session significantly reduces likelihood of Sofia engaging with clinical concern.** Sofia leaves believing focus is on homework/friends, not alcohol. Her confidence in status quo has likely increased. No change talk expressed. No ambivalence explored. Psychiatrist has inadvertently reinforced precontemplation stage and moved Sofia further from openness to exploration of clinical issue.

### Recommendation
Psychiatrist demonstrates natural warmth and collaborative skills that are valuable foundation. However, these must be deployed strategically toward clinical goal. Recommend focused training on: (1) clarifying and maintaining clinical direction while remaining client-centered; (2) strategically softening sustain talk; (3) evoking change talk using DARN-CAT framework; (4) exploring ambivalence; (5) positioning clients as experts on own experience. With focused practice, this psychiatrist's strengths could be channeled into highly effective MI practice.
