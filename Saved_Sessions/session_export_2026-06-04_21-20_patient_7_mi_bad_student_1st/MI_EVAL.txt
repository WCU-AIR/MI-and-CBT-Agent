Uses controlling language: "You have to quit completely right now—no ifs, ands, or buts," "No, you don't get to hesitate," "Why haven't you stopped already?" Client explicitly requests autonomy ("I want to do it on my own terms," "not just because someone is forcing it on me"), and psychiatrist responds by intensifying pressure. ## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.0/5**

**Assessment:** This session represents a **critical failure in motivational interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates zero competency across all nine MI domains. The approach is fundamentally antithetical to MI principles—expert-driven, authoritarian, dismissive of client autonomy, and characterized by repeated demands that trigger psychological reactance rather than motivation for change.

**Key Failures:**
- ☐ Incorrect problem identification (smoking vs. alcohol)
- ☐ Absence of unconditional positive regard and acceptance
- ☐ No reflective listening or empathic attunement
- ☐ Repeated confrontational demands (verbatim repetition four times)
- ☐ Explicit rejection of four client requests for collaboration
- ☐ Dismissal of substantial change talk
- ☐ Amplification of defensive resistance through control
- ☐ Violation of adolescent developmental needs for autonomy

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. **Fundamental reorientation to MI spirit** (highest priority)—shift from expert/authority to collaborative/partnership stance
2. **Formal MI training and supervision** required before conducting further MI-based sessions
3. **Development of reflective listening skills** as foundation of empathy
4. **Recognition and amplification of existing change talk** rather than ignoring it
5. **Replacement of controlling language** with autonomy-supportive language

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation:** This session **actively undermines** client motivation for change. Client enters with contemplation-stage ambivalence and change talk; exits more defensive and entrenched. Therapeutic alliance damaged. Continued use of this approach is contraindicated and potentially harmful.

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Do not permit further MI-based clinical work until competency-based training and supervision completed.
