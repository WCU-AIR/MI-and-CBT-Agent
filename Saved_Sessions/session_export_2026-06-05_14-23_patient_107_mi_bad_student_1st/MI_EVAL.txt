The opening directive ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do") immediately signals a hierarchical, non-accepting relationship. ### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While the psychiatrist maintains clear focus on smoking cessation (appropriate), the manner of maintaining direction is fundamentally inconsistent with MI principles. Direction is achieved through coercion and repetition rather than through skillful evocation of the client's own motivation. Repeated closed yes/no questions ("Yes or no?" appears 5+ times) shut down exploration rather than opening it. The psychiatrist prescribes specific behaviors (throw away half your cigarettes, avoid smoking spots, keep busy) without any exploration of what might actually work for this particular person in her circumstances. The approach ignores the patient's stage of change—Natalia repeatedly signals she is in contemplation, not preparation, yet the psychiatrist escalates pressure for immediate action.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** This session is a masterclass in autonomy violation. The psychiatrist actively suppresses client choice at every turn using explicitly controlling language: "You have to," "You must," "No exceptions," "Non-negotiable," "No ifs or buts" (15+ instances throughout). When Natalia states she is not ready to commit, the psychiatrist responds: "You have to commit now, no excuses." Her choice is explicitly rejected. When Natalia states, "Forcing me like this just makes me want to hold on to the cigarettes more," the psychiatrist ignores this crucial insight about psychological reactance and continues with the same coercive strategy. There is zero emphasis on client ownership. Natalia is positioned as a passive recipient of directives, not as the agent of her own change. The psychiatrist systematically violates autonomy through repeated "you must" language and explicit rejection of the patient's right to determine her own readiness and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session contains virtually no evocation—the heart of MI. The psychiatrist imposes motivations and reasons for change without ever exploring what matters to the patient herself. Natalia spontaneously offers multiple instances of change talk ("I know it's not good for him," "I really care about my son, and I don't want to harm him," "I want to figure out a way to do better"), yet these are golden opportunities for evocation that are completely ignored. The psychiatrist tells Natalia why she should quit rather than asking her what matters to her. There is no curiosity about her values. The psychiatrist never asks: "What would it mean for your son if you could reduce smoking?" or "What do you want your son to remember about you?" The approach is entirely "telling" rather than "asking." He tells her what to do, why to do it, and when to do it, never asking what she thinks, wants, or believes might work.

## Overall Session Summary

**Total Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Assessment:** The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal MI competence. The session is characterized by expert-driven directives, coercive language, dismissal of the patient's experience, and systematic suppression of the patient's own motivation for change. This is not MI practice—this is authoritarian medical paternalism.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance and empathy
- Systematic violation of autonomy through coercive language
- Complete absence of collaboration and partnership
- Failure to evoke or amplify spontaneous change talk
- Confrontational approach to sustain talk
- Misalignment with patient's stage of change (contemplation)
- Active escalation of coercion when patient explicitly states it backfires

**Impact on Client Engagement:** This session is likely to **decrease** motivation for change and **increase** resistance. The patient explicitly states that coercion backfires for her, yet the psychiatrist escalates coercion. By session end, Natalia has moved from initial openness to explicit refusal. The therapeutic relationship has been damaged, and genuine behavior change is now less likely.

**Critical Recommendation:** Formal MI training and ongoing supervision are essential. This clinician requires fundamental reorientation from directive/expert stance to collaborative/evocative stance, development of genuine reflective listening skills, and commitment to respecting client autonomy and evoking intrinsic motivation.
