## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Therapist Strengths:**
- Excellent interpersonal rapport and therapeutic alliance
- Warm, empathic, non-judgmental stance
- Patient felt safe disclosing sensitive information
- Appropriate professional boundaries
- Session maintained focus without significant tangents

**Critical Areas for Immediate Improvement:**
1. **Structured Agenda-Setting** – Establish explicit, collaborative session agendas with priorities and time allocation
2. **Self-Injury Assessment** – Conduct thorough exploration of frequency, severity, triggers, function; develop safety plan
3. **Case Conceptualization** – Articulate clear, coherent understanding of patient's problem and treatment strategy
4. **Symptom Tracking** – Introduce daily mood/symptom tracking to confirm PMDD diagnosis and measure progress
5. **Guided Discovery** – Replace therapist-provided answers with Socratic questioning
6. **Structured Tools** – Introduce thought records, behavioral logs, and written materials
7. **Homework Follow-Up** – Review homework systematically in every session; adjust approach based on results

**Competency Level:** Below-average CBT competence. Foundational interpersonal skills strong; core CBT competencies weak or absent. Requires intensive supervision and training on CBT fundamentals.

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change:** Low. While patient may experience temporary relief from supportive conversation, lasting symptom improvement is unlikely without systematic CBT implementation, particularly for serious concerns (self-injury) and complex presentation (PMDD with mood cycling).
