**SUPERVISOR NOTES:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from evidence-based CBT practice**. While the therapist demonstrated basic interpersonal skills and did not cause harm, the clinical work was inadequate for a patient with two active anxiety-related diagnoses requiring specific, evidence-based interventions.

**Most Critical Issues (in order of priority):**

1. **Zero CBT techniques applied** – This is not CBT; it is supportive counseling
2. **No agenda or treatment strategy** – Session was aimless and unstructured
3. **Excessive self-disclosure** – Violated therapeutic boundaries; shifted focus from patient
4. **Directive advice-giving instead of collaboration** – Positioned patient as passive recipient
5. **No homework or between-session assignments** – Treatment does not extend beyond session

**Immediate Recommendations:**

- **Do not continue treating this patient without supervision and retraining**
- Engage in weekly CBT-focused supervision with recorded session review
- Complete CBT training on anxiety disorders in children
- Develop written treatment plans with specific interventions before each session
- Practice Socratic questioning and guided discovery techniques
- Learn and apply cognitive restructuring, exposure, and behavioral techniques

**Prognosis if Current Approach Continues:**

Patient is unlikely to experience meaningful symptom improvement. Generic reassurance and positive thinking do not address the mechanisms maintaining separation anxiety or tics. Patient may develop secondary demoralization about therapy effectiveness, complicating future treatment engagement.
