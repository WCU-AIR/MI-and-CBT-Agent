## Overall Summary

### **Session MI Competency Score: 1.3/5**

**Critical Deficiencies:**

1. **Pervasive Expert Stance** — Psychiatrist operates from hierarchical, expert-driven position throughout, violating fundamental MI principle of partnership.

2. **Active Suppression of Autonomy** — Controlling language ("must," "need to," "have to," "not optional") removes choice and communicates coercion rather than support.

3. **Dismissal of Client-Generated Ideas** — When patient offers small, realistic changes, psychiatrist rejects them as inadequate, suppressing emerging motivation.

4. **Failure to Evoke or Amplify Change Talk** — Patient spontaneously offers multiple instances of change talk; psychiatrist ignores all of them and continues prescribing.

5. **Argumentative Responses to Sustain Talk** — Rather than skillfully softening resistance, psychiatrist argues directly against patient's barriers, which strengthens resistance and damages alliance.

6. **Ignoring Client Frustration** — Psychiatrist completely ignores patient's legitimate frustration about 45-minute wait, signaling that patient's experience doesn't matter.

7. **Reliance on Fear & Pressure** — Psychiatrist uses fear-based motivation ("You're risking heart disease") rather than evoking intrinsic motivation.

8. **Minimal Empathic Engagement** — Little genuine effort to understand patient's perspective, work pressures, ambivalence, or lived experience.

---

### **Likelihood of Evoking Sustainable Change:**

**UNLIKELY.** Patient's final agreement to try small changes appears to be compliance to end uncomfortable interaction, not genuine motivation rooted in own values. Without intrinsic motivation, adherence will be poor and behavior change unlikely to be sustained. Therapeutic alliance has been damaged, making future engagement more difficult.

---

### **MI Spirit Assessment:**

This session does **NOT embody MI spirit**. Four core elements are absent:
- **Partnership:** Absent—expert dominance maintained
- **Acceptance:** Absent—judgmental and controlling
- **Compassion:** Minimal—agenda prioritized over patient experience
- **Evocation:** Absent—psychiatrist imposes rather than evokes

This is directive advice-giving with controlling stance, not Motivational Interviewing. Research associates this approach with **increased resistance and worse outcomes**.

---

### **Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Shift from expert to collaborative stance** — Foundational change required
2. **Stop arguing against patient barriers** — Validate and explore instead
3. **Actively amplify change talk** — Patient offered multiple instances; all were ignored
4. **Support autonomy** — Replace prescriptive language with language emphasizing choice
5. **Develop genuine curiosity** — Approach with authentic interest in patient's perspective, not predetermined agenda
6. **Seek formal MI training and supervision immediately** — Current approach is ineffective and potentially harmful

**FORMAL RECOMMENDATION:** Psychiatrist requires immediate formal Motivational Interviewing training (minimum 20 hours) and regular clinical supervision focused on MI skill development. Current practice significantly departs from evidence-based MI principles and is likely to harm patient outcomes and therapeutic relationship.
