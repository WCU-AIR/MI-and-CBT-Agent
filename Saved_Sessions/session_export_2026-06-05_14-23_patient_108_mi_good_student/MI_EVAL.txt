# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Strong validation of coffee's role as both functional and comforting ("coffee plays a pretty important role in your daily life—not just as a pick-me-up but as a comforting routine"). However, subtle assumption that client *should* be managing intake slightly tilts toward change agenda. Could strengthen by more explicitly affirming client's right to maintain current habit without change.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate, multi-layered reflections capturing ambivalence ("It's like you're balancing the comfort and motivation coffee gives you against the hope for a calmer, possibly healthier state, but with some uncertainty about whether the trade-off would be worthwhile"). Effectively captures both emotional and cognitive dimensions. Minor gaps: misses opportunities to explore emotional texture of uncertainty and self-doubt underlying client statements; does not fully explore frustration or confusion when client questions whether he's "doing it wrong."

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** General focus on coffee habit maintained, but direction subtly shifts from "exploring ambivalence" toward "planning reduction experiment" without explicit collaborative agreement. Questions like "How do you imagine things might be different if you were to adjust your coffee intake?" and importance ruler begin steering toward change consideration. Lacks clear check-in confirming whether client has genuinely committed to change direction or is being gently guided there.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine partnership throughout. Language consistently reflects co-construction ("what would you like to focus on today," "It sounds like you're weighing"). Clinician builds on client's ideas rather than imposing solutions. When client suggests cutting back one cup, psychiatrist affirms as "thoughtful and gentle approach" rather than redirecting. Minor compromise: structured importance ruler can create implicit expert-client dynamic; assumption that "this change" is already agreed upon slightly reduces collaborative power-sharing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent affirmation of client's right to choose with avoidance of prescriptive language. Frames potential change as "low-stakes experiment" emphasizing freedom to try without commitment. Does not push back when client expresses ambivalence about necessity of change. However, insufficient explicit affirmation of client's right to *not* change. Framing of "next steps" and "trying this out" subtly assumes experimentation is the direction, which mildly pressures autonomy.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic evocation through open questions, but inconsistent and lacking depth. Minimal strategic amplification of change talk—when client offers "Maybe I'd feel less jittery for sure, which would be nice," psychiatrist reflects without deepening. Limited exploration of values related to health and wellbeing. Insufficient evocation of client's own recognition of discrepancy between enjoying coffee and experiencing jitteriness. Does not deeply explore client's reasoning about why hassle might or might not be worth it.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active fostering of collaboration and power-sharing. Clinician consistently invites client input, builds on contributions, and treats them as valuable. Avoids expert positioning by asking questions rather than providing unsolicited advice. Affirms client's wisdom about his own situation. Language positions client as expert on his experience. Minor compromise: structured assessment tools and assumed direction subtly reduce collaborative equality in moments.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. While change talk is present in client's speech, psychiatrist does not strategically amplify, deepen, or reinforce it. Missed opportunities include: not following up when client says "I could probably stick with it for a bit" (ability/commitment language); not amplifying "I'm pretty good at noticing how my body feels" (self-efficacy); not asking evocative follow-ups to "Maybe I'd sleep a bit better or feel calmer overall." Reflects change talk but moves quickly without building momentum. This represents substantial gap in core MI competency.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic awareness of sustain talk with accurate reflection, but minimal strategic softening or redirection. When client expresses "I'm not convinced yet that it's worth the hassle," psychiatrist reflects without using MI techniques like double-sided reflection or reframing. Appropriately avoids confrontation and arguing against sustain talk, but absence of strategic softening means sustain talk remains relatively unchallenged. Could use techniques like: "On one hand you value the energy coffee gives you, and on the other hand you're curious about whether there's a way to stay alert without the jitteriness."

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Strengths:**
- Solid foundational MI skills in acceptance, empathy, and collaboration
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance
- Genuine partnership with respect for client expertise
- Appropriate pacing that allows space for exploration
- Avoidance of confrontation and pressure

**Session Gaps:**
- Insufficient cultivation and amplification of change talk (most significant)
- Limited evocation of values and client's own recognition of discrepancy
- Minimal strategic softening of sustain talk
- Subtle drift toward change agenda without explicit collaborative agreement
- Insufficient affirmation of client's right to maintain status quo

**Client Outcomes:**
Client leaves with tentative plan to reduce coffee (confidence 6/10, importance 4/10). Low importance rating suggests psychiatrist has not successfully evoked client's personal reasons for change. Change appears more compliant than genuinely motivated. Without stronger evocation of values and intrinsic motivation, client may struggle to maintain change plan beyond session.

**Overall MI Competency Level:** Intermediate—Strong foundational skills with significant gaps in advanced techniques. With focused practice on strategic change talk cultivation and values evocation, clinician could develop into highly skilled motivational interviewer.

**Most Critical Improvements Needed:**
1. Strategic amplification of change talk through follow-up questions
2. Deeper evocation of values and client's own discrepancy recognition
3. More skillful softening of sustain talk using MI techniques
4. Explicit affirmation of autonomy regarding status quo

**Overall Session Score: 3.4/5**
