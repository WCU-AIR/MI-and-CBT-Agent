## Summary Assessment

**Competency Level:** Poor – Significant gaps in Motivational Interviewing practice

**Key Findings:**

This session demonstrates **fundamental misalignment with Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly all domains. While the psychiatrist is warm and well-intentioned, the session operates from an expert, directive stance rather than a collaborative, evocative one. This is supportive counseling with advice-giving, not true MI.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- **Absence of genuine collaboration:** Unilateral decision-making without client input; clinician-driven, not co-created
- **Prescriptive language and pressure:** Repeated "should" and "would probably help" undermines autonomy support
- **Failure to evoke change talk:** Amira's regret and openness are acknowledged but not developed or amplified
- **Inadequate response to sustain talk:** Hopelessness met with reassurance and cheerleading rather than empathic validation and skillful softening
- **Misattunement to core concern:** Amira's primary barrier (isolation and lack of support) is acknowledged factually but never explored emotionally or addressed as genuine barrier
- **Superficial empathy:** Factual reflections lack emotional depth and complex understanding

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to **decrease** intrinsic motivation for change and reinforce Amira's sense of helplessness. Her compliance ("I guess I could try") reflects external motivation, not genuine commitment. Without partnership, exploration of her own reasons for change, and validation of real barriers, Amira is unlikely to sustain change efforts.

**Recommendations:**

1. Shift from expert to collaborative partner
2. Validate and explore Amira's core concern (isolation and lack of support)
3. Amplify and develop change talk through strategic questioning
4. Use double-sided reflection to soften sustain talk without confrontation
5. Explore barriers and past attempts before prescribing solutions
6. Eliminate prescriptive language; use collaborative language instead
7. Support autonomy explicitly; emphasize client choice and control
8. Explore client's values, strengths, and past successes to build intrinsic motivation
9. Assess readiness and barriers collaboratively before suggesting interventions
10. **Receive formal Motivational Interviewing training and supervised practice** – Essential for developing true MI competence

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustainable Client Motivation:** **Low**

Formal MI training and supervised practice are essential for this clinician to develop competence needed to effectively work with ambivalent clients.
