--- DISORDER ---
Other Specified Depressive Disorder (Depressive Episode with Insufficient Symptoms)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ian Campbell, a 32-year-old architect, presented for psychiatric evaluation due to impaired concentration and work difficulties. His current episode followed the breakdown of his 6-year marriage. Previously, he had experienced brief episodes of cognitive and motivational impairment during college and at prior jobs, often in the context of personal loss or romantic setbacks.

Family psychiatric history was notable for maternal major depressive disorder (mother and sister) and a maternal uncle who died by suicide.

During the current episode, Mr. Campbell reported apathy, diminished energy, slowed speech, and morning worsening of symptoms. He did not report sadness, guilt, hopelessness, suicidal thoughts, sleep or appetite changes. Despite partial symptomatology, his psychiatrist observed her own affective response to him—feeling glum and pessimistic—which suggested a depressive syndrome.

Mr. Campbell agreed to brief psychotherapy focused on coping with loss, supplemented by antidepressant medication. Within weeks, his functioning improved to baseline. No additional depressive symptoms emerged during treatment.

Discussion: Mr. Campbell’s presentation illustrates a subthreshold depressive episode. He did not meet full DSM-5 criteria for major depressive disorder (MDD) because he lacked sufficient symptoms, but his functional impairment and clinician-observed affect suggest clinically significant depression. This case exemplifies “other specified depressive disorder,” highlighting that depressive syndromes exist along a continuum of severity and can cause substantial suffering even when criteria for MDD are not fully met. Recurrent brief or subthreshold episodes confer increased risk for future MDD and other adverse outcomes.
