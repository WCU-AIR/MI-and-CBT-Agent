## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### Competency Level: **Inadequate**

This session does **not** constitute evidence-based cognitive behavioral therapy. The therapist demonstrated basic interpersonal warmth and professionalism but failed to implement core CBT components.

### Critical Deficiencies:

1. **No structural framework:** Absent agenda, case conceptualization, and treatment strategy
2. **No functional analysis:** Maintaining factors of insomnia not identified or targeted
3. **Advice-giving, not collaborative empiricism:** Patient positioned as passive recipient rather than active partner
4. **No guided discovery:** Socratic questioning absent; therapist prescribed solutions
5. **No targeted interventions:** Generic wellness suggestions offered without coherent strategy
6. **No homework:** Between-session work absent; no structured practice or experimentation
7. **Excessive self-disclosure:** 6+ personal anecdotes shifted focus from patient to therapist
8. **Repetition without progress:** Same suggestions cycled without deepening exploration

### Likelihood of Therapeutic Change: **LOW**

Without targeted intervention addressing insomnia maintenance factors, structured homework, and systematic application of CBT-I techniques, the patient is unlikely to experience significant symptom improvement. The session provided support but not the evidence-based treatment necessary for insomnia disorder.

### Immediate Recommendations:

1. **Formal CBT training required** – Particularly CBT-I protocols
2. **Supervision with experienced CBT therapist** – Before continuing to treat insomnia
3. **Study case conceptualization** – Learn functional analysis and treatment planning
4. **Master guided discovery** – Replace advice-giving with Socratic questioning
5. **Implement structured homework** – Assign specific, measurable, rationale-based tasks

---

**Evaluator Note:** This therapist would benefit from intensive training in CBT fundamentals before continuing to treat patients with insomnia disorder. The current approach, while supportive, does not meet standards for evidence-based cognitive behavioral therapy.
