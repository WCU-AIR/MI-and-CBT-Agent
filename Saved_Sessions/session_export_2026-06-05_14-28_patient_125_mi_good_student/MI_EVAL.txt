### Acceptance

**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard throughout the session. Early normalization of Sofia's experience ("That's a really common experience—using something that feels like a relief, even if others have concerns") communicates acceptance without judgment. ### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear, strategic direction toward exploring stress-management alternatives to marijuana use. Session progression is logical: exploring current use and ambivalence → identifying wishes for change → assessing importance and confidence → exploring past successes → building on resources → developing concrete action step. Direction remains responsive to Sofia's pace and readiness. Minor areas for improvement: (1) Does not explicitly explore discrepancy between Sofia's values and current behavior; (2) When Sofia rates importance at 5/10, clinician acknowledges but does not strategically explore what would move that number higher. ### Collaboration

Inclusive language ("together," "we") and consistent invitation for Sofia's input rather than clinician imposing direction. ### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of Sofia's own motivations, values, and resources through strategic questioning. Psychiatrist consistently asks rather than tells, drawing out Sofia's internal motivation. Key evocative moments include asking about past successes with stress management, inviting Sofia to build on sister connection, and asking what small step Sofia feels willing to take. Sofia generates her own ideas rather than receiving prescriptive advice. However, three missed opportunities: (1) Does not explicitly evoke Sofia's values or long-term goals, which would create powerful intrinsic motivation; (2) When Sofia expresses doubt about marijuana's effectiveness, clinician could have more deeply explored this ambivalence; (3) Does not explicitly ask about Sofia's concerns or reasons for change ("What concerns you most about marijuana use?").

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership demonstrated with genuine power-sharing and positioning of Sofia as expert on her own life. Clinician actively avoids expert role by not prescribing strategies, lecturing, or telling Sofia what to do. Sofia's contributions substantially shape session direction. Clinician builds on Sofia's insights rather than imposing solutions. Two minor areas for improvement: (1) Metaphors and poetic language are clinician-generated rather than co-constructed with Sofia ("like needing a safe harbor..."). True partnership would invite Sofia to generate her own metaphors; (2) Introduction of assessment tools could include more explicit invitation for Sofia's input on whether tools feel helpful. These small shifts would ensure language and framing come from Sofia's own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent cultivation of change talk. Sofia articulates change language ("I guess that's different from using pot because it's more about facing it instead of just numbing it," "Maybe I could try talking to my sister...," "I'd say maybe a 6" on confidence), but psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reinforce, or strategically evoke this language. When Sofia expresses change talk, clinician reflects accurately but then pivots to exploring barriers rather than first amplifying the change language. Significant missed opportunities: (1) Does not explicitly ask about Sofia's desires, abilities, reasons, needs, or commitment to change; (2) Does not strategically ask "What appeals to you about that approach?" or "Tell me more about that insight"; (3) Limited exploration of what would increase importance or confidence ratings. Sustain talk is reflected but not strategically addressed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent softening of sustain talk. Sofia articulates several status-quo statements ("It's not a big deal," "Everyone's kinda doing it," "It's easier just to light up and zone out," "It just doesn't seem like it'd work for me"). Psychiatrist reflects these accurately and uses some strategic redirects (asking about past successes, pivoting to open questions) but does not consistently address sustain talk. Missed opportunities: (1) Does not explore discrepancy between Sofia's minimization ("It's not a big deal") and her earlier doubt about dependence; (2) Does not address social comparison sustain talk ("Everyone's doing it") with questions like "Is that what you want for yourself?"; (3) Limited use of double-sided reflections highlighting values-behavior discrepancy; (4) Does not strategically explore what Sofia might be avoiding by numbing out. Most critical gap: Psychiatrist does not explicitly connect Sofia's stated values (family expectations, academic success, feeling less alone) to her current marijuana use to create powerful discrepancy.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 3.9/5**

**Clinician Competency Level:** Competent but Developing MI Practitioner

**Session Strengths:**
- Exemplary empathy with complex, accurate reflections capturing emotional nuance
- Strong autonomy support with consistent emphasis on Sofia's freedom and ownership
- Genuine acceptance and unconditional positive regard throughout
- Excellent partnership with power-sharing and client-centered agenda-setting
- Strategic direction with clear progression toward exploring alternatives
- Collaborative problem-solving with Sofia generating her own ideas
- Skillful use of open-ended questions inviting reflection and self-discovery
- Validation of ambivalence without judgment or pressure

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Strategic amplification of change talk** – Sofia articulates change language but clinician does not consistently amplify or reinforce it
2. **Explicit exploration of values-behavior discrepancy** – Most powerful motivator in MI is absent; clinician does not connect Sofia's values to current behavior
3. **Softening sustain talk through discrepancy exploration** – Sofia's minimization and barriers are reflected but not strategically addressed
4. **Explicit evocation of concerns and reasons for change** – Limited exploration of what concerns Sofia about marijuana or what reasons she has for change
5. **Strategic use of scaling questions** – When Sofia rates importance at 5/10, clinician does not explore what would increase this rating

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
Session likely to maintain Sofia's engagement and openness to exploring alternatives but may not substantially increase her motivation or readiness for change. Sofia leaves with concrete action step (texting sister) but without deepened sense of why change matters personally. Importance rating remains at 5/10; confidence remains tentative at 6/10. Session demonstrates solid foundational MI competence but lacks strategic application needed to maximize intrinsic motivation for change.

**Highest-Leverage Recommendations:**
1. Amplify change talk when Sofia articulates it through follow-up questions that deepen her own articulation
2. Explicitly explore Sofia's values and long-term goals, then connect to current marijuana use
3. Use double-sided reflections to highlight values-behavior discrepancy
4. Ask directly about concerns and reasons for change
5. Follow scaling questions with strategic exploration of what would increase ratings
