# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist uses repeated judgmental language ("You really should," "You really need to," "You must realize") and disapproving stance throughout. Fails to normalize ambivalence or recognize autonomy. Demands for commitment feel coercive rather than supportive. No affirmation of willingness to consider change. Compliance obtained through sustained pressure, not genuine acceptance-based engagement.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand client's perspective. When patient expresses concern about drastic change ("feels like a big leap"), psychiatrist does not reflect or explore. Uses patient's emerging insight about fall as ammunition rather than exploring with curiosity. Completely misses emotional and practical difficulty patient expresses. Treats hesitation as resistance to overcome rather than information to understand.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains extremely clear and consistent focus on target behavior (reducing to 2 drinks/week) throughout all exchanges. Never allows conversation to drift. However, direction is achieved through directive control and pressure rather than collaborative exploration. Strategic focus is present but fundamentally inconsistent with MI principles of autonomy support and partnership.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Establishes hierarchical expert-client dynamic from opening ("I'll be giving you advice on what you should do"). Makes unilateral decisions about what patient "should," "must," and "has to" do. Dismisses patient's compromise proposal (4-5 drinks) without negotiation or exploration. No joint problem-solving or genuine partnership. Power dynamic entirely one-directional. Does not engage when patient requests collaborative support and planning.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively undermines autonomy throughout session with repeated imperatives ("you must," "you have to," "you really need to"). Never affirms patient's freedom to choose differently. Frames patient's hesitation as irrational ("Why wouldn't you want to protect your health?") rather than respecting as valid expression of autonomy. Final agreement is capitulation under pressure, not autonomous choice. No statements affirming patient's right to self-determination.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates almost no evocation. Imposes reasons for change rather than drawing out patient's own motivation. When patient spontaneously generates change talk ("Maybe my drinking was part of that fall," "I want to be healthier"), psychiatrist does not amplify or explore. Uses change talk as ammunition for pressure instead. Asks pressuring questions ("Are you going to do it or not?") rather than exploratory questions. Never explores patient's values, social connections, or what alcohol means to her.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively assumes and maintains expert role throughout with no genuine power-sharing. Positions self as sole source of expertise and wisdom. Uses directive language consistently ("you should," "you need to," "you must") with no inclusive language ("we," "let's," "together"). Never acknowledges that patient knows her own life and constraints. Does not honor patient's explicit request for collaborative problem-solving and support planning.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient generates spontaneous change talk ("Maybe my drinking was part of that fall," "I want to be healthier," "I'm willing to try cutting back"), but psychiatrist fails to evoke, amplify, or reinforce it. Instead uses change talk as evidence for pressure and immediately negates by returning to demands. Commitment obtained through sustained pressure, not skillful evocation. Never explores patient's confidence or ability to make change. Assumes willpower is issue rather than exploring actual capacity and resources.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses confrontation and direct contradiction to address sustain talk rather than skillfully softening through empathetic reflection. When patient expresses sustain talk ("Seven drinks doesn't seem like that much," "It's part of my social life"), psychiatrist responds with direct contradiction and imperatives rather than double-sided reflections. Sustain talk appears to decrease due to capitulation under pressure, not genuine MI-consistent softening. Does not actively reinforce sustain talk, but fails to skillfully soften it through gentle discrepancy-building.

---

## Overall Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.3/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates fundamental incompatibility with Motivational Interviewing principles. The psychiatrist is not practicing MI; instead, practicing directive advice-giving with confrontation and pressure. While directional focus is maintained (Score: 5), this is achieved through coercive control rather than collaborative exploration. All other core MI competencies score 1-2, indicating minimal to absent implementation.

**Critical Findings:**
- Authoritarian stance replaces partnership
- Pressure and demand replace evocation
- Dismissal of autonomy throughout
- Confrontation replaces empathy
- Compliance obtained through coercion, not intrinsic motivation

**Clinical Impact:** This approach is likely to **harm** patient motivation for change and damage therapeutic relationship. Research indicates directive, confrontational approaches increase client defensiveness and decrease intrinsic motivation. Patient's final agreement represents capitulation under pressure, not authentic motivation. Likelihood of sustained behavior change is **very low**.

**Immediate Recommendation:** This clinician should not continue using this approach with patients presenting ambivalence about behavior change. **Formal MI training is essential** before continuing work with patients in contemplation stage. Current approach violates core MI principles and is contraindicated for this clinical presentation.
