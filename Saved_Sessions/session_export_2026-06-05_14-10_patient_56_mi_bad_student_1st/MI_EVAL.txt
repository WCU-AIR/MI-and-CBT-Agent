## Overall Summary

### **Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

### Strengths:
- **Clear focus on target behavior:** Psychiatrist consistently maintains focus on alcohol use and does not allow conversation to drift to unrelated topics.
- **Explicit about concerns:** Psychiatrist clearly communicates health concerns about client's drinking.

### Critical Deficiencies:

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist employs a directive, confrontational, expert-driven approach that is directly contraindicated in MI and empirically associated with increased resistance and decreased motivation for change.

**Core deficiencies:**
1. **Authoritarian stance:** Psychiatrist positions self as sole expert and decision-maker, explicitly denying client's right to their own perspective ("You're wrong," "This isn't about what you feel").
2. **Systematic autonomy violation:** Repeatedly demands compliance, denies choice, escalates pressure when client appropriately asserts autonomy.
3. **Confrontational approach to resistance:** Argues against sustain talk rather than skillfully handling it, which predictably strengthens resistance.
4. **Zero evocation:** Tells rather than asks, imposes reasons for change rather than evoking them, shows no curiosity about client's internal world.
5. **Absence of empathy:** No reflections, no attempt to understand client's perspective, explicit rejection of client's emotional experience.
6. **Escalating coercion:** As client resists, psychiatrist escalates pressure ("That's final," "You must," "No ifs, ands, or buts")—least effective response.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change:

This session is likely to **decrease** client's motivation for change and damage therapeutic relationship. Client enters in precontemplation (not seeing alcohol use as problematic) and exits in active resistance, explicitly stating they will not commit to change. Psychiatrist's approach teaches client that:
- Their perspective is invalid and not worth considering
- Their autonomy is not respected
- External pressure and coercion are psychiatrist's tools
- Compliance is demanded, not collaboration sought

Research on MI consistently shows this approach increases reactance and decreases intrinsic motivation. Client's final statement—"I'll think about it, but like I said, I'm not making any promises right now"—represents surface compliance at best, with no genuine commitment to change. Session has moved in opposite direction from evoking client's own motivation for behavior change.

### Clinical Recommendation:

**This psychiatrist should not continue conducting sessions using this approach without formal MI training and supervision.** Current approach is likely to harm client outcomes and damage therapeutic relationships. Immediate engagement with MI training and supervision is essential. This is not "below average MI practice"—it is non-MI practice that is contraindicated for evoking behavior change.
