### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains appropriate focus on blood pressure management with logical progression: exploring meaning → assessing importance → identifying strengths → discussing options → narrowing to specific changes. Client-directed opening establishes clear focus. Direction softens when client dismisses alcohol; psychiatrist accepts without exploring or maintaining strategic focus on alcohol-hypertension connection. Misses opportunity to gently redirect: "I'm wondering—when we talk about things that can affect blood pressure, alcohol does come up. What's your understanding of how that might connect?"

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine invitations for client input: "Before we get started, I want to make sure that this time is all about what matters most to you" and "What would feel most helpful for you right now?" Co-constructs understanding effectively ("It's like you're balancing between..."). Collaboration weakens when presenting three lifestyle factors without first eliciting client perspective. More collaborative approach would ask: "What do you think might be contributing to your blood pressure?" before offering suggestions.

### Autonomy Support

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate skill in evoking change talk but insufficient amplification and reinforcement. Successfully elicits desire ("better to avoid problems later on...Staying healthy for myself and my spouse") and ability (smoking cessation example). However, fails to strengthen these with follow-up questions. After smoking recall, could ask: "What was it about that experience that showed you that you could make a change when it mattered to you?" Most significantly, fails to evoke any change talk around alcohol—the most clinically relevant behavior. Misses opportunity to explore ambivalence in statement "I do have a drink or two now and then, but...I don't think it's a big issue."

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal skill in addressing sustain talk; allows it to persist without strategic intervention. Sustain talk appears throughout ("not sure it's that bad yet," "hard to think it's a big deal," "feels a bit far off," "not really into big changes right away," alcohol "not a big issue"). Psychiatrist reflects accurately but fails to strategically soften or reframe. Most critically: alcohol sustain talk is accepted without challenge ("you're already mindful about salt and alcohol"). Should use double-sided reflection: "On one hand, you see your drinking as occasional and controlled. On the other hand, alcohol does affect blood pressure, and I'm wondering if there's any part of you that wonders whether even occasional drinking might be playing a role?" Fails to address "I'm not really into big changes" with reframing that these are small, manageable adjustments.

## **OVERALL SESSION SCORE: 3.6/5**

### **Summary Assessment**

**Strengths:**
- Excellent opening establishing client-centered focus and genuine partnership
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and affirmation of strengths
- Accurate reflective listening capturing ambivalence and internal conflict
- Strategic use of importance scaling (0-10)
- Skillful evocation of client resources (smoking cessation)
- Genuine autonomy support with authentic choice-offering
- Logical session progression and warm, respectful tone

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient evocation and amplification of change talk**, particularly around alcohol
- **Weak handling of sustain talk** with minimal strategic reframing or softening
- **Inadequate exploration of alcohol use** despite clinical relevance to hypertension; client minimization accepted without deeper inquiry
- **Premature presentation of clinician expertise** without first exploring client perspective
- **Missed opportunities to strengthen emerging commitments** with follow-up evocation
- **Insufficient use of double-sided reflections** to address ambivalence
- **No explicit plan** for revisiting dismissed topics in future sessions

### **Clinical Significance**

The most significant gap is **inadequate exploration of alcohol use**. Andrei minimized his drinking, and the psychiatrist accepted this without strategic exploration. This is clinically problematic because: (1) alcohol directly contributes to hypertension, (2) minimizing language suggests potential ambivalence, (3) no plan was made to revisit this critical factor, and (4) acceptance may reinforce minimization rather than gently challenge it.

### **Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation**

Session moderately supports motivation but leaves significant potential untapped. Importance remains at 5/10 (middle ground); confidence not assessed. Dismissal of alcohol without exploration means a key lever for change remains unaddressed. If pattern continues, client may not develop sufficient intrinsic motivation to sustain behavioral change.

### **Recommendations for Development**

This clinician has strong MI foundation. To advance to good (4/5) or excellent (5/5) practice, prioritize: (1) developing stronger sustain talk softening techniques, (2) deepening exploration of ambivalence (especially alcohol), (3) amplifying change talk through follow-up questions, and (4) using strategic questioning to elicit client perspective before offering expertise. With targeted practice, this clinician can become an excellent motivational interviewer.
