Strengths are explicitly affirmed ("valuable strength," "good sense of self-control"). Normalizes adolescent peer influence appropriately. Tone remains warm and collaborative throughout. Minor limitation: could have more explicitly affirmed Marcus's willingness to engage in conversation itself or personalized acceptance beyond drinking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking. Captures both content and emotional nuance ("more like background noise in your social world"). Accurately identifies core value of peer acceptance and belonging. Reflections demonstrate genuine understanding of what matters to Marcus. Limitation: could have explored more deeply the emotional experience of peer pressure or fear of being "the odd one out" with fuller emotional resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use throughout session. Strategic progression through MI domains: normality → consequences → values → strengths → next steps. Questions consistently return to target behavior while remaining open-ended. Limitation: could include more comprehensive behavioral assessment (frequency, quantity, contexts, escalation patterns) and exploration of protective factors beyond drinking.

### Collaboration

Uses collaborative language ("I wonder," "At the same time"). Seeks Marcus's perspective rather than imposing one. Scaling questions invite self-assessment. Minor limitation: near session end, shifts toward clinician-suggested steps ("If you were to think...") rather than asking Marcus to generate ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and affirms Marcus's autonomy and right to choose. No pressure, coercion, or directive language present. Validates current approach without suggesting mandatory change. Emphasizes Marcus's agency and control throughout. Frames potential change as optional exploration. Minor limitation: could more explicitly name Marcus's freedom to continue drinking without change (e.g., "You're free to make your own choices about this").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation of Marcus's strengths and resources but significant limitations in strategic change talk development. Successfully elicits self-control as strength and builds on it. However, fails to deeply explore Marcus's actual values and goals beyond peer acceptance. Minimal strategic amplification of emerging change talk ("Maybe if it started messing with school..."). Does not explore range of drinking behavior or potential harms. Limited exploration of discrepancy between values and current behavior.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership stance with genuine power-sharing and respect for Marcus's expertise about his own life. Positions Marcus as equal partner throughout. Uses collaborative language and invites input into process. Avoids expert role by asking Marcus to explain his own reasoning. Minor shift toward end: begins suggesting direction rather than purely exploring Marcus's own ideas, representing subtle erosion of full partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. Demonstrates minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce Marcus's change talk. While clinician does not suppress change talk, there is striking absence of targeted techniques. When Marcus produces change talk ("Maybe if it started messing with school..."), clinician reflects but doesn't develop through follow-up questions. Missing: strategic questioning to elicit change talk, amplification of emerging statements, reinforcement of change talk, exploration of discrepancy, and open questions targeting change talk. Session ends with Marcus expressing commitment to status quo with minimal change talk produced.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate skill in avoiding confrontation and using reflection to soften sustain talk, but misses opportunities for deeper work. Uses reflection appropriately rather than confrontation. However, insufficient use of double-sided reflection that acknowledges both sustain talk AND emerging change talk. Limited reframing of peer normalization ("everyone does it"). Insufficient exploration of what "broken" would look like. No strategic pivot to change talk. Acceptance of sustain talk sometimes feels like simple validation rather than gentle exploration of underlying ambivalence.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Strengths:**
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and warm, nonjudgmental stance
- Strong, specific reflective listening that captures emotional nuance
- Excellent affirmation of Marcus's self-control and self-awareness
- Genuine collaborative stance with power-sharing and respect for autonomy
- Appropriate pacing, tone, and focus on target behavior
- Skillful avoidance of confrontation

**Session Limitations:**
- **Critical gap:** Insufficient cultivation and amplification of change talk
- Limited exploration of Marcus's actual values, goals, and identity
- Weak use of double-sided reflection to highlight ambivalence
- Insufficient assessment of actual drinking patterns and risk
- Minimal exploration of peer pressure dynamics and identity formation
- Clinician-suggested rather than co-constructed next steps

**Overall MI Competency Level:** Solid MI *spirit* (partnership, acceptance, compassion) with weak MI *technique* (particularly evocation and change talk cultivation). Psychiatrist has built excellent alliance but has not leveraged it to help Marcus explore his own reasons for reconsidering drinking.

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Marcus remains engaged and feels respected throughout, indicating successful alliance-building. However, session is unlikely to significantly shift Marcus's motivation for change. Marcus leaves with sustain talk largely intact ("I just want to have fun and not let drinking mess things up for me. That seems like enough for now") and minimal change talk produced or reinforced.

**Recommendations for Improvement (Priority Order):**
1. **Strategically evoke and amplify change talk** – Follow every instance of change talk with open questions designed to develop and strengthen it
2. **Explore and develop discrepancy** – Help Marcus articulate gap between his values/goals and current drinking behavior
3. **Use double-sided reflection** – Explicitly acknowledge both sustain talk and change talk in single reflections
4. **Deepen exploration of peer pressure and identity** – Help Marcus distinguish between his own values and peer-driven behavior
5. **Conduct more thorough behavioral assessment** – Gather specific information about patterns, contexts, and potential harms
6. **Ask Marcus to generate next steps first** – Invite his ideas before offering suggestions
7. **Explore protective factors and alternative sources of belonging** – Help identify other sources of identity and fun beyond drinking
8. **Use scaling questions more strategically** – Follow up with exploration of both ends of scale and conditions for change
9. **Strengthen reflections to gently challenge sustain talk** – Use curious, exploratory reflections rather than simple validation
10. **Develop clearer MI case conceptualization** – Prepare before sessions by identifying stage of change, key ambivalence, and target change talk

**Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**
