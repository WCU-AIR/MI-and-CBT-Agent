
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. **Immediate Psychiatric Evaluation and Medication Consideration**
- **Action:** Refer patient to psychiatry for comprehensive evaluation including medication assessment. Antipsychotics are evidence-based first-line treatment for schizophreniform disorder.
- **Example:** "Based on what you've shared about the voices and how they're affecting your life, I'd like to connect you with a psychiatrist who specializes in these symptoms. They may recommend medication to help reduce the voices and improve your focus. This is a standard treatment that helps many people."
- **Practice:** Familiarize yourself with antipsychotic medications, their mechanisms, side effects, and evidence base for first-episode psychosis. Understand when psychiatric referral is appropriate.

### 2. **Conduct Systematic Psychiatric Assessment**
- **Action:** Gather detailed information about psychotic symptoms before offering interventions. Use a structured assessment tool (e.g., PANSS, SAPS/SANS).
- **Example:** "Tell me more about the voices. How often do you hear them? What exactly do they say? Do they ever tell you to do things? How much do they distress you on a scale of 1-10? How do they affect your ability to work or study?"
- **Practice:** Learn to assess: frequency, intensity, content, distress, functional impact, command compliance, suicidality, self-harm, and ideas of reference. Document systematically.

### 3. **Eliminate Excessive Self-Disclosure**
- **Action:** Remove personal anecdotes from clinical work. Focus entirely on the patient's experience, not the clinician's.
- **Example:** Instead of "When I was stressed, I told myself 'This too shall pass,'" say: "Many people find it helpful to remind themselves that difficult periods do pass. What has helped you through tough times in the past?"
- **Practice:** Record sessions and count instances of self-disclosure. Aim for zero clinician-centered personal stories. Redirect any impulse to share personal experience back to the patient's experience.

### 4. **Establish Clear Agenda and Treatment Plan**
- **Action:** Collaboratively set session goals and develop a coherent treatment strategy.
- **Example:** "Today I'd like to understand more about the voices—when they started, what they say, how they affect you. Then we can talk about what treatment might help. Does that sound okay? Is there anything else you'd like to focus on today?"
- **Practice:** At the start of each session, explicitly state: (1) What you'll discuss, (2) Why it matters, (3) How long you'll spend on each topic, (4) What you hope to accomplish.

### 5. **Apply Evidence-Based CBT Techniques for Psychosis**
- **Action:** Learn and implement CBT for psychosis (CBTp), which includes reality-testing, coping strategies, and behavioral activation.
- **Example:** "You mentioned the voices tell you you're a failure. Let's examine that together. What evidence do you have that you're a failure? What evidence contradicts that? How do you know the voices are telling you the truth?"
- **Practice:** Complete training in CBT for psychosis. Practice thought records, behavioral experiments, reality-testing, and coping strategy development with supervision.

### 6. **Conduct Safety Assessment**
- **Action:** Explicitly assess suicidality, self-harm, and command compliance given guilt-content hallucinations.
- **Example:** "The voices tell you you're a failure and make you feel guilty. Have you ever thought about hurting yourself? Do the voices ever tell you to hurt yourself? If they did, do you think you would do it?"
- **Practice:** Learn suicide risk assessment. Develop safety planning skills. Know when hospitalization is indicated.

### 7. **Address Ideas of Reference with Reality-Testing**
- **Action:** Systematically examine the patient's belief that people are "whispering or judging."
- **Example:** "You mentioned feeling like people notice something's off and are judging you. What specifically makes you think that? Have you asked anyone directly? What would happen if you approached someone and asked what they're thinking?"
- **Practice:** Learn to conduct behavioral experiments that test delusional beliefs in a collaborative, non-confrontational way.

### 8. **Develop Behavioral Activation Plan**
- **Action:** Schedule specific valued activities (drawing, social contact) to address negative symptoms and functional impairment.
- **Example:** "You mentioned drawing used to help you feel calm. Let's schedule specific times this week when you'll draw—say, 15 minutes on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 7 PM. We'll track how you feel before and after. Does that work?"
- **Practice:** Learn behavioral activation techniques. Use activity scheduling, graded task assignment, and values-based goal-setting.

### 9. **Provide Psychoeducation About Psychotic Illness**
- **Action:** Educate the patient about schizophreniform disorder, prognosis, treatment, and recovery.
- **Example:** "What you're experiencing—the voices and the feelings that people are judging you—are symptoms of a condition called schizophreniform disorder. It's a treatable condition. With proper treatment, many people recover fully or significantly improve. Let me explain what's happening in your brain and how treatment helps."
- **Practice:** Develop a psychoeducation module covering: what psychosis is, causes, prognosis, treatment options (medication, therapy, lifestyle), recovery, and hope.

- **Example:** "Your girlfriend and sister care about you and want to help. Let's meet together so I can explain what's happening and we can develop a plan to support you. Family support is one of the strongest predictors of recovery."
