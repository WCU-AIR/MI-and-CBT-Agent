### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1.5/5
**Comments:** Evocation—core of MI—demonstrates minimal implementation. Rather than asking questions to elicit Timur's own motivations and values, psychiatrist tells him what he should want. Closed, leading questions ("Are you exercising regularly? Are you eating healthy meals every day?") designed to confirm predetermined agenda, not evoke Timur's motivations. Psychiatrist fails to evoke existing change talk: 22-day sobriety achievement, relationship motivation with Fiona, recognition of small joys, commitment language ("I'm that kinda guy—once I make a decision, I stick to it"). This existing motivation is minimized rather than explored and amplified. Focus remains on what Timur is NOT doing rather than building on what he IS doing. Only in third exchange does psychiatrist ask open question designed to evoke ("Can you tell me what changes you feel ready to try right now?"), representing single instance of genuine evocation after extensive prescriptive approach. Timur responds with change talk when invited, suggesting capacity for evocation exists but remains largely underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled poorly in first two exchanges through confrontation and pressure, which typically amplifies rather than softens resistance. Timur expresses sustain talk ("If I get a job that I hate, I just won't do it"; "I'm lazy"; "It's not that simple for me"; "Telling me to cut out all the junk like that sounds easy in theory, but in my world, it's a lot harder"). Psychiatrist's response is confrontational: "Why didn't you start a proper routine earlier? You need to take this seriously or you'll never get anywhere." This direct confrontation typically amplifies sustain talk rather than softening it. Psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections to acknowledge validity of concerns while gently exploring tension between values and fears. No reframing used to help Timur see sustain talk differently (e.g., "I'm lazy" reframed as protective wisdom rather than character flaw). In third exchange, psychiatrist shifts toward more collaborative stance that implicitly softens sustain talk by inviting small, manageable steps. This is gentler approach but lacks skillful MI-specific techniques for addressing sustain talk. Overall approach relies on pressure rather than strategic softening.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.8/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **critical failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles** in first two exchanges, followed by significant but reactive correction in third exchange. Psychiatrist's initial approach—characterized by directive prescriptions, judgment, pressure, and minimization of client's existing motivation—directly violates core MI spirit and undermines therapeutic alliance. Late-stage recognition of ineffectiveness and shift toward collaboration is positive but does not compensate for extensive damage caused by directive, judgmental approach.

**Key Strengths:**
- Late-stage recognition and explicit acknowledgment of ineffectiveness ("I realize now that pushing you to change immediately and telling you what to do isn't helpful")
- Shift toward open questions inviting client input
- Explicit affirmation of effort and progress
- Invitation to genuine collaboration in final exchanges

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- Eliminate directive, prescriptive approach from opening of sessions
- Develop ability to evoke and amplify existing change talk rather than minimizing it
- Cultivate genuine acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- Maintain MI consistency throughout entire session rather than shifting only after directive failure
- Develop complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and ambivalence
- Use double-sided reflections to address ambivalence without confrontation
- Recognize and reframe self-defeating beliefs with compassion
- Establish true partnership by explicitly inviting client expertise

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Initial approach directly undermines Timur's intrinsic motivation. His explicit statement—"I need to find the right way to do it that I can stick with, not just a demand to 'do it now'"—indicates pressure triggers reactance. However, final exchange demonstrates that when psychiatrist adopts MI-consistent stance, Timur responds with genuine change talk and articulates manageable steps. This suggests capacity for MI competence exists but requires significant training and practice.

**Recommendation:** Intensive MI training with supervised practice, feedback, role-play, and audio/video review of sessions essential to developing true MI competence.
