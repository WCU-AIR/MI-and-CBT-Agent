### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal and ineffective sustain talk management. Uses confrontation and argument rather than skillful techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing. Patient expresses sustain talk throughout ("it's hard to stop," "I'm scared," "I'm not sure I'm ready"), and psychiatrist responds with confrontation ("you really have to push through that feeling," "waiting longer just makes things worse"). Patient sustain talk actually increases as session progresses, suggesting approach is counterproductive. Single skillful moment near end uses double-sided reflection and reframing ("It sounds like cutting back is a step...What do you think would help?") but occurs only once and cannot offset pervasive confrontational approach.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.3/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor motivational interviewing practice** with pervasive violations of MI spirit and technique across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist assumes an authoritarian, expert-driven stance and uses coercive, shame-based language to attempt behavioral control. The approach is confrontational rather than skillful in handling ambivalence and sustain talk, likely strengthening resistance rather than evoking motivation for change.

**Strengths Identified:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (smoking cessation)
- Expresses genuine concern for patient welfare
- Demonstrates knowledge of health risks
- Demonstrates capacity for MI-consistent approach in final exchange

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Authoritarian stance with prescriptive, controlling language
- Absence of acceptance and genuine empathy
- Coercive pressure and shame-based motivation
- Failure to evoke change talk through questioning
- Confrontational approach to sustain talk
- Rejection of patient's ideas without exploration
- Undermining of patient autonomy and self-determination

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Session is unlikely to evoke intrinsic motivation for change. Patient's sustain talk increases and commitment appears to weaken throughout session. Coercive approach may create short-term compliance but not sustained behavior change.

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should prioritize formal MI training with ongoing supervision. Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based practice for behavior change and risks damaging therapeutic alliance.
