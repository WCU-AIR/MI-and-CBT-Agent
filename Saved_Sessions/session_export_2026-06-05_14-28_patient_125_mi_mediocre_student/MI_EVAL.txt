## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5 – Below Basic**

**Key Findings:**

This session demonstrates **fundamental deficiencies in Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist operates almost entirely in an **expert-driven, directive mode** rather than the **collaborative, evocative mode** that defines MI. The psychiatrist identifies problems, prescribes solutions, and expects Sofia to comply—this is the opposite of MI spirit.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

- **Minimal evocation:** Sofia's own motivations, values, and resources are not explored
- **No genuine collaboration:** Sofia is positioned as a passive recipient, not a partner
- **Prescriptive language throughout:** "Should," "would probably help," and "you might want to" subtly pressure rather than support autonomy
- **Poor handling of ambivalence:** Sofia's hesitation and concerns are dismissed rather than explored
- **Shallow empathy:** Reflections are factual, not emotionally attuned
- **Lack of acceptance:** Sofia's strengths are not affirmed; the stance is subtly judgmental

**Likelihood of Evoking Intrinsic Motivation:** **Low.** While Sofia agrees to try the suggested plan, her agreement appears to be compliance rather than genuine commitment. She expresses minimal change talk, significant ambivalence, and concern about stopping marijuana. The directive approach may increase defensiveness or resistance over time.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**

1. Shift from expert to collaborative stance (highest priority)
2. Develop genuine evocation skills through open-ended questioning
3. Amplify and reinforce Sofia's change talk
4. Explore Sofia's values and connect them to behavior change
5. Use double-sided reflections to address ambivalence
6. Validate concerns about stopping marijuana
7. Explore the function of marijuana before prescribing reduction
8. Affirm Sofia's strengths and resilience
9. Minimize prescriptive language; maximize collaborative language
10. Negotiate shared agenda rather than imposing one
