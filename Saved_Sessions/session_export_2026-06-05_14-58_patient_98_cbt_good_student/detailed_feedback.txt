
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. **Conduct Comprehensive Functional Analysis of Substance Use (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
- **Action:** In the next session, systematically explore the functional relationship between substance use and mood/stress. Use the following framework:
  - Triggers: "What situations, feelings, or thoughts lead you to use cocaine or alcohol?"
  - Function: "What does using do for you? How does it make you feel in the moment?"
  - Consequences: "What happens after you use? How do you feel the next day?"
  - Cycle: "How does this cycle affect your mood over time?"
- **Example:** "Frank, I want to understand how cocaine and alcohol fit into what we've been talking about. When you felt that shame at work, you used alcohol to numb it. Can you walk me through what happens? What do you feel when you use? What happens the next day? How does this affect your mood over time?"
- **Practice:** Role-play this conversation with a supervisor or colleague. Practice asking open-ended questions about substance use without judgment. Review case formulations of patients with substance-induced mood disorders to understand the temporal relationship.

### 2. **Psychoeducation About Substance-Induced Depression (SECOND PRIORITY)**
- **Action:** Provide clear, evidence-based information about how cocaine use affects mood. Explain the acute vs. chronic effects and the depression-substance use cycle.
- **Example:** "Frank, I want to share something important. Cocaine gives a temporary high, but it also depletes the brain chemicals that regulate mood. Over time, regular use leads to depression—especially when you're not using. Your depression started about 3 months after you began using cocaine regularly. This suggests the cocaine is contributing to your low mood. If we can help you reduce or stop using, your mood may improve significantly. But we need to address both the substance use and the mood together."
- **Practice:** Develop a one-page handout explaining substance-induced depression. Practice delivering this information in a non-judgmental, collaborative way. Review neurobiology of cocaine and depression.

### 3. **Develop Integrated Treatment Strategy Addressing Both Mood and Substance Use**
- **Action:** Explicitly articulate a treatment plan that addresses both problems as interconnected. Share this with the patient.
- **Example:** "Here's how I'm thinking about your situation: Your low mood and substance use are connected. Cocaine use is maintaining your depression, and depression is driving you to use substances to cope. So we need to work on both. First, we'll help you understand what substance use is doing for you emotionally and develop other ways to cope with shame and stress. Second, we'll continue working on your thinking patterns and how you respond to setbacks. Third, we'll monitor your mood as you reduce substance use to see how much improves. Does this make sense? What questions do you have?"
- **Practice:** Write out a treatment plan for a patient with comorbid depression and substance use. Practice explaining it to the patient in clear, collaborative language.

### 4. **Assess and Address Suicidal Ideation and Safety**
- **Action:** Conduct a thorough suicide risk assessment, including frequency, intensity, and content of suicidal thoughts; presence of intent or plan; protective factors; and access to means. Develop a safety plan.
- **Example:** "Frank, I want to check in about something. The case notes mention you've had some thoughts about not wanting to be around. Can you tell me more about that?
