## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.8/5 – Below Basic**

**Session Effectiveness Assessment: Poor**

This session demonstrates **fundamentally inconsistent motivational interviewing practice** that violates core MI principles and spirit. The psychiatrist employs a controlling, expert-driven approach characterized by pressure, confrontation, and dismissal of the patient's perspective throughout most of the interaction.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Pervasive use of controlling language ("must," "have to," "should," "need to") that directly violates patient autonomy
- Minimal acceptance and empathy; judgment and dismissal replace understanding
- Expert positioning that undermines collaboration and partnership
- Confrontational handling of sustain talk that likely increases defensiveness
- Failure to amplify or reinforce multiple instances of patient change talk
- Inconsistent application of improved techniques; reversion to controlling language undermines progress

**Strengths:**
- Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation throughout
- Final exchanges demonstrate improved MI technique with open-ended questions and validating reflections
- Shows awareness that approach needs adjustment by shifting tone midway through session
- Asks some exploratory questions that invite patient input

**Impact on Patient Engagement:**
Patient explicitly states need for autonomy ("I have to do it when I'm ready, not just to please others") and freedom from pressure ("I don't want to feel pressured about it"), yet psychiatrist continues with controlling directives. This directly triggers reactance and defensiveness. When psychiatrist shifts to collaborative, exploratory stance in final exchanges, patient becomes more engaged and thoughtful ("Yeah, I can go along with that. Taking small steps sounds more realistic"), indicating patient is capable of MI-consistent dialogue but psychiatrist's early approach actively prevented engagement.

**Predicted Outcomes:**
- Increased client reactance and defensiveness
- Reduced intrinsic motivation for change
- Damaged therapeutic alliance
- Missed opportunities to strengthen motivation through change talk amplification
- Low likelihood of sustained behavior change without significant approach modifications

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist requires intensive MI training and supervision before continuing MI-focused sessions. Current approach, while well-intentioned, is likely counterproductive and may harm therapeutic relationship and client outcomes.
