--- DISORDER ---
Enuresis, Nocturnal Only

--- CASE STUDY ---

Zack was an 8-year-old boy brought to the outpatient child psychiatry clinic by his mother because of increasing tantrums and somatic complaints without an apparent cause. Symptoms appeared related to nights spent at his aunt's home, which began recently when his mother worked night shifts.

For 2 months prior to consultation, Zack reported nausea and headaches on Fridays and complained that his aunt's house was "creepy." He continued to visit reluctantly, once calling his mother at work demanding to be picked up. Recently, Zack screamed and hid when it was time to go to his aunt's home.

Zack had no prior behavioral or emotional issues, achieved all developmental milestones on time, and had only routine medical visits. He had not yet achieved consistent overnight continence, wetting the bed approximately twice weekly. There were no daytime voiding symptoms or constipation. Family history was negative for psychiatric illness on the mother’s side; little is known about the father.

Mental status examination: Zack was cooperative, well-nourished, and well cared for. He separated easily from his mother initially. Speech was spontaneous and age-appropriate. Eye contact was appropriate. Affect was mildly anxious, calming quickly except when discussing sleepovers, where he demonstrated fidgeting, decreased eye contact, and mild irritability. He appeared embarrassed when discussing bed-wetting, reporting teasing by his cousin and his aunt’s boyfriend, which caused him to feel scared.

Discussion: Zack presents with temper tantrums, somatic complaints, and refusal to spend the night at his aunt’s home. Clinical concern shifted to enuresis, which appears to be primary and nocturnal. His distress seems secondary to teasing. No other psychiatric disorders are indicated; separation anxiety, oppositional defiant disorder, conduct disorder, or mood dysregulation disorder are unlikely given context-specific symptoms.
