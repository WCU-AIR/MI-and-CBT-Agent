## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamentally Misaligned with Evidence-Based Practice

**Session Score: 1.2/5**

### Critical Deficiencies:

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates a **directive, expert-driven, coercive approach** that is actively harmful to therapeutic alliance and client motivation.

**Key Problems:**
- Authoritarian stance explicitly positions psychiatrist as sole expert
- Rejection of patient autonomy through language like "it's not optional" and "you must"
- Pressure and coercion through fear appeals and ultimatums
- Systematic dismissal of all patient input and realistic alternatives
- Complete failure to evoke, explore, or amplify patient's intrinsic motivation
- Weaponization of patient's core values (retirement with wife) as threats rather than motivational anchors
- Repetitive demands without adaptation or exploration of barriers

### Likely Patient Outcomes:

- Patient likely to leave session feeling pressured, unheard, and resentful
- May agree to demands in moment to end conversation but unlikely to follow through
- Risk of guilt/shame if unable to meet arbitrary daily exercise standard, leading to plan abandonment
- Intrinsic motivation (staying active with wife) remains unexplored and unamplified
- Therapeutic alliance damaged, making future health conversations more difficult
- **Overall likelihood of evoking sustained behavior change: DECREASED**

### Immediate Recommendations:

1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** (foundational priority)
2. **Respect and support autonomy** (remove controlling language)
3. **Evoke rather than impose motivation** (ask questions that help patient discover own reasons)
4. **Amplify change talk** (listen for and reinforce patient's own motivation)
5. **Explore barriers collaboratively** (treat hesitation as valuable information)
6. **Seek formal MI training** (this psychiatrist should not conduct behavior change counseling without structured MI training and supervised practice)

---

**Recommendation:** This psychiatrist demonstrates pre-MI level competency and should not continue conducting behavior change counseling without formal Motivational Interviewing training, role-play practice, and supervised feedback. Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based practice and likely to harm patient outcomes.
