## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL FEEDBACK

### Strengths
- **Maintains focus on target behavior**: Psychiatrist consistently keeps discussion focused on orange juice consumption and walking, which is appropriate.
- **One collaborative moment**: In middle of first session, psychiatrist briefly shifts to collaborative stance ("How about we come up with a plan together...?"), representing closest the session comes to MI spirit.
- **Client develops concrete plan**: Despite psychiatrist's approach, Marco articulates specific, step-wise plan for reducing juice intake and involving wife, suggesting resilience in client's own motivation.
- **Session ends with client commitment**: Marco states "I'm ready to commit to cutting down like that starting today," indicating that some motivation for change persists despite psychiatrist's approach.

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement

**Fundamental departure from MI principles across nearly every competency domain:**

1. **Directive prescription replaces evocation**: Rather than drawing out Marco's own motivations and insights, psychiatrist tells him what to do. When Marco offers ideas (gradual reduction, involving wife, realistic goals), these are dismissed as insufficient.

2. **Confrontation of ambivalence increases resistance**: Psychiatrist directly confronts Marco's legitimate concerns ("You're making excuses," "Why didn't you just stop already?") rather than skillfully exploring them. This confrontational approach typically increases psychological reactance and sustain talk, as evidenced by Marco's escalating pushback.

3. **Absence of affirmation and acceptance**: Marco's genuine strengths—initial 5-pound weight loss, pride in efforts, insight about juice, confidence rating of 10/10, practical problem-solving—are never acknowledged or built upon. Psychiatrist communicates judgment rather than unconditional positive regard.

4. **Control undermines autonomy**: Language consistently removes client choice ("You must," "You have to," "This isn't optional," "I expect you"). Psychiatrist uses fear and pressure to coerce compliance rather than supporting autonomous decision-making.

5. **Expert positioning replaces partnership**: Psychiatrist assumes role of expert who knows what's best and positions Marco as someone who must comply. No genuine power-sharing or co-creation of change process. Never asks questions that would position Marco as expert on his own change.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**Immediate impact**: Marco's escalating pushback throughout session ("I'm not trying to make excuses," "I get that you're pushing," "I'm worried if I try to stop all at once") indicates growing resistance. Client is defending his position rather than exploring change—a red flag that approach is not working.

**Likelihood of sustained change**: Research shows that change motivated by external pressure and fear is less likely to be sustained than change motivated by intrinsic values and autonomous decision-making. Psychiatrist's approach may produce short-term compliance but is unlikely to support long-term behavior change.

**Therapeutic alliance**: Psychiatrist's judgmental stance and dismissal of Marco's concerns damage therapeutic alliance. Marco may feel unheard, judged, and controlled rather than supported and understood.

**Paradoxical effect of pressure**: Escalating pressure may actually decrease Marco's motivation through psychological reactance. When people feel controlled, they often become more resistant to suggested change.

---

### Recommendations for Improvement (Priority Order)

1. **Shift from directive to evocative stance** – Ask rather than tell; draw out Marco's own motivations and resources
2. **Develop genuine acceptance and affirmation** – Actively affirm Marco's strengths, efforts, and insights
3. **Explore and soften ambivalence** – Use double-sided reflections and curiosity rather than confrontation
4. **Support autonomy** – Explicitly acknowledge Marco's right to make own choices; offer genuine choice
5. **Build collaborative partnership** – Share power; let client input shape session direction
6. **Evoke and amplify change talk** – Actively listen for and reinforce Marco's own language about change
7. **Avoid imposing additional goals** – Explore what's realistic and important to Marco before suggesting new goals
8. **Replace fear-based motivation with values-based motivation** – Connect behavior change to what Marco values
9. **Develop skill in reflective listening** – Move beyond surface-level reflections to deeper, emotionally attuned reflections
10. **Receive feedback and adjust in real-time** – Notice when Marco expresses resistance and adjust approach accordingly

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Current MI competency level**: Below-average to poor MI practice

**Fundamental issue**: Psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, expert-driven approach that violates core MI principles across acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support, evocation, and partnership.

**Most critical need**: Philosophical shift from expert-driven prescription to client-centered evocation. Psychiatrist must learn to ask rather than tell, to listen rather than lecture, and to trust that Marco's wisdom about his own change resides within him, not in the clinician's expertise.

**Likelihood of sustained behavior change**: While Marco expresses commitment at end of session, psychiatrist's approach creates significant risk for poor long-term outcomes. Without fundamental changes to stance and technique, likelihood of sustained behavior change is low.

**Opportunity**: Psychiatrist has clear opportunity to develop genuine MI competence through focused training, practice, and supervision in the nine competency domains outlined above.
