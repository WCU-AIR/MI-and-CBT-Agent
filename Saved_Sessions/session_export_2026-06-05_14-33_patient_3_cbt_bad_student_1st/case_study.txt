--- DISORDER ---
Adjustment Disorder with Depressed Mood
Suicidal Behavior Disorder (current, high lethality, impulsive)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Hannah, a 16-year-old white girl with no prior psychiatric history, was brought to the emergency department following an overdose of unknown sedating medications. She arrived with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 8, requiring intubation and admission to the pediatric intensive care unit. After stabilization, she was transferred to an inpatient psychiatric unit.

Collateral information from her parents and best friend indicated she had been in her usual cheerful mood until the evening of the overdose, when she received a text that her boyfriend had been seen kissing another girl. Within hours, she was found by her parents, barely arousable, with a note stating: "I just want to sleep forever." Hannah had no previous psychiatric symptoms, suicidal ideation, self-injurious behaviors, trauma, or substance use.

During psychiatric evaluation, Hannah reported that suicidal thoughts arose suddenly after reading the text and were immediately followed by the overdose. She denied ongoing mood, anxiety, sleep, appetite, or concentration disturbances. She expressed horror and relief after surviving the overdose. Hannah remained euthymic and free of suicidal ideation throughout hospitalization and was discharged to outpatient cognitive-behavioral therapy focused on coping skills for anger and disappointment. She remained well for 6 months, at which point treatment was concluded with plans to resume if future difficulties arose.

Discussion: Hannah’s symptoms represent a marked, disproportionate reaction to a psychosocial stressor, without evidence of underlying psychiatric disorder, consistent with adjustment disorder with depressed mood. Her impulsive, high-lethality suicide attempt qualifies as suicidal behavior disorder per DSM-5-TR. Collateral and family history highlight maternal anxiety and past suicide attempts but no direct psychiatric predisposition in Hannah. Treatment focused on skill-building and coping strategies, with full recovery and remission.
