## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.1/5**

### Critical Deficiencies Identified:

1. **Complete absence of MI spirit** – Session characterized by authoritarian, expert-driven, controlling approach fundamentally incompatible with MI principles.

2. **Misalignment with presenting concern** – Psychiatrist ignores actual referral reason (oral health concerns related to piercing) and imposes unrelated agenda (smoking cessation) that client does not accept as relevant.

3. **Active undermining of therapeutic alliance** – Communicates disbelief of client's self-report, judges client as irresponsible, pressures compliance with imposed agenda. Damages trust and increases resistance.

4. **Coercive and controlling language** – Repeated use of "must," "have to," "you need to," and demands for promises communicates that client's autonomy is conditional on compliance.

5. **Failure to evoke change talk** – Lectures and prescribes rather than asking questions designed to draw out client's own motivations, values, and resources.

6. **Intensification of resistance** – Rather than softening resistance through skillful MI techniques, psychiatrist argues and pressures, which intensifies client's resistance and entrenchment.

### Impact on Client Engagement:

This session is likely to have negative impact on Marisol's engagement and likelihood of change. Controlling, judgmental approach violates core MI principles and increases resistance rather than evoking motivation. Client explicitly requests collaboration but is met with continued lecturing and pressure. By session end, client is more entrenched in her position and less likely to engage with psychiatrist or consider recommendations. Psychiatrist has communicated that client's perspective is not valued, autonomy is not respected, and actual concerns are irrelevant. Low likelihood of follow-through on recommendations or return for further sessions.

### Recommendation:

**This psychiatrist requires comprehensive retraining in MI principles and techniques before continuing to conduct MI sessions.** Current approach is not only ineffective but potentially harmful to client engagement and therapeutic outcomes. Immediate action needed through formal training, supervision, and skill development focused on shifting from directive counseling to genuine MI practice.
