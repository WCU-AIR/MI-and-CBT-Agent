### Acceptance

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated throughout. Accurate reflections capture both content and emotion ("You're carrying a heavy weight, feeling worn down by those persistent clashes, yet there's a clear hope for mutual understanding and respect"). Shows emotional attunement by naming specific feelings Amina expressed. However, opportunities exist for deeper, more complex reflections. When Amina expresses worry about how conflict might affect her son's emotional development, the clinician could have reflected the underlying fear and protective instinct more explicitly to deepen the reflection beyond surface-level acknowledgment.

### Direction

Logical progression from exploring core concern and values, to eliciting change talk, to assessing confidence, to identifying solutions and concrete next steps. However, session could have maintained tighter focus when Amina mentioned worry about how conflict might affect her son's emotional development—this rich area for understanding core values was passed over relatively quickly. Could more explicitly connect each intermediate step back to the overarching goal of improving co-parenting communication.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes collaborative stance by consistently inviting Amina's input and building on her ideas rather than imposing solutions. Opening question positions Amina as expert on her own experience. When Amina identifies mediator/counselor idea, clinician affirms as "thoughtful and realistic" rather than suggesting independently. Language is generally inclusive and reflects Amina's agency. However, collaboration could be strengthened by inviting more independent idea-generation from Amina before offering clinician reflections. Confidence scaling questions could be introduced more collaboratively by first asking if this framework would be helpful.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and affirms Amina's autonomy and right to choose. Language emphasizes client choice and agency ("What do you feel would be the very next step you're willing to take?"). Validates uncertainty and the right to doubt without pressure. Avoids prescriptive language by framing action as Amina's choice rather than clinician directive. However, could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging that Amina may choose a different path and that her autonomy is respected regardless. Confidence scaling language could include more explicit autonomy support by acknowledging that readiness is Amina's to determine, not something the clinician will impose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes Amina's own motivations, values, and resources through strategic questioning. Rather than telling Amina why she should improve co-parenting, clinician asks questions that draw out intrinsic motivation ("What do you think might be some reasons or motivations that keep you wanting to work toward that understanding?"). Powerful change talk emerges and is not generated by clinician. Evokes past resources and successes effectively ("Thinking back, have there been moments or ways you've managed to communicate...?"). However, could be deepened by asking more strategic questions when Amina expresses doubt: "What makes you think it might be possible, even with those doubts?" This would draw out her own evidence of resilience and capability.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively fosters partnership and power-sharing by consistently inviting Amina's input and positioning her as expert on her own experience. Incorporates Amina's contributions in ways that substantially influence conversation direction. Affirms her ideas and builds on them rather than redirecting. Language reinforces partnership through phrases like "It sounds like," "You're expressing," "You're seeing that." However, partnership could be strengthened by inviting more co-construction of meaning rather than clinician articulating understanding fully. Lengthy reflections, while excellent, can subtly shift balance toward clinician as the one who articulates understanding. More concise reflections inviting Amina to articulate her own meaning would strengthen partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces Amina's change talk throughout the session. Strong change talk emerges early and is consistently affirmed: desire ("I love him dearly, and I really want the best for him"), ability ("change doesn't have to be all or nothing—maybe small steps can add up"), reasons ("My son is the biggest reason"), need ("I don't want him to feel torn or sad because of us"), and commitment ("I want to try because I don't want my boy caught in the middle"). Strategically reinforces change talk by reflecting it back with emphasis and emotional resonance. However, missed opportunities exist to cultivate even stronger change talk by asking "Despite those doubts, what keeps you wanting to try?" when Amina expresses doubt, or "What does it say about you that you're willing to prepare carefully?" to cultivate ability and commitment talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk emerges at several points ("I'm not sure where to start or if his dad even wants to meet me halfway," "I worry his dad might not be open to that, or that it'll just end up causing more fights," "It feels a bit scary"). Clinician acknowledges sustain talk but does not consistently use strategic techniques to soften it or pivot toward change talk. When Amina expresses doubt about co-parenting partner's willingness, clinician could have used double-sided reflection: "So there's a risk that reaching out could backfire. And at the same time, you've been thinking carefully about how to approach this conversation in a way that minimizes blame..." When Amina rates confidence at 4, clinician could have asked "What's keeping you from being at a 2 or 3?" to highlight change talk embedded in ambivalence. Fear expressed ("It feels a bit scary") could have been reframed as evidence of her caring and commitment rather than simply validated. This represents a significant gap in MI skillfulness.

### **Overall Session Score: 4.0/5**

## Summary

**Strengths:**
- Consistent warmth, acceptance, and genuine nonjudgmental regard throughout
- Solid reflective listening with accurate capture of both content and emotion
- Skillful evocation of intrinsic motivation centered on Amina's love for her son
- Clear strategic direction with logical progression from exploration to concrete action steps
- Strong collaborative stance with consistent power-sharing and incorporation of client ideas
- Consistent cultivation of change talk with regular reflection and reinforcement
- Effective autonomy support emphasizing client choice and agency

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Softening sustain talk:** Does not consistently use strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing, strategic questioning) to pivot sustain talk toward change talk—most significant gap in MI skillfulness
- **Deepening reflections:** While accurate, reflections occasionally lack complexity and emotional depth that would facilitate greater self-discovery
- **Exploring ambivalence more thoroughly:** Could explore doubt and mixed feelings more deeply before moving toward solution-building
- **Inviting independent idea-generation:** At times offers reflections before fully inviting Amina to generate her own ideas
- **Reducing subtle expert positioning:** Lengthy reflections and confidence scaling can subtly shift balance toward clinician-led framing
- **Connecting intermediate steps to goals:** Could more explicitly link each action step back to ultimate goal of improving co-parenting communication

**Clinical Impact:**
This session demonstrates solid, competent MI practice that likely increases Amina's engagement and motivation for change. The psychiatrist creates a safe, supportive environment where Amina feels heard and respected. Amina moves from confidence level of 4 to 6 and identifies a concrete, manageable first step, suggesting increased readiness for action. However, more skillful handling of sustain talk and deeper exploration of ambivalence would enhance the session's effectiveness in supporting lasting behavior change.
