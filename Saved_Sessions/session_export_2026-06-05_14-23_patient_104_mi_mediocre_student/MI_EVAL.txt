# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance

**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal acceptance and actively undermines it through dismissal of client's primary concern regarding systemic disrespect and time pressure. Uses "but" statements that negate client's emotional experience. No genuine affirmation of client's worth or validation of anger as reasonable response to feeling disrespected. Repeatedly redirects away from client's stated concern, communicating that the client's perspective is unimportant compared to the psychiatrist's predetermined agenda.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level empathy only. Acknowledges frustration and stress superficially ("I understand waiting can be frustrating," "stress is a big part of the problem") but fails to achieve accurate, deep understanding of client's emotional experience. No complex reflections that capture emotional meaning or client's worldview. Misses repeated opportunities to demonstrate genuine empathy regarding client's sense of powerlessness and disrespect. Does not explore underlying hopelessness or emotional subtext of exhaustion and overwhelm.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maintains narrow, predetermined direction toward habit change (diet, exercise, smoking) despite clear client resistance and misalignment with presenting concern. Direction is strategically misplaced—client's primary concern is systemic disrespect, not health behaviors. Never clarifies what client's actual change goals are. Repeated conversation resets suggest rigid repetition rather than strategic adjustment. Direction is imposed rather than collaboratively clarified based on client feedback.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal collaboration; reflects expert-driven, hierarchical stance. Unilateral agenda-setting with predetermined list of topics. Client input is acknowledged but not integrated into session planning. Prescriptive rather than exploratory approach ("Maybe you should try," "It would probably help if"). No shared decision-making or genuine inquiry about what client wants to focus on. Client's expertise about his own life, constraints, and previous attempts is treated as resistance rather than valuable information.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively undermines autonomy support through repeated prescriptive suggestions despite clear client resistance. Uses soft directives ("maybe you should," "it would probably help") that subtly pressure compliance. Minimizes legitimate barriers by suggesting they can be easily overcome. Never affirms client's right to choose or emphasizes ownership of change process. Frames self-care as moral obligation rather than choice. No exploration of ambivalence or acknowledgment that client may validly choose not to pursue habit change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates virtually no evocation. Imposes external reasons for change rather than drawing out client's own motivations, values, and resources. No exploration of client's reasons for change, values, past successes, or self-efficacy. Asks closed questions that funnel toward predetermined answers rather than open-ended questions inviting exploration. Minimal change talk elicited; existing change talk not amplified or reinforced. Sustain talk dominates unchallenged. No curiosity about client's perspective or what would motivate him.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Assumes expert role and maintains control throughout session. Client's contributions do not substantially influence session direction. Power is retained by psychiatrist rather than shared. Implicit message communicated that client cannot figure this out himself. Client's repeated statements about primary concern (systemic disrespect) are acknowledged but dismissed, not honored as valid input. No co-construction of solutions. Unequal power dynamic maintained throughout; client role is passive and compliant.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no cultivation of change talk. Minimal change talk elicited from client ("Yeah, I get what you're saying," "I've tried before"). Existing change talk not amplified or explored. No strategic open-ended questions designed to evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change. Sustain talk dominates unchallenged ("It's the system that's the problem," "I'm not sure any of that would stick"). No reinforcement of client's previous attempts or recognition of his efforts. Psychiatrist does not pursue opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal skill in softening sustain talk. While avoiding direct confrontation or harsh criticism, psychiatrist does not use strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploration of ambivalence. Implicitly argues against sustain talk through repetition and redirection using "but" statements, which likely strengthens resistance. Allows client's arguments for status quo to dominate unchallenged. One mild validation attempt ("I understand your frustration") immediately negated by "but" statement that follows.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current MI Competency Level:** Minimal/Poor (1.3/5 average)

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Fundamental misalignment with client's presenting concern; psychiatrist pivots to predetermined agenda without exploring client's actual priorities
- Dismissal of client's legitimate emotional experience regarding systemic disrespect and time pressure
- Expert-driven rather than client-centered approach throughout
- Failure to adapt strategy despite repeated client resistance and three identical session openings
- Minimal evocation of client's own motivations, values, and resources
- Sustain talk dominates unchallenged; no skillful MI techniques employed

**Impact on Therapeutic Relationship:**
Client enters with some willingness ("I guess, fine, we can talk if it helps") and exits with clear skepticism and resistance. Psychiatrist has not built trust, demonstrated genuine understanding, or moved client toward contemplation of behavior change. Session likely to decrease motivation and damage alliance.

**Likelihood of Behavior Change:** Very low. Client remains in precontemplation regarding habit change with no movement toward contemplation.

**Likelihood of Continued Engagement:** Low. Client may not return if feeling unheard and dismissed.

**Primary Recommendations:**
1. Shift from expert-driven to client-centered approach; begin by genuinely understanding client's agenda
2. Develop genuine empathy and validation of client's emotional experience before redirecting
3. Learn to evoke rather than impose through open-ended questioning
4. Build explicit collaboration into every interaction
5. Seek formal MI training with supervised clinical application and fidelity monitoring

---

**Recommendation for Supervision:** This clinician requires structured MI training, including didactic instruction, skills practice, and regular supervision with MI-trained supervisor using recorded session review and MITI fidelity assessment.
