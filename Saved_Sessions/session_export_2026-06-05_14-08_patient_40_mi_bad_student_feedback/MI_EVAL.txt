### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Approach to sustain talk is confrontational and pressure-based rather than skillful. Client expresses fear ("I'm afraid of making things worse"), doubt ("I can't be certain it will work"), and uncertainty ("If I'm feeling weak that day, what then?"). Psychiatrist responds with direct contradiction and pressure rather than validation and exploration. One skillful response occurs late in session ("It sounds like starting slower might feel more manageable for you, and that's okay. How about we work together to find a pace that feels right?"). However, overall pattern is confrontational; direct confrontation of sustain talk typically strengthens resistance rather than softens it.

### Overall Clinical Summary

**Current MI Competency Level: 1.3/5 (Below Threshold for Adequate MI Practice)**

**Critical Deficiencies:**

This session demonstrates fundamental violations of Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance prioritizing prescription and pressure over evocation and collaboration. Core MI competencies—acceptance, empathy, autonomy support, evocation, and partnership—are essentially absent.

**Key Problems Identified:**

1. **Dismissal of legitimate concerns:** Client's fear about exacerbating condition treated as "excuses" and "willpower" issues rather than valid concerns deserving validation.

2. **Coercive language:** Repeated use of "must," "have to," "should," and implicit threats ("If you don't, you're just going to stay stuck") undermines intrinsic motivation and violates autonomy support.

3. **Missed evocative opportunities:** Client's description of experience as "very, very beautiful" and identification of peace and nature connection ignored; external pressure substituted for exploration of intrinsic motivation.

4. **Confrontational approach to resistance:** When client expresses doubt or fear, psychiatrist responds with pressure and contradiction, which typically strengthens resistance rather than evoking change.

5. **Minimal empathetic engagement:** Almost no reflective listening; little effort to understand client's perspective from internal frame of reference.

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Behavior Change: LOW**

Client is more likely to experience this interaction as controlling, dismissive, and judgmental. While client agrees to one-minute walk, this appears to be compliance with external pressure rather than genuine commitment. Without fundamental shift toward true MI principles, likelihood of sustained engagement and behavior change is limited.

**Most Critical Improvements Needed (Priority Order):**

1. Shift from prescription to evocation—replace directive statements with open questions
2. Validate rather than confront sustain talk—use double-sided reflections and exploration
3. Demonstrate genuine acceptance and affirmation—communicate unconditional positive regard
4. Establish true collaboration—position client as expert on own experience
5. Develop reflective listening skills—use accurate, empathetic reflections as primary tool

**Recommendation:** Immediate, substantial changes in MI practice essential. Client deserves clinician who respects autonomy, validates concerns, and genuinely partners in exploring change.
