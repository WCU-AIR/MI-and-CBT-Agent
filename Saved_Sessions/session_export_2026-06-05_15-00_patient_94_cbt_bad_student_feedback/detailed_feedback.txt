
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

### 1. Agenda
### **Score: 0/6**

**Explanation:**

No agenda was established at any point during this session. The therapist opened with a generic greeting ("How are you doing today?") without collaboratively identifying session goals, target problems, or time allocation. Throughout the entire transcript, there is no evidence of the therapist and patient working together to prioritize which symptoms or concerns to address, what the session should accomplish, or how time should be spent. The conversation drifts organically from topic to topic (leg symptoms → sleep → online research → distraction → outdoor activity) without any structured framework guiding the work.

In CBT, agenda-setting is foundational. It establishes collaborative partnership, clarifies what will be addressed, prevents aimless discussion, and creates accountability for progress. The absence of agenda means the session lacks direction and structure. For a patient with Somatic Symptom Disorder who engages in excessive symptom documentation and research, an explicit agenda is particularly critical—it would help redirect focus from symptom cataloging toward behavioral and cognitive targets relevant to treatment.

The therapist never asked: "What would be most helpful to focus on today?" or "Of all the things you're dealing with, what's most important to address in our time together?" or "How much time should we spend on sleep versus the worry about symptoms?" This represents a complete failure to implement a core CBT structural element.

**Summary:**

No agenda was established or maintained throughout the session. The therapist failed to collaboratively identify session goals, prioritize problems, or structure time allocation—a fundamental CBT requirement that was entirely absent.

## Recommendations

### 1. **Learn and Apply CBT Techniques for SSD (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**

**Action:** Develop competence in the specific CBT techniques used for Somatic Symptom Disorder. These include behavioral experiments, cognitive restructuring, exposure, activity scheduling, and stimulus control.

**Example:** 
- **Behavioral experiment:** "You mentioned worrying that activity will make your pain worse. What if we designed a small experiment to test that? You could predict what will happen if you take a 10-minute walk, then do it and see what actually happens. We'd gather evidence about whether your fear is accurate."
- **Cognitive restructuring:** "You said 'If the doctors can't find anything, something serious must be wrong.' Let's examine that thought. What evidence supports it? What evidence contradicts it? What's another way to think about it?"
- **Exposure:** "I notice you've stopped going outside because you fear it will worsen your symptoms. What if we gradually increased your activity, starting with something small, to help you learn that activity doesn't cause harm?"

**Practice:** 
- Complete formal CBT training in SSD (e.g., through workshops, supervision, or certification programs)
- Review treatment manuals for SSD (e.g., "Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Somatic Symptom Disorder" by Eileen Oster et al.)
- Practice applying techniques in role-plays with supervisors or colleagues
- Record sessions and review them with a supervisor to identify opportunities to use techniques
