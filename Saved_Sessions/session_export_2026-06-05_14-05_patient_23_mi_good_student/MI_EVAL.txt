Significant missed opportunities for deeper evocation: when Camila identifies her own strengths, psychiatrist immediately suggests applying them to drinking rather than asking if she sees the connection. Doesn't consistently amplify change talk statements (e.g., "I'm willing to try that"). Limited strategic questioning to deepen commitment, clarify reasons, or strengthen intrinsic motivation.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.9/5**

### Strengths:
- ✓ Strong collaborative stance with consistent invitations for client input
- ✓ Solid reflective listening capturing dual motivations and underlying values
- ✓ Effective sustain talk management without confrontation
- ✓ Consistent autonomy support emphasizing client choice and control
- ✓ Practical planning with manageable, concrete action steps
- ✓ Strength-based approach identifying and amplifying existing coping strategies
- ✓ Appropriate pacing allowing adequate exploration before planning

### Areas for Improvement:
- ⚠ Limited strategic evocation—frequently provides insights rather than drawing them from client
- ⚠ Insufficient amplification of change talk—reflects but doesn't consistently follow up with deepening questions
- ⚠ Missed opportunities for deeper empathy regarding emotional drivers (social anxiety, fear of judgment)
- ⚠ Clinician-directed structures (confidence scales) rather than allowing natural client expression
- ⚠ Subtle directiveness in suggesting strategies rather than purely evoking them
- ⚠ Incomplete response to key statements (e.g., "I don't feel like an alcoholic")

### Clinical Impression:

This session demonstrates **solid foundational MI competence** with strong collaborative stance, effective sustain talk management, and practical planning. The psychiatrist successfully establishes partnership and helps Camila move from ambivalence toward concrete action. However, the session falls short of **excellent MI practice** due to **insufficient strategic evocation of change talk** and **missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation** through targeted questioning.

The psychiatrist frequently provides insights and suggestions rather than drawing them from Camila, which subtly reduces her sense of ownership and self-efficacy. While reflections are generally accurate, they don't consistently amplify or strengthen Camila's own change talk. The session would be significantly strengthened by: (1) asking more follow-up questions after change talk to deepen motivation, (2) allowing Camila to discover connections between her strengths and goals rather than suggesting them, and (3) exploring emotional drivers of ambivalence more deeply before planning.

**The session embodies the collaborative spirit of MI but underutilizes evocation**—the core mechanism through which MI generates intrinsic motivation for change. Camila leaves with a concrete plan and reasonable confidence (6-7/10), but her intrinsic motivation and sense of ownership could have been significantly strengthened through more skillful evocation of her own reasons, values, and commitment to change.
