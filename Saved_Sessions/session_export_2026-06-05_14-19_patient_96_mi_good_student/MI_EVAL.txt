### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong skill demonstrated in softening sustain talk without confrontation or argument. Clinician validates patient's hopelessness, sense of being trapped, and suicidal ideation without reinforcing or arguing against them. ## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 4.2/5**

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional acceptance and unconditional positive regard; patient's autonomy and explicit needs honored without exception
- Sophisticated empathic attunement with complex, accurate reflections capturing emotional nuance and deeper meaning
- Authentic partnership and power-sharing with patient positioned as expert on her own experience
- Consistent autonomy support with language emphasizing choice, ownership, and freedom rather than prescription
- Skillful softening of sustain talk without confrontation, argument, or false reassurance
- Genuine warmth and presence creating safety and trust—particularly important given patient's suicidal ideation and history of feeling misunderstood
- Respect for patient's pace without subtle pressure or agenda-pushing

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Inconsistent amplification of change talk: While clinician evokes change talk through good initial questions, several missed opportunities to strategically deepen and reinforce patient's emerging hope, willingness, and ability statements
- Limited exploration of concrete next steps: While maintaining non-pressuring stance, missed opportunities to collaboratively explore what support might look like in practical terms or how patient might access connection beyond therapy room
- Insufficient safety planning or resource discussion: Given active suicidal ideation, no explicit discussion of crisis resources, safety planning, or what patient would do if urges intensify
- Limited strategic use of confidence scaling: Clinician asks patient to rate confidence (2-3) but does not explore what would move her from 2 to 3 or what she imagines a 4 might look like
- Insufficient exploration of values and what matters: Limited explicit inquiry into patient's values, relationships, or what would make life feel more worth living—critical for building motivation in context of suicidality

**Recommendations for Future Sessions:**
1. Strategically amplify and deepen change talk through follow-up questions and explicit highlighting of significance
2. Explore and strengthen patient's values, relationships, and what gives her life meaning
3. Use confidence scaling strategically to evoke self-efficacy ("What would move you to a 4?")
4. Collaboratively discuss safety planning and crisis resources while maintaining partnership
5. Explore barriers to connection more deeply and help patient distinguish past experiences from present possibilities
6. Explicitly connect present engagement to future possibilities to build hope
7. Develop collaborative relapse prevention plan for sustain talk
8. Identify small, concrete behavioral steps that move patient toward connection and away from isolation

**Clinical Competency Level:** Solid competence in MI fundamentals with particular strength in creating safety, validating suffering, and maintaining partnership. Clinician would benefit from more strategic, intentional cultivation of change talk and deeper exploration of patient's values and intrinsic motivation. With focused practice on these areas, clinician could move toward exemplary MI practice with high-risk clients.
