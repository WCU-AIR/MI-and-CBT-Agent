# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal unconditional positive regard. While opening is warm, clinician quickly shifts to problem-focused, directive stance. Fails to normalize Camila's ambivalence about competing needs (social connection vs. health). Missing explicit affirmation of her struggle and worth as a person. Responses are transactional rather than relational. Opportunities to validate legitimate concerns about losing friendships are missed in favor of prescriptive advice.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level reflective listening present but lacks emotional depth and attunement. Clinician reflects content factually ("social pressure is the main thing") but misses critical emotional nuances: underlying anxiety in social situations, fear of rejection, shame about low confidence, and the weight of feeling trapped between competing needs. Complex reflections that capture emotional meaning are absent. When Camila reveals anxiety and uses self-critical language ("I'll probably mess up"), clinician does not explore or reflect the emotional significance.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing weekday drinking and establishing limits, providing appropriate structure. However, direction is clinician-imposed rather than collaboratively developed. Moves quickly to solutions without adequately exploring underlying drivers (why alcohol feels necessary for managing anxiety). Fails to clarify what "cutting back" means to Camila or what success looks like. Does not explore Camila's own ideas about solutions or learn from her past attempts at change.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal true collaboration despite performative language ("we can work on"). Psychiatrist repeatedly prescribes solutions using "should" language disguised as gentle suggestions ("Maybe you should try setting limits," "it would probably help if you talked to your friends"). All major strategies originate from clinician. When Camila expresses doubt or resistance, psychiatrist reframes rather than genuinely exploring her concerns. Power dynamic is clearly unequal: psychiatrist is expert with solutions; Camila is patient expected to comply.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy undermined through repeated prescriptive language and subtle pressure to comply with clinician's agenda. Frequent use of "should" and "probably" communicates expectation rather than genuine choice. When Camila expresses doubt about friends' support, clinician reintroduces suggestion rather than validating her autonomy to decide. No explicit affirmation of her right to choose differently or to fail. Closing statement frames permission to fail as permission within psychiatrist's plan, not affirmation of fundamental autonomy.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation throughout session. Psychiatrist tells rather than asks. No exploration of Camila's own reasons for change, values, past successes, or ideas about solutions. When Camila mentions powerful change talk ("I'm feeling tired all the time," "it's messing with my routine," "I've stopped exercising"), clinician does not amplify or explore. No open-ended questions designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. Clinician generates all solutions; Camila's role is to evaluate pre-formed ideas. This represents fundamental MI failure.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist adopts expert role throughout, positioning self as authority with knowledge and solutions while positioning Camila as patient needing guidance. Unidirectional information flow: clinician asks questions to gather data, then generates solutions. Camila's role is to provide information and receive recommendations. All major strategies clinician-generated; Camila asked to evaluate rather than co-create. When Camila expresses concerns, these are not used as data to reshape approach. True partnership—where both parties' contributions substantially influence session—is largely absent.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No strategic cultivation of change talk despite Camila spontaneously offering powerful statements ("I just wanna stop," "it's messing with my routine," "I'm hopeful this will be a start"). Existing change talk is minimally reinforced; clinician moves past opportunities to explore and amplify. No open-ended evocative questions to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. When Camila rates confidence at 5/10, clinician does not explore what gives her that 5 or what would increase it. Session characterized by clinician telling and Camila listening rather than Camila discovering own motivations through skillful questioning.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal skill in softening sustain talk; some responses inadvertently reinforce it. When Camila expresses fear of social consequences ("I don't wanna quit altogether... all my friends drink"), clinician acknowledges then prescribes rather than using double-sided reflection or reframing. Low confidence (5/10) and difficulty maintaining limits in social situations not adequately addressed through exploration. Concerns about peer pressure and social consequences met with strategy prescriptions rather than skillful reframing. Clinician's approach is to acknowledge sustain talk briefly and prescribe workarounds rather than address underlying barriers.

---

## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.8/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Basic counseling skills present (warmth, structure, practical thinking), but fundamental MI principles largely absent. Clinician has not internalized MI spirit of partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

**Key Strengths:**
- Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (reducing weekday drinking)
- Warm, non-threatening opening
- Recognition of ambivalence and competing concerns
- Practical, concrete strategy suggestions
- Affirmation of client effort and willingness to try
- Explicit offer of ongoing support

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Absence of evocation; tells rather than asks
- Minimal empathy and emotional attunement
- Prescriptive rather than collaborative approach
- Inadequate softening of sustain talk
- Minimal cultivation of change talk
- Undermined autonomy support through directive language
- Expert-driven rather than partnership-based stance

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Motivation:**
Low. While Camila expresses willingness to try, this appears to be compliance rather than intrinsic motivation. Her low confidence (5/10) and expressed doubt ("I'll probably mess up") suggest limited belief in approach. Failure to address underlying anxiety means session targets behavior but not function. Directive stance may increase resistance over time.

**Priority Recommendations:**
1. Shift from expert to evocative stance; replace prescriptive advice with open-ended questions
2. Develop complex reflective listening to capture emotional nuance and ambivalence
3. Explore underlying anxiety and social fears driving drinking behavior
4. Validate and explore ambivalence using double-sided reflections
5. Strengthen autonomy support by eliminating prescriptive language
6. Strategically cultivate change talk through amplification and reinforcement
7. Soften sustain talk through reframing and exploration, not workarounds
8. Build genuine partnership by co-creating solutions with client
9. Deepen empathy by normalizing struggle and affirming client worth
10. Assess readiness and motivation before prescribing strategies

**Recommendation for Clinician Development:**
Significant training and supervised practice needed to develop genuine MI competence. Focus should be on internalizing MI spirit and developing skill in evoking client motivation rather than prescribing solutions.
