### **SUMMARY ASSESSMENT**

**Fundamental Failure to Implement Motivational Interviewing**

This session represents a critical failure to implement MI principles. The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal competence across all nine MI domains, with scores of 1/5 in seven of nine categories. Rather than evoking intrinsic motivation for change, the psychiatrist employs coercion, pressure, confrontation, and explicit rejection of patient autonomy—approaches explicitly contraindicated in MI and empirically shown to reduce motivation and increase resistance.

**Critical Concerns:**

- **Therapeutic Relationship Damaged:** Patient enters with openness ("I guess quitting sounds like a good idea") and willingness to consider options. By session end, patient has moved from ambivalence to explicit resistance and feels unheard.

- **Resistance Increased, Not Decreased:** Patient's sustain talk escalates throughout session. Patient explicitly states: "being pushed like this makes me feel like my concerns aren't being heard" and "I need to feel like I have some say in the plan."

- **Autonomy Violated:** Patient's right to choose, readiness timeline, and legitimate concerns are systematically rejected in favor of pressure to comply immediately.

- **Competing Priorities Ignored:** Patient's stated concern about new cholesterol medication is never addressed or integrated, despite being mentioned multiple times.

- **Likely to Reduce Treatment Engagement:** This approach will likely reduce patient's likelihood of engaging in smoking cessation treatment. If patient does attempt to quit, it will be in response to external pressure rather than intrinsic motivation, associated with lower success rates and higher relapse.

**HIGHEST PRIORITY: Do not continue using MI with patients until formal training completed.**

1. **Enroll in certified MI training program** (MINT-certified trainer recommended)
2. **Obtain regular clinical supervision** with MI-trained supervisors

6. **Develop skills in recognizing and amplifying change talk**

8. **Practice validating and exploring patient concerns** rather than dismissing them
