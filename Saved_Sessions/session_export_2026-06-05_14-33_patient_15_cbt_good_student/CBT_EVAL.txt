## CLINICAL IMPACT & RECOMMENDATIONS

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change:**  
This session addresses anxiety about sleepovers but does not address the primary diagnosis (enuresis). While anxiety reduction may help Zack feel more comfortable, bed-wetting will likely persist without specific behavioral interventions. **Current approach is unlikely to produce significant improvement in the primary symptom.**

**IMMEDIATE ACTIONS REQUIRED:**

1. **Conduct comprehensive enuresis assessment** — frequency, timing, severity, family history, previous treatments, relationship to anxiety
2. **Develop integrated treatment conceptualization** linking enuresis, anxiety, shame, and avoidance; share with patient and mother
3. **Involve mother as treatment partner** with clear roles, responsibilities, and written instructions for behavioral interventions
4. **Teach and practice coping skills in session** (not just mention them); provide written instructions and visual aids
5. **Structure homework with explicit data collection** — what to notice, how to record, how to review in next session
6. **Implement specific enuresis interventions** — fluid restriction, scheduled voiding, positive reinforcement, consider bedwetting alarm
7. **Establish clear success criteria and measurement plan** — baseline measurements, weekly progress tracking, defined goals
8. **Develop thought record worksheet** for ongoing cognitive work between sessions
9. **Plan obstacle management and relapse prevention** — anticipate barriers and develop contingency plans
10. **Increase precision in cognitive work** — focus on shame/embarrassment specifically related to bed-wetting
