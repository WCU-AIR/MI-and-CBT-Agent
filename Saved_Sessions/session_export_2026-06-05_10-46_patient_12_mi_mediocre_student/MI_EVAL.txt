**Comments:** Minimal evocation despite patient's abundant change talk. Patient enters with powerful statements: "I feel like the activity piece is the easiest place to start," "I just want to feel stronger," "If I'm more active, my body confidence would improve, and I'd have better mood and energy." Rather than evoking and deepening this motivation, clinician asks factual questions then provides solutions. When clinician does ask about barriers, patient provides thoughtful answers but clinician does not explore further. ## Overall Session Score: 2.1/5

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates **below-average motivational interviewing practice** with significant deficiencies in core MI competencies. While psychiatrist maintains basic focus on target behavior and eventually accepts patient's stated preference, overall approach is fundamentally misaligned with MI principles and spirit.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Expert-driven, directive stance undermines autonomy support, collaboration, partnership, and evocation
- Pressure applied when patient expresses appropriate caution ("But it's important to push a little")
- Abundant patient change talk neither evoked nor amplified
- Premature advice-giving before exploring patient's own ideas
- Minimal empathic reflection; patient must repeat concerns before clinician reflects them
- No genuine partnership or co-creation of change plan

**Clinical Impact:**
Despite patient's strong intrinsic motivation (8/10) and clear thinking about her own needs, psychiatrist's directive, pressuring approach may create defensiveness and undermine patient's sense of ownership and autonomy. Patient may comply with agreed-upon plan (two days per week), but compliance likely driven by external pressure rather than intrinsic motivation—research shows this is less likely to lead to sustained behavior change.

**Recommendation:**
This session requires **significant retraining in core MI competencies**, particularly: (1) shifting from expert-driven to collaborative stance, (2) evoking rather than imposing motivation, (3) respecting patient autonomy, and (4) amplifying rather than dismissing patient's own wisdom about what will work for her.
