## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

**Session Strengths:**
- Excellent rapport-building and interpersonal warmth
- Collaborative, patient-centered agenda-setting
- Accurate empathic reflection and understanding of Ashley's experience
- Skillful use of Socratic questioning to facilitate self-discovery
- Specific, relevant homework assignment with clear rationale
- Appropriate pacing and logical session flow

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Developmental mismatch:** Cognitive restructuring applied without adequate assessment of Ashley's severe intellectual disability (IQ 31, Verbal IQ 23) or consideration of research indicating behavioral/environmental interventions are more effective for this population
- **Inadequate focus on self-injury function:** Ashley explicitly stated self-injury serves emotion-regulation ("I had to do it to calm down"), but therapist did not develop functional analysis or behavioral intervention plan
- **Limited behavioral intervention:** No systematic application of behavioral techniques (functional analysis, competing responses, environmental modification, sensory regulation)
- **No caregiver/systems involvement:** Therapist did not involve parents, school staff, or discuss environmental modifications critical for supporting Ashley's functioning
- **Insufficient exploration of core ASD patterns:** Did not deeply explore Ashley's fundamental difficulty with uncertainty and need for sameness
- **Lack of concrete supports:** No written or visual instructions provided for homework despite Ashley's reliance on visual supports

**Prognosis with Current Approach:** Moderate to low likelihood of sustained reduction in self-injury without more comprehensive, developmentally tailored intervention incorporating behavioral, environmental, and caregiver-based components.

**Recommended Next Steps:**
1. Conduct functional analysis of self-injury (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. Develop behavioral intervention plan with alternative coping strategies
3. Shift treatment emphasis from cognitive to behavioral/environmental interventions
4. Involve caregivers and school staff as active treatment partners
5. Create visual supports and concrete tools for coping
6. Assess cognitive capacity for thought-challenging before continuing cognitive work
7. Provide psychoeducation to family about ASD, IDD, and Kleefstra syndrome
