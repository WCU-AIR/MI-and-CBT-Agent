
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. **Establish Explicit, Collaborative Agenda at Session Start**
- **Action:** At the beginning of each session, collaboratively set a specific agenda with the patient. Identify 1-3 target problems, prioritize them, and allocate time.
- **Example:** "Today we have about 50 minutes. I know you're dealing with stress from the breakup, money worries, and school struggles. Which of these would be most helpful to focus on today? And how much time should we spend on it?"
- **Practice:** Role-play agenda-setting with a supervisor. Practice asking prioritization questions and establishing time boundaries. Review session recordings to ensure agenda is set in the first 5 minutes.

### 2. **Implement Systematic Homework Review at Session Start**
- **Action:** Begin each session by reviewing the previous week's homework. Ask what the patient did, what worked, what was difficult, and what barriers arose. Adjust the plan based on feedback.
- **Example:** "Last week we talked about noticing your thoughts and taking breaks when overwhelmed. How did that go? Did you try it? What happened? What made it easy or hard?"
- **Practice:** Create a homework review checklist to ensure consistency. Practice troubleshooting barriers (e.g., "You forgot to track thoughts—what would help you remember? Should we set a phone reminder?").

### 3. **Develop and Communicate a Clear Treatment Strategy**
- **Action:** Explicitly articulate a coherent CBT conceptualization and treatment plan. Explain how the patient's thoughts, behaviors, and mood interact, and how interventions address these factors.
- **Example:** "Since the breakup, you've been withdrawing and avoiding activities, which makes you feel more isolated and hopeless. When you're isolated, your mind focuses on worries and 'what-ifs,' which feels overwhelming. So we're going to work on three things: (1) getting you more active to improve your mood, (2) challenging those worry thoughts, and (3) reconnecting with what matters to you. Does that make sense?"
- **Practice:** Write out a one-page treatment plan for each patient. Review it with the patient and update it as needed. Practice explaining the plan in simple, clear language.

### 4. **Use Socratic Questioning Instead of Direct Advice**
- **Action:** Replace advice-giving with guided discovery. Ask questions that help the patient examine thoughts, generate alternatives, and reach insights independently.
- **Example:** Instead of "You could think 'I'm doing my best right now,'" ask: "When you think 'I can't handle this,' what evidence do you have for that? What evidence do you have against it? Have there been times when you thought you couldn't handle something but actually did?"
- **Practice:** Record sessions and count the ratio of therapist statements to questions. Aim for at least 60% questions. Practice Socratic questioning with a supervisor using role-play.

### 5. **Prioritize and Sequence Interventions**
- **Action:** Introduce one or two core techniques per session. Ensure mastery before moving to new techniques. Sequence interventions logically (e.g., behavioral activation before cognitive work, or vice versa, depending on the case).
- **Example:** "Today, let's focus on one thing: getting you more active. We'll identify activities that used to make you feel good and create a plan to do at least one this week. Next session, we'll work on managing the worry thoughts."
- **Practice:** Develop a session-by-session treatment plan for each patient. Limit each session to 1-2 core interventions. Practice saying "no" to introducing new techniques prematurely.

### 6. **Assign Specific, Structured Homework with Clear Rationale**
- **Action:** Assign 1-2 focused homework tasks per session. Provide a clear rationale, specific instructions, and a structured format. Ensure the patient understands and commits.
- **Example:** "This week, I'd like you to track your thoughts using this form. Here's why: tracking helps us see patterns and test whether your thoughts are accurate. You'll write down: (1) the situation, (2) the thought, (3) your mood, and (4) what you did. Let's do one example together. Do you have any questions? When will you do this—daily, or when you notice strong emotions?"
- **Practice:** Create homework forms and instructions for common assignments. Practice explaining rationale clearly. Role-play homework assignment and commitment-checking with a supervisor.

### 7. **Elicit and Respond to Feedback Throughout Session**
- **Action:** Regularly check understanding and solicit reactions. Ask: "Does this make sense?" "What are your thoughts?" "Is this helpful?" "Do you have concerns?" Adjust based on feedback.
- **Example:** After explaining cognitive reframing, ask: "I'm wondering if this approach makes sense to you. Do you think it's something you could try? What questions do you have?"
- **Practice:** Set a goal to elicit feedback at least 3 times per session. Practice responding to concerns (e.g., "That's a great point. Let's think about how to address that.").

### 8. **Conduct Behavioral Analysis and Implement Structured Behavioral Activation**
- **Action:** Assess the patient's activity level, withdrawal patterns, and avoidance behaviors. Develop a structured activity schedule targeting mood improvement and behavioral change.
- **Example:** "Let's look at what you're doing each day. You mentioned withdrawing and pushing through without breaks. Let's identify activities that used to make you feel good or energized—things with friends, hobbies, exercise. Then we'll create a plan to do at least one each day, even if it's small."
- **Practice:** Learn the behavioral activation protocol. Practice conducting activity monitoring and scheduling. Role-play with a supervisor.

### 9. **Systematically Challenge and Test Catastrophic and Hopelessness Thoughts**
- **Action:** Identify core automatic thoughts and beliefs (e.g., "I can't handle this," "Things won't get better"). Use evidence examination, behavioral experiments, and thought records to test and modify these thoughts.
- **Example:** "You mentioned thinking 'Maybe things won't get better.' Let's look at that thought. What evidence do you have that things won't get better? What evidence do you have that they might? What would need to happen for things to improve? Let's test this thought by trying some of the strategies we discussed and seeing what happens."
- **Practice:** Learn cognitive restructuring protocols. Practice identifying core beliefs and generating evidence-based alternatives. Role-play with a supervisor.

### 10. **Explore and Address Contextual and Cultural Factors**
- **Action:** Assess the patient's cultural identity, systemic stressors (racial discrimination, first-generation status), and how these relate to the presenting problem. Incorporate culturally sensitive interventions.
- **Example:** "You mentioned being a first-generation student on a predominantly white campus and experiencing racial discrimination. How has that affected your stress and mood? How does that connect to your worries about the future? Let's talk about how we can address both the breakup and these broader stressors."
- **Practice:** Engage in cultural competency training. Learn about systemic stressors affecting diverse populations. Practice asking about cultural identity and systemic factors in a respectful, integrated way.

### 11. **Monitor Symptoms and Progress Objectively**
- **Action:** Use standardized measures (e.g., PHQ-9 for depression, GAD-7 for anxiety) at baseline and regularly throughout treatment. Track homework completion, behavioral changes, and thought patterns. Adjust treatment based on progress.
- **Example:** "Let's use this brief questionnaire to track your mood and anxiety each session. This helps us see if the strategies are working. We'll also track how many times you took breaks, how many activities you did, and what thoughts came up."
- **Practice:** Learn to administer and score common outcome measures. Practice reviewing progress data with patients and adjusting treatment accordingly.

### 12. **Ensure Session Continuity and Treatment Planning**
- **Action:** Maintain a written treatment plan that is reviewed and updated each session. Reference prior sessions, progress, and homework. Ensure consistency across sessions.
- **Example:** "Last session we worked on noticing your thoughts and taking breaks. This session, let's build on that by learning how to challenge those worry thoughts. Here's where we are in the treatment plan..."
- **Practice:** Create a session-by-session treatment plan template. Practice referencing prior sessions and maintaining continuity. Review recordings to ensure consistency.
