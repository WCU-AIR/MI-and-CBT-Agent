## Summary of Clinical Findings

### **MI Spirit Embodiment: NOT PRESENT**

This session demonstrates a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and represents practice that is **actively harmful** to the therapeutic relationship and client motivation. The psychiatrist uses an authoritarian, expert-driven approach that violates every core MI principle.

### **Critical Deficiencies:**

1. **Explicit invalidation of patient autonomy and emotional experience** – Psychiatrist tells patient "you don't get to decide how you feel," which is psychologically harmful and violates basic therapeutic ethics.

2. **Confrontational approach** – Repeated use of confrontation, labeling, and identical directives that research demonstrates *increase* resistance and defensiveness rather than decrease it.

3. **Complete absence of reflective listening** – Zero reflective statements in entire session; psychiatrist talks *at* rather than *with* patient.

4. **Failure to evoke or amplify change talk** – Patient's spontaneous change talk is ignored rather than reflected or amplified; no questions asked to draw out intrinsic motivation.

5. **Rejection of patient requests for collaboration** – When patient explicitly asks "Can we work on that together?", psychiatrist ignores request and repeats same controlling statements.

6. **Developmentally inappropriate approach** – Patient explicitly states need for autonomy ("I need to feel like I'm in control if I'm going to make real changes"). Psychiatrist responds by increasing control, which is developmentally inappropriate for adolescent and therapeutically counterproductive.

### **Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation: VERY LOW**

Patient enters session with clear change talk and willingness to engage. Psychiatrist's controlling, invalidating approach is likely to trigger **psychological reactance** – patient will become more defensive and more committed to sustain talk as way of asserting autonomy. Patient is likely to leave session feeling disrespected and **less motivated to change** than upon entry.

### **Recommendations for Immediate Action:**

1. **Seek formal MI training** through accredited program with didactic instruction, skill practice, and feedback on recorded sessions.
2. **Eliminate invalidating statements** – Stop telling patients they cannot decide how they feel.
3. **Develop reflective listening skills** – Learn to reflect before responding.
4. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** – Adopt true partnership approach.
5. **Learn to evoke rather than impose** – Ask questions that draw out patient's own motivations.
6. **Receive clinical supervision** on recorded sessions with feedback on MI adherence.
