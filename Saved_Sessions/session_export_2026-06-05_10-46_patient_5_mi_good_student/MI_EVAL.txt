### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. While some change talk emerges naturally (past success, stress-symptom observations), clinician does not strategically amplify, reinforce, or deepen this language. Critical missed opportunity: Aizada provides clear hints of emerging insight ("When my son got married, I was too busy to notice the pain"; "It gets worse when my husband is away"), but psychiatrist simply reflects these back without evoking her own theory or understanding. Does not strategically use scaling questions to cultivate change talk. Does not evoke deeper reasons for change beyond immediate goal of referral. Core change talk regarding acceptance of psychological factors remains largely uncultivated.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates moderate skill in softening sustain talk through validation and reflective listening, avoiding direct confrontation of her resistance ("I don't think it's just in my head"). However, approach is somewhat passive rather than strategically active. Does not use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both biomedical concerns and psychological dimension. Does not use reframing to soften her either/or thinking. Does not strategically redirect focus toward what she can control (stress management, coping). Avoidance of amplification is good MI practice, but more sophisticated techniques could significantly strengthen this area.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**

### Key Strengths:
- ✓ Excellent opening and collaborative agenda-setting
- ✓ Consistent validation and empathic attunement
- ✓ Strong collaborative stance treating Aizada as equal partner
- ✓ Skillful avoidance of confrontation regarding biomedical concerns
- ✓ Effective elicitation of past success with migraines
- ✓ Concrete action-planning with increased client confidence (7/10)

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ Insufficient strategic cultivation of change talk, particularly regarding stress-symptom connections
- ✗ Lack of clear strategic direction toward core change goal of integrated biopsychosocial understanding
- ✗ Passive rather than active handling of sustain talk regarding psychological factors
- ✗ Missed opportunities to deepen exploration of underlying grief and trauma
- ✗ No explicit invitation to co-create treatment frame or explore psychological factors

### Clinical Impact:
Aizada leaves feeling more heard, validated, and empowered to advocate for herself—positive outcomes. However, session may inadvertently reinforce health anxiety by supporting further medical testing without helping her develop integrated understanding of symptoms or build tolerance for uncertainty. Session represents good supportive counseling but falls short of strategic MI practice needed to evoke deeper motivation for transformative biopsychosocial change.
