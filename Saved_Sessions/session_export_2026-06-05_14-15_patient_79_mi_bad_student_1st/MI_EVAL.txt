No affirmation of worth, no warmth, and complete dismissal of client's perspective. Violates foundational MI principle of acceptance.

However, this direction is imposed through coercion rather than collaboration, which severely compromises overall MI effectiveness and undermines other competencies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematic violation of autonomy at every turn. Psychiatrist explicitly denies client's right to choose: "No, you don't get to decide what helps" and "This is non-negotiable." Uses coercive language framing non-compliance as moral failure ("choosing to harm yourself," "avoiding responsibility"). Creates false dichotomies and psychological manipulation. ### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist tells rather than asks, imposes rather than explores. Lucía's change talk ("common sense somewhere in there") is ignored. When Lucía expresses "I wish I could just pick up the patches right now and be done with it," this rich material for evocation is completely missed. Only closed, leading questions used ("Don't you want to feel better?"). No exploration of values, no inquiry into intrinsic motivation, no discovery of client's own reasons for change. Assumes fear of disease is only relevant motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Active rejection of partnership throughout. Opening statement explicitly positions psychiatrist as sole source of wisdom: "I'll be telling you exactly what steps you must take." All client contributions systematically dismissed. When Lucía suggests different approach, psychiatrist refuses: "No, you don't get to choose the approach here." No inclusive language ("we," "together"), no acknowledgment of client's knowledge about own life, no genuine invitation for perspective to shape session. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist fails to evoke, recognize, or reinforce any meaningful change talk. Initial change talk ("common sense somewhere in there… smoking isn't great for me") is ignored rather than developed. Lucía's desire statement ("I wish I could just pick up the patches") is missed. No open questions asked to elicit change talk. Result: Lucía's change talk is minimal and underdeveloped. By session end, client has retreated into sustain talk: "maybe someday, but not today." Change talk actually decreases across session as defensiveness increases.

### Softening Sustain Talk

When Lucía expresses fatigue with repetitive warnings, psychiatrist responds by repeating identical warnings, confirming her belief this conversation will be unhelpful. When Lucía states pressure makes her want to tune out, psychiatrist escalates demands. Uses coercion rather than strategic reframing. Minor positive: does not explicitly validate sustain talk. ## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Anti-MI

**Average Score: 1.3/5**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of motivational interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist operates from a directive, authoritarian, confrontational stance that is fundamentally incompatible with MI principles. Only one domain (Direction) received a score above 1, and that score is misleading because direction is delivered through coercion rather than collaboration.

**Critical Findings:**
- Psychiatrist believes role is to convince, pressure, or shame client into compliance
- Coercive approach activates psychological reactance and strengthens resistance
- Therapeutic relationship is severely damaged; client unlikely to return or implement recommendations
- Session is likely to **decrease** Lucía's intrinsic motivation for change, not increase it
- Client enters with partial awareness and openness; leaves more defensive and entrenched in sustain talk

**Most Urgent Improvements Needed:**
1. Fundamental shift from expert/authority stance to collaborative partnership
2. Implementation of reflective listening; elimination of lectures
3. Genuine respect for client autonomy and right to choose
4. Development of curiosity about client's perspective rather than assumptions

**Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires comprehensive MI training including 2-3 day workshop, regular supervision with MI-trained supervisor, audio/video review with feedback, and foundational reading. Without significant training and attitude shift, sessions are likely to harm rather than help clients with ambivalence about change.
