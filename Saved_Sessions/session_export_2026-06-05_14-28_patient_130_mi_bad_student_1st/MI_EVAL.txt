# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard throughout the session. Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent judgment and demeaning language ("You're fooling yourself," "Are you just not strong enough?"). No moments of affirming Tyler's inherent worth or normalizing his struggle. Dismissive tone and contempt evident in closing statement ("Now go and do it"). Unconditional positive regard is entirely absent, replaced by authoritarian control and disapproval.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No genuine empathic understanding demonstrated. Reflective listening is absent; psychiatrist dismisses and contradicts Tyler's stated experience rather than seeking to understand it. When Tyler discloses vulnerable information about peer belonging ("they're the only ones who get me right now"), psychiatrist invalidates this rather than reflecting emotional nuance. No complex reflections capturing Tyler's internal world. Psychiatrist substitutes their own agenda for Tyler's actual experience throughout.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear focus on substance use reduction but pursues direction through authoritarian prescription rather than collaborative exploration. Target behaviors are imposed (reduce to couple times weekly, avoid peer contexts) without co-construction or client input. Direction is maintained through control, not through collaborative clarification of shared goals. Focus is consistent but method undermines MI-consistent direction.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of collaborative stance. Hierarchical, expert-driven model established from opening statement ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do"). Psychiatrist dismisses Tyler's self-assessment repeatedly and prescribes without exploring his perspective. Tyler explicitly signals absence of collaboration ("It feels like you're just trying to boss me around"). Zero evidence of joint problem-solving or shared decision-making. Power entirely held by psychiatrist.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematic elimination of client autonomy through controlling language throughout ("have to," "must," "need to," "non-negotiable"). When Tyler assesses his own readiness, psychiatrist responds "No, that's not good enough. You have to commit now," directly contradicting his autonomy. Peer pressure concerns are met with prescription rather than exploration of Tyler's agency. No statements affirming Tyler's right to choose. Capitulation to external pressure evident by session end, not autonomous motivation.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no genuine evocation demonstrated. Closed, interrogative questions used ("Do you use them daily?") but no open questions designed to elicit Tyler's own perspective, values, or motivations. When Tyler mentions friends, psychiatrist never asks what appeals to him about the group. No exploration of Tyler's own reasons for change, values, strengths, or resources. Psychiatrist imposes reasons for change through rhetoric rather than evoking them through genuine curiosity.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively assumes sole expert role and explicitly dismisses Tyler's self-knowledge throughout. Opening statement establishes unequal power dynamic. Psychiatrist repeatedly corrects Tyler's self-assessment ("You're fooling yourself," "This isn't just about fitting in or friends—it's about your health"). No moments where psychiatrist conveys "You know yourself best" or values Tyler's perspective. Tyler's stated experience is consistently invalidated and replaced with psychiatrist's framework. True partnership—where client wisdom is valued—completely absent.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No skill demonstrated in cultivating change talk. Few instances of weak change talk from Tyler ("I guess I could try," "Maybe someday") are met with pressure and criticism rather than amplification. When Tyler offers tentative openness, psychiatrist responds with "You really have to try harder. A week without it is nothing," which suppresses rather than strengthens change talk. No open questions designed to elicit change talk. Overall approach generates defensiveness and sustain talk rather than cultivating motivation for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk, which predictably strengthens resistance. When Tyler expresses "I don't really see why I should," psychiatrist responds with attack ("You're fooling yourself if you think this isn't a problem"). When Tyler states "I'm not ready to quit," psychiatrist responds "No, that's not good enough. You have to commit now." No double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic redirection used. Confrontational approach generates more entrenched defensiveness. Tyler's sustain talk intensifies throughout session, indicating ineffective handling.

---

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.0/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates a confrontational, authoritarian approach that is contraindicated for adolescent substance use treatment. The psychiatrist scores 1/5 on eight of nine MI domains, indicating pervasive deficiencies across nearly every aspect of MI practice.

**Critical Findings:**
- **MI Spirit Absent:** Partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation—the four pillars of MI—are completely absent. Judgment, control, and contempt characterize the interaction instead.
- **Authoritarian Expert Role:** Psychiatrist assumes total authority and dismisses Tyler's perspective entirely, generating defensiveness and resistance.
- **Confrontational Approach:** Direct confrontation of sustain talk strengthens resistance rather than softening it, as research consistently demonstrates.
- **Autonomy Eliminated:** Controlling language ("have to," "must," "non-negotiable") removes Tyler's sense of choice, particularly harmful with developmentally autonomy-focused adolescents.
- **Change Talk Suppressed:** Weak change talk is met with pressure rather than amplification, generating defensive compliance rather than intrinsic motivation.

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Tyler's motivation has **decreased throughout the session**. He moved from weak openness ("I guess I could try") to defensive resistance ("I'm not gonna make any big promises"). Therapeutic alliance is damaged. Likelihood of treatment engagement or behavior change is essentially zero.

**Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires **immediate, intensive training and supervision in Motivational Interviewing** before continuing to work with substance-using adolescents. Current approach is contraindicated and actively harmful to client outcomes.
