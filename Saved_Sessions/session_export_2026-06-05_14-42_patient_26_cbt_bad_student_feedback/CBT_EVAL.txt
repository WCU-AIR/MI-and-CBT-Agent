## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Session Context:** Patient presents with Adjustment Disorder with Anxiety, Alcohol Use Disorder (mild), and Cannabis Use Disorder (mild) following exposure to traumatic videos of police brutality in his community. Patient reports nightmares, avoidance, hypervigilance, escalating substance use (six beers nightly, cannabis nightly), and safety concerns affecting functioning.

**Critical Observations:**

1. **Absence of CBT Framework:** This session was conducted as generic supportive counseling rather than evidence-based CBT. No CBT techniques were applied, no case conceptualization was developed, and no systematic assessment occurred.

2. **Trauma Not Adequately Addressed:** Patient disclosed exposure to traumatic videos and vivid nightmares about being chased by police. Therapist never systematically assessed trauma exposure, explored its impact, or discussed trauma-specific interventions. Instead, therapist shared personal anecdote about flying dream, demonstrating profound misunderstanding of trauma symptomatology.

3. **Substance Use Concerns Minimized:** Patient disclosed escalating alcohol use (six beers nightly) and cannabis use (nightly), acknowledged dependency concerns, and expressed ambivalence. Therapist normalized this ("I totally get it. Sometimes a little something to unwind feels necessary") without conducting proper assessment or developing intervention plan. This represents critical clinical failure given two diagnosed substance use disorders.

4. **Excessive Self-Disclosure:** Therapist repeatedly shared personal anecdotes about exam stress, gym avoidance, dreams, and busy schedule. This shifted focus from patient's experience to therapist's experience and suggested therapist viewed patient's trauma-related anxiety as equivalent to ordinary life stress. Inappropriate and potentially harmful.

5. **Poor Agenda-Setting:** No agenda established for first 80% of session. Only at end did therapist attempt agenda-setting, which came too late to structure session appropriately. Final agenda lacked specificity regarding which problems would be prioritized.

6. **Minimal Feedback-Seeking:** Therapist made virtually no attempt to elicit patient feedback about understanding, helpfulness, or agreement. When patient stated positive thinking "doesn't always work," therapist did not adjust course or seek feedback on ineffectiveness.

7. **No Guided Discovery:** Therapist relied entirely on direct advice and suggestion rather than Socratic questioning. This violates core CBT principle and reduces patient's active engagement in problem-solving.

8. **No Homework Assignment:** Despite multiple opportunities, therapist did not assign formal homework with clear rationale, specificity, and follow-up plans. Suggestions were made informally without structure needed for between-session learning.

**Recommendations for Supervisor:**

- **Immediate:** Provide intensive training in CBT fundamentals, particularly case conceptualization, guided discovery, and application of CBT techniques.
- **Short-term:** Require supervision on recorded sessions with focus on: (1) developing coherent case conceptualizations, (2) using Socratic questioning, (3) applying CBT techniques, (4) systematic assessment of trauma and substance use, (5) agenda-setting and time management.
- **Ongoing:** Require attendance at CBT training workshops, particularly trauma-focused CBT (Prolonged Exposure, Cognitive Processing Therapy) and substance use interventions.
- **Patient Care:** Consider reassigning patient to more experienced CBT therapist or providing intensive supervision to ensure evidence-based treatment is delivered.

**Prognosis with Proper CBT:** Good. Patient's symptoms are recent (4 months), he has good social support, and he is motivated to improve. With proper trauma-focused CBT addressing anxiety, avoidance, and substance use, significant improvement is likely.

**Prognosis with Current Approach:** Poor. Without coherent treatment strategy or evidence-based interventions, patient will not develop skills to manage anxiety or reduce substance use. Risk of continued substance use escalation and functional deterioration.
