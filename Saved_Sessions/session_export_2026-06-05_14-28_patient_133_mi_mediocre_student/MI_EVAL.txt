## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 2.2/5**

### Summary of Key Deficiencies

This session demonstrates fundamental deficiencies in MI practice. While the psychiatrist maintains a respectful, non-confrontational tone and provides practical behavioral guidance, the session is dominated by an **expert-driven, prescriptive approach that directly contradicts core MI principles**. The clinician tells Yasmin what to do rather than evoking her own motivations, wisdom, and resources.

**Critical Failures:**
- Pervasive prescriptive language undermines autonomy and intrinsic motivation
- Genuine empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and partnership are largely absent
- Core ambivalence about weight loss versus sustainable health—the heart of her struggle—remains unexplored and unvalidated
- Client's own motivations, values, and resources are not evoked; clinician-generated solutions are imposed instead
- Expert-driven stance positions psychiatrist as problem-solver rather than facilitator of client discovery

### Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Motivation for Change

**Low to Moderate.** While Yasmin agrees to a behavioral plan, her agreement appears to reflect compliance with clinician authority rather than genuine internal motivation. Yasmin's confidence rating of 5/10 suggests moderate uncertainty about sustainability. Without exploration of her own values, motivations, and resources, long-term behavior change is questionable. The prescriptive approach may produce short-term compliance but undermines intrinsic motivation necessary for lasting change.

### MI Spirit Assessment

**The session does not embody the spirit of MI.** While the psychiatrist demonstrates compassion and respect, fundamental principles of partnership, evocation, and autonomy support are compromised. The clinician assumes the expert role and directs the change process rather than facilitating Yasmin's own discovery and self-determination.

### Primary Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from prescriptive to evocative stance** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) — Replace "should" language with genuine curiosity and open-ended questions
2. **Develop genuine empathy through deeper reflection** — Move beyond surface-level reflections to capture emotional nuance and validate experience
3. **Explore ambivalence rather than resolve it prematurely** — Use MI techniques to help client integrate conflicting desires
4. **Evoke client motivation** — Ask questions that help Yasmin discover her own reasons for change
5. **Build true collaboration and partnership** — Actively invite input, incorporate ideas, position client as expert on own life
6. **Strengthen autonomy support** — Consistently affirm right to choose and ownership of change process
7. **Strategically cultivate change talk** — Pause and deepen when client expresses motivation; ask follow-up questions
8. **Develop comfort with silence** — Allow space for client-led exploration rather than filling silence with solutions

### Training Recommendation

This clinician would benefit from **intensive MI training** focused on understanding and embodying MI spirit, developing comfort with open-ended questions and client-led exploration, practicing reflective listening and complex reflections, and learning to evoke rather than impose motivation. Supervised practice with feedback on prescriptive language and expert-driven stance is essential for developing genuine MI competence.

**Evaluator Note:** With focused training and deliberate practice, this clinician can develop authentic MI competence. The foundation of respect and non-confrontation is present; the critical work involves shifting from expert-driven problem-solving to genuine evocation of client wisdom and intrinsic motivation.
