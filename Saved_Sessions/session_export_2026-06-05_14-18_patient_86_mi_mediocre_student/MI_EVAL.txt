### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation minimal and largely replaced by advice-giving. Psychiatrist rarely asks open-ended questions designed to draw out Marco's motivations, values, ideas, or resources. Questions asked are often closed or leading: "Do you think cutting down on the juice would be hard for you?" (closed, presupposes difficulty); "Have you tried any stress-relief techniques?" (closed, factual); "How confident are you?" (closed, leading). True evocation would explore: "What made you feel so proud?" "What do you think would happen if you cut back?" "What ideas do you have?" "What's worked for you before?" "What would need to be true for you to feel confident?" When Marco identifies juice as problem, psychiatrist accepts but does not deepen through evocation. Misses opportunities to explore Marco's values (health, pride, family support), past successes (initial weight loss, exercise initiation), and self-efficacy. Provides external reasons and solutions rather than evoking internal motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist adequately handles sustain talk but does not skillfully soften it. Marco expresses sustain talk twice: "boy, do I love that OJ" and "I'll miss it for sure." These are legitimate expressions of ambivalence—Marco values juice and fears loss. Psychiatrist's response acknowledges difficulty ("Cutting down on it is going to be tough because it's kind of my go-to drink") then immediately pivots to solutions. This is basic redirect but not sophisticated. More skilled approach would involve: (1) Reflecting sustain talk with empathy; (2) Exploring ambivalence ("What is it about the juice that you love?"); (3) Using double-sided reflection ("On one hand you love it; on the other hand you see how it affects your weight"); (4) Reframing ("The juice feels good in the moment but gets in the way of feeling proud"). Psychiatrist does not explore what Marco would gain by reducing juice or what he might replace it with emotionally. Does not use Marco's own change talk to contextualize sustain talk. Sustain talk is not reinforced but also not skillfully softened through deeper exploration or reframing.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 2.3/5**

### Strengths
- Clear, organized session structure with logical flow and comprehensive summary
- Acceptance of Marco's self-identified problem (juice consumption) without dismissal or reframing
- Recognition and affirmation of Marco's 10/10 confidence rating
- Practical, actionable, and realistic suggestions (phone reminders, meal planning, spouse involvement)
- Support for Marco's collaborative idea of involving his wife in behavior change

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Expert-driven stance dominates:** Psychiatrist consistently prescribes solutions rather than evoking Marco's own ideas and motivations, undermining collaborative spirit of MI
- **Minimal evocation of change talk:** Strong change talk produced by Marco is not strategically amplified or deepened; opportunities to strengthen commitment are missed
- **Lack of empathic depth:** Reflections are factual rather than emotionally attuned; fails to explore Marco's feelings about repeated failure cycle, pride in success, or ambivalence about giving up juice
- **Prescriptive language throughout:** "You should," "it would probably help if," "maybe you should" communicate psychiatrist knows what is best rather than supporting Marco's autonomy and choice
- **Multiple goals without prioritization:** Six distinct action items introduced (juice reduction, deep breathing, short breaks, meal planning, phone reminders, follow-up) without collaborative prioritization; risks overwhelming Marco and reducing adherence
- **Missed exploration of ambivalence:** When Marco expresses love for juice, psychiatrist does not explore this ambivalence or use it to deepen understanding of Marco's values and barriers
- **Sustain talk not skillfully softened:** Redirects sustain talk but does not use sophisticated techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing
- **Autonomy not emphasized:** No affirmation of Marco's right to choose, ownership of change process, or expertise about his own life

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change Sustainability
While Marco enters with strong change talk and high confidence (10/10), the psychiatrist's expert-driven, prescriptive approach risks inadvertently shifting Marco's locus of control from internal to external. Marco may comply with the plan because the psychiatrist recommended it, not because Marco has deeply explored and committed to it. Multiple goals without prioritization may overwhelm Marco and reduce his sense of efficacy. The psychiatrist's failure to evoke and amplify Marco's change talk means Marco leaves with his own motivations underexplored and underutilized. Given Marco's history of initial success followed by relapse, without deeper exploration of his values, barriers, and self-efficacy, Marco may struggle to maintain motivation when the psychiatrist is not present. The session is supportive and practical but not optimally designed to evoke and sustain intrinsic motivation for lasting behavior change.

## Recommendations for Clinical Development

1. **Shift from prescriptive to evocative language:** Replace directive statements with open-ended questions that invite Marco to generate his own ideas and solutions.

2. **Evoke and amplify change talk strategically:** Respond to Marco's change talk with reflections that deepen it, followed by open-ended questions that invite more change talk.

3. **Develop complex, emotionally attuned reflections:** Move beyond factual reflections to capture emotional nuance, ambivalence, and meaning. Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of conflict.

4. **Explore ambivalence rather than redirect it:** When Marco expresses sustain talk, resist urge to immediately problem-solve. Instead, explore the ambivalence with curiosity and empathy.

5. **Emphasize autonomy and client expertise:** Explicitly affirm Marco's right to choose, his ownership of the change process, and his expertise about his own life.

6. **Prioritize goals collaboratively:** Collaborate with Marco to identify which change feels most important and most doable right now, rather than imposing multiple targets.

7. **Use scaling questions to evoke commitment:** When Marco rates confidence, use follow-up questions to explore reasoning behind the rating and deepen commitment.

8. **Build on Marco's own ideas:** When Marco suggests an idea, explore it deeply and build on it collaboratively rather than pivoting to new tasks.

9. **Explore Marco's values and past successes:** Deliberately explore what matters most to Marco and what has worked for him in the past to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

10. **Develop genuine partnership:** Explicitly invite Marco to shape session direction and decision-making. Use language that conveys shared expertise and equal partnership.
