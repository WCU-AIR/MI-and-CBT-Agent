# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist demonstrates explicit judgment and disapproval throughout ("You're not being realistic or responsible," "That's not smart at all"). Fundamental misidentification of presenting concern (repeatedly asks about smoking when Dylan's referral is for alcohol-related conduct violation). Dylan treated with contempt rather than respect. No affirmation of strengths, no normalization of struggle, no genuine warmth. Authoritarian and punitive stance rather than supportive engagement.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand Dylan's perspective. Psychiatrist talks *at* Dylan rather than *with* him. Dylan's concerns ("I was just at the party," "it wasn't like I was causing trouble") never explored or reflected back. Emotional expressions ignored ("I don't appreciate being told I'm being stupid or reckless"). Dylan's values (social connection, friendship) dismissed as evidence of irresponsibility rather than explored empathetically. Repeated failure to identify correct presenting concern demonstrates fundamental breakdown in listening and therapeutic relationship.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on behavior change (avoiding parties with alcohol) but accomplished through coercion and ultimatum rather than collaborative exploration. Direction imposed rather than co-created. No exploratory questions to clarify direction collaboratively ("What would need to happen for you to feel ready to make changes?"). Repeated misidentification of presenting concern severely compromises utility of directional focus. Consistency in focus undermined by rigidity in approach and fundamental misalignment with client's actual presenting concern.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Complete absence of collaboration. Psychiatrist adopts authoritarian expert stance from opening ("we need to talk about your smoking habit and how you're going to quit"). Repeated use of unilateral authority language: "You have to," "You really need to," "This isn't a negotiation," "There's no halfway here," "This is non-negotiable." Dylan's attempts to propose alternatives explicitly shut down ("No, you don't get to negotiate here"). Dylan's perspective never explored or incorporated; dismissed as rationalization. Power dynamic entirely one-directional: psychiatrist commands, Dylan resists.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Systematic elimination of choice and imposition of control. Language explicitly minimizes autonomy: "You have to stop going to these parties—there's no other way," "This is non-negotiable," "You don't get to negotiate here." Conditional framing of compliance as only path to positive future. When Dylan asserts autonomy ("I'm not ready to make that promise today"), psychiatrist intensifies pressure rather than respecting boundary. Dylan explicitly states resistance to control ("I don't respond well to that," "it makes me want to push back even more"); psychiatrist responds by intensifying controlling behavior. Triggers reactance—psychological resistance to perceived autonomy threats.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation present. All questions closed, rhetorical, or designed to shame rather than invite exploration. No open-ended questions to elicit Dylan's perspective. Rhetorical questions used to shame: "Why would you even risk it?" "Don't you understand that one mistake can cost you big time?" "Are you seriously saying you're okay risking your future?" Dylan's values (social life, friendships) never explored; dismissed as immaturity. No exploration of Dylan's own reasons for change. Limited change talk offered by Dylan actively suppressed: when Dylan says "Maybe I can be smarter about it," psychiatrist responds "No, you don't get to negotiate here." Missed opportunities to evoke change talk throughout session.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role and explicitly rejects partnership. Repeated assertions of unilateral authority: "You have to," "This is non-negotiable," "This isn't a negotiation." Dylan's attempts to contribute perspective explicitly rejected ("No, you don't get to negotiate here"). No curiosity about Dylan's expertise regarding his own life, peer context, social needs, or capacity for change. Psychiatrist operates from assumption that he knows what Dylan needs better than Dylan knows himself. Repeated misidentification of presenting concern further undermines partnership and suggests lack of genuine interest in understanding Dylan's actual situation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Zero cultivation of change talk. Dylan offers limited but meaningful change talk: "Maybe I could have been more careful," "Maybe I can be smarter about it," "I'm not trying to mess up my future," "I'm willing to think about how I handle things differently," "I'm not saying I won't think twice or be more careful." Each instance met with suppression rather than exploration. When Dylan says "Maybe I can be smarter about it," psychiatrist responds "No, you don't get to negotiate here." No open-ended questions designed to evoke change talk. No reinforcement or amplification of change talk offered. Psychiatrist's controlling approach increases sustain talk and defensiveness rather than cultivating change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts Dylan's sustain talk with judgment and ultimatums, intensifying rather than softening it. Dylan's sustain talk ("I'm almost 21," "People my age hang out and have a few drinks," "It wasn't like I was doing anything wrong," "I was just unlucky to get caught," "Avoiding every party with alcohol? That's just not gonna happen") met with confrontation: "No, you don't get it," "That's not smart at all," "You're not being realistic or responsible." No use of strategic MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing, strategic questioning). Confrontation triggers defensiveness and reactance. Each confrontation increases Dylan's commitment to status quo. By session end, Dylan more entrenched in sustain talk than at beginning.

---

## Overall Session Score: 1.0/5

---

### Summary of Critical Findings

**Strengths:** None identified. No instances of skillful MI practice, genuine collaboration, or evidence that psychiatrist understands or values MI principles.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of MI spirit (judgment, control, dismissal rather than partnership, acceptance, compassion)
- Authoritarian stance with explicit rejection of collaboration
- Fundamental misidentification of presenting concern (smoking vs. alcohol) suggesting profound inattention or dismissal
- Suppression of change talk and cultivation of sustain talk
- Failure to understand client's perspective through reflective listening
- Use of shame and judgment as motivators (research-contraindicated)
- Systematic elimination of autonomy triggering reactance
- Active harm to therapeutic relationship and likelihood of behavior change

**Impact:** Dylan enters with some openness; exits more defensive, more entrenched in resistance, explicitly unwilling to commit to change. Repeated misidentification of presenting concern damages credibility. Session actively counterproductive to behavior change goals.

**Recommendation:** This session does not meet acceptable MI practice standards. Psychiatrist requires comprehensive MI retraining and supervised practice before conducting additional MI-based interventions. Current approach is likely counterproductive and may harm clients through reactance, damaged alliance, and decreased intrinsic motivation for change.
