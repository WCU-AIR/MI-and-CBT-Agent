## CLINICAL RISK ASSESSMENT

**Safety Concern**: Without explicit safety planning, patient may not know when to seek emergency care if self-harm urges escalate. **This is a significant clinical risk requiring immediate intervention in next session.**

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change**: With current approach, patient may experience modest improvement in relationship communication and some anxiety reduction through cognitive intervention. However, without addressing safety, self-harm function, and emotion regulation comprehensively, unlikely to achieve significant, sustained symptom improvement. Risk of continued self-harm and potential escalation is concerning.

**Evidence-Based Practice**: Session incorporates core CBT elements but lacks several evidence-based components critical for this presentation: safety planning, functional analysis, emotion regulation skills, and PMDD-specific interventions. Does not meet full standards for evidence-based treatment of PMDD with comorbid self-harm.

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR NEXT SESSION (PRIORITY ORDER)

1. **IMPLEMENT EXPLICIT SAFETY PLANNING** – Conduct comprehensive safety assessment; develop written safety plan with crisis resources and emergency care criteria
2. **Conduct functional analysis of self-harm** – Systematically explore antecedents, behaviors, consequences; identify specific function(s); develop targeted alternatives
3. **Introduce prospective daily symptom monitoring** – Provide tracking form for menstrual cycle, mood, anxiety, irritability, energy, self-harm urges, coping strategies
4. **Expand emotion regulation skills** – Teach distress tolerance (ice cubes, intense exercise), opposite action, mindfulness, self-soothing beyond deep breathing
5. **Explore core beliefs and self-concept** – Use Socratic questioning to identify and address deeper beliefs about self-worth and ability to manage condition
6. **Develop comprehensive multi-session treatment plan** – Create written case conceptualization explaining relationships between PMDD, anxiety, self-harm; articulate sequence of interventions
7. **Introduce written cognitive tools** – Provide thought record template for independent use between sessions
8. **Conduct behavioral activation** – Assess current activity level; collaboratively develop activity schedule with daily, work, social, and pleasurable activities
9. **Provide psychoeducation** – Educate about PMDD, CBT model, and treatment rationale; provide written materials
10. **Extend behavioral experiments** – Design additional experiments beyond single communication strategy to test predictions and gather evidence
