## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Overall Clinical Impression:**

This session demonstrates a **significant departure from evidence-based CBT practice** and represents **inadequate treatment for moderate cocaine use disorder**. The therapist's warm, supportive demeanor is undermined by a fundamental lack of clinical structure, systematic conceptualization, and application of evidence-based techniques.

**Key Concerns:**

1. **Absence of Functional Analysis:** The therapist never explored what Oliver's substance use provides (social identity, sexual confidence, belonging, escape from anxiety) or how to meet these needs differently.

2. **Missed Core Cognitions:** Critical beliefs about social rejection, FOMO, and identity—explicitly stated by the patient—were never explored or challenged.

3. **No Behavioral Targeting:** The specific behavioral chain (alcohol → cocaine) and high-risk situations (Friday/Saturday nights, social apps, sexualized contexts) were never systematically addressed.

4. **Excessive Self-Disclosure:** Repeated personal anecdotes shifted focus from patient and implicitly communicated that therapist's experience was analogous to patient's clinical situation.

5. **No Homework or Between-Session Work:** Critical gap eliminating opportunity for real-world practice and skill development.

6. **Poor Session Structure:** Sessions drifted without clear agenda, priorities, or time allocation to specific interventions.

**Immediate Actions Required:**

- Supervisor should review session recording with therapist
- Provide direct feedback on deficiencies in CBT implementation
- Establish clear expectations for evidence-based practice
- Implement structured supervision with session planning and review
- Consider additional training in CBT for substance use disorders
- Develop treatment protocol with clear conceptualization, agenda-setting, and technique application

**Patient Safety Consideration:**

While this session is unlikely to cause direct harm, the lack of evidence-based intervention means the patient is not receiving adequate treatment for a clinically significant substance use disorder. Continued inadequate treatment may result in treatment failure, demoralization, and continued substance use with associated medical and occupational risks.
