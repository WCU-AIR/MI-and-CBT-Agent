## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- **Clear focus maintained:** Psychiatrist sustains consistent focus on target behaviors (exercise and diet) rather than allowing session to wander aimlessly.
- **Persistence:** Psychiatrist does not give up or become passive in face of patient resistance.

### Critical Deficiencies

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles**. The psychiatrist demonstrates:

- **Complete absence of MI spirit:** Session characterized by control, coercion, and judgment rather than partnership, acceptance, and compassion.
- **Active suppression of autonomy:** Explicit denial of patient's right to participate in decision-making ("No, you don't get to decide how you start").
- **Dismissal of legitimate concerns:** Health anxiety and fear of missed diagnosis treated as character flaws rather than understandable human responses.
- **Failure to evoke intrinsic motivation:** Reliance on external pressure and ultimatums rather than drawing out patient's own reasons for change.
- **Confrontational approach to resistance:** Escalation of confrontation predictably strengthens resistance rather than softening it.
- **Missed opportunities for collaboration:** Each compromise or request for explanation is shut down rather than explored.

### Impact on Client Engagement

**This session is likely to be harmful.** Aizada enters with ambivalence and emerging change talk. The psychiatrist's coercive approach will predictably:
- Strengthen resistance through reactance
- Damage therapeutic relationship and erode trust
- Externalize motivation, undermining intrinsic change drivers
- Confirm patient's fears of being dismissed and minimized
- Reduce likelihood of future engagement

### Recommendation

**Fundamental reorientation required.** Psychiatrist must shift from directive, prescriptive expert model to collaborative partnership model. This requires not just new techniques but genuine philosophical shift in how she views her role and relationship with patients. The goal is to help patients discover their own reasons for change and build confidence in their ability to make that change—not to convince them to comply with the psychiatrist's plan.
