Only closed, accusatory questions asked ("Why haven't you...?" "Are you going to do it or not?"). No elicitation of client's own ideas or resources.

### Critical Clinical Findings

**This session represents a fundamental violation of Motivational Interviewing principles and evidence-based therapeutic practice.** The psychiatrist's approach is authoritarian, coercive, and actively counterproductive to evoking client motivation for sustainable behavior change.

**Key deficiencies:**

1. **Authoritarian expert stance replacing collaboration** — Psychiatrist imposes solutions and explicitly forbids negotiation
2. **Coercion and threat replacing autonomy support** — Fear and ultimatums used to force compliance rather than support intrinsic motivation
3. **Judgment and shame replacing acceptance** — Client labeled as "weak" and "avoiding" rather than affirmed
4. **Dismissal replacing empathy** — Client's experience never reflected, explored, or validated
5. **Prescription replacing evocation** — Psychiatrist tells rather than asks; client's own motivation never accessed
6. **Threat-based compliance replacing intrinsic motivation** — Client complies out of fear, not internal commitment

---

### Likely Clinical Outcomes

**This approach is likely to produce:**
- Short-term compliance followed by long-term non-adherence
- Damaged therapeutic alliance and reduced trust
- Suppressed intrinsic motivation
- Increased client resistance and defensiveness
- Missed clinical opportunities (client's past success and change talk ignored)

**Research evidence:** Threat-based, coercive approaches consistently produce worse outcomes than collaborative, autonomy-supportive approaches for sustained behavior change.

---

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Seek formal MI training** — This is not a minor skill gap; it is fundamental misalignment with evidence-based practice
2. **Shift from expert/authority to collaborative partnership** — Reframe role and use inclusive language
3. **Replace threat with autonomy support** — Eliminate coercive language; support client's right to choose
4. **Develop empathic listening** — Learn reflective listening; practice 2 reflections for every 1 directive
5. **Amplify change talk** — Explore and strengthen client's own reasons for change
6. **Receive supervision** — Record sessions and review with MI-trained supervisor for feedback
