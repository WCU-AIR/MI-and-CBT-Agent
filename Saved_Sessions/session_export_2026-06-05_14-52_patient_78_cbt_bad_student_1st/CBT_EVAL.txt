## SUPERVISOR SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

**Therapist Competency Level:** Below threshold for safe, effective PTSD treatment

**Primary Strengths:**
- Demonstrated basic interpersonal warmth; not hostile or demeaning
- Showed willingness to engage with patient
- Acknowledged some patient coping strategies (gardening, woodworking)
- Maintained generally supportive tone

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of agenda-setting and session structure
- No CBT techniques applied; only generic advice offered
- Fundamental misunderstanding of PTSD pathophysiology
- No case conceptualization or treatment strategy
- Inappropriate therapeutic boundaries (excessive self-disclosure)
- No guided discovery; directive advice instead of Socratic questioning
- No focus on key trauma-related cognitions or behaviors
- No structured homework assignment
- Poor time management; essential clinical material never addressed
- Minimal feedback elicitation; no checks for understanding

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. **Suspend independent PTSD treatment** until formal training completed
2. **Engage in weekly supervision** with PTSD-specialized supervisor
3. **Complete formal training** in Prolonged Exposure, Cognitive Processing Therapy, or Trauma-Focused CBT
4. **Study PTSD psychoeducation** and case conceptualization before next patient contact
5. **Establish professional boundaries:** eliminate self-disclosure, maintain therapist-patient roles
6. **Learn Socratic questioning:** replace directive advice with guided discovery
7. **Implement structured session format:** agenda → assessment → intervention → homework → feedback
8. **Assign specific, rationale-based homework** with written instructions and follow-up plans

**Prognosis for Improvement:** Moderate to good if therapist commits to formal training and supervision. Therapist demonstrated capacity for engagement and warmth; deficiencies are primarily in technical knowledge and skill application, which are learnable through structured training and supervision.

**Risk to Patient:** Moderate. Current approach unlikely to produce therapeutic change and may increase patient demoralization about therapy's utility. For guarded trauma survivor, ineffective treatment may reduce likelihood of future engagement with mental health services.
