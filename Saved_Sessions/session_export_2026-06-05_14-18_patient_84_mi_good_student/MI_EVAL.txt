## Summary of Clinical Feedback

### Strengths

This session demonstrates **solid motivational interviewing practice** with several exemplary elements. The psychiatrist establishes genuine collaborative partnership from the opening, explicitly inviting Mai to set the agenda and consistently positioning her as an equal contributor. **Autonomy support is exceptional**—the clinician never pressures Mai toward medication adherence and consistently reinforces her right to choose. **Strong reflective listening** captures both Mai's cognitive confusion and emotional concerns. **Change talk is cultivated effectively** throughout, with strategic reinforcement of her own reasons for change, past successes, and concrete commitments. The psychiatrist **skillfully handles sustain talk** through empathic reflection and reframing rather than confrontation. The session achieves meaningful outcome, moving Mai from **4/10 to 7/10 confidence** with concrete plan and identified support needs.

### Areas for Improvement

While demonstrating solid MI practice, notable gaps prevent excellence:

1. **Premature problem-solving:** Moves quickly to solutions when Mai expresses hesitation (4/10) rather than deeply exploring what anchors that hesitation and what already supports change.

2. **Subtle expert positioning:** Shifts from collaborative questioning to offering specific solutions ("I'll provide a guide") rather than evoking what Mai thinks would be most helpful.

3. **Surface-level emotional exploration:** Reflections remain somewhat surface-level regarding deeper emotional experience (fear of bodily vulnerability, existential anxiety about health).

4. **Missed commitment statement:** Summarizes Mai's plan but does not ask Mai to articulate her own commitment in her own words, which would strengthen change talk.

5. **Limited meaning-making:** Does not deeply explore what staying healthy means to Mai personally or what specifically about preventing heart attack resonates with her values.

### Impact on Client Engagement

Despite gaps, session successfully engages Mai and moves her toward change. Mai progresses from confusion and hesitation (4/10) to clear readiness and concrete planning (7/10). She generates own change talk, identifies own strategies, and explicitly requests support. Psychiatrist's consistent autonomy support and collaborative stance likely contribute to engagement. However, impact could be stronger with deeper exploration of existing motivations and personal meaning. Deeper evocation of what already supports her and what health means personally would likely move confidence higher and strengthen intrinsic motivation for long-term adherence.

## Key Recommendations for Improvement

### 1. Deepen Exploration at Moments of Hesitation
When clients express lower confidence, pause problem-solving and explore what anchors that hesitation. Ask: "What's the 4 about?" or "What do you already believe about this medication that supports even that 4?"

### 2. Maintain Client-Centered Direction When Offering Support
Before offering specific solutions, evoke what client thinks would be most helpful: "What kind of support would feel most helpful?" or "How could we best work together?"

### 3. Deepen Exploration of Emotional Nuance and Personal Meaning
Move beyond surface-level reflection to explore deeper emotional experience: "What does staying healthy mean to you personally?" or "What would it feel like if...?"

### 4. Elicit Explicit Commitment Statements
After co-constructing a plan, ask client to articulate commitment in their own words: "Tell me in your own words what you're committing to."

### 5. Explore What Already Motivates Change
Use scaling questions strategically to identify existing motivations: "What's the [number] about?" rather than focusing only on what's missing.

### 6. Avoid Subtle Shifts Toward Expert Positioning
Monitor language for shifts from collaborative ("What do you think?") to expert-driven ("I will provide"). Maintain consistent collaborative language throughout.

### 7. Strengthen Intrinsic Motivation Through Values Connection
Help clients connect medication adherence to deeper personal values: "What does this mean for your life?" or "What would become possible if you stay healthy?"

### 8. Use Double-Sided Reflections Strategically
When clients express ambivalence, acknowledge both sides but subtly emphasize change talk: "On one hand [sustain talk], and on the other hand [change talk, which is the stronger foundation]."
