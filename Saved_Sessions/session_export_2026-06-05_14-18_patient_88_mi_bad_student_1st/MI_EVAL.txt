## Summary Assessment

**MI Competency Level:** Minimal to None

**Clinical Approach:** Directive, coercive, expert-driven practice that fundamentally violates Motivational Interviewing principles across nearly every domain.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Authoritarian stance with repeated "you have to/must" language
- Dismissal of patient perspective as "excuses" rather than validation
- Absence of reflective listening; rigid yes/no interrogation format
- Suppression of spontaneous change talk through dismissal
- Coercive pressure and fear-based threats
- Complete absence of collaboration or partnership
- Failure to evoke patient's intrinsic motivation

**Impact on Patient Engagement:** Likely to **decrease** motivation and **increase** resistance. Patient demonstrates clear reactance—pushing back against pressure, maintaining autonomy by refusing compliance, becoming more entrenched in ambivalence. Patient explicitly states: "pushing me like this just makes me feel more stuck." Confidence remains at 6/10 despite pressure. This approach is likely to increase dropout rates and reduce treatment efficacy.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. Shift from directive to collaborative stance (eliminate "you have to" language)
2. Implement reflective listening (demonstrate understanding before guidance)
3. Amplify change talk (recognize and reinforce patient's own motivation)
4. Support autonomy (remove pressure and threats; affirm patient choice)
5. Evoke rather than tell (ask questions to draw out patient wisdom)
6. Enroll in formal MI training and ongoing supervision

**Clinical Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires formal Motivational Interviewing training and ongoing clinical supervision before continuing MI-focused sessions. Current approach is counterproductive and potentially harmful to patient outcomes.
