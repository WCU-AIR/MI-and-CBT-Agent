## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- **Strong collaborative approach:** Therapist invited patient to set agenda, involved her in designing behavioral experiment, and solicited feedback about session helpfulness.
- **Effective guided discovery:** Friend perspective technique was skillfully applied and helped patient access own wisdom about managing eating anxiety.
- **Warm and professional interpersonal style:** Therapist created safe, respectful environment that encouraged disclosure and engagement.
- **Appropriate use of behavioral experimentation:** Designed specific, testable behavioral experiment to examine patient's worry about eating.
- **Accurate empathic reflection:** Demonstrated understanding of patient's emotional experience and internal conflict.
- **Reasonable pacing:** Session maintained focus and avoided significant tangents.

### Critical Deficiencies:

**1. Failure to Address Medical Severity and Safety (Most Critical)**
- Did not establish medical safety parameters, discuss vital sign monitoring, or address serious medical consequences of malnutrition (BMI 14.7, amenorrhea, cognitive impairment).
- No discussion of medical management, coordination with medical providers, or nutritional rehabilitation targets.
- **Clinical Risk:** Patient's medical condition may worsen without explicit monitoring or intervention.

**2. Inadequate Case Conceptualization**
- Did not develop explicit understanding of what maintains patient's eating restriction.
- Accepted surface-level explanations without systematically examining whether these are primary drivers or secondary rationalizations.
- No exploration of core beliefs about weight and body, despite evidence that cultural factors may be central.
- No assessment of whether non-fat-phobic presentation reflects cultural differences in eating disorder manifestation.

**3. Insufficient Focus on Critical Maintaining Behaviors**
- Did not assess or address excessive physical activity (3-4 hours daily walking), a significant maintaining behavior in eating disorders.
- No exploration of whether activity serves weight-loss or calorie-burning function.

**4. Inadequate Strategy for Change**
- Strategy is too modest and slow for medical severity of patient's condition.
- No explicit nutritional rehabilitation plan with specific caloric targets or meal structure.
- No systematic exposure to eating and discomfort tolerance.
- No clear plan for addressing cultural or contextual maintaining factors.

**5. Shallow Cognitive Work**
- While therapist identified one thought about eating and discomfort, cognitive work did not explore deeper beliefs or origin of thought.
- No use of written thought records, comprehensive cognitive restructuring, or advanced cognitive techniques.

**6. Limited Application of CBT Techniques**
- Therapist applied only few CBT techniques (thought record, evidence analysis, friend perspective, behavioral experiment).
- Missing techniques: psychoeducation about eating disorders, behavioral activation, exposure therapy, coping skills training, problem-solving, activity scheduling.

**7. Insufficient Attention to Cultural and Contextual Factors**
- Did not explore how patient's recent immigration, cultural adjustment, religious beliefs, or family dynamics maintain eating restriction.
- No assessment of whether cultural messages about body size or family expectations influence restriction.

**8. Incomplete Homework Assignment**
- Homework lacked specific monitoring instructions, written materials, or clear plan for review.
- Nutritional target (adding one small portion) is insufficient for medical severity.
- No contingency planning or coping strategies for obstacles.

---

## CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

**Competency Level:** Adequate for general anxiety or depression treatment, but **insufficient for eating disorder treatment**, particularly severe anorexia nervosa.

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change:** Moderate to low. Patient may experience some benefit from cognitive work and behavioral experiment, but without addressing medical severity, nutritional rehabilitation, maintaining behaviors, and cultural factors, likelihood of sustained recovery is low.

**Standard of Care:** **This session does not meet the standard of care for eating disorder treatment.** Failure to establish medical safety parameters and nutritional rehabilitation targets represents serious clinical gap.

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. Establish medical safety and nutritional rehabilitation as primary treatment goals
2. Develop comprehensive case conceptualization
3. Coordinate with medical providers and establish multidisciplinary treatment team
4. Assess whether more intensive intervention (day treatment, residential treatment) is needed
5. Expand range of CBT techniques and develop more ambitious treatment strategy
6. Assess and address excessive physical activity
7. Explore and address cultural and contextual factors
8. Establish clear plan for homework review and monitoring
