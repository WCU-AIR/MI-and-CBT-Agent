## Summary of Clinical Findings

### **Strengths:**
- Maintains clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (smoking cessation and exercise)
- Prevents aimless session drift through repeated redirection to health-related behavior change
- Demonstrates genuine concern for client's health through medically accurate messaging about smoking risks

### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates confrontational, expert-driven counseling that is **clinically counterproductive**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no MI competence across all nine domains.

**Most Significant Deficiencies:**
1. **Complete absence of MI spirit** — No acceptance, empathy, collaboration, or autonomy support
2. **Dismissal of client emotions** — Repeatedly invalidates fear, ambivalence, and overwhelm as "excuses"
3. **Ignores intrinsic motivation** — Client provides multiple change talk statements that are completely unacknowledged
4. **Coercive language and demands** — Uses ultimatums and guilt to attempt to override client autonomy
5. **Confrontational approach to resistance** — Directly attacks sustain talk, likely strengthening psychological reactance

### **Impact on Client Engagement and Outcomes:**

This session is **likely to be counterproductive** for Anisa's motivation to change. While she enters with substantial change talk and genuine desire to quit, the psychiatrist's confrontational, dismissive approach will likely:
- Activate psychological reactance (tendency to resist when autonomy is threatened)
- Increase defensiveness and entrenchment in ambivalence
- Reinforce shame and fear of failure
- Reduce willingness to engage in future treatment
- Set client up for failure by demanding immediate action without realistic planning

### **Clinical Recommendation:**

The psychiatrist should **not continue conducting smoking cessation counseling using this approach**. **Formal Motivational Interviewing training is essential** before continuing this type of work. The current approach is clinically ineffective and potentially harmful to client outcomes and therapeutic relationship.
