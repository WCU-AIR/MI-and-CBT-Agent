## Overall Session Score: **1.1/5**

### Summary Assessment

**Critical Deficiencies Identified:**

This session represents a **severe departure from motivational interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, expert-driven practice that actively undermines client motivation and autonomy across all nine MI domains.

**Most Damaging Aspects:**

1. **Complete absence of acceptance and empathy** – Judgmental language, shaming, and invalidation communicate disapproval rather than unconditional positive regard
2. **Authoritarian, non-collaborative stance** – Expert-driven hierarchy with patient treated as passive recipient of directives
3. **Active suppression of change talk** – Patient's emerging motivation (concern about cancer, desire to protect son, willingness to reduce smoking) is dismissed and minimized
4. **Undermining of autonomy** – Repeated pressure and criticism when patient chooses differently; right to own pace actively undermined
5. **Failure to evoke** – Clinician tells rather than asks; no genuine exploration of patient's motivations, values, or resources

**Likely Clinical Impact:**

This session is likely to **decrease** intrinsic motivation and **increase resistance**. Patient enters with emerging motivation but leaves feeling judged, misunderstood, and pressured. Research demonstrates confrontational, expert-driven approaches trigger psychological reactance, making clients more defensive and less likely to change.

**Recommendation:**

**This clinician requires immediate, intensive training in MI principles and practice.** Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based practice and likely to harm patient outcomes. Specific interventions needed include: adoption of MI spirit, development of genuine empathy, elimination of judgmental language, shift from telling to asking, amplification of change talk, autonomy support, collaborative goal-setting, skill development in handling ambivalence, and regular supervision with MI fidelity assessment.
