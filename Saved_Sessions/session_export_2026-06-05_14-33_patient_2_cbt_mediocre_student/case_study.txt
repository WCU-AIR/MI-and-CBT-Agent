--- DISORDER ---
Adjustment Disorder with Depressed Mood

--- CASE STUDY ---

Franklin Sims, a 21-year-old single African American man, sought treatment at a university-affiliated community mental health clinic for feeling "stressed out," withdrawn, and "worried about money." He reported feeling depressed for 3 months following the end of a 3-year romantic relationship that had included discussions of marriage.

Mr. Sims, the son of working-class parents, had been financially independent since high school. He became increasingly preoccupied with money, his grades, and his future after the breakup. He worked full-time on a midnight-shift at a warehouse while attending college, making concentration difficult in class. On days off, his sleep and energy were normal, and he maintained interest in socializing with friends.

He had no significant change in appetite or physical health, but academic performance declined, and he experienced frustration with financial and familial pressures. Mr. Sims also reported stress related to being a first-generation student on a predominantly white campus and experiencing racial discrimination.

He had no prior mental health care, occasional alcohol and marijuana use without impairment, and no legal history. On examination, Mr. Sims was punctual, cooperative, well-groomed, coherent, and articulate. Affect was constricted but appropriate; insight and judgment were good. He denied suicidality, homicidality, and psychosis.

Discussion: Mr. Sims exhibits depressive and anxious symptoms in response to an identifiable stressor (breakup), with associated impairment in social and academic functioning. Sleep and appetite are largely normal, and symptoms remit when routine allows rest. He does not meet criteria for major depressive disorder. His presentation is consistent with an adjustment disorder with depressed mood. Therapeutic support should consider cultural and gender-related factors, his resilience, and long-term goals.
