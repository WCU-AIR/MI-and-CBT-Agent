### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes client's own motivations, values, and resources throughout most of session. Strong open question: "What do you think would be some good reasons for you to avoid drinking?" Explores past success: "Can you tell me about a time when you managed to stick to your goals or resist pressure like this before?" Builds on client ideas rather than imposing solutions. Consistently reflects and affirms client's change talk statements, strengthening intrinsic motivation. Notable gap near end: when client introduces concern about peer pressure and friendships, clinician shifts to advice-giving rather than continuing to evoke client's own ideas. Stronger evocative response would have asked: "What do you think might help you stay connected to your friends while also protecting your goals?"

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exemplary skill in evoking and reinforcing change talk throughout session. Strategically elicits desire: "What do you think would be some good reasons for you to avoid drinking?" Client responds with multiple change talk statements: "I don't want to get addicted," "I could hurt my chances of getting a scholarship." Reinforces ability: "You showed a lot of self-control and confidence in that moment, which is impressive." Evokes commitment: "What do you think you might do next time?" Amplifies change talk: "An 8 shows you have strong confidence in your ability to use those prepared responses to stick to your goals. That's a great place to be." Uses 0-10 scales strategically to evoke and measure change talk. Builds on past success to strengthen confidence. No instances of reinforcing or amplifying sustain talk. Session progressively strengthens client's motivation and commitment to avoiding alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully handles client's sustain talk without confrontation or reinforcement. Uses double-sided reflection to address initial minimization: "It sounds like you're feeling a bit unsure about why we're focusing on drinking when it's only happened a couple of times...At the same time, you're thinking ahead about how this might impact your soccer and school goals." Reframes sustain talk by pivoting toward values rather than arguing. Normalizes ambivalence: "It sounds like you're in that space of figuring things out and weighing what fits best for you." Addresses peer pressure sustain talk by evoking past success in resisting it. Validates concern about friendships without dismissing it. No instances of arguing against sustain talk, confronting minimization, or reinforcing reasons for maintaining status quo. Consistently maintains collaborative MI spirit while gently pivoting toward change talk and values.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 4.3/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Exceptional cultivation of change talk with consistent strategic evocation and reinforcement
- Skillful handling of sustain talk through double-sided reflections and reframing rather than confrontation
- Strong direction and focus on alcohol use connected to client's core goals
- Solid reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking
- Consistent genuine affirmations tied to specific behaviors
- Collaborative agenda-setting with client input genuinely shaping direction
- Strong autonomy support emphasizing client ownership of change process

### Areas for Improvement:
- Shift toward advice-giving near end rather than continuing to evoke client's own ideas
- Limited exploration of deeper emotional complexity and internal conflict
- Insufficient exploration of ambivalence underlying 7/10 importance rating
- Limited barrier planning and relapse prevention discussion
- Could deepen reflections to capture more complex emotional nuance

### Clinical Impact:
This session is likely to increase client's intrinsic motivation for change. Change talk is consistently amplified, past successes are evoked, and autonomy and self-efficacy are supported. Client leaves with concrete action plan, high confidence (8/10), and clear connection between behavior and values. Collaborative stance and genuine affirmation strengthen therapeutic alliance. With refinement in maintaining evocative stance throughout and deepening exploration of ambivalence and barriers, clinician could move from good to excellent MI practice.
