## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

### Fundamental Problem
**Acute delirium is a medical emergency. CBT is contraindicated.** The patient presents with:
- Fluctuating consciousness and disorientation
- Perceptual disturbances (visual/auditory hallucinations)
- Paranoid ideation
- Cognitive dysfunction (MMSE 16/30)
- Impaired insight and judgment
- Symptoms secondary to polypharmacy (opioids, benzodiazepines, anticholinergic cyclobenzaprine)

**Appropriate intervention:** Medical stabilization, medication review, investigation of underlying causes, family psychoeducation, environmental support.

**Inappropriate intervention:** Psychotherapy, CBT, positive thinking, self-care advice.

### Therapist Competency Assessment
- **Delirium recognition:** Failed
- **Medical-psychiatric knowledge:** Inadequate
- **Safety assessment:** Failed
- **CBT technique application:** None
- **Clinical judgment:** Poor
- **Coordination with medical team:** None
- **Family involvement:** None

### Immediate Actions Required
1. **Refer patient for urgent medical evaluation** (emergency department or primary care)
2. **Conduct safety assessment** (guns + paranoia + impaired judgment = high risk)
3. **Coordinate with medical team and family**
4. **Discontinue psychotherapy** until delirium resolves and patient has capacity to engage
5. **Seek immediate supervision** for case review and retraining

### Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit
**Very low.** Delirium symptoms will not resolve with positive thinking or self-care. Only medical intervention will resolve underlying cause. This session delays appropriate care and may cause harm.
