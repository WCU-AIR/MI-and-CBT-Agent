## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Session Context:**
- Patient: Trevor, presenting with contamination obsessions (delusional beliefs about COVID-19 and germs) and compulsive washing/cleaning rituals
- Diagnosis: OCD with absent insight
- Severity: Significant functional impairment (unable to work, socially isolated, controlling parents' behavior)
- Prior treatment: Previous CBT helped him complete high school, suggesting capacity to benefit from proper treatment

**Critical Clinical Concerns:**

1. **Misalignment with Evidence-Based Practice:** Therapist's approach contradicts current OCD treatment guidelines. Positive thinking and distraction are contraindicated for OCD with absent insight.

2. **Risk of Harm:** If this approach continues, Trevor may conclude therapy "doesn't work" for his current symptoms, leading to treatment dropout and symptom worsening.

3. **Lack of Specialized Competence:** Therapist demonstrates no knowledge of OCD phenomenology, absent insight, or evidence-based interventions (ERP, cognitive restructuring for OCD).

4. **Failure to Respond to Patient Feedback:** Therapist ignored four explicit rejections of suggestions without modification—a fundamental violation of collaborative practice.

**Recommended Supervisor Actions:**

- Provide immediate feedback on session performance
- Require completion of OCD-specific training (workshop, consultation group, or formal certification)
- Assign monthly supervision focused on CBT technique and OCD interventions
- Request recording of next 3-5 sessions for review
- Consider temporary case reassignment if therapist cannot rapidly develop competence
- Provide resources: "Cognitive Therapy of Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder" (Salkovskis & Kirk), OCD consultation groups, ERP training

**Prognosis with Corrected Approach:**

With proper CBT structure, OCD-specific interventions (ERP, cognitive work), and collaborative empiricism, Trevor has good prognosis for symptom improvement. His prior response to CBT and his motivation to change ("I want to feel better") are positive prognostic indicators.
