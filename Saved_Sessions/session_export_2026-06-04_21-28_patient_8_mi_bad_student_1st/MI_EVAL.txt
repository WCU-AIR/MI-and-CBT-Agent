#### **Critical Deficiencies:**

This session represents a **systematic violation of Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates an approach that is **contraindicated for this client population** and likely to **harm the therapeutic relationship** and **reduce the likelihood of behavior change**.

**Core Problem:** The psychiatrist operates from a directive, expert-driven model in which the clinician decides what the client needs and demands compliance. This approach is fundamentally incompatible with MI and is particularly harmful for a client who has explicitly stated "I don't like being told what to do."

---

### Whether Session Embodies MI Spirit

**Assessment:** This session does **NOT** embody the MI spirit. It violates all four core elements:

- **Partnership:** Session is entirely expert-driven, not collaborative ✗
- **Acceptance:** Clinician demonstrates judgment and disapproval rather than unconditional positive regard ✗
- **Compassion:** Approach is controlling and pressuring rather than prioritizing client's welfare ✗
- **Evocation:** Clinician tells rather than asks; imposes motivation rather than evoking it ✗

---

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change

**Assessment:** **Very Low.** The psychiatrist's approach is likely to **decrease** Amina's intrinsic motivation and **increase her resistance**. By end of session, client has reasserted boundaries and provided minimal engagement ("Mm-hmm"), suggesting therapeutic relationship has been damaged rather than strengthened. Any behavior change that occurs is likely to be compliance-based and short-lived rather than reflecting genuine intrinsic motivation.

---

### Critical Recommendations for Improvement

**HIGHEST PRIORITY:**

1. **Adopt collaborative, partnership-based stance** – Replace directive, expert-driven approach with genuine MI partnership. Position yourself as collaborator rather than authority figure.

2. **Respond to client's explicit feedback about autonomy** – When client states "I don't like being told what to do," acknowledge this as crucial therapeutic information and adjust approach accordingly. This is not resistance to overcome; it is feedback about what will and won't work.

3. **Develop genuine reflective listening skills** – Learn to use reflections to demonstrate empathy and understanding. Aim for at least one reflection for every question asked.

4. **Ask open-ended questions to evoke client's own motivation** – Replace directive statements with genuine questions designed to draw out client's reasons for change, concerns, values, and ideas.

5. **Soften resistance through validation** – When client expresses sustain talk, validate underlying need and use double-sided reflections rather than confrontation.

**FOUNDATIONAL:**

6. **Seek formal training and supervision in Motivational Interviewing** – This session demonstrates fundamental departure from MI principles. Enroll in MI training program and seek regular supervision with MI-trained clinician.

---

### Clinical Recommendation

**This clinician requires immediate, intensive training and supervision in Motivational Interviewing before continuing to work with clients using this approach. The current style is contraindicated and likely to harm therapeutic outcomes.**
