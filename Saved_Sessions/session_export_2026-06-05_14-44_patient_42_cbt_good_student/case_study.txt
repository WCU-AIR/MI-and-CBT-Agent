--- DISORDER ---
Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder (Conversion Disorder), with attacks/seizures, persistent

--- CASE STUDY ---

Paulina Delcampo, a 32-year-old single woman with a history of epilepsy since adolescence, was admitted to an academic medical center after her family found her convulsing at home. Emergency medical services administered lorazepam, which did not stop the activity; a loading dose of fosphenytoin in the ER successfully terminated the episode. Blood tests revealed therapeutic antiepileptic levels, normal metabolic parameters, and negative toxicology. She was admitted for further monitoring.

During video-EEG (vEEG) monitoring, Ms. Delcampo exhibited multiple convulsive episodes, none associated with epileptiform activity. Her antiepileptic medications were tapered and discontinued. Psychiatric consultation was requested.

Ms. Delcampo denied prior psychiatric diagnoses or treatment and reported no mood, sleep, energy, concentration, or appetite disturbances. She denied suicidal or homicidal thoughts, and there was no history of mania, psychosis, or psychiatric illness in her family. She had recently moved to begin graduate school and expressed excitement about her studies and future. Her main concerns were the impact of hospitalization on classes and financial costs.

Upon learning that her vEEG findings suggested nonepileptic seizures, Ms. Delcampo became irritable, asking, "So, everyone thinks I'm just making this up?" She was not reassured by staff explanations and left the hospital against medical advice.

Discussion: Ms. Delcampo presents with persistent convulsive episodes not explained by neurological findings, causing significant distress and functional impairment. Her presentation fits DSM-5-TR criteria for functional neurological symptom disorder (conversion disorder), persistent, with the seizure subtype. While differential diagnoses include somatic symptom disorder, illness anxiety disorder, factitious disorder, or malingering, her history and examination support this diagnosis.
