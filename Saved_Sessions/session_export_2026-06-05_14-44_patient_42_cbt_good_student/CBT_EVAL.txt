## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths:
- **Excellent interpersonal effectiveness (6/6):** Created warm, safe, non-threatening environment; expertly navigated patient's diagnostic defensiveness without triggering resistance
- **Strong understanding/empathy (6/6):** Consistently accurate, nuanced, empathic reflection of patient's internal experience
- **Good feedback elicitation (5/6):** Regular checks for understanding and satisfaction throughout session
- **Skillful guided discovery (5/6):** Effective Socratic questioning that facilitated patient's own cognitive reframing
- **Collaborative agenda-setting (5/6):** Clear, specific, mutually agreed-upon session focus
- **Concrete homework (5/6):** Specific, achievable, collaboratively developed with barrier planning
- **Appropriate pacing (5/6):** Session flowed logically without tangents or rushing

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Failure to address core presenting problem (Item 8: 3/6):** Session focused on academic stress rather than seizure symptoms. Key seizure-related cognitions identified but not systematically explored or treated
- **Lack of FNSD-specific strategy (Item 9: 3/6):** No clear, coherent strategy for treating Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder. Missing psychoeducation, behavioral experiments, graded exposure, and explicit treatment rationale
- **Limited technique application (Item 10: 4/6):** Relied almost exclusively on cognitive restructuring and behavioral planning; missing essential FNSD techniques
- **Homework does not address primary problem (Item 11: 5/6):** Addresses secondary stressors but not seizure symptoms or testing beliefs about them

### Clinical Impact:
This session demonstrates competent general psychotherapy skills but falls significantly short of evidence-based CBT for FNSD. While patient reported feeling somewhat better (stress 8→5) and left with concrete action step, session did not address core mechanisms maintaining seizure symptoms. Without direct, systematic treatment of seizure-related cognitions, anxiety, and avoidance, seizure symptoms are unlikely to improve significantly. Risk of inadvertently reinforcing patient's defensiveness about diagnosis by not explicitly addressing her beliefs about symptoms.

### Immediate Recommendations:
1. **Develop FNSD-specific case formulation** explicitly addressing seizure-related catastrophic thoughts, anxiety, and avoidance cycle
2. **Provide psychoeducation about FNSD** emphasizing symptoms are real and responsive to psychological treatment
3. **Systematically explore seizure-related catastrophic thoughts** using guided discovery and evidence examination
4. **Design behavioral experiments and graded exposure** to test predictions and resume normal functioning
5. **Explicitly connect stress/anxiety to seizure symptoms** to build understanding of mind-body relationship
