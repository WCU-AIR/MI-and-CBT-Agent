# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates profound lack of acceptance throughout most of the session, using overtly judgmental language and character attacks ("If you really cared about your baby, you'd just quit drinking right now"). Shame-based interventions dominate early exchanges. Only the final two exchanges show minimal movement toward unconditional positive regard with statements like "I can see this is really tough for you." Sustained pattern of disapproval and contempt severely damages therapeutic relationship and violates MI spirit fundamentally.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal empathic capacity demonstrated throughout majority of session. Psychiatrist fails to reflect Sofia's emotional experience or recognize her genuine ambivalence between maternal responsibility and social identity. Dismisses fear and guilt rather than validating them ("Feeling guilty doesn't change anything"). Final two exchanges show emerging empathic reflection ("It sounds like you're feeling torn between wanting to protect your baby and the social pressures you face"), but these late attempts are insufficient and lack emotional depth. No recognition of competing values that legitimately matter to Sofia.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains strategic focus on target behavior (alcohol abstinence during pregnancy) but uses directive, prescriptive approach inconsistent with MI principles. Employs controlling language ("You have to quit now," "You must take this seriously") that leaves no room for client input or collaborative exploration. Introduces secondary directive (cutting off friends) without exploring necessity or feasibility. Only final exchange demonstrates MI-consistent direction with collaborative language ("Let's work together to find a way forward that feels manageable for you"), but this represents insufficient redemption of earlier authoritarian stance.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no collaboration demonstrated. Psychiatrist adopts expert, authoritarian stance throughout majority of session, using hierarchical language ("You have to," "You must," "You need to") that positions clinician as sole authority. Never asks Sofia what she thinks would help, what barriers she perceives, or what she has tried. Responds to Sofia's offered information with directives rather than curiosity. Only final exchange shows movement toward partnership ("Let's work together," "Can you tell me more?"), which is insufficient to demonstrate MI-consistent collaboration. Power dynamics remain fundamentally unequal.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively undermines autonomy throughout session using controlling, prescriptive language that removes choice from Sofia's hands. Employs threat-based pressure ("If you don't quit now, the consequences could be irreversible," "If you don't, you're only setting yourself up for failure") that violates autonomy support principles. Yes/no questions demanding immediate commitment ("Are you ready to commit to stopping alcohol completely?") leave no room for exploration of readiness or development of intrinsic motivation. Makes unilateral decisions without consultation ("You have to cut them off to protect your baby"). Only final exchange begins to support autonomy with word "manageable," but this single statement cannot offset sustained pattern of control and pressure.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal evocation demonstrated. Psychiatrist tells rather than asks, failing to draw out Sofia's own motivations, values, or ideas. Sofia offers substantial change talk ("I wanna be responsible for this thing," "I don't wanna let my kid down," "I think I'm gonna have to be successful for my kid"), but psychiatrist never amplifies or deepens this language. Responds to ambivalence with directives rather than evocative questions. Only final exchange demonstrates genuine evocative questioning ("Can you tell me more about what makes it hard to stop drinking? What support do you think would help you the most right now?"). This represents one substantive evocative exchange out of seven total, insufficient to demonstrate consistent MI practice.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no partnership stance demonstrated. Psychiatrist positions self as expert authority who knows what Sofia must do, with no acknowledgment that Sofia possesses valuable insights about her own situation. Dismisses Sofia's perspective as "excuses" rather than treating it as legitimate information about lived experience. Makes unilateral decisions ("You have to cut them off," "You should definitely do that") without collaborative input. Fails to recognize Sofia's expertise regarding her own barriers and solutions. Only final exchange demonstrates partnership language ("Let's work together," "Can you tell me more?"), which cannot redeem six exchanges of expert-driven, hierarchical interaction. Fundamental power imbalance undermines MI principles throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offers substantial change talk throughout session that psychiatrist fails to recognize, amplify, or strengthen. When Sofia expresses desire and commitment ("I wanna be responsible for this thing," "I don't wanna let my kid down"), psychiatrist responds with judgment and pressure rather than amplification. When Sofia states "I think I'm gonna have to be successful for my kid," psychiatrist adds external threat rather than deepening intrinsic motivation. Only final exchange creates space for Sofia to articulate own motivations ("Can you tell me more about what makes it hard to stop drinking?"), but psychiatrist does not subsequently amplify change talk or ask follow-up questions to deepen commitment. Significant missed opportunities to build intrinsic motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no skill in softening sustain talk, instead using direct confrontation that violates MI principles and likely increases defensiveness. Sofia's legitimate barriers and competing values ("Weekends are hard," "I can't just cut off my friends," "I'm not going to have another chance") are framed as character flaws and "excuses" rather than normal ambivalence requiring skillful exploration. Confrontational responses ("You're just making excuses," "You really need to stop justifying your behavior") escalate defensiveness rather than reducing it. No use of double-sided reflections to validate both change motivation and concerns about cost of change. Only final exchange shows movement away from confrontation ("It sounds like you're feeling torn between..."), but this single response cannot offset sustained pattern of confrontation and pressure that likely hardens Sofia's resistance.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competence Score: 1.3/5 – Poor**

### Key Findings:

**Fundamental Violations of MI Spirit:**
- Sustained use of shame, judgment, and confrontation throughout majority of session
- Authoritarian, expert-driven stance that violates collaborative MI principles
- Threat-based pressure tactics that undermine autonomy support
- Character attacks implying Sofia doesn't care about her baby ("If you really cared...")

**Missed Opportunities:**
- Sofia's substantial change talk never amplified or strengthened
- Genuine ambivalence never explored or validated through double-sided reflection
- Sofia's stated need ("I just need to figure out how to do this without feeling totally alone") never addressed collaboratively
- No collaborative problem-solving regarding actual barriers (weekend pressure, fear of isolation, social identity)

**Impact on Therapeutic Relationship:**
- Sofia enters with openness and change talk; exits with increased defensiveness
- Pattern consistent with research showing confrontation increases resistance
- Therapeutic alliance likely damaged by shame-based interventions
- Intrinsic motivation likely decreased rather than increased

**Positive Elements:**
- Maintains appropriate focus on target behavior (alcohol use during pregnancy)
- Underlying motivation to protect baby is clinically sound
- Final exchange demonstrates some MI awareness
- Allows client adequate space to speak

### Clinical Recommendation:

This session demonstrates **poor MI competence with fundamental violation of MI spirit**. While the psychiatrist shows awareness of MI principles in the final exchange, the overwhelming pattern is authoritarian and confrontational—directly contradicting MI principles and likely to decrease Sofia's intrinsic motivation for change. **Significant training, supervision, and deliberate practice are required** to develop genuine MI competence. Recommend formal MI certification program and regular clinical supervision focused on skill development.
