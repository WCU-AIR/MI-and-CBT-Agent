--- DISORDER ---
Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) with Intellectual Developmental Disorder (IDD) – associated with Kleefstra syndrome

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ashley, a 17-year-old girl, was referred for diagnostic reevaluation after having carried diagnoses of autism and intellectual disability for most of her life.
Recently, she was diagnosed with Kleefstra syndrome, and her family wanted to reconfirm her earlier diagnoses and assess genetic risk for her sisters.

At reevaluation, Ashley attended a special school with a focus on functional skills.
She could dress herself but could not shower independently or stay home alone.
She could read and spell at a second-grade level but understood little of what she read.
She became irritable with schedule changes or increased demands, often hurting herself (biting wrist) or others (pinching, hair pulling).

Testing results:
- Nonverbal IQ: 39
- Verbal IQ: 23
- Full Scale IQ: 31
- Adaptive score: 42 (average = 100)

Developmental history:
- Services began at 9 months for motor delays.
- Walked at 20 months. Toilet trained at 5 years.
- First word at 6 years.
- Diagnosed at age 3 with developmental delay, and at age 4 with autism, obesity, and static encephalopathy.
- Early genetic testing noted possible facial dysmorphology but was noncontributory.

Ashley knew hundreds of single words and simple phrases. She had a strong interest in license plates, drawing them from memory for hours. Her strongest skill was memory. She was affectionate toward family and babies but showed little interest in peers.

Family history:
- Father: dyslexia
- Paternal uncle: epilepsy
- Maternal cousin: possible Asperger’s
- Two sisters: in college, doing well

Examination:
- Overweight young woman, inconsistent eye contact, sometimes peered out of the corner of her eye.
- Smiled beautifully, sometimes laughed to herself, but mostly subdued expressions.
- Did not initiate joint attention, often ignored others.
- To request objects, rocked and pointed.
- Examined objects by sniffing or pressing them to lips.
- High-pitched, unusual intonation.
- Used short, somewhat rote but communicative phrases, e.g., “I want to clean up,” “Do you have a van?”

Recent changes:
- Increasing apathy in recent months.
- Diagnosed with Kleefstra syndrome following genetic testing after recurrent urinary tract infections.
- Parents tested negative, lowering risk for sisters.

Discussion:
Ashley meets criteria for both Intellectual Developmental Disorder (severe) and Autism Spectrum Disorder.
Her social and behavioral deficits exceed those expected from intellectual disability alone.
The ASD diagnosis guides treatment, emphasizing social skills, motivation, and structured environments, while also recognizing her cognitive strengths (memory, visual skills).
Kleefstra syndrome explains the underlying cause and highlights risks of medical comorbidities (e.g., heart, kidney).
Genetic findings suggest minimal risk for her sisters’ children.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---

- Rocking and pointing to request objects
- Sniffing/pressing objects to lips
- Self-injury (biting wrist), aggression (pinching, hair pulling)
- High-pitched, unusual intonation in speech
- Subdued facial expression, occasional inappropriate laughter
- Repetitive interest in license plates

--- ASD & IDD SYMPTOMS from DSM-5 ---
Autism Spectrum Disorder:
- Deficits in social communication (poor eye contact, minimal peer interest, lack of joint attention)
- Restricted, repetitive behaviors (license plate obsession, rocking, stereotyped speech)
- Symptoms present since early development
- Significant functional impairment
- Deficits beyond intellectual level

Intellectual Developmental Disorder:
- Low IQ (Full Scale IQ 31)
- Significant deficits in adaptive functioning (overall score 42)
- Impaired conceptual, social, and practical domains
