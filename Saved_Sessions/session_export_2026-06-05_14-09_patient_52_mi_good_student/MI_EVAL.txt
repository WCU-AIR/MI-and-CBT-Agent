### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

Could be strengthened by explicitly naming patient's right to choose depth of engagement and ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Minimal strategic evocation of patient's own motivations, values, ideas, or resources. Asks some open questions but does not develop them to elicit change talk or deeper exploration. Critical gaps: no exploration of what medication safety means to patient, no elicitation of her own ideas about managing the interaction, no exploration of existing resources or strategies, premature provision of information without first exploring her concerns. Session is clinician-directed rather than client-centered.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially demonstrated through agenda-setting and respect for preferences. However, psychiatrist frequently assumes expert role, particularly in unilateral delivery of medication interaction information without exploring patient's existing knowledge or co-constructing understanding. Limited incorporation of patient's contributions into problem-solving. Positions psychiatrist as clinical authority rather than inviting patient's reasoning about importance and risk. Partnership present but inconsistently applied.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significantly underdeveloped. Psychiatrist affirms some implicit change talk ("I definitely don't want to put myself at risk") but does not strategically evoke, develop, or amplify it. Missed opportunities: no exploration of desire for change, ability/confidence, or reasons/need for medication safety. No commitment language or follow-up questions strengthening stated plan to check with doctor. Minimal reinforcement when patient articulates her plan. Session is information-provision and affirmation rather than strategic change talk cultivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully manages patient's primary sustain talk (time constraint and competing priorities) without confrontation or pressure. Accepts boundary respectfully and reframes time pressure as evidence of efficiency and self-awareness. Provides information concisely, honoring stated preference. Avoids pushing for deeper exploration. Minor gap: could gently explore whether time is the only barrier using double-sided reflection, acknowledging both time pressure and underlying desire for safety.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.4/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Consistent acceptance, warmth, and unconditional positive regard
- Strong autonomy support and respect for patient boundaries
- Solid collaborative stance on agenda-setting and session structure
- Skillful management of sustain talk without confrontation
- Accurate, timely clinical information provided clearly and concisely
- Appropriate affirmations of patient's proactive health approach

### Key Areas for Improvement:
- **Minimal evocation:** Fails to strategically draw out patient's own motivations, values, and resources
- **Underdeveloped change talk cultivation:** Does not evoke, amplify, or reinforce change talk regarding medication safety or behavioral commitment
- **Limited partnership in information-sharing:** Delivers clinical information unilaterally without exploring patient's knowledge or preferences
- **Insufficient direction and planning:** Lacks specific behavioral goals, action steps, timeline, or follow-up plan
- **Surface-level empathy:** Accurate but lacks emotional depth; misses opportunities to explore underlying tensions
- **Missed opportunities for deeper exploration:** Does not probe existing medication management strategies, barriers, resources, or confidence in follow-through

### Clinical Impact:
While the psychiatrist provides necessary medication safety information and maintains a respectful, supportive stance, the session is primarily clinician-directed information provision rather than client-centered evocation of motivation. Patient leaves with accurate information and stated plan but without strategically evoked intrinsic motivation, explored confidence, or identified resources. For a time-constrained patient with competing priorities, lack of change talk cultivation may result in lower follow-through once session ends. Session is clinically sound but misses critical opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy—core mechanisms of sustained behavior change.

---

**Recommendation:** Develop focused training on strategic evocation, change talk amplification, and collaborative information-sharing to advance from adequate to exemplary MI practice.
