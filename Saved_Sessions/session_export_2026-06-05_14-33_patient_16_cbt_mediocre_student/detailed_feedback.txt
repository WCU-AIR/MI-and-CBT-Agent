
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## CRITICAL PRELIMINARY NOTE

**This session represents a fundamental failure in psychiatric care for a patient in acute psychiatric crisis.** The therapist is treating severe postpartum psychosis with psychotic features—a psychiatric emergency requiring immediate hospitalization and pharmacological intervention—as if it were mild anxiety amenable to cognitive restructuring and sleep hygiene. This is not a CBT competency issue; it is a diagnostic and safety failure that endangers both patient and infant.

**The patient presents with:**
- Postpartum psychosis (6 days postpartum)
- Fixed delusional beliefs (conviction she harmed/killed her infant)
- Severe sleep deprivation (inability to sleep due to racing thoughts)
- Pressured speech and flight of ideas
- Mood lability (euphoria alternating with tearfulness)

- High risk to self and child

**The appropriate intervention is psychiatric hospitalization, not outpatient CBT.**

### 1. Agenda
### **Score: 1/6**

**Explanation:**

The therapist opens with a vague, non-specific agenda: "talk a bit about anything that's been on your mind recently and see if we can figure out some ways to feel a bit better." This is repeated identically in the second session ("talk a bit about anything that's been bothering you lately and see if we can find some ways to feel a little better"), indicating no deliberate agenda-setting strategy.

For a patient presenting with postpartum psychosis, the agenda should have been: (1) **immediate safety assessment** (risk to self, risk to infant, command hallucinations, intent), (2) **psychiatric hospitalization discussion**, (3) **medication evaluation**, and (4) **coordination with obstetrics and pediatrics**. None of these occurred.

The therapist never established priorities, never discussed the severity of the presentation, never addressed the acute nature of the crisis, and never collaborated on realistic treatment goals. A proper agenda for this case would explicitly state: "Given what you're describing—the fixed beliefs about harming your baby, the complete sleep loss, and the racing thoughts—I'm concerned this needs immediate hospital-level care. Let's talk about getting you admitted today and getting you on medication that can help quickly."

Instead, the therapist drifted into a generic psychoeducational conversation about thought patterns and sleep hygiene—appropriate for mild postpartum anxiety, catastrophically inappropriate for postpartum psychosis.

**Summary:**

No meaningful agenda was established. The therapist failed to prioritize the acute psychiatric emergency, never discussed hospitalization, and proceeded with outpatient CBT techniques unsuitable for the clinical presentation.

**Summary:**

## Recommendations

### 1. URGENT: Recognize Postpartum Psychosis and Psychiatric Emergencies
- **Action:** Immediately refer this patient to the emergency department for psychiatric hospitalization. Do not proceed with outpatient CBT.
- **Rationale:** Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency requiring hospitalization, medication, and intensive monitoring. It is not amenable to outpatient psychotherapy alone. ### 2. Conduct Rapid Safety Assessment in All Psychiatric Presentations
- **Action:** In the first 5 minutes of any psychiatric evaluation, assess: (a) suicidal ideation and intent, (b) homicidal ideation and intent, (c) command hallucinations, (d) access to means, (e) current supervision/support.
- **Rationale:** Safety assessment determines the appropriate level of care. A patient with infanticidal ideation requires hospitalization; a patient with anxiety does not. Without safety assessment, clinical decisions are made blindly.
- **Example:** "I want to make sure you're safe. Have you had any thoughts of hurting yourself? Have you had any thoughts of hurting your baby? Are you hearing any voices? If you did have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, what would stop you? Who is with your baby right now?"
- **Practice:** Develop a standardized safety assessment protocol. Practice asking these questions directly and non-judgmentally. Learn to assess intent, plan, and access to means. Understand the difference between passive ideation ("I wish I were dead") and active ideation with intent and plan ("I'm going to kill myself tonight by overdose").

### 3. Assess Insight and Capacity to Consent to Treatment
- **Action:** Directly ask the patient: "Do you think you're ill?" "Do you think you need to be in a hospital?" "Are you willing to be admitted?" Assess whether the patient has capacity to make informed decisions about treatment.
- **Rationale:** Patients with psychosis often lack insight into their illness. A patient who denies being ill or refuses hospitalization may require involuntary commitment. Without assessing insight and capacity, the therapist cannot determine whether voluntary or involuntary hospitalization is appropriate.
- **Example:** "Do you think what you're experiencing is a medical condition that needs treatment? Do you think you need to be in a hospital right now? What are your concerns about hospitalization? - **Practice:** Learn the legal and ethical standards for capacity assessment. Understand the difference between voluntary and involuntary hospitalization. Practice having difficult conversations about hospitalization with patients who lack insight.

### 4. Distinguish Between Cognitive Distortions and Delusions
- **Action:** Learn to recognize the difference between anxious/depressive thoughts (which can be reality-tested and are amenable to cognitive restructuring) and delusions (which are fixed false beliefs resistant to logic and require antipsychotic medication).
- **Rationale:** Cognitive restructuring is ineffective for delusions and may be harmful. Applying CBT techniques to psychotic symptoms delays appropriate treatment.
- **Example:** 
  - **Cognitive distortion (anxiety):** "I might have made a mistake at work and my boss will fire me." (Can be reality-tested: "What evidence do I have? What's the likelihood? What would I do if it happened?")
  - **Delusion (psychosis):** "I have already killed my baby and the doctors are lying about her being alive." (Cannot be reality-tested; resistant to logic; requires antipsychotic medication)
- **Practice:** Study the DSM-5 criteria for delusions. Review case studies of patients with delusions vs. anxiety. Practice distinguishing between the two in supervision.

- **Action:** Learn that postpartum psychosis requires antipsychotic medication and mood stabilizers. Psychotherapy alone is insufficient. Coordinate with psychiatry for medication initiation.
- **Rationale:** Postpartum psychosis is a biological illness requiring biological treatment. Cognitive restructuring and behavioral interventions do not treat the underlying neurobiological dysfunction.
