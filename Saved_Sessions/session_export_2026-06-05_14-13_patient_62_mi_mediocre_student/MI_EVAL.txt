### Acceptance

**Comments:** Minimal unconditional positive regard demonstrated. ### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Severely limited perspective-taking and reflective listening. Psychiatrist responds to emotional content with factual questions rather than reflecting back emotional weight. Reflections are superficial and factual (e.g., "It sounds like your schedule and responsibilities make it tough") rather than emotionally attuned. Misses Jonathan's ambivalence, fear of failure, and sense of helplessness. Never captures the deeper emotional nuance or helps Jonathan articulate what's underneath the overwhelm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear target behavior (diabetes self-management) maintained throughout session, preventing aimless wandering. However, direction is pursued through directive means rather than collaborative exploration. Psychiatrist steers toward specific behavioral tasks without first understanding Jonathan's own goals, values, or readiness. Direction feels imposed rather than co-constructed. Limited responsiveness to Jonathan's skepticism about small steps approach.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no genuine collaboration present. Psychiatrist assumes expert role throughout, treating Jonathan as passive recipient of advice rather than equal partner. Consistent pattern: psychiatrist asks questions, Jonathan responds, psychiatrist offers solutions. Multiple examples of directive instruction without exploring Jonathan's ideas ("Maybe you should try setting reminders," "Maybe you should start with just one small thing"). No invitation for Jonathan's input or co-construction of approaches.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Critically deficient autonomy support. Psychiatrist repeatedly pressures Jonathan toward specific behaviors using language of obligation ("you should try," "maybe you should," "probably help"). Frames change as external obligation rather than Jonathan's choice. When Jonathan expresses doubt, psychiatrist reframes concern rather than validating his autonomy to make judgments. Final statement about Jonathan's choice contradicts entire session's message that psychiatrist knows what Jonathan should do.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost entirely absent. Psychiatrist consistently tells rather than asks, imposes solutions rather than evoking Jonathan's ideas. Uses leading questions ("Is it about feeling healthier, avoiding complications?") rather than genuine discovery. Misses repeated opportunities to explore Jonathan's resources (wife's support, past attempts, values). When Jonathan mentions past attempts ("I've tried stuff like that before"), psychiatrist dismisses without curiosity. No effort to draw out intrinsic motivations or help Jonathan articulate his own reasons for change.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Completely absent. Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role for entire interaction with no genuine power-sharing. Language ("maybe you should," "have you thought about") creates illusion of collaboration while actually prescribing what psychiatrist believes Jonathan should do. Never asks Jonathan about his expertise regarding his own life, work schedule, or family dynamics. Final acknowledgment of partnership ("you're the one who knows what works best for you") is superficial and contradicts entire session's expert-driven stance.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost entirely absent and actively suppressed by directive approach. Jonathan offers minimal change talk (weak ability talk: "I guess I could try checking it once a day"; weak commitment: "I can try setting a reminder"). Psychiatrist fails to amplify or reinforce any statements. Directive approach preempts Jonathan's self-discovery and opportunity to generate own reasons, express commitment, or articulate ability. No open-ended questions designed to evoke change talk about desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is present throughout (overwhelm, feeling buried, doubt about ability to keep up, past failures). Psychiatrist's handling is inadequate—uses minimal validation ("I hear you," "it's tough") followed by immediate redirection to solutions ("But maybe it would probably help if..."). This dismisses rather than genuinely softens resistance and may reinforce it. Never uses double-sided reflections, reframing, exploration, or normalization. Acknowledges resistance minimally then pivots to prescriptions.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.4/5 – POOR**

**MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Non-MI

**Key Strengths:**
- Maintains clear focus on target behavior (diabetes management)
- Recognizes and names Jonathan's overwhelm
- Offers concrete, potentially helpful practical strategies
- Token attempt at autonomy support in final statement

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Pervasive expert-driven stance fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit
- Minimal empathy and superficial reflective listening
- Absence of genuine evocation—tells rather than asks
- Suppression of change talk through directive approach
- Inadequate handling of sustain talk
- No genuine collaboration or power-sharing
- Missed opportunities to explore Jonathan's resources and past successes

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is likely to **decrease** Jonathan's intrinsic motivation and strengthen resistance. Jonathan's tentative agreement ("I guess we'll see how it goes") suggests compliance rather than genuine commitment. The psychiatrist has missed critical opportunity to help Jonathan move from pre-contemplation toward contemplation by exploring ambivalence, validating overwhelm, and evoking intrinsic motivation. Without significant approach changes, Jonathan is unlikely to sustain behavioral changes, as they will be motivated by external pressure rather than personal values.

**Fundamental Issue:** Psychiatrist is doing *to* Jonathan rather than doing *with* Jonathan. MI requires genuine shift in this dynamic.

---

## Priority Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from expert to collaborative stance** – Reframe role from authority who knows what Jonathan should do to collaborative partner helping Jonathan discover his own path
2. **Develop complex reflective listening** – Move beyond factual restatement to reflections capturing emotional nuance, ambivalence, and deeper meaning
3. **Evoke rather than impose** – Replace directive suggestions with genuine curiosity about Jonathan's ideas, past successes, values, and resources
4. **Amplify change talk** – When Jonathan expresses any desire, ability, reason, or commitment to change, pause and explore it to strengthen his own motivation
5. **Validate sustain talk** – Explore resistance with genuine curiosity using double-sided reflections rather than dismissing with solutions
6. **Explore resources** – Actively seek out and leverage Jonathan's existing resources (wife's support, past successes) rather than imposing new strategies
7. **Support autonomy genuinely** – Consistently communicate throughout session that Jonathan has right to choose and that change is his decision
8. **Develop collaborative language** – Replace "should," "probably," "maybe you should" with genuine questions positioning Jonathan as expert on his own life
