### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Operates almost entirely from expert stance throughout. Repeatedly positions psychiatrist as authority offering unilateral suggestions. Psychiatrist's contributions dominate; Jason's contributions are minimized and often dismissed. Absence of co-construction—solutions are built unilaterally and Jason is asked to adopt them (compliance, not partnership). When disagreement occurs, psychiatrist's view is presented as correct. Psychiatrist assumes responsibility for change process rather than supporting Jason's ownership. Minimal acknowledgment of Jason's expertise about his own life, values, and social context.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal skill in softening sustain talk. Jason produces substantial sustain talk ("I don't see drinking as a huge problem," "It's just part of college," "having one or two drinks sounds lame," "not worth messing up the vibe"), but psychiatrist does not use reflective listening to acknowledge and soften it. Instead, responds with leading questions and advice that can trigger defensiveness and strengthen sustain talk. Uses confrontational "but" statements that negate Jason's perspective rather than double-sided reflections that integrate both perspectives. Misses opportunities to explore the values underlying sustain talk (peer belonging, fun, social connection). One moment of partial softening when reflecting "drinking at parties is kind of a normal thing for you," but immediately undermined by leading question.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.4/5**

**Key Strengths:**
- Warm opening tone establishes basic rapport
- Maintains general focus on drinking and parties
- One autonomy-supporting statement near session end
- Willingness to engage Jason in conversation

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Fundamental departure from MI spirit:** Expert-driven problem-solving and advice-giving replace collaborative exploration and evocation
- **Minimal evocation:** Very few open questions designed to draw out Jason's own motivations, values, and ideas
- **Poor handling of sustain talk:** Jason's arguments for status quo met with confrontation and advice rather than reflective listening
- **Lack of genuine collaboration:** Operates almost entirely from expert stance; unilateral suggestions; dismisses Jason's perspective
- **Insufficient empathy:** Minimal effort to understand Jason's internal experience, values, and social context from his perspective
- **Premature advice-giving:** Solutions offered before adequate exploration and before discrepancy established
- **Failure to build discrepancy:** Does not help Jason recognize gap between current behavior and his values or goals

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is unlikely to increase Jason's intrinsic motivation for change and may trigger defensiveness. Jason produces minimal change talk and only tentatively agrees to "try" limits with weak commitment ("No promises, though. We'll see how it goes"). Underlying ambivalence remains unresolved. Directive, expert-driven approach may damage therapeutic alliance and reduce Jason's willingness to engage in future sessions.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**
1. Adopt genuinely collaborative stance; invite Jason's perspective and build agenda together
2. Develop complex empathic reflections that capture ambivalence and internal conflict
3. Ask open questions to evoke Jason's own motivations, values, concerns, and ideas
4. Strategically evoke and reinforce change talk through follow-up questions and reflections
5. Soften sustain talk through reflective listening and double-sided reflections (avoid "but" statements)
6. Explore Jason's values and social context as important data, not obstacles
7. Delay advice-giving until Jason has explored his perspective and expressed readiness
8. Build discrepancy between current behavior and Jason's values/goals through strategic questioning
9. Use scaling questions strategically; always follow up with "What would help move that number up?"
10. Establish clear MI competency goals and seek regular supervision with MI trainer; focus on evocation, collaboration, and softening sustain talk
