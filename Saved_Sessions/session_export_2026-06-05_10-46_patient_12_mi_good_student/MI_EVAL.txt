### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent evocation of client's motivations and resources. Asks some open-ended questions that allow client to articulate reasons for change. Invites exploration of past success effectively. However, significant missed opportunities: does not ask evocative questions before offering suggestions; does not deeply explore or amplify change talk about mood, energy, and confidence; does not ask follow-up questions after confidence scaling ("What would it take to move from 8 to 9?"); does not systematically elicit different types of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment).

### Partnership

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent cultivation of change talk. Client enters with strong spontaneous change talk about mood, energy, and confidence; clinician reflects accurately but does not amplify through strategic follow-up questions. When client rates confidence at 8/10, psychiatrist does not ask critical follow-ups ("What would it take to move to 9?"). Does not systematically explore different types of change talk. Misses opportunities to deepen commitment through questions about specific benefits and past successes. Confidence scaling used appropriately but not leveraged for deeper exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk

Skillfully reframes past difficulty with consistency as wisdom supporting current plan. Normalizes concern about guilt if missing days and connects it to sustainable change. Does not reinforce ambivalence or hesitation. Minor limitation: does not proactively explore potential barriers early in session ("What might get in the way?"). Could address obstacles more thoroughly before they emerge as problems.

## Summary of Clinical Feedback

### **Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative partnership with explicit agenda-setting and permission-seeking
- Consistent warm acceptance and genuine, specific affirmations of client self-awareness
- Accurate reflective listening capturing both content and strategic thinking
- Respectful autonomy support and clear messaging that decisions are client's to make
- Skillful reframing of barriers and past struggles as wisdom rather than resistance
- Maintains clear focus on stated goal with logical progression to concrete planning

### **Areas for Improvement:**
- Inconsistent evocation: Does not systematically ask "What do you think?" before offering suggestions
- Limited amplification of change talk: Reflects client motivation but does not ask follow-up questions deepening commitment
- Insufficient confidence scaling follow-up: Does not ask "What would it take to move from 8 to 9?" or "What's already in place?"
- Proactive barrier exploration: Does not ask early about potential obstacles or concerns
- Limited emotional depth: Does not consistently explore underlying emotions, vulnerabilities, or perfectionism
- Suggestion timing: Offers specific ideas relatively quickly before fully exploring client's own ideas

### **Impact on Outcomes:**
Session successfully engages client and establishes solid foundation for behavior change (confidence rating 8/10, concrete plan articulated). However, gaps in evocation and change talk cultivation represent missed opportunity to deepen intrinsic motivation and strengthen commitment. Research demonstrates that more client-generated change talk correlates with better follow-through on behavior change. By not asking strategic follow-up questions, clinician may have left motivation on the table. Proactive barrier exploration would have better prepared client for obstacles. Collaborative stance and autonomy support are excellent foundations; deeper evocation would amplify likelihood of sustained behavior change.
