
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## CRITICAL PRELIMINARY NOTE

**This evaluation reveals a fundamentally inappropriate therapeutic approach for the patient's clinical presentation.** Wesley Brown presents with **acute delirium**—a medical emergency characterized by fluctuating consciousness, disorientation, perceptual disturbances, and cognitive dysfunction secondary to physiological causes (medication toxicity, possible infection, metabolic derangement). 

**CBT is contraindicated in acute delirium.** The appropriate intervention is urgent medical evaluation, medication review, and treatment of underlying medical causes—not psychotherapy. The psychiatrist's repeated deployment of generic self-help advice, positive thinking, and lifestyle modifications while failing to recognize or address the medical emergency represents a serious clinical failure that endangers patient safety.

This evaluation proceeds with the understanding that **no CBT score can be "good" when the fundamental clinical decision—to attempt psychotherapy rather than medical intervention—is wrong.**

## 1. Agenda

### **Score: 0/6**

**Explanation:**

No agenda was established at any point in this session. The psychiatrist never collaboratively identified session goals, prioritized problems, or established a structured plan for the encounter. 

In the opening exchange, the psychiatrist asks a generic social question ("How's your day been so far?") without transitioning to any therapeutic agenda. The patient immediately describes acute cognitive dysfunction, perceptual disturbances, and disorientation—red flags for delirium—yet the psychiatrist never says, "Let's focus on understanding what's happening with you" or "Here's what I want to accomplish today."

Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly cycles through the same generic interventions (positive thinking, hydration, sleep, routine, family communication) without ever establishing why these are being suggested or how they connect to a coherent treatment plan. There is no prioritization of problems, no time allocation, and no structured session framework.

Most critically, the psychiatrist fails to establish an agenda around the **medical emergency** that should be obvious: acute onset confusion, visual/auditory hallucinations, paranoia, disorientation, fluctuating consciousness, dry mouth, mydriasis, tachycardia (implied by patient's dizziness), and urinary retention. These are delirium symptoms requiring immediate medical workup, not CBT agenda items.

The repeated cycling through identical advice (breathing, positive thinking, hydration, sleep) across multiple exchanges suggests the psychiatrist has no coherent plan and is simply offering generic reassurance without diagnostic clarity or treatment strategy.

**Summary:**

No collaborative agenda was established. The psychiatrist failed to identify session goals, prioritize the acute medical emergency, or create any structured therapeutic framework. This represents a fundamental failure in basic session structure.
