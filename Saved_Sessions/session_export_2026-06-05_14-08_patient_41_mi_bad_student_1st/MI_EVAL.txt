## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Summary of Findings:

This session represents a **severe departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly every domain. The psychiatrist operates from a fundamentally incompatible expert-driven, authoritarian, confrontational model that violates core MI principles.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance and empathy; patient is judged and shamed rather than respected
- Active suppression of autonomy through coercion and control
- Failure to evoke change talk; reasons for change are imposed rather than drawn out
- Confrontational handling of ambivalence, likely to amplify rather than soften resistance
- Authoritarian partnership; clinician assumes total expert authority and dismisses patient self-knowledge
- Repeated cycling through same closed questions with no adaptation based on patient responses

**Strengths (Limited):**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behaviors (smoking and drinking)
- Avoids most harmful techniques such as mockery or ridicule
- Clearly communicates health risks (though in non-MI manner)

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is **highly likely to harm** patient motivation and therapeutic engagement. Research demonstrates that confrontation increases resistance, shame reduces change, and autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation. Patient's final response ("I need a bit more time to get to that point") suggests withdrawal and entrenchment rather than movement toward change—the predictable outcome of this approach.

**Recommendation:**

This clinician requires **comprehensive formal MI training and ongoing supervision** before continuing to work with patients on behavior change. Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based practice and likely to harm patient outcomes. Immediate priority areas: (1) fundamental philosophical shift from expert-driven to collaborative, (2) development of reflective listening skills, (3) elimination of confrontational language, (4) learning to evoke rather than impose motivation, (5) enrollment in MINT-certified MI training program.
