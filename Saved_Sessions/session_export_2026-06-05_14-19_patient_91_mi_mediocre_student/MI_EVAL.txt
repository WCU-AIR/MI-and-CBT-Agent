## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.7/5**

**Clinical Summary:**

This session demonstrates **poor MI practice** with significant deficiencies across nearly all competencies. The psychiatrist operates from an expert-prescriber stance rather than a collaborative partnership, uses pervasive directive language, and fails to evoke the patient's own motivations and values. While the clinician demonstrates basic listening and maintains focus on asthma management, the approach is fundamentally inconsistent with MI spirit and is unlikely to build intrinsic motivation for change.

**Key Strengths:**
- Basic structure and organization; maintains clear focus on asthma management
- Acknowledgment of patient's statements demonstrates basic listening
- Some flexibility when patient expresses burden (accepts walks over breathing exercises)
- Clinically accurate acknowledgment that stress worsens asthma

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Pervasive directive stance** (8+ "should" statements) undermines autonomy, collaboration, and partnership
2. **Minimal evocation** of patient's motivations, values, and ideas; tells rather than draws out
3. **Failure to explore ambivalence** regarding competing values (health vs. dog attachment, desire for change vs. overwhelm)
4. **Inadequate empathy** lacking emotional depth and complex reflections
5. **Lack of genuine collaboration** with unilateral decision-making and plan development
6. **Insufficient autonomy support** despite patient's expressed low confidence
7. **Missed opportunities** to understand patient's values and what matters most
8. **Minimal change talk cultivation** and sustain talk softening

**Impact on Patient Engagement and Change:**

This session is likely to have a **negative impact on patient motivation and engagement**. While patient initially expresses some willingness, the directive, expert-driven approach undermines conditions necessary for sustained intrinsic motivation. Specific concerns: reduced autonomy from "should" statements; overwhelm reinforced by multiple prescribed interventions; values not honored regarding dog attachment; ambivalence not resolved; low confidence not addressed; external motivation (compliance) likely rather than intrinsic motivation (own commitment).

**Likelihood of sustained change:** Low. Patient may attempt some interventions (walks, medication adherence) but external motivation is less likely to be sustained when facing obstacles or competing demands.

## Recommendations for Clinician Development

**Priority 1: Eliminate Directive Language**
- Replace all "should" statements with collaborative exploration
- Record practice sessions and count directive language instances; aim for zero
- Example: Instead of "Maybe you should try to find ways to reduce stress," ask "What do you think might help with the stress you're experiencing?"

**Priority 2: Develop Genuine Evocative Skills**
- Use strategic questions to draw out patient's own reasons for change, values, and ideas
- Before offering advice, ask: "What could I ask to help this patient think this through themselves?"
- Develop repertoire of evocative questions for different situations

**Priority 3: Explore and Honor Patient Values**
- Recognize dog represents stability and family to patient
- Explore what dog means rather than prescribing compromise
- Help patient think through balancing competing values rather than imposing solutions

**Priority 4: Establish True Partnership**
- Genuinely share power in decision-making
- Involve patient in deciding what to focus on and how to structure plan
- Use collaborative summarization: "Of these options, what feels most doable to you?"

**Priority 5: Develop Complex Empathic Reflections**
- Move beyond simple reflections that restate content
- Capture emotional meaning, underlying values, and both sides of ambivalence
- Example: "So there's a part of you that really wants to feel better, and at the same time, you're already stretched so thin that adding more feels impossible."

**Priority 6: Respect Low Confidence as Important Information**
- When patient expresses low confidence, explore what this reveals about readiness
- Ask: "What would need to be different for you to feel more confident?"
- Do not prescribe interventions despite low confidence

**Priority 7: Amplify and Reinforce Change Talk**
- Recognize instances of patient's change talk (desire, ability, reasons, commitment)
- Ask questions that deepen patient's own language in favor of change
- Example: When patient says "I want to get better," ask "Tell me more about that. What would getting better mean for you?"

**Priority 8: Use Double-Sided Reflections for Ambivalence**
- Acknowledge both sides of patient's conflict
- Example: "On one hand you want to feel better, and on the other hand you're already overwhelmed. That's a tough spot."

**Recommended Next Steps:**
- Engage in focused MI training with emphasis on evocation, collaboration, and autonomy support
- Record and review sessions with MI trainer to identify patterns of directive language
- Practice asking rather than telling; develop skill in genuine power-sharing
- Study MI spirit: evoking rather than installing motivation; supporting patient's autonomy and values
