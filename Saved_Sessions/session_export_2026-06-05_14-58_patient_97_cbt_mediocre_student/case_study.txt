--- DISORDER ---
Steroid-Induced Bipolar and Related Disorder, with Mania, Severe, with Mood-Congruent Psychotic Features and Catatonic Features

--- CASE STUDY ---

Taaj Mustafa, a 22-year-old recent college graduate, was brought to the ER by friends after disappearing for 3 days and appearing bizarrely silent. He had recently undergone treatment for testicular cancer, including chemotherapy with cisplatin, etoposide, and dexamethasone. In the ER, he demonstrated mutism, stupor, bizarre posturing with waxy flexibility, and stereotypic movements. Initial labs showed elevated CPK and mild tachycardia. Neurological workup, including head CT, MRI, EEG, and toxicology, was unremarkable.

Mr. Mustafa had a history of depression following his cancer diagnosis 1 year prior and was taking sertraline 50 mg/day. Family history included a maternal uncle with severe bipolar disorder treated with ECT. After administration of intravenous lorazepam, his rigidity resolved, speech became fluent and pressured, and manic behaviors emerged, including hyperactivity, grandiosity, and elated mood.

Discussion: Mr. Mustafa presents with catatonia, evidenced by mutism, stupor, waxy flexibility, and stereotypy. Catatonia can occur in the context of mood disorders, medical conditions, or substance/medication effects. In this case, the manic episode appears triggered by steroid chemotherapy. The patient’s severe mania emerged after catatonia resolved with lorazepam, consistent with steroid-induced bipolar and related disorder. Early recognition and benzodiazepine treatment were critical to prevent morbidity and clarify the underlying psychiatric condition.
