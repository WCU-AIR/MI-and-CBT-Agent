--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar I Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Nancy Ingram was a 33-year-old married African American woman and stock analyst brought to the emergency room by her husband after 10 days of a “cycle of depression,” marked by irritability, tearfulness, and nearly no sleep. Her husband reported that she had experienced at least six similar episodes in the past year, typically improving after restarting fluoxetine. He noted that she often increased her use of alcohol and clonazepam during these episodes.

During this presentation, Ms. Ingram had created a personal blog titled Nancy Ingram's Best Stock Picks, working around the clock, neglecting meals, work, and childcare responsibilities. She insisted she was fine and that the blog would “make them as rich as Croesus.”

Her psychiatric history included a diagnosis of depression in college following her father’s suicide and intermittent psychotherapy. Outpatient psychiatrists had documented persistent depressive symptoms but had limited awareness of her hypomanic/manic periods, as she often refused evaluation during these episodes. Family history included her father’s erratic behavior and alcohol abuse, and a grandmother with unspecified “nervous breakdowns.”

On examination, Ms. Ingram paced angrily, had glazed and unfocused eyes, rapid and pressured speech, poor insight, and impaired judgment. She denied hallucinations but claimed a unique ability to predict the stock market. She refused cognitive testing and appeared dismissive and mocking. These behaviors, combined with diminished need for sleep without fatigue, distractibility, and poor judgment, indicated a manic episode with mixed features and rapid cycling.
