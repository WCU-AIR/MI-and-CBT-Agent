## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Critical Findings:

This session represents a **fundamental departure from motivational interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist operates from a directive, expert-driven, authoritarian model that is antithetical to MI across all nine competency domains.

**Core Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of acceptance and empathy
- Elimination of patient autonomy through prescriptive language and fear-based coercion
- Failure to evoke intrinsic motivation; reliance on external pressure
- Rejection of collaboration and patient input
- Counterproductive approach that increases patient resistance

**Impact on Therapeutic Engagement:**
Patient enters with openness and willingness ("Hi. Thank you"). By session end, patient expresses clear resistance, feeling unheard, and explicitly states psychiatrist's approach is not working ("the way you're pushing makes me feel kind of trapped"). Patient's change talk is minimal and unamplifed; sustain talk increases progressively.

**Developmental Considerations:**
Patient appears to be adolescent (references to school). Directive, autonomy-suppressing approach is particularly contraindicated for this developmental stage and likely to damage future engagement in health interventions.

**Research Alignment:**
Session demonstrates principle consistently supported by behavior change research: directive, confrontational approaches increase resistance and decrease intrinsic motivation. This session is a clear clinical example of this evidence.

### Recommendations for Immediate Action:

1. **Foundational Retraining:** Enroll in formal MI training (MINT-certified workshop or equivalent)
2. **Supervision:** Engage in ongoing MI-focused clinical supervision with MI-trained supervisor
3. **Self-Reflection:** Examine underlying beliefs about patient motivation and change; shift from "expert who directs" to "collaborative partner who evokes"
4. **Skill Development:** Practice empathic listening, reflective responding, open-ended questioning, and change talk amplification
5. **Session Recording Review:** Record and review sessions with supervisor; identify prescriptive language and missed opportunities for collaboration

**Current MI Practice Status:** This clinician is **not practicing motivational interviewing**. Session reflects traditional directive medical model contraindicated for behavior change work. **Immediate comprehensive retraining and supervision required before continuing MI-based interventions.**

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Client Motivation:** **Very Low.** Without significant changes to clinician approach, this patient is unlikely to engage in sustained behavior change. Current approach likely to decrease intrinsic motivation and increase resistance.
