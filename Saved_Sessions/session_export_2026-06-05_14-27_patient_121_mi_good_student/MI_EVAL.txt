# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Effectively affirms Sofia's ambivalence without criticism ("carrying a lot of responsibility and concern...at the same time, there's a part of you that really values your social life"). However, misses opportunities to explicitly normalize guilt about early pregnancy drinking and affirm Sofia's identity beyond motherhood. Acceptance is solid but could deepen affirmation of inherent worth separate from role as mother or ability to change.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking. Captures Sofia's core conflict effectively ("standing at a crossroads, wanting to protect your baby but also not wanting to miss out"). Reflects understanding of specific fears about social exclusion and ambivalence. However, misses deeper emotional exploration when Sofia discloses fear about already harming baby—focuses on information rather than emotional weight. Does not fully explore identity conflict or grief about losing sense of self. Empathy is good but could be more emotionally attuned.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on target behavior (alcohol use during pregnancy) throughout session. Opens with client-centered question, progresses logically through exploration of ambivalence, motivation, confidence, past successes, and concrete next steps. Uses strategic questions effectively ("What would be the biggest reason or motivation for you to make changes?"). However, does not deeply explore specific barriers to change (weekends, peer pressure) or develop detailed, situation-specific coping plans. Direction is clear but could be more strategic in barrier management.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial collaboration with genuine power-sharing in opening (inviting Sofia to set agenda) and permission-seeking before psychoeducation. However, collaboration breaks down significantly during information delivery—presents research findings in expert-driven, one-directional manner without interspersing Sofia's thoughts. Does not truly co-construct solutions; affirms Sofia's ideas rather than collaborating on implementation. Does not invite Sofia to identify barriers or solutions before offering suggestions. Collaboration is present but inconsistent; clinician frequently shifts to expert/validator role.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally supports Sofia's autonomy through open-ended questions and permission-seeking. Opening question explicitly invites Sofia to direct session. Affirms her agency in identifying solutions ("brave and thoughtful first step"). However, psychoeducation framing is subtly directive ("The good news is that stopping...can make a big difference"), presenting abstinence as obvious choice rather than inviting Sofia's consideration. Does not explicitly affirm her right to make own choice, even if different from clinician's recommendation. Autonomy support is solid but could more consistently emphasize Sofia's right to choose.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation through open-ended questions ("What do you think would be the most important thing to focus on?") and exploration of past successes (quitting smoking). However, significantly underutilizes evocative techniques. Does not deeply explore Sofia's own reasons for change before providing information. Does not evoke deeper values and meaning ("What does being a good mother mean to you?"). Does not explore what Sofia truly values about social life beyond "not losing friends." Relies more on affirmation and information-giving than evoking intrinsic motivation. Evocation is present but underdeveloped and not strategically deployed.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial partnership through collaborative opening and permission-seeking, but frequently assumes expert role that undermines true power-sharing. Psychoeducation delivery is expert-driven without inviting Sofia's perspective or acknowledging her existing knowledge. Does not co-create change plan; affirms Sofia's ideas rather than collaborating on implementation. Does not invite Sofia to identify barriers or solutions first. Maintains clinician in expert/validator role rather than genuine equal partnership. Language like "You're really taking thoughtful steps" affirms but does not invite genuine partnership. True power-sharing is limited and inconsistent.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic affirmation of Sofia's change talk ("deep and heartfelt commitment to your baby's well-being") but does not strategically cultivate, amplify, or deepen it. Misses multiple opportunities to use targeted questioning to evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment. When Sofia produces strong change talk ("I wanna be responsible...I don't wanna let my kid down"), clinician affirms but does not explore further ("Tell me more about that"). Does not use evocative questions systematically ("What would be different if you stopped drinking?" "What strengths will help you?"). Does not reinforce change talk when Sofia identifies first step. Change talk cultivation is present but underdeveloped and not strategically deployed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic acknowledgment of Sofia's sustain talk but does not strategically soften it using MI-consistent techniques. Sofia's sustain talk ("Weekends are hard...I can't just cut off my friends altogether," "I'm worried I've already put the baby at risk") is acknowledged as part of ambivalence but not directly addressed. Does not use double-sided reflections that emphasize change side. Does not reframe perceived losses or catastrophic thinking. Does not explore whether concerns are realistic or use strategic questioning to redirect toward agency. Sustain talk persists largely unaddressed, potentially undermining motivation during difficult moments. Softening sustain talk is underdeveloped.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 3.4/5 – Adequate MI Competence with Notable Room for Improvement**

**Strengths:**
- Strong opening with genuine collaborative agenda-setting
- Consistent nonjudgmental, warm stance throughout
- Accurate reflective listening capturing Sofia's core ambivalence
- Appropriate permission-seeking before psychoeducation
- Effective exploration of past successes (quitting smoking)
- Sofia's confidence increases from 4-5 to 7 during session
- Identifies concrete next steps

**Critical Gaps:**
- Insufficient strategic evocation of intrinsic motivation through targeted questioning
- Inadequate amplification and deepening of change talk
- Sustain talk acknowledged but not strategically addressed using MI techniques
- Expert-driven rather than collaborative psychoeducation delivery
- Limited exploration of specific barriers and situation-specific coping plans
- Affirmation-focused rather than co-constructive problem-solving
- Missed opportunities for deeper emotional exploration of fear and grief

**Impact on Sustained Change:**
Sofia demonstrates increased confidence and identifies manageable first step (telling friends), suggesting short-term follow-through is likely. However, longer-term adherence to abstinence may be vulnerable to sustain talk and social pressure, particularly weekend situations and peer influence, because psychiatrist did not strategically address these barriers or deeply strengthen commitment language. Session embodies MI spirit but does not fully leverage MI's most powerful techniques for evoking and strengthening intrinsic motivation.

**Primary Recommendations:**
1. Strategically evoke and amplify Sofia's change talk through targeted questioning about values, reasons, and commitment
2. Strategically soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections and reframing
3. Deepen collaborative problem-solving by inviting Sofia's perspective first
4. Develop situation-specific coping plans for identified barriers (weekends, peer pressure)
5. Use collaborative rather than expert-driven psychoeducation
6. Explicitly affirm Sofia's autonomy and right to choose throughout session
7. Deepen emotional exploration when Sofia expresses strong emotions
8. Strengthen confidence-building through detailed exploration of past successes and transferable skills
