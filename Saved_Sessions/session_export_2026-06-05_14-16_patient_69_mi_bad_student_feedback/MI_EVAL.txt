# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist employs judgmental language including "weak-willed," which is contemptuous and shaming. Kevin's ambivalence is framed as character defect rather than normal human experience. No warmth, genuine support, or affirmation of Kevin's worth. Repeated ultimatums communicate that Kevin's autonomy and perspective are irrelevant. Fundamentally incompatible with MI spirit.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains virtually no reflective listening or genuine perspective-taking. Psychiatrist talks *at* Kevin rather than *with* him. Kevin's stated reasons for drinking (stress management, social connection) are never acknowledged or validated. No reflections of Kevin's emotional experience (embarrassment, anxiety, fear of isolation). Psychiatrist reframes Kevin's statements as "rationalizations" and "excuses"—the opposite of empathic understanding. Zero attempt to understand Kevin's internal world.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Target behavior (alcohol reduction) remains in consistent focus, which is appropriate. However, direction is maintained through coercion and directive authority rather than collaborative exploration. Psychiatrist imposes unilateral goal of complete abstinence without exploring Kevin's readiness, concerns, or preferences. Never asks whether abstinence is Kevin's goal or what intermediate steps might be realistic. Achieves focus through authoritarian control rather than partnership.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero collaboration demonstrated. Psychiatrist assumes unilateral expert role and explicitly rejects Kevin's input at every turn. Kevin requests collaboration ("Can we work with that?") and is refused ("There's no middle ground here"). Authoritarian language throughout: "You have to," "You need to," "That's what you have to do." Kevin's expertise about his own life, stress management needs, and social connections is completely disregarded. No genuine partnership or shared decision-making.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively undermines autonomy through binary ultimatums ("Yes or no?") while communicating that any answer other than complete abstinence is unacceptable. Explicitly denies choice: "There's no middle ground here." Uses blame as manipulation: "If you don't quit now, you're choosing to let this injury get worse. That's on you." Kevin's honest self-assessment of readiness is met with blame rather than respect. No genuine choice offered; coercive control disguised as clinical concern.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains zero evocation. Psychiatrist tells Kevin what to do rather than asking what Kevin thinks or wants. Kevin provides spontaneous change talk ("I want to get better," "I want my ankle to heal," "I'm willing to consider cutting back") which is completely ignored or dismissed. Never asks about Kevin's concerns, values, past successes, or readiness. Imposes external reasons for change without exploring Kevin's intrinsic motivation. Kevin's self-awareness and willingness are treated as weaknesses rather than resources.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates opposite of partnership. Psychiatrist assumes total expertise and communicates that Kevin's self-knowledge is irrelevant. Dismisses Kevin's accurate self-assessment ("Quitting completely feels impossible") as lack of willpower. Pathologizes Kevin's insight ("You're just rationalizing"). Imposes goal of complete abstinence without collaborative exploration. Never conveys that Kevin possesses valuable knowledge about himself. Operates as sole expert; Kevin's perspective is irrelevant to treatment planning.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Kevin provides multiple instances of spontaneous change talk (regret, embarrassment, acknowledgment of harm, recognition of need, commitment to consider cutting back, desire to heal) that should be amplified and reinforced. Psychiatrist ignores all of it. Instead, dismisses Kevin's willingness to cut back as insufficient and demands complete abstinence. This suppresses rather than cultivates change talk. By end of session, Kevin is more defensive and entrenched. Psychiatrist's pressure actually *decreases* Kevin's stated commitment and motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist does attempt to address Kevin's sustain talk (drinking helps relax, maintains friendships, quitting feels impossible) but uses direct confrontation rather than skillful MI techniques. Argues that Kevin is "rationalizing" and "avoiding responsibility" rather than using double-sided reflections or reframing. Direct confrontation typically *strengthens* sustain talk and increases defensiveness. Kevin becomes increasingly entrenched in his position. No use of strategic questioning, validation of both sides of ambivalence, or collaborative exploration of concerns.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol reduction)
- Correctly identifies concerning drinking level (10-15 drinks/week)
- Recognizes ankle injury as teachable moment

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Absence of acceptance and empathy; uses shaming language ("weak-willed")
- Coercive rather than collaborative; imposes goals without exploration
- Suppresses rather than cultivates change talk
- Confrontational approach increases defensiveness and entrenchment
- Fundamental violation of MI spirit: control and judgment rather than partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is **counterproductive**. Kevin enters with ambivalence and some willingness to consider change. He exits more defensive and explicitly refuses the psychiatrist's demand. Research demonstrates confrontational, directive approaches with ambivalent clients typically increase resistance, decrease intrinsic motivation, damage therapeutic relationship, and reduce likelihood of behavior change. This session exemplifies these negative outcomes.

**Recommendation:**
**HIGHEST PRIORITY:** Fundamental MI training and attitude shift from expert-prescriber to collaborative partner. Eliminate judgmental language. Develop genuine reflective listening. Respect client autonomy and readiness. Seek ongoing MI supervision with recorded session review. Current approach, while clinically well-intentioned, is psychologically counterproductive and requires comprehensive retraining in MI principles and spirit.
