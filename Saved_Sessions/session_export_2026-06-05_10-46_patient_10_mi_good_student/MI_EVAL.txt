**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid skill in evoking and reinforcing Amina's change talk through strategic questioning that elicits desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. Change talk is reinforced through reflections that amplify emotional weight and meaning. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates inconsistent skill in softening sustain talk and redirecting client language toward change. Multiple instances of Amina's doubt, fear, and ambivalence are validated but not strategically reframed. For example, when Amina states "I'm not sure about whether he'd go for that or if I'd be able to keep showing up consistently when it's so draining," the psychiatrist affirms her resilience but does not explore what would help her sustain effort when fatigued. Double-sided reflections are underutilized. When Amina expresses worry about her partner's response, the clinician does not explore whether this concern is based on past evidence or assumption. Direct techniques for softening sustain talk—such as exploring evidence, reframing obstacles as solvable problems, and using double-sided reflections—are minimally employed. This represents the most significant gap in MI competence, as unresolved sustain talk may undermine follow-through on action steps.

## Clinical Impact

**Positive Outcomes:** Amina's confidence increased from 4/10 to 6/10; she generated concrete action steps; she expressed clear commitment to her son's well-being. The psychiatrist's empathic understanding and collaborative stance create psychological safety supporting engagement and motivation.

**Potential Risks:** Insufficient softening of sustain talk means Amina's doubts about her partner's willingness and her own ability to sustain effort remain somewhat unresolved. This could undermine follow-through on the proposed check-in, particularly if she encounters resistance or experiences fatigue. More strategic response to ambivalence would increase durability of commitment and resilience when facing implementation challenges.
