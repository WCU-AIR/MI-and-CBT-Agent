# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist repeatedly uses judgmental language ("stop feeling sorry for yourself," "you're just making excuses," "you have to stop being weak") that communicates disapproval rather than acceptance. No warmth, validation, or affirmation of client's worth. Escalating confrontational tone creates climate of shame rather than safety. Client is pathologized rather than accepted as struggling with genuine difficulties.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Minimal effort to understand client's internal emotional experience. When client expresses hopelessness and exhaustion, psychiatrist responds with prescriptive directives rather than reflective listening. No empathic reflections of client's emotional state despite multiple explicit communications of being "tired," "drained," "barely holding on." Client's direct feedback about impact ("feels like you're pushing me off a cliff") is dismissed rather than recognized. Change talk ("I want to get better") is contradicted rather than explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maintains narrow, rigid focus on specific behavioral targets (social media/TV elimination, scheduling, financial advisor) but imposes these without collaboration or exploration of client priorities. Direction is not strategically guided or progressively developed. No inquiry into client's actual goals or what matters most to him. Lacks collaborative foundation necessary for MI. Focus is prescriptive rather than client-centered.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely expert-driven, authoritarian stance throughout. Consistent use of imperatives ("You have to," "You must," "You need to," "Just do it") communicates unilateral instruction rather than partnership. Client's attempts to collaborate are dismissed ("No, you're just hesitating"). Fear and consequences used as leverage rather than collaborative problem-solving. No genuine invitation for client input. Explicitly rejects client's request to "work on something more realistic."

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Actively pressures, controls, and minimizes client choice through directives and ultimatums ("Are you ready to commit right now?" "Are you going to do this or not?"). Removes client's sense of choice by stating "There's no room for half measures" and "There's no time for one step at a time." Uses fear-based consequences to coerce compliance rather than support autonomous decision-making. No acknowledgment of client's right to choose pace or approach. Directly suppresses autonomy when client proposes gradual changes.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates no evocation. Client's change talk ("I want to get better," "I'm open to talking about small steps," "Maybe I could try cutting back") is ignored, contradicted, or undermined rather than amplified. No evocative questions asked to explore client's motivations, values, or resources. Closed, prescriptive questions dominate ("Have you tried making a schedule?" "Are you sticking to it?"). External motivation (fear, shame, consequences) replaces exploration of intrinsic motivation. Client's own reasons for change are never elicited or strengthened.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains expert role throughout with no genuine partnership. Client expertise about his own life, barriers, and resources is dismissed. Unilateral assertions of authority ("I'm here to tell you," "You need to," "There's no room for half measures") reinforce expert-client hierarchy. When client offers perspective ("I've tried plans before but life throws curveballs"), this is labeled as excuse-making rather than explored as valuable information. Client's explicit request for partnership is rejected by reasserting expert authority.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Client's multiple instances of change talk are systematically ignored, contradicted, or undermined. Desire statements ("I want to get better—I really do," stated twice) are met with "You're just making excuses." Ability statements ("Maybe I could try cutting back") are countered with "No, you're just hesitating." Willingness ("I'm open to talking about small steps") is rejected with "There's no time for one step at a time." No evocative questions used to strengthen or elaborate change talk. No strategic techniques employed to build confidence or commitment. Change talk is actively suppressed rather than cultivated.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rather than skillfully softening sustain talk, psychiatrist directly confronts it ("You're just making excuses," "You're just hesitating," "You have to stop being weak"). Confrontational approach reinforces defensiveness and entrenchment. Fear-based consequences ("If you don't commit, you'll keep failing," "things will only get worse") paradoxically strengthen client's hopelessness and belief that "deck's stacked against me." No validation of barriers combined with evoking possibility. No double-sided reflections. Pressure-based redirection replaces skillful reframing. Sustain talk is reinforced rather than softened.

---

## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly every dimension. The psychiatrist demonstrates a confrontational, expert-driven, authoritarian approach that is directly contraindicated for a client in early contemplation with significant ambivalence and hopelessness. 

**Core deficiencies:**
- Absence of acceptance and empathy; client is judged as weak and making excuses
- Coercive rather than collaborative; directives and ultimatums replace partnership
- Change talk is contradicted and invalidated rather than amplified
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than skillfully softened
- Autonomy is pressured rather than supported
- Client's own motivations are never evoked or explored

**Impact on therapeutic relationship and change:** This approach is likely to **decrease** client motivation for change and damage the therapeutic alliance. Client explicitly communicates feeling pushed and misunderstood ("feels like you're pushing me off a cliff," "not really hearing how stuck I am"). Client is likely to disengage from treatment or become more entrenched in resistance and hopelessness.

**MI Competency Level:** **Minimal to no MI competence.** While opening statement briefly suggests collaborative intention, immediate pivot to confrontation, blame, and coercion reveals fundamental misalignment with MI principles. Psychiatrist either does not understand MI or does not believe in its approach.

**Critical recommendation:** Immediate training, supervision, and deliberate practice in MI principles is essential before this clinician continues work with clients presenting with ambivalence and low motivation for change.
