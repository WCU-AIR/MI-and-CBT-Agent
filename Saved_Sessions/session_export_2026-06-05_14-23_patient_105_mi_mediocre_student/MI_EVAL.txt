### Evocation

No inquiry into her own ideas, resources, strengths, or what she's already tried. Advice-giving replaces evocation throughout. Nadine's own wisdom and resilience remain completely untapped. Critical failure to draw out intrinsic motivation.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Fidelity Score: 1.9/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor fidelity to Motivational Interviewing principles**. While the psychiatrist maintains a respectful, non-judgmental demeanor and provides practical, evidence-based suggestions, the fundamental MI spirit is absent. The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven, prescriptive stance that undermines collaboration, autonomy support, and evocation—the core pillars of MI. The session reads more like a directive consultation than a motivational interview.

**Strengths:**
- Non-judgmental, respectful demeanor maintained throughout
- Clear focus on target behavior without significant topic drift
- Acknowledgment that Nadine's situation is difficult
- Practical, evidence-based suggestions provided
- Commitment to follow-up and continued support

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Expert-driven rather than client-centered approach
- Minimal evocation; tells rather than asks
- Weak empathy and surface-level reflections
- Prescriptive language undermines autonomy
- Missed opportunities to amplify change talk
- Inadequate softening of sustain talk
- Pronounced power imbalance; unilateral decision-making

**Impact on Client Engagement:** While Nadine appears compliant at session end, this compliance may be superficial rather than reflecting genuine intrinsic motivation. Psychiatrist has not helped Nadine connect proposed changes to her own values, addressed her underlying fears adequately, or built self-efficacy. Without intrinsic motivation and addressed concerns, adherence is likely inconsistent and motivation may wane when challenges arise.

**Likelihood of Sustained Change:** Moderate to low. Nadine may attempt suggested interventions but lacks the intrinsic motivation, addressed fears, and internal locus of control necessary for sustained behavior change.

---

## Key Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from Expert to Collaborative Partner** – Reframe role from prescriber to explorer; ask before advising
2. **Develop Evocation Skills** – Ask strategic questions to deepen Nadine's own reasons for change; explore her values and vision as a parent
3. **Strengthen Empathy** – Use complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and internal conflict, not just content
4. **Replace Prescriptive Language** – Eliminate "should," "might want to," "would probably help"; use autonomy-supportive language
5. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk** – Use double-sided reflections and strategic questioning rather than reassurance or dismissal
6. **Co-Construct Plans** – Develop solutions collaboratively with Nadine, not for her
7. **Explore and Address Barriers** – Fully understand Nadine's fears and concerns before offering solutions
8. **Build Self-Efficacy** – Help Nadine recognize her own strengths, resources, and problem-solving capacity
9. **Seek Formal MI Training** – Engage in structured MI training, supervision, and deliberate practice with recorded sessions
10. **Practice Deliberately** – Focus on lowest-scoring domains; use MI Fidelity Scale for self-assessment

**Recommended Next Steps:** Formal MI training with MINT-certified trainer; supervision focused on MI fidelity; peer consultation groups; monthly session recording and review with supervisor.
