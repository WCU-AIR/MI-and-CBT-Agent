### Evocation

Client's sustain talk includes: "I can't see myself changing now," "I've tried before, didn't work," "I don't like lectures about it," "I'm not ready to say yes today." Psychiatrist fails to recognize that confrontation is strengthening resistance rather than weakening it.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.0/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on smoking cessation as target behavior (though pursued in harmful manner)
- Demonstrates persistence on topic (though manifests as rigidity rather than skillful persistence)

### Critical Deficiencies:

**This session represents a fundamental failure to practice Motivational Interviewing.** The psychiatrist demonstrates the opposite of MI practice across nearly every dimension:

1. **Authoritarian stance replacing partnership:** Unilateral expert role; explicit rejection of collaboration ("You don't get to negotiate here")
2. **Confrontation instead of evocation:** Lectures, directs, and demands rather than drawing out client's own motivations
3. **Suppression of autonomy:** Ultimatums and pressure eliminate choice rather than supporting self-determination
4. **Absence of empathic listening:** Not a single reflective statement in entire session; client's perspective systematically ignored
5. **Judgment and contempt:** Language communicates disapproval rather than unconditional positive regard
6. **Ignoring change talk:** Spontaneous change talk completely ignored while sustain talk intensified through confrontation
7. **Reactance generation:** Approach triggers psychological reactance; client moves from ambivalent to explicitly resistant

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change:

**This session is actively harmful to client's motivation for change.** Rather than increasing intrinsic motivation, psychiatrist's approach:
- Damages therapeutic relationship; client explicitly states approach makes him "dig in deeper" and feels disrespected
- Triggers reactance; client moves from "I'm always open to listen" to "No. I'm not going to commit"
- Reinforces hopelessness by dismissing client's past experiences and current readiness level
- Violates MI spirit through contempt, judgment, and disrespect

**Likelihood of behavior change: Extremely low.** Research demonstrates confrontational, expert-driven approaches are ineffective and often counterproductive.

### Recommendations:

**HIGHEST PRIORITY: Fundamental reorientation to MI spirit.** Psychiatrist must understand MI is fundamentally incompatible with authoritarian, confrontational approach. Must commit to viewing client as expert on own life and genuinely respecting client autonomy.

**Additional critical actions:**
1. Eliminate confrontation; adopt reflective listening (goal: 80% reflections)
2. Develop genuine curiosity about client perspective through open-ended questions
3. Recognize and amplify change talk; use DARN-CAT framework
4. Support autonomy; eliminate ultimatums and demands
5. Use double-sided reflections to handle sustain talk
6. Establish genuine partnership with inclusive language
7. Develop empathic accuracy through reflective listening training
8. Assess personal readiness to practice MI with genuine respect for autonomy
9. Seek formal MI training and ongoing supervision with MI-certified trainer

**Critical assessment:** Psychiatrist does not demonstrate MI competence at any level. This is not adequate practice with room for improvement—this is fundamental misunderstanding of MI principles. Psychiatrist should not conduct further MI sessions without significant training, supervision, and genuine commitment to understanding MI spirit. Continuing to practice in this manner will harm clients and undermine their motivation for change.
