## CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Session Strengths:**
- Genuine warmth and patience; non-hostile demeanor
- Created safe environment where reluctant adolescent felt comfortable disclosing
- Appropriate validation of emotions as normal responses
- Consistent supportive tone throughout

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of CBT structure and evidence-based trauma interventions
- Excessive self-focus (40% of session time on personal anecdotes)
- Failure to conduct systematic trauma assessment
- No identification of avoidance as maintaining factor
- Ineffective or counterproductive interventions (positive thinking, distraction)
- No homework or between-session tasks
- Minimal collaboration and guided discovery
- No case conceptualization or treatment strategy

**Impact on Patient Care:**
This session is unlikely to produce meaningful symptom improvement. Dylan will leave with generic suggestions but no understanding of his trauma response, no evidence-based interventions to practice, and no clear treatment plan. Avoidance will likely continue and intensify. Intrusive images will persist. Sleep disturbance will continue. Most concerning, Dylan may develop reduced confidence in therapy and disengage from treatment, reducing likelihood of recovery during critical acute phase.

**Current Competency Level:**
Below competence in CBT for trauma. Therapist has foundational interpersonal skills but lacks specialized knowledge and technical skills required to treat Acute Stress Disorder. Therapist is conflating supportive counseling with evidence-based CBT.
