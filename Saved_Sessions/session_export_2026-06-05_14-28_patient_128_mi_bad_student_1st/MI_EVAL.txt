## Overall Assessment Summary

**Total MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Clinical Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamentally Inconsistent with MI Principles

**Key Findings:**

This session represents a **catastrophic failure of Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no competence across all nine MI dimensions and actively violates core MI principles. The approach is expert-driven, judgmental, dismissive, pressuring, and confrontational—the antithesis of MI spirit.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Zero acceptance or unconditional positive regard
- Complete absence of empathic understanding and reflective listening
- Rigid hierarchical expert stance with no collaboration
- Active undermining of patient autonomy and choice
- Systematic dismissal of patient's own change talk and values
- Confrontational rather than skillful handling of resistance

**Impact on Therapeutic Engagement:**
This approach is **actively counterproductive**. Patient explicitly states pressure causes shutdown and reactance ("It just makes me feel boxed in and a bit like I'm gonna rebel"). Psychiatrist's response is to repeat identical pressuring approach, further entrenching resistance and damaging therapeutic alliance.

**Predicted Outcomes:**
- High likelihood of patient dropout and non-compliance
- Strengthened resistance and sustain talk
- Decreased intrinsic motivation for change
- Damaged therapeutic relationship
- Decreased likelihood of sustained behavior change

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Fundamental shift from expert-driven to collaborative, client-centered stance
2. Formal MI training (MINT-certified) and ongoing clinical supervision
3. Elimination of judgment; replacement with genuine validation
4. Implementation of reflective listening and evocative questioning
5. Explicit affirmation and amplification of patient change talk
6. Support for patient autonomy and collaborative goal-setting
7. Connection of health behaviors to patient's own values and goals

**This clinician should not continue seeing patients using this approach without immediate retraining and supervision.**
