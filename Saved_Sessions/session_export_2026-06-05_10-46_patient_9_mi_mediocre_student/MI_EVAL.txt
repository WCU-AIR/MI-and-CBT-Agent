### Direction

Clinician has predetermined that reducing phone use is the goal and guides session toward that endpoint. Fails to clarify discrepancy between client's values and current behavior—essential element of MI direction. ## Overall MI Competency Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 2.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates significant deficiencies in MI practice. While the psychiatrist begins with warmth and maintains focus on target behavior, the session quickly devolves into expert-driven advice-giving and problem-solving. The clinician demonstrates minimal evocation (the core of MI), weak reflective listening, imposed direction, and limited autonomy support. Most critically, the psychiatrist fails to help the client discover her own reasons for change or resolve her ambivalence through genuine exploration.

**MI Spirit Assessment:**

This session does not embody the spirit of MI. While some acceptance is demonstrated (warmth, lack of confrontation), the session lacks genuine partnership, evocation, and autonomy support. The clinician's stance is fundamentally expert-driven rather than collaborative. The session prioritizes the clinician's agenda over the client's own exploration and discovery.

**Likelihood of Sustained Change:**

**Low.** The client leaves with a plan but without genuine intrinsic motivation to follow it. She is complying with the clinician's suggestion, not pursuing her own goal. Research demonstrates that externally motivated change is far less likely to be sustained than intrinsically motivated change. The client will likely attempt the plan for a few days, find it difficult, and return to previous habits. This outcome could damage the therapeutic relationship and reduce willingness to engage in future change efforts.

**Most Critical Improvements Needed:**

1. **Shift from advice-giving to evocation** – Learn to ask open questions that invite client exploration rather than immediately offering solutions
2. **Develop genuine reflective listening** – Replace diagnostic/leading questions with complex reflections capturing emotional nuance and ambivalence
3. **Build intrinsic motivation** – Help client discover her own reasons for change by exploring values and discrepancy between values and behavior
4. **Strengthen autonomy support** – Consistently affirm client's right to choose and emphasize her ownership of change process
5. **Develop collaborative stance** – Share power and co-create solutions rather than imposing expert-driven plans
