Phrases like "you should try," "it would probably help if," and "It's important to get this sorted out" carry coercive undertones. No explicit affirmation of Maria's right to choose. Does not employ more skillful MI techniques like double-sided reflection or strategic redirection. One validating reflection offered ("It sounds like you're not ready to commit") but immediately undermined by prescriptive suggestion. Sustain talk not deeply explored or understood.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.3/5 – POOR**

**Key Findings:**

This session demonstrates **significant deficiencies in MI practice** across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist operates from a traditional, expert-driven, prescriptive model rather than an MI-based, evocative model. The session is characterized by:

- **Directive stance** with repeated "should" language that undermines autonomy
- **Minimal evocation** of client's own motivations, values, and resources
- **Missed change talk opportunities** that could have strengthened Maria's motivation
- **Insufficient empathy** with factual rather than emotional reflections
- **Non-collaborative process** with unilateral agenda-setting and solution prescription
- **Inadequate sustain talk management** relying on reassurance rather than skillful reflection

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This approach is **likely to decrease intrinsic motivation** and strengthen resistance. Maria leaves the session having agreed only to symptom tracking—a minimal intervention she herself suggested. No commitment to medication adherence or behavioral change is established. Her sustain talk increased throughout the session, indicating the psychiatrist's prescriptive approach strengthened rather than softened her resistance.

**Critical Recommendations:**

1. **Shift from prescriptive to evocative stance** – Replace "should" language with open-ended questions
2. **Develop change talk recognition and amplification** – Pause and explore when clients offer change talk
3. **Cultivate genuine empathy** – Move to emotional reflections capturing internal experience and values
4. **Establish true collaboration** – Invite client input on session direction and solutions
5. **Explicitly support autonomy** – Use language affirming client's right to choose
6. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** – Significant competency gaps require structured learning

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change: LOW**

Maria would need to experience a clinician who validates her concerns, respects her autonomy, draws out her own reasons for change, and partners collaboratively. This session did not provide that experience.
