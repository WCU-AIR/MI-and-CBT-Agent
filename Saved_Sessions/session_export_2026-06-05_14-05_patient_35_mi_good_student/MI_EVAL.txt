# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and validates Dylan's frustration throughout the session. Effectively normalizes his developmental stage and affirms his autonomy ("It's clear you know how to have fun responsibly when you want to"). However, deeper affirmation of Dylan's core values (friendship, independence, social connection) as legitimate and worthy could strengthen acceptance. Missed opportunity to explicitly normalize his ambivalence or acknowledge underlying fear/shame accompanying his defensiveness about the conduct violation.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated through complex reflections capturing Dylan's ambivalence ("You're in a bit of a bind—wanting to enjoy the normal college experience but also wanting to steer clear of consequences"). Accurately reflects Dylan's values and social context. However, empathy could deepen by exploring underlying emotions beneath surface content—when Dylan expresses stress about explaining himself repeatedly or skepticism about change, the clinician reflects content but doesn't explore the fear, embarrassment, or doubt beneath these statements.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session maintains some focus on target behavior (underage drinking, party attendance, conduct violations) early on but becomes increasingly diffuse. Drifts toward broader themes of social balance, friendship dynamics, and campus activities without explicitly connecting them back to the original referral issue. Critically, the psychiatrist never directly addresses Dylan's minimization of the alcohol violation itself, specific consequences of underage drinking, university policy rationale, or Dylan's actual responsibility in the incident. Direction lacks strategic clarity on the specific conduct violation.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated through opening question ("what's on your mind?") that establishes genuine partnership. Clinician frequently seeks Dylan's input and co-constructs understanding rather than imposing solutions. Validates Dylan's ideas and builds on them collaboratively. However, subtle shift toward end where summarization begins to shape Dylan's goals in clinician's language rather than purely reflecting Dylan's own articulation. Could more explicitly invite Dylan to co-create the support plan.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes Dylan's freedom to choose and respects his right to make decisions. Language throughout reflects autonomy support ("You're balancing your social needs," "You're thoughtful about your choices"). Explicitly avoids prescribing behavior, positioning Dylan as decision-maker. However, missed opportunities to more explicitly reinforce Dylan's right to choose differently or maintain status quo. When Dylan expresses skepticism, could have affirmed: "That's completely your call. You get to decide what you're ready for."

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions present, particularly about past successes ("Can you tell me about a time when you managed to have fun with your friends but also avoided getting into trouble?"). However, limited depth in exploring Dylan's own reasons, values, ability, and commitment to change. When Dylan states "avoiding hassle would be nice," clinician reflects but doesn't deepen: "Tell me more about that. What else matters to you about staying out of trouble?" No systematic exploration of values conflict, ability, or genuine commitment language. Evocation remains surface-level rather than strategically deepening motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine partnership demonstrated throughout most of session. Clinician positions Dylan as expert on his own life and experiences. Actively incorporates Dylan's contributions into session direction rather than imposing predetermined agenda. When Dylan mentions low-key hangout, psychiatrist builds on it rather than dismissing. However, final summarization consolidates Dylan's ideas into clinician's framework rather than inviting Dylan's own articulation. Could have asked: "How would you summarize what we've talked about today?" Never explicitly acknowledges Dylan's expertise about his peer group and social context.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** **Significant weakness.** Minimal strategic cultivation of change talk; what emerges is largely incidental rather than deliberately evoked and reinforced. Change talk present includes "avoiding trouble would be nice" (desire), "I could try talking to close friends" (ability/commitment), and "I'm willing to try" (weak commitment). However, no systematic amplification when change talk emerges. When Dylan mentions successful low-key hangout, clinician affirms but doesn't ask: "What did you do that made that work?" No explicit recognition or labeling of change talk. Weak commitment language ("6 on confidence," "willing to try") not deepened through follow-up questions. Critical MI skill gap limiting session effectiveness in building motivation for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Skillful moments include use of double-sided reflections ("You're in a bit of a bind") and validation of skepticism without dismissal. However, critical sustain talk inadequately addressed: Dylan's repeated minimization ("I didn't bring the alcohol," "It's not my fault") accepted without exploration of his actual role; age rationalization ("I'm almost 21") never directly addressed; no exploration of responsibility or discrepancy between "it's not my fault" and his presence at the incident; early sustain talk about fairness ("It's just not fair") reflected but not gently explored. Psychiatrist avoids confrontation (appropriate) but also avoids gently exploring discrepancies (missed opportunity).

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.4/5 – Adequate MI Competence**

**Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing
- Consistent acceptance and unconditional positive regard maintained throughout
- Solid reflective listening capturing Dylan's ambivalence and values
- Effective autonomy support emphasizing client choice
- Skillful evocation of past successes
- Validation of ambivalence and acknowledgment of Dylan's difficult position
- Avoidance of confrontation maintaining therapeutic rapport

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Strategic cultivation of change talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) – Must systematically evoke, amplify, and reinforce Dylan's own language about change through targeted open questions and explicit recognition
2. **Exploration of responsibility and discrepancy** – Dylan maintains "it's not his fault" throughout; psychiatrist must gently help him develop nuanced thinking about his role without confrontation
3. **Maintenance of strategic direction** – Session drifts from specific conduct violation toward broader themes; must connect back to referral issue and directly address underage drinking
4. **Depth in evocation** – Change talk emerges but lacks follow-up exploration; must deepen Dylan's articulation of reasons, ability, and commitment
5. **Softening sustain talk about responsibility** – Dylan's minimization and fairness arguments require gentle, curious exploration rather than acceptance

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
Session successfully maintains rapport and engagement—Dylan is not defensive by end. However, unlikely to significantly shift motivation for change. Dylan remains in pre-contemplation with minimal genuine recognition that behavior is problematic or that he bears responsibility. While willing to "try" strategies, he hasn't developed intrinsic motivation rooted in his own values or clear understanding of his role in the incident. Supportive environment is necessary but insufficient without strategic MI techniques to help Dylan discover his own reasons for changing.

**Recommendation:**
Psychiatrist demonstrates solid foundational MI skills but operates at "adequate" rather than "proficient" level. Must develop strategic depth in cultivating change talk, exploring responsibility, and maintaining focus on specific conduct violation to effectively move Dylan from pre-contemplation toward genuine contemplation and preparation for change.
