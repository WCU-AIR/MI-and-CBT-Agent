## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.0/5**

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **fundamental failure of Motivational Interviewing practice** across all nine competency domains. The psychiatrist operates from a confrontational, authoritarian, expert-driven model that is **directly contradictory to MI principles and research-based practice**.

### Key Violations of MI Spirit

- **No genuine partnership:** Rigid expert authority maintained throughout; patient positioned as passive recipient
- **No acceptance:** Pervasive judgment, contempt, and dismissal of patient's emotional experience
- **No empathy:** Zero reflective listening; active dismissal of patient's perspective
- **No evocation:** External reasons for change imposed; patient's intrinsic motivation ignored
- **No autonomy support:** Coercive language removes choice; compliance demanded
- **Confrontational approach:** Language known to increase resistance used repeatedly

### Impact on Patient

Patient's responses demonstrate **increasing entrenchment in sustain talk and disengagement** as session progresses. By conclusion, patient is repeating "I'm not ready" and "No one really wants to help me"—suggesting psychiatrist's approach has **reinforced hopelessness and isolation rather than evoking hope or possibility**. Patient explicitly states psychiatrist's approach is not working; psychiatrist does not adjust.

### Research Alignment

Research on MI demonstrates that confrontational, expert-driven approaches **increase resistance and decrease intrinsic motivation for change**. This session exemplifies the antithesis of evidence-based MI practice.

### Recommendations

**Immediate action required:**
1. Comprehensive MI training through MINT-certified trainer
2. Regular clinical supervision focused on MI competence
3. Recording and review of sessions with MI-trained supervisor
4. Fundamental shift from expert/directive to collaborative/evocative stance
5. Development of genuine empathic listening and reflective responses
6. Elimination of confrontational language and judgment

**Without significant change in clinical approach, this clinician should not conduct MI-based interventions.** Current practice is likely to harm patient outcomes and damage therapeutic relationships.
