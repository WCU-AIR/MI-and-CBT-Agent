# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance with genuine validation of patient's experience ("It's understandable to feel overwhelmed"). Affirms resilience and worth throughout. Minor opportunity: could more fully normalize low confidence (2/10) as realistic starting point rather than reframing it as containing hope, which may feel dismissive of actual emotional reality.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening with good perspective-taking. Strong example: "It sounds like breaking free from this cycle is really important to you, and that feeling normal again—feeling steady and whole—is something you deeply long for." Sophisticated understanding of defensive mechanisms evident. However, some reflections remain at surface level; missed opportunities to capture deeper emotional texture (e.g., grief in exhaustion, pain of relapse). Could deepen exploration of emotional nuance in patient's experience.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** General focus on change goals maintained (reducing drug use, feeling normal, building support) but direction is diffuse and lacks strategic clarity. Session drifts between themes without clear prioritization or sequencing. Missed opportunity to strategically explore "odd jobs" as concrete strategy after patient identified it as helpful. Confidence scale introduced but not leveraged strategically. Final agreed-upon step (returning to talk) is positive but vague—lacks specificity about targets, measurement, or concrete actions between sessions.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with excellent opening question establishing partnership: "What would you like to focus on?" Consistently seeks patient's perspective and invites input into agenda. Explicitly affirms autonomy ("It's entirely your choice how to build from here"). Minor opportunities: confidence scale presented somewhat didactically rather than collaboratively; could more explicitly invite co-construction of solutions rather than primarily offering reflections and questions.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect for and active fostering of patient's perception of choice throughout session. Explicit affirmation of decision-making authority ("completely up to you how to move forward"). Uses invitational language ("I'm curious," "What do you think?") rather than prescribing solutions. Minor opportunity: could more explicitly validate patient's right to feel stuck and not ready, rather than consistently reframing statements toward possibility, which risks subtle pressure toward change readiness.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to evoke patient's own motivations and resources but inconsistently applied and lacking depth. Good questions asked ("What matters most to you?" "Can you tell me about a time when you managed to make a positive change?"). However, insufficient amplification of change talk when it appears. Premature focus on barriers without first fully evoking motivations creates imbalance. Limited exploration of core values, meaning, and intrinsic motivations. Missed opportunity with confidence scale—did not ask evocative follow-ups like "What's keeping you at a 2 instead of a 1?"

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance positioning patient as expert on own experience. Opening question immediately establishes shared power. Uses inclusive language and validates patient's wisdom ("You've identified..."). Avoids expert role through consistent questioning rather than telling. Explicitly affirms autonomy. Minor opportunities: limited explicit co-construction of solutions; could more directly name this as joint effort with shared responsibility; could be more transparent about MI process itself.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** **SIGNIFICANT WEAKNESS.** Minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, or reinforce patient's change talk despite low motivation (2/10 confidence). Change talk present ("I wish I could get out of this cycle," "I want to feel normal," "I managed to stay clean for a couple weeks") but reflected without amplification or deepening. When patient identifies two-week sobriety, psychiatrist asks about building on it but doesn't sufficiently amplify as evidence of capability. No explicit naming or reinforcement of change talk as evidence of motivation. Missing: strategic questioning to evoke more change talk, amplification of existing change talk, summaries highlighting change talk, explicit recognition of change talk. Critical gap given patient's low motivation and high sustain talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** **SIGNIFICANT WEAKNESS.** Substantial sustain talk present ("I just feel helpless," "No one wants to help me," "I don't see how things could really change," "I can't trust that support's there, so I just don't even try") but not skillfully softened or redirected. Primary response is accurate reflection and validation without strategic pivoting toward change. Occasional double-sided reflections used but inconsistently. Missing: consistent use of double-sided reflections emphasizing change side, strategic reframing of barriers as surmountable, exploration of exceptions and contradictions, softening language acknowledging sustain talk without reinforcing it. By session end, sustain talk remains largely unaddressed and potentially reinforced through validation without redirection. Risks leaving patient more entrenched in hopelessness rather than opening space for change.

---

## **Overall MI Competency Assessment**

**Session Score: 3.2/5 – Adequate but Inconsistent**

**Summary:** Psychiatrist demonstrates foundational MI skills (empathy, collaboration, autonomy support, acceptance) with warm, respectful tone. However, session lacks strategic sophistication needed to work effectively with low-motivation client (2/10 confidence, high sustain talk). Critical gaps in cultivating change talk and softening sustain talk represent core MI deficiencies. Session validates barriers without redirecting and reflects change talk without amplifying—missing opportunities to build momentum for change.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation:** Currently **unlikely to significantly increase intrinsic motivation**. While patient commits to return (positive), confidence remains at 2/10, sustain talk unaddressed, change talk unamplified. Validation of experience is genuine strength but must be paired with strategic MI technique for maximum effectiveness.

**Priority Recommendations:**
1. **Strategically cultivate and amplify change talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. **Soften sustain talk without reinforcing hopelessness** (SECOND PRIORITY)
3. Deepen exploration of values, meaning, and intrinsic motivation
4. Leverage confidence scale strategically with follow-up questions
5. Establish clear, specific, co-constructed goals and action planning
6. Use summaries to highlight change talk and reinforce motivation
7. Mine past successes more thoroughly for clues about what supports change
8. Strengthen explicit co-construction of solutions
9. Normalize low confidence as realistic starting point, not barrier
10. Explicitly name and validate ambivalence without resolving prematurely

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Focused training on change talk amplification and sustain talk softening techniques. Strong foundation present; strategic MI technique refinement needed for optimal effectiveness with low-motivation clients.
