### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Partnership—understanding that expertise and wisdom about change reside within the client—is fundamentally absent. Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role and positions patient as non-compliant individual needing correction. Statements like "I want to help you quit smoking completely," "You really need to quit completely," "Why didn't you quit cold turkey like you should have?" demonstrate expert-driven stance. Patient's assessment (gradual cutting down feels manageable, cold turkey feels overwhelming, step-by-step approach necessary) is treated as resistance rather than wisdom. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no skill in cultivating change talk—strategically evoking and reinforcing client's own language favoring change. In fact, actively suppresses and dismisses patient's change talk. Patient provides multiple instances that should be amplified: desire ("I want to do better," "I'm definitely working on it"), ability ("I managed to cut down," "I'm down two cigarettes"), reasons ("I'm worried about long-term health effects"), commitment ("I'm serious about quitting," "I'm committed to cutting down more"). Patient expresses legitimate sustain talk: "It's tough—especially at work, the urges hit hard," "I'm not sure if I can fully quit just yet," "Forcing it all at once makes me worry I'll end up back where I started." Final interaction shows slight improvement through open question allowing fuller exploration of ambivalence, but does not represent skillful softening of sustain talk.

## Summary Assessment

### Session Alignment with MI Spirit and Principles

**This session does NOT embody the spirit of Motivational Interviewing.** The MI spirit is characterized by partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. This session is characterized by judgment, directive pressure, dismissal, and confrontation.

### Critical Findings

**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on smoking cessation as target behavior
- Demonstrates genuine concern for patient health
- Shows persistence in attempting engagement
- Final interaction demonstrates capacity to recognize and adjust approach through open question

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Fundamental misalignment with MI principles across nearly all domains
- Authoritarian expert stance rather than collaborative partnership
- Active suppression of patient's genuine change talk and progress
- Confrontation of sustain talk rather than skillful softening
- Dismissal of harm reduction as legitimate stage of change
- Imposition of external motivation (threats) rather than evocation of intrinsic motivation
- Undermining of patient autonomy through repeated directive pressure

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation

**Very Low.** This session is likely to **decrease** patient motivation for change rather than increase it. Research on motivational psychology demonstrates that:
- Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation; pressure decreases it
- Confrontation typically strengthens resistance rather than weakening it
- Extrinsic motivation (threats) is less effective than intrinsic motivation
- Affirmation of progress increases engagement; dismissal decreases it

Patient enters session with genuine change talk, concrete progress (cutting down by two cigarettes), and realistic goals. By session end, these have been dismissed as inadequate, and patient has been pressured and judged. Likely outcomes: disengagement from treatment or increased defensiveness and resistance.

### Recommendations for Improvement

**Highest Priority:**
1. Shift from directive pressure to collaborative partnership
2. Affirm and build on existing progress rather than dismissing it
3. Evoke intrinsic motivation rather than imposing external threats
4. Develop skill in reflective listening and empathic understanding
5. Eliminate confrontation; develop skill in softening sustain talk

**For this psychiatrist to become an effective MI practitioner, fundamental changes in stance, approach, and skill are required.** With commitment to learning and practice, this clinician can develop the skills and stance necessary to help patients evoke their own intrinsic motivation for change.
