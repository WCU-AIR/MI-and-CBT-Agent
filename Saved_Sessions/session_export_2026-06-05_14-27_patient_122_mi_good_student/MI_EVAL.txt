# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and validates Sofia's emotional experience throughout the session. Early reflections effectively communicate understanding without judgment (e.g., "It sounds really exhausting to feel misunderstood and targeted like that"). Affirms Sofia's insight and effort appropriately. However, when Sofia expresses low confidence (3/10), the psychiatrist reframes positively without fully exploring or normalizing the ambivalence underlying that low score. Additionally, could have more explicitly affirmed the legitimacy of Sofia's desire to defend herself before exploring alternatives. Maintains warm, supportive tone and avoids judgmental language about avoidance behaviors (skipping school, leaving class), appropriately demonstrating acceptance of her coping strategies as understandable responses to stress.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with complex reflections that capture both emotional experience and underlying tension (e.g., "You're really caught between wanting the drama to stop because it's so hurtful and feeling like the people around you aren't being honest or fair"). Accurately reflects multiple layers of Sofia's experience including effort, external barriers, reactive patterns, and institutional misunderstanding. However, misses opportunities to fully explore the emotional weight of isolation and perceived betrayal underlying her defensive stance. When Sofia expresses that "standing up for myself means I have to fight back," could have reflected the underlying fear or hurt before moving to problem-solving. Reflections tend toward cognitive reframing rather than sitting with Sofia's pain before moving forward, occasionally missing the deepest emotional nuances.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on Sofia's peer conflicts, school avoidance, and her capacity to manage these situations more effectively. Session stays strategically directed toward understanding what would help Sofia feel "less caught up in all that drama" and exploring her ability to respond differently. Logical progression from opening with what's on her mind → exploring what would help → identifying confidence level → discovering concrete success → building on that success → identifying practical tool → planning first step. However, when Sofia says "the problem is that even when I try to stay out of it, the drama still finds me," could have more clearly redirected toward what Sofia can control versus what she cannot. Could have been more explicit about session direction earlier by collaboratively establishing the focus. Occasionally allows Sofia to remain in victim narrative without explicitly redirecting toward her agency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial collaboration with notable imbalances in power-sharing. Opening question ("what's on your mind that you'd like to focus on?") is genuinely collaborative and invites Sofia's agenda. However, as session progresses, psychiatrist increasingly directs conversation toward specific solutions (phone reminder, bracelet) without fully co-constructing these ideas with Sofia. When Sofia expresses uncertainty about what would help, psychiatrist responds by affirming and expanding on ideas rather than genuinely exploring them together. Positions psychiatrist as validator of Sofia's ideas rather than true co-explorer. Introduction of confidence scale (0-10) is clinician-directed assessment tool rather than collaboratively developed, subtly shifting power toward clinician's framework. Does incorporate Sofia's contributions and allow her ideas to shape conversation, but doesn't fully invite her expertise or co-construct solutions in balanced way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong autonomy support throughout session with consistent emphasis on Sofia's choice and agency. Key examples include affirming Sofia's internal motivation ("Recognizing that you want to keep calmer and maybe ignore the drama more shows a lot of insight and a real desire to find a way through this") and emphasizing her goals ("it sounds like it could be a useful tool to support your goal of staying calmer"). Avoids prescriptive language; asks "What do you think would help you feel more able to choose walking away?" rather than "You should try a phone reminder." Respects Sofia's ambivalence and doesn't pressure her toward change. Consistently frames Sofia as expert on her own life and agent of change. Minor opportunity exists to avoid subtle assumptions about commitment—when psychiatrist says "which shows your commitment to finding ways to handle this better," slightly assumes Sofia has committed to change rather than exploring what feels right to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation but with significant missed opportunities to draw out Sofia's own motivations, values, and resources more deeply. Effectively elicits Sofia's own example of capability and success through question "Can you think of any times, even small ones, when you managed to stay calm or step away from the drama successfully?" However, relies heavily on reflective listening and validation rather than strategic questioning to evoke Sofia's deeper motivations. When Sofia expresses low confidence (3/10), could have asked "What would need to happen for that number to move from a 3 to a 4 or 5?" or "What's one thing you've done before that you felt proud of?" Doesn't deeply explore Sofia's values or intrinsic motivations for change. Questions like "What matters most to you about your friendships?" or "What would it feel like to have even one person at school who really understood you?" could have evoked deeper desires for connection and belonging. Doesn't explore potential source of motivation when Sofia mentions friend who might reconnect. Questions tend to be exploratory rather than strategically designed to amplify Sofia's own change talk and intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial partnership but increasingly assumes expert role as session progresses. Opening is genuinely collaborative and invites Sofia's expertise about her own situation. However, as session develops, psychiatrist subtly shifts into expert-validator role. When Sofia expresses uncertainty about what would help, psychiatrist responds with affirmation and expansion of tentative ideas rather than genuine co-exploration. Positions psychiatrist as expert who validates and elaborates on Sofia's ideas rather than true partner who explores them together. Introduction of clinical tools (0-10 confidence scale) without explicitly inviting Sofia's input on whether this framework is useful positions psychiatrist as expert structuring conversation. Does incorporate Sofia's contributions and allow her ideas to influence session direction, but doesn't fully foster sense that Sofia's wisdom about her own life is equally valued. Psychiatrist's reflections, while accurate, sometimes subtly reframe Sofia's experience in clinical language rather than staying with her own framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some cultivation of change talk but misses significant opportunities to strategically amplify and reinforce Sofia's own language about change, ability, and commitment. Effectively reinforces Sofia's capability when she describes walking away example: "That moment when you chose to walk away instead of engaging...really stands out as a powerful example of your ability to take control." Also reinforces her reasoning: "It sounds like your exhaustion with the constant conflict and your awareness that engaging wouldn't help really motivated you to step away that day." However, when Sofia says "I don't think I'm really good at keeping calm," could have asked "Tell me about a time when you did keep calm, even a little bit" to evoke counter-examples. When Sofia expresses desire for "something real to make me actually stick to that," could have asked "What would it feel like to actually follow through on walking away more often?" to evoke Sofia's own vision of change. Doesn't ask about reasons for wanting to repair friendships or values around connection. When Sofia says "it might be worth a shot," could have asked "What makes you think it might work?" to evoke Sofia's own confidence and reasoning. Doesn't strategically use open questions to draw out Sofia's own reasons, ability statements, or commitment language throughout session.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate handling of sustain talk through reflective listening and gentle redirection but doesn't consistently use strategic MI techniques to actively soften Sofia's arguments for status quo. Generally uses reflective listening to acknowledge sustain talk without reinforcing it, which is appropriate. When Sofia says "It's like no matter what I say, it just turns around on me," psychiatrist reflects this without amplifying hopelessness. However, could have used more strategic techniques: (1) Double-sided reflection—when Sofia says "it's hard when you feel like everyone's against you," could have reflected both sides: "On one hand, it feels like everyone's against you, and on the other hand, you mentioned one friend who might want to talk to you again." (2) Reframing—when Sofia says "standing up for myself means I have to fight back," could have reframed: "I'm wondering if there are other ways to stand up for yourself—like walking away, like you did last week—that don't require fighting back?" (3) Amplified reflection—could have used this technique to invite Sofia to correct exaggerations. (4) Exploring discrepancies—when Sofia expresses low confidence (3/10) but describes successful walking-away example, could have explicitly highlighted: "You said you're not very confident, but you just told me about a time when you successfully walked away. How do you make sense of that?" Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirects toward Sofia's agency, but doesn't consistently use strategic MI techniques to actively soften or reframe her arguments for status quo.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.5/5**

### Strengths
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and validation throughout session
- Accurate, complex reflective listening that captures key tensions in Sofia's experience
- Clear, strategic session direction focused on Sofia's capacity to manage peer conflicts
- Strong autonomy support with consistent emphasis on Sofia's choice and agency
- Effective elicitation and reinforcement of Sofia's success example (walking away)
- Genuine collaborative opening that invites Sofia's agenda
- Warm, supportive tone maintained throughout; avoids judgment about avoidance behaviors

### Areas for Development
- Limited strategic evocation of Sofia's deeper motivations, values, and intrinsic reasons for change
- Session focuses on behavioral strategies without anchoring to core values or emotional drivers
- Subtle shift toward expert-validator role as session progresses rather than maintaining true partnership
- Missed opportunities to deepen change talk when Sofia expresses tentative openness
- Limited use of strategic sustain talk techniques (double-sided reflection, reframing, amplified reflection, discrepancy exploration)
- Insufficient exploration of discrepancies between Sofia's stated low confidence and demonstrated capability
- Premature movement to solutions without fully exploring Sofia's own ideas or deeper motivations

### Clinical Impact
Sofia leaves session with concrete behavioral strategy (phone reminder) and some recognition of capability. However, intrinsic motivation—the most powerful driver of sustained change—remains largely untapped. Session demonstrates competent supportive counseling with solid foundational MI skills but falls short of strategic evocation and change talk amplification that characterizes excellent MI practice. Sofia's ambivalence remains largely unresolved, and deeper connection to *why* change matters to her (e.g., genuine connection, being understood, belonging) hasn't been fully activated, which may limit likelihood of sustained behavior change.
