### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic evocation and reinforcement of client's change talk throughout (desire: "I don't want to feel that way again"; ability: "I could build on those moments"; reasons: "my family means to me"; commitment: "I think it's a plan that could work"). Reflections amplify and deepen change talk effectively. Could strengthen by exploring intrinsic reasons for change more deeply, asking about confidence in ability to reach out, and deepening commitment language about following through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent skill in addressing sustain talk. Some skillful double-sided reflections acknowledge sustain talk without reinforcing it ("A 6 shows you're already halfway there"). However, significant gaps exist: core ambivalence about internal motivation is reflected but not strategically redirected; fears about being a burden are not deeply explored; when client says "It still feels like a battle," clinician reflects but doesn't strategically pivot toward change talk. More direct challenge of sustain talk and explicit reframing needed.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 4.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates solid, competent motivational interviewing practice with particular strengths in empathic listening (5/5), collaboration (4/5), and autonomy support (4/5). The psychiatrist creates a strong therapeutic alliance that allows the client to feel heard and understood. The client generates meaningful change talk and identifies a concrete, achievable action step (reaching out to sister) that emerges from his own insight.

**Key Strengths:**
- Exemplary empathic accuracy with complex, emotionally attuned reflections
- Consistent collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing
- Strong autonomy support with language emphasizing client ownership
- Skillful evocation of client's motivations and resources
- Genuine warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout
- Effective reinforcement of change talk through strategic reflections

**Primary Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Insufficient softening of core sustain talk** about internal motivation—the central issue client identified. Ambivalence is reflected but not strategically redirected toward change talk.
2. **Limited deep exploration of intrinsic motivation** independent of family expectations. Session could more explicitly evoke personal values and reasons for change beyond external pressures.
3. **Occasional expert-like interpretations** that position clinician as interpreter rather than co-constructor of meaning. Checking in with client about whether interpretations resonate would deepen partnership.

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to increase client engagement and willingness to try the action step, with confidence moving from baseline to 6/10. However, the session may not fully resolve the client's core ambivalence about whether he is stopping for himself or for others—the fundamental issue he identified as central to his struggle. With refinement in the areas noted above, this clinician could move from good to excellent MI practice and significantly enhance client motivation for sustained change.

**Recommended Focus Areas for Development:**
- Deepen exploration of intrinsic motivation through targeted evocative questioning
- Strengthen strategic softening of sustain talk with more direct reframing
- Practice co-constructing interpretations with clients rather than offering them
- Explicitly normalize shame and ambivalence as common recovery experiences
- Deepen exploration of past successes and resilience in more specific detail
