Expressions like "it's not so simple," "it's complicated," "I don't want to set myself up to fail" are expressions of ambivalence and fear, not arguments against change. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on relevant health behaviors (exercise, diet, sleep)
- Provides one moment of positive feedback (affirms non-smoking status)
- Prevents session from drifting into unrelated areas

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Complete absence of acceptance and empathy:** Judges, dismisses, and shames rather than validates and understands
- **Authoritarian stance:** Tells rather than asks; imposes solutions; communicates client has no expertise about own life
- **Undermines autonomy:** Uses controlling language; demands compliance; punishes client for exercising choice
- **Ignores client motivation:** Substantial change talk completely ignored and never amplified; imposes external motivation through shame
- **Creates defensiveness:** Repeated accusations transform motivated client into defensive one
- **Non-responsive:** Repeats opening statement verbatim multiple times, suggesting scripted approach

### Impact on Client Engagement:
**This session is likely to DECREASE intrinsic motivation and INCREASE resistance.** Despite entering with strong change talk and genuine desire to improve, client leaves increasingly defensive and less likely to follow through. Psychiatrist has transformed motivated client into resistant one—opposite of MI's goal. Client likely to seek different provider or abandon change effort rather than comply with demands.

### Critical Recommendation:
**This psychiatrist should not continue providing MI-focused care until completing formal Motivational Interviewing training and demonstrating competence through supervised practice and MITI coding.** Current approach is contraindicated for behavior change counseling and violates collaborative, client-centered principles essential to effective health behavior intervention.
