Direction could be strengthened by explicitly checking whether conversation framing is the priority or if other barriers (timing, location, managing anxiety) should be addressed first. Maintains strategic focus without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine invitation for Oliver's input on language and approach. Oliver actively shapes the suggested message ("That feels more me"), demonstrating true partnership. Could strengthen collaboration by exploring Oliver's existing ideas before offering clinician suggestions, and by asking what he thinks might help before proposing solutions.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Oliver's right to choose with explicit permission-seeking ("Would it be okay if I shared a few ideas?"). Avoids pressure language and positions Oliver as decision-maker. Minor opportunities to avoid subtle approval-seeking language (e.g., "It's really encouraging to hear...") and more explicitly affirm Oliver's right to move at his own pace or not pursue change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation of Oliver's perspective early in session through open questions ("What would make this more manageable?"). However, significant shift toward advice-giving in latter half rather than drawing out Oliver's intrinsic motivation. Missed opportunities to amplify Oliver's own change talk statements (e.g., "I have to be more assertive," "I told him a couple of times") and explore his existing competence and wisdom.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early partnership gradually shifts toward expert-driven stance, particularly around conversation framing. Psychiatrist positions suggested language as "the approach" before exploring Oliver's existing knowledge and communication style. True partnership would involve asking "What have you already thought about saying?" before offering clinician suggestions, positioning Oliver's experience as primary source of wisdom.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Multiple instances of Oliver's change talk go largely unaddressed: "I have to be more assertive," "My job is done," "I told him a couple of times," "I just need to get past that hesitation." Psychiatrist does not systematically evoke or amplify desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment for change. Missed opportunities to ask "What helped you move from 5 to 7?" or "Tell me more about that realization" to deepen motivation language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate use of double-sided reflections acknowledging ambivalence, but inconsistent strategic redirection of sustain talk. When Oliver expresses self-criticism ("I'm weak in that area") or concerns about hurting his friend, psychiatrist does not adequately reframe these as evidence of values and strengths. Could strengthen by saying "Your concern shows how much you care—that's a strength you can use in this conversation."

---

## Overall Summary

**Strengths:**
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and warm, nonjudgmental stance
- Accurate reflective listening capturing emotional complexity and nuance
- Strong collaborative approach to developing conversation language with genuine invitation for Oliver's input
- Explicit respect for autonomy and choice throughout session
- Clear, logical session structure maintaining focus on assertiveness and boundary-setting
- Effective affirmations of Oliver's values and strengths
- Skillful use of scaling questions to assess readiness

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Insufficient cultivation and amplification of Oliver's change talk; multiple motivation statements go unaddressed
- Gradual shift from partnership toward expert-driven advice-giving, particularly around conversation framing
- Limited exploration of Oliver's existing knowledge and experience before offering suggestions
- Inconsistent strategic redirection of sustain talk; missed opportunities to reframe concerns as strengths
- Minimal evocation of intrinsic motivation; session becomes increasingly clinician-directed in latter half
- Subtle directiveness toward higher readiness without fully accepting current ambivalence
- Limited exploration of what Oliver's hesitation specifically means or what fears underlie it

---

## Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Systematically cultivate and amplify Oliver's change talk** by explicitly reflecting and building upon statements reflecting desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. Use follow-up questions to deepen motivation language rather than moving to other topics.

2. **Explore Oliver's existing knowledge before offering suggestions** by asking "What have you already thought about?" and "What does your gut tell you?" Position his experience as primary source of wisdom before introducing clinician ideas.

3. **Strategically reframe sustain talk as strengths** by connecting Oliver's concerns (guilt, worry about hurting friend) to underlying values. Example: "That guilt tells me how much you care—that same care can help you have this conversation respectfully."

4. **Deepen exploration of hesitation** by asking curious questions about what specifically Oliver fears rather than encouraging him past doubt. Ask "What would need to be different for you to feel ready?"

5. **Maintain stronger partnership by asking before telling** in at least 80% of advice-giving moments. Frame suggestions as options to consider rather than expert recommendations.

6. **Use scaling questions more strategically** by following up with questions that evoke change talk: "What's helped you get to a 7?" or "Why is a 7 and not a 4?"

7. **Avoid subtle approval-seeking language** that pressures toward change. Replace "It's encouraging to hear..." with neutral acknowledgment: "You've moved to a 7. That's where you are right now."

8. **Explicitly affirm Oliver's right to self-determination** by periodically reminding him he gets to decide timing, approach, and pace of change with no "right" speed.

---

### **Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates adequate foundational MI competence with solid relational skills (acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support) but significant gaps in core MI technical skills (evocation, cultivation of change talk, strategic handling of sustain talk). The psychiatrist creates a warm, respectful environment and maintains clear focus, which are important strengths. However, the session falls short of exemplary MI practice by not systematically cultivating Oliver's intrinsic motivation through amplification of his own change talk. The gradual shift toward expert-driven advice-giving, while collaborative in form, subtly positions the psychiatrist's suggestions as the primary driver of change rather than Oliver's own wisdom and values. Oliver demonstrates increasing readiness (5-6 to 7) and agrees to have the conversation, but this appears somewhat compliance-based rather than deeply intrinsically motivated. To improve, focus on systematically cultivating change talk, exploring existing knowledge before suggesting, reframing sustain talk as strengths, and maintaining stronger partnership through asking before telling.
