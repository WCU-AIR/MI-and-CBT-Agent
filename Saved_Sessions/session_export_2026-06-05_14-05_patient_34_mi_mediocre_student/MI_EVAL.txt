### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Collaboration is virtually absent—one of the most significant deficiencies. Psychiatrist consistently uses directive language ("you should," "it would probably help if," "maybe you should") rather than collaborative language. Suggestions framed as expert recommendations rather than joint exploration. Psychiatrist never asks Diego what barriers he perceives or what solutions he might generate. Power dynamic entirely expert-driven. Diego's ideas acknowledged but not genuinely incorporated into session direction. Final plan summarized as psychiatrist's plan rather than co-created with Diego.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy support minimal and frequently undermined. While no overt pressure or threats, language consistently implies Diego should be doing more than ready for. Phrase "It would probably help if..." appears multiple times, subtly pressuring toward psychiatrist's agenda. When Diego states "Taking it slow feels better for me," psychiatrist responds with "but making some moves now could keep you safer"—subtle contradiction that undermines autonomy. Conditional acceptance rather than genuine support. No explicit affirmation of Diego's right to choose his own path or pace.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation largely absent—critical MI failure. Psychiatrist rarely asks open-ended questions to draw out Diego's motivations, values, and resources. Instead uses closed or leading questions guiding toward predetermined answers ("Do you think your heroin use is causing problems?" "Have you noticed difficulties?"). Diagnostic rather than evocative approach. When Diego spontaneously generates strong change talk ("I'm really ready to do that—like 10 out of 10 ready"), psychiatrist does not pause to explore or amplify. Immediately adds new suggestions, implicitly communicating Diego's ideas are insufficient. Never asks about values, hopes, strengths, or Diego's own ideas for change.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership essentially absent. Psychiatrist assumes expert role throughout, never genuinely sharing power with Diego. Expert-driven framing ("I thought we could talk about ways to improve your well-being"). Consistent use of language positioning psychiatrist as knower and Diego as recipient ("It sounds like you use it to relax...which is pretty common"; "It would probably help if..."; "It's important to keep taking these steps"). Never acknowledges Diego's expertise about his own life, needs, or readiness. Summary frames plan as psychiatrist-constructed, not co-created. Opposite of true partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk present in Diego's speech but not skillfully cultivated or amplified. Diego generates strong change talk spontaneously ("I'm really ready to do that—like 10 out of 10 ready"; "I'll look for info on safer injecting and talk to my housemate"). Psychiatrist fails to pause, reflect, or amplify. Instead immediately pivots to new questions and introduces competing goal (quit date) Diego not ready for, potentially undermining confidence. Psychiatrist never asks about benefits, strengths, or reasons for change. Diagnostic questions about problems and barriers used instead of strategic evocation. Change talk acknowledged minimally but not deepened or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal but adequate handling of sustain talk. Psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify Diego's sustain talk ("It helps me relax and forget"; "It really helps me forget the stuff I don't want to face"), which is appropriate. However, does not skillfully soften or reframe using double-sided reflection. Maintains neutral stance but misses opportunities to explore ambivalence more deeply. When Diego expresses difficulty stopping, psychiatrist responds with questions about barriers rather than reflecting both sides of ambivalence. Never explicitly validates relief heroin provides while simultaneously exploring concerns—essence of softening sustain talk in MI.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Fidelity Score: 1.9/5**

---

### Summary of Strengths
- Maintains generally respectful, non-confrontational tone throughout
- Appropriately focuses on harm reduction rather than abstinence-only goals, aligning with Diego's stage of change
- Eventually helps Diego commit to specific, concrete, achievable goals (safer injecting info, talking to housemate)
- Acknowledges that "taking it slow is okay," showing some respect for Diego's pace
- Avoids explicit criticism or labeling of heroin use

---

### Summary of Critical Areas for Improvement

**Absence of Genuine Collaboration and Partnership:**
Psychiatrist maintains expert-driven stance throughout, using directive language ("you should," "it would probably help if") rather than collaborative exploration. This is the foundational MI principle most absent in this session.

**Minimal Acceptance and Affirmation:**
Fails to affirm Diego's existing harm-reduction efforts, readiness for change, or emotional struggles. No explicit validation of strengths or willingness to engage.

**Shallow Empathy:**
Reflects content but misses emotional depth. Core issue—Diego's pain and unresolved trauma—never explored or reflected.

**Failure to Evoke:**
Asks leading or closed questions rather than open-ended exploration. When Diego spontaneously generates strong change talk, psychiatrist does not pause to amplify it.

**Prescriptive Rather Than Evocative:**
Tells Diego what to do rather than drawing out Diego's own ideas, motivations, and resources.

**Undermining Autonomy:**
Uses subtle pressure ("but making some moves now could keep you safer") and conditional acceptance rather than genuine autonomy support.

**Missed Opportunities to Amplify Change Talk:**
Diego's statement "I'm really ready to do that—like 10 out of 10 ready" minimally acknowledged and not explored further.

**Introduction of Competing Goals:**
Suggests quit date when Diego explicitly stated not ready for that, potentially undermining confidence in harm-reduction goals he *is* ready for.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Likelihood of Sustained Change

**Session Does NOT Embody MI Spirit:**
While Diego remains cooperative and agrees to specific goals, his intrinsic motivation is not deepened or strengthened. Psychiatrist used compliance-gaining techniques (directive suggestions, leading questions) rather than MI techniques (open-ended exploration, amplification of change talk, genuine partnership). Diego leaves with a plan but may not feel genuinely understood, supported, or empowered—hallmarks of effective MI and drivers of lasting change. Likelihood of sustained engagement with harm reduction or eventual movement toward abstinence is lower than with skillful MI practice. Short-term compliance achieved at cost of long-term intrinsic motivation and therapeutic alliance.

---

### Primary Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative partnership** — Replace "you should" with "What do you think about...?" Position Diego as expert on his own life.

2. **Amplify change talk rather than introducing new goals** — When Diego expresses strong readiness, pause and deepen that motivation through exploration and reflection.

3. **Establish genuine acceptance and affirmation** — Explicitly affirm Diego's existing harm-reduction efforts, readiness for change, and emotional struggles.

4. **Deepen empathy by exploring emotional content** — Move beyond surface reflections to capture emotional nuance, meaning, and underlying pain.

5. **Ask open-ended questions to evoke** — Replace closed and leading questions with genuine exploration designed to draw out client's own motivations and resources.

6. **Support autonomy by respecting pace** — Remove subtle pressure language and explicitly affirm Diego's right to choose his own direction and pace.

7. **Avoid introducing competing goals** — Focus exclusively on client's primary goal until they express readiness for additional goals.

8. **Use double-sided reflection** — Acknowledge both sustain talk and change talk to soften sustain talk without reinforcing it.

9. **Summarize as co-created plan** — Invite client to confirm and refine plan, positioning it as jointly created rather than psychiatrist-constructed.

10. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** — Attend MINT-certified MI training and engage in ongoing supervision with MI-trained supervisor using MITI coding system.

---

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist would benefit substantially from formal Motivational Interviewing training and ongoing clinical supervision to develop authentic MI competence and shift from expert-driven to collaborative, evocative practice.
