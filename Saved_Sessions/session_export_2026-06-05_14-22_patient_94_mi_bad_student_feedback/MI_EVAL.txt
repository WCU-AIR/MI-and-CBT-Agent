**Comments:** Systematic undermining of client autonomy throughout session. Repeated use of controlling language: "You really should quit," "You have to take this seriously," "You must quit now," "Are you going to stop drinking entirely starting today? It's the only way." Explicit denial of autonomy ("You don't get to decide that") represents autonomy violation, not support. ### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost no evocation present. Psychiatrist tells client what to do rather than asking what client thinks, wants, or values. Opening statement ("You really should quit those habits because they're harmful") installs psychiatrist's reasons rather than evoking client's own. When client produces change talk ("I hadn't realized my drinking was connected to my fall," "Maybe I should think about cutting down"), psychiatrist does not evoke or amplify it; instead dismisses modest goal as insufficient ("Cutting back is just a half-measure"). Closed questions used throughout ("Are you ready to commit?", "Are you going to stop entirely today?") do not invite exploration of client's own motivations, values, or resources. No questions like "What concerns do you have?" or "What would be the benefits?" Single evocative question in later exchange ("What are some small steps you feel comfortable taking?") represents only instance of genuine evocation and comes after significant damage.

### Summary of Critical Deficiencies

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance that violates core MI values across nearly every dimension:

- **Absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard** creates hostile therapeutic environment
- **Lack of genuine empathy** prevents understanding of client's perspective and context
- **Authoritarian rather than collaborative approach** treats client as object to be controlled
- **Failure to evoke intrinsic motivation** imposes external pressure likely to increase resistance
- **Systematic undermining of autonomy** violates fundamental MI principle of self-determination
- **Dismissal of change talk** when it does occur reduces client's own motivation
- **Confrontational approach to sustain talk** entrenches defensiveness rather than skillfully redirecting

**Most egregious violation:** Explicit denial of client's right to participate in decision-making ("You don't get to decide that") is dehumanizing and directly contradicts MI's core principle that clients possess inherent worth and dignity.

---

### Predicted Impact on Client Engagement and Change

**Likelihood of evoking client motivation for change: DECREASED**

This session is likely to have a **negative impact** on Margaret's motivation and therapeutic engagement:

- **Increased sustain talk:** Client more likely to generate reasons why she can't or won't change in response to pressure
- **Damaged therapeutic relationship:** Client feels judged, disrespected, and unheard; undermines trust and collaboration
- **Reduced intrinsic motivation:** External pressure reduces likelihood client will develop own reasons for change
- **Increased likelihood of dropout:** Client may decide not to return for follow-up given hostile, controlling nature of interaction
- **Reactance pattern evident:** Client's responses show increasing defensiveness as session progresses—classic pattern when autonomy is threatened

### Critical Recommendations for Improvement

**HIGHEST PRIORITY:**

1. **Adopt genuine partnership stance** – Begin sessions with authentic curiosity about client's perspective rather than judgment
2. **Eliminate controlling language** – Remove "you have to," "you must," "you should"; replace with autonomy-supportive language
3. **Develop empathic listening skills** – Learn to reflect and explore client's experience rather than dismiss it
4. **Learn to evoke rather than install** – Ask questions that draw out client's own reasons for change
5. **Respect client autonomy** – Acknowledge client's right to choose, even when you disagree

**ESSENTIAL ACTION:**

**Enroll in formal Motivational Interviewing training program immediately.** This clinician is operating at pre-MI competency level and should not continue conducting MI sessions without structured training, supervised practice, and feedback. Current approach is likely to harm clients by increasing defensiveness, damaging therapeutic relationships, and reducing intrinsic motivation for change.
