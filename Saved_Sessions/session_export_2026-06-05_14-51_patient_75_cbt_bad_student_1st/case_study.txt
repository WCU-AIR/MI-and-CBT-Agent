--- CASE STUDY ---

Vance Orren was a 28-year-old self-reported cisgender heterosexual man who was arrested after pushing a stranger in front of an oncoming subway train. He believed the man was going to "tell everyone that I was a faggot" and acted to "protect himself from the homosexual conspiracy."

Mr. Orren had a history of psychotic disorder, cocaine use disorder, alcohol use disorder, and repeated nonadherence to psychiatric medications and psychotherapy. He was unemployed at the time of the incident. In court, he entered a plea of not guilty by reason of mental disease ("insanity defense") and underwent a full psychiatric evaluation, including a structured sex offender assessment.

His sexual history began in foster care at age 12, with sexual contact involving his uncle and older cousin. By ages 14–15, he reported sexual activity with minors and adults, often involving sex in exchange for money or drugs. As an adult, he reported a primary sexual attraction to girls aged 8–13. He had acted on these urges and demonstrated arousal to children through formal evaluation tools, including penile plethysmography and Viewing Time tests.

Mr. Orren experienced multiple childhood disruptions, including placement in foster care at age 7, early criminal behavior, and early substance use beginning at age 11. He dropped out of school in eighth grade and had at least seven psychiatric hospitalizations for auditory hallucinations, persecutory delusions, and psychosis of uncertain type. He had a history of repeated cocaine and alcohol abuse, intermittent periods of sobriety during hospitalizations or incarceration, and chronic nonadherence to treatment. His grandmother described him as "reckless, dishonest, and angry."

Discussion: Mr. Orren meets criteria for several DSM-5 disorders: unspecified schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder, cocaine and alcohol use disorders, antisocial personality disorder with conduct disorder onset before age 15, and pedophilic disorder, nonexclusive type. He demonstrates sexual arousal to prepubescent children, has acted on these urges, and exhibits longstanding antisocial behaviors unrelated solely to substance acquisition or psychosis.
