## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Quality Rating: POOR (0.8/6)**

**Key Findings:**

This session represents a fundamental failure to deliver evidence-based cognitive-behavioral therapy. The therapist demonstrated minimal competency across core CBT domains and failed to address serious clinical safety concerns, most notably active suicidal ideation.

**Critical Safety Issue:**

The patient explicitly stated "Sometimes I think people would be better off if I just disappear." This disclosure of suicidal ideation was not assessed, explored, or addressed. The therapist's response was to suggest finding things that make you smile and watching funny movies. This represents a dangerous clinical and ethical lapse requiring immediate remediation.

**Structural Deficiencies:**

- No agenda established; session drifted aimlessly
- No guided discovery or Socratic questioning employed
- No CBT techniques applied; only generic advice offered
- No homework assigned
- No coherent treatment strategy developed
- No focus on central cognitions/behaviors maintaining patient's distress

**Therapeutic Relationship Deficiencies:**

- Excessive self-disclosure by therapist (personal anecdotes dominated)
- Invalidation of patient's genuine concerns through premature reassurance
- Minimal feedback elicitation; therapist did not adjust when patient said interventions felt "fake and pointless"
- Collaboration minimal; patient became increasingly passive and disengaged

**Clinical Deficiencies:**

- Failed to systematically assess active psychotic symptoms
- Failed to assess substance use patterns and triggers
- Failed to explore recent violent offense or current risk factors
- Failed to establish clear evaluation purpose in forensic psychiatric context
- Failed to develop individualized treatment plan

**Recommendation:**

This therapist requires comprehensive remediation before continuing independent practice with complex psychiatric patients. Immediate priorities: (1) Safety assessment training for suicidal ideation; (2) Structured CBT training with competency verification; (3) Specialized training in psychosis and substance use disorders; (4) Clinical supervision with competency-based feedback; (5) Reassessment before independent practice resumption.
