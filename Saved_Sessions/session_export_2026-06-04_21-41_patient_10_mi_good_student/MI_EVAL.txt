# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth with genuine affirmations ("You've shown a lot of strength in managing those conversations"). Validation of emotional experience is present throughout. However, opportunities for explicit character affirmation as a committed mother and deeper normalization of core identity are missed. Direct statements affirming her worth and character would strengthen the therapeutic alliance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with several emotionally attuned reflections capturing nuance ("You're carrying a heavy sense of responsibility"; "You want to believe in the possibility of change but feel unsure if that's realistic"). Perspective-taking is evident. However, some deeper emotional undertones are missed—particularly the exhaustion and demoralization when she expresses feeling like "the only one trying," and the anxiety underlying her protective instinct about making things worse.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus maintained on improving co-parenting communication without wandering. Session progresses naturally from general concern to specific barriers to past successes to concrete action steps. Client-centered guidance is collaborative rather than directive. However, deeper exploration of longer-term vision and what would move her from confidence level 4 to 6 or 7 could provide stronger directional clarity and deeper motivation.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with frequent invitations for Amina's input ("What do you think would be the most important thing to focus on?"). Clinician builds on her ideas and treats suggestions as valuable contributions. However, collaborative problem-solving around her concern about her ex-partner's willingness to engage could be deeper. Follow-up support could be co-created rather than offered unilaterally.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout. Consistent emphasis on Amina's choice, agency, and control ("something you can start on your own, on your own terms"). Clinician avoids prescription, acknowledges limits of what she can control while focusing on her agency, and validates her hesitation as reasonable. No coercion or pressure present. This represents exceptional MI practice.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic evocation present through open questions ("What have you found in the past that helped?"), but significant opportunities missed to deeply draw out motivations, values, and resources. Limited exploration of what "the best for him" means, why staying calm works, or what her capabilities reveal about her. Insufficient strategic amplification of change talk. Confidence scaling used as measurement rather than strategic tool to evoke reasons for hope. Surface-level rather than strategic motivation-building.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid partnership demonstrated with genuine power-sharing and positioning of Amina as the expert on her own experience. Clinician builds on her ideas and invites her wisdom. However, partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging limits of clinician's role and inviting collaborative co-creation of support. When Amina expresses uncertainty about her ex-partner, deeper collaborative problem-solving around her own thinking would enhance partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk present and reflected back ("You've shown a lot of strength"), but limited strategic, deliberate cultivation. Missed opportunities to amplify existing change talk about loving her son, staying calm, and willingness to try. Insufficient use of open questions to evoke reasons for change, ability talk, and commitment language. When confidence scaling occurs, follow-up questions to explore "what's the 4 or 5 about?" are absent. Session demonstrates supportive counseling rather than strategic MI motivation-building.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate management of sustain talk through validation and redirection toward Amina's agency ("something within your control"). However, limited use of sophisticated MI techniques. Double-sided reflections are absent. Reframing of barriers as opportunities for problem-solving is minimal. Deep exploration of fears underlying sustain talk (worry about making things worse, doubt about ex-partner's willingness) is insufficient. Clinician validates concerns but doesn't help Amina examine them closely or access evidence that contradicts them.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Current Competency Level:** Intermediate MI Competence

**Overall Session Score:** 3.7/5

### Key Strengths
- ✓ Exemplary autonomy support with consistent emphasis on client choice and agency
- ✓ Strong collaborative stance positioning client as expert on own experience
- ✓ Solid acceptance with consistent warmth, validation, and nonjudgmental support
- ✓ Good reflective listening with several emotionally attuned reflections
- ✓ Clear, appropriate direction maintained throughout session
- ✓ Practical outcomes: specific, manageable action step with improved confidence (4-5 to 6)

### Areas for Development
- ⚠ **Strategic evocation of change talk:** Limited deliberate cultivation of client's own language in favor of change; surface-level rather than deep exploration of values, desires, reasons, and ability
- ⚠ **Depth of motivation exploration:** Reflects client's love for son but doesn't deeply probe what this means, what kind of mother she wants to be, or what values guide her parenting
- ⚠ **Past successes exploration:** Identifies that staying calm helped but doesn't deeply explore why, how, or what it reveals about capabilities
- ⚠ **Strategic confidence scaling:** Uses scaling as measurement rather than strategic tool to evoke change talk and reasons for hope
- ⚠ **Sophisticated sustain talk management:** Validates concerns but lacks double-sided reflections, reframing, and deep exploration of underlying fears
- ⚠ **Character affirmation:** Limited explicit affirmation of identity as committed mother and resilience
- ⚠ **Co-creation of support:** Offers support unilaterally rather than inviting collaborative design of follow-up

### Impact on Client Engagement
The session maintains Amina's engagement and supports movement toward change. Her confidence increased and she identified concrete action steps, suggesting solid foundational MI practice. However, the session falls short of generating the strongest possible intrinsic motivation for sustained change effort, particularly when facing obstacles or resistance from her ex-partner.

### Recommendations for Growth
1. Strategically evoke and amplify change talk through deliberate, repeated open questions
2. Use confidence scaling as strategic MI tool with follow-up questions exploring reasons for ratings
3. Deeply explore values and identity as a mother to tap intrinsic motivation
4. Employ double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without reinforcing sustain talk
5. Thoroughly explore past successes to build ability talk and self-efficacy
6. Provide explicit character affirmations grounded in observed strengths
7. Reframe barriers as opportunities for problem-solving within client's control
8. Invite collaborative co-creation of support and accountability structures
9. Develop deeper exploration of fears underlying sustain talk
10. Increase frequency and strategic use of open questions (target 60-70% of questions)

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** This clinician demonstrates solid foundational MI skills with particular excellence in autonomy support and collaboration. With focused practice on strategic evocation, deeper exploration of values and motivations, and sophisticated management of ambivalence, advancement to advanced MI competence is highly achievable. Recommend targeted supervision focusing on recording and analyzing change talk cultivation and sustain talk management.
