## Summary of Findings

### Critical Deficiencies

This session represents a **complete departure from motivational interviewing principles** and demonstrates **confrontational, directive, authoritarian practice** that is clinically harmful. The psychiatrist operates from a "tell and convince" model rather than an "evoke and explore" model. Every core MI principle is violated across all nine domains.

**Key Problems:**
- Judges and shames rather than accepts
- Interrogates rather than listens
- Prescribes rather than collaborates
- Controls rather than supports autonomy
- Imposes rather than evokes
- Increases defensiveness and sustain talk rather than decreasing it

### Impact on Patient Engagement and Outcomes

This approach will **significantly decrease the likelihood of sustained behavior change**. Patient has explicitly stated inability to commit to plan that doesn't feel like his own. Therapeutic relationship has been damaged. Patient has learned this clinician is not safe for exploring ambivalence or vulnerability. From developmental perspective, denying autonomy and framing social drinking as moral failure positions psychiatrist as adversary rather than ally, particularly harmful for 17-year-old for whom peer relationships and autonomy are developmentally central.

### Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Foundational shift:** Adopt genuine MI spirit—belief that patient has wisdom about his own life and motivation resides within him
2. **Replace interrogation with open-ended questions:** Eliminate "yes or no" questions; generate genuine exploratory questions
3. **Implement reflective listening:** Pause after each patient statement and reflect back what was heard
4. **Soften sustain talk:** Use double-sided reflections instead of confrontation
5. **Amplify change talk:** Immediately reflect and explore patient's own motivations for change
6. **Shift to collaborative partnership:** Explicitly invite patient input; use collaborative language
7. **Respect autonomy:** Eliminate prescriptive language; replace with autonomy-supportive alternatives
8. **Develop genuine empathy:** Spend time understanding patient's developmental stage and perspective
9. **Eliminate shaming; affirm strengths:** Remove judgmental language; explicitly affirm patient's strengths
10. **Engage in formal MI training and supervision:** Attend MI workshop; engage in ongoing supervision with MI-trained supervisor; record and review sessions

---

**Overall Assessment:** This psychiatrist requires fundamental retraining in motivational interviewing principles and practice. Current approach is contraindicated and likely to harm patient outcomes. Commitment to formal training, supervision, and paradigm shift is essential.
