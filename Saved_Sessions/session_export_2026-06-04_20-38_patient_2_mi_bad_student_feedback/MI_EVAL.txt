## Overall Session Assessment

### **Overall MI Competency Score: 1.0/5**

---

### Summary of Strengths
- **Consistent focus on target behavior:** Psychiatrist maintains clear attention on smoking cessation and does not allow conversation to wander
- **Genuine concern communicated:** Clinician clearly expresses concern for Ahmed's health
- **Session engagement:** Psychiatrist remains attentive and engaged throughout interaction

---

### Summary of Critical Deficiencies

**1. Systematic Use of Coercive Language and Control Tactics**
Repeated use of "You have to," "You must," "This isn't optional," "No more delays" communicates that Ahmed's autonomy is not respected and compliance is demanded. Ahmed explicitly responds with increased resistance: "I need to be the one to decide when it's time." Psychiatrist intensifies pressure in response, violating core MI principles.

**2. Complete Absence of Genuine Empathy and Perspective-Taking**
Psychiatrist never seeks to understand Ahmed's worldview, never reflects his experience, and never demonstrates curiosity about his reasoning. Instead, clinician repeatedly argues against Ahmed's perspective. Ahmed explicitly articulates his need ("You want me to want this, not just be ordered to do it"), and psychiatrist ignores this entirely.

**3. Failure to Evoke Ahmed's Own Motivation for Change**
Rather than exploring Ahmed's values, goals, and intrinsic reasons for change, psychiatrist imposes external motivation through fear-based lectures. Ahmed has not been invited to explore what matters to him or how change might serve his goals. This approach is ineffective because it does not address Ahmed's actual ambivalence.

**4. Confrontation of Sustain Talk Rather Than Skillful Softening**
Each time Ahmed expresses a reason against change, psychiatrist responds with direct argument. This predictably strengthens Ahmed's sustain talk and increases defensiveness. By end of session, Ahmed's resistance has intensified rather than diminished.

**5. Authoritarian Expert-Driven Stance with Zero Collaboration**
Psychiatrist makes unilateral decisions and demands compliance rather than co-constructing solutions with Ahmed. Explicitly announces: "I'm going to tell you what you need to do to quit." Ahmed's contributions are consistently dismissed rather than incorporated.

---

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change Likelihood

**This session is highly likely to DECREASE Ahmed's motivation for change and DAMAGE the therapeutic relationship.**

Ahmed has moved from precontemplation (not thinking about change) to active resistance (defending against pressure). Psychological reactance has been triggered—Ahmed becomes more entrenched in defending current behavior precisely because autonomy is threatened. Ahmed is unlikely to return for follow-up or trust this clinician's guidance.

**Research on motivational interviewing consistently demonstrates that coercive, confrontational approaches are ineffective for behavior change and often backfire.** This session represents a fundamental departure from evidence-based practice.

---

### Critical Recommendations for Improvement

**1. Eliminate Coercive Language**
Replace "You have to/must" with collaborative invitations: "I'm wondering if," "What do you think about," "Would you be open to exploring?"

**2. Develop Genuine Empathic Listening**
Before responding, reflect back what you hear to demonstrate understanding. Use complex reflections that capture both content and emotion.

**3. Evoke Rather Than Impose Motivation**
Ask open-ended questions about Ahmed's values, goals, and intrinsic reasons for change. Explore what matters to him rather than lecturing about consequences.

**4. Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk**
Use double-sided reflections, reframing, and strategic agreement rather than direct argument when Ahmed expresses reasons against change.

**5. Respect and Support Autonomy**
Consistently affirm Ahmed's right to choose. Emphasize that change must come from his own motivation. Avoid pressure or ultimatums.

**6. Cultivate and Reinforce Change Talk**
Listen for any statements Ahmed makes about desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change. Reflect these back and ask open questions to develop them further.

**7. Establish Genuine Partnership**
Invite Ahmed's input on session direction. Acknowledge his expertise about his own life. Co-construct solutions rather than prescribing them.

**8. Seek Formal MI Training and Supervision**
Engage in MINT-certified MI training and participate in regular supervision with MI-trained supervisors. Record and review sessions for MI fidelity using the MITI scale.
