### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely absent. Opening explicitly rejects collaboration: "I'll be telling you exactly what steps to take." Hierarchical, expert-driven dynamic established from beginning. Unilateral decisions made without seeking client input or considering his perspective valuable. Client's attempts to introduce concerns (court requirements, fear of judgment) dismissed as obstacles rather than incorporated into discussion. No joint problem-solving or shared decision-making. Questions are accusatory, not collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Fundamentally violated throughout. Repeated controlling language: "you have to," "you must," "you need to," "you can't," "this isn't optional." Communicates that client has no choice; compliance is mandatory. Pressure applied when client expresses hesitation ("No, you can't keep putting it off"). Client's prioritization of court requirements dismissed: "Your health has to come first, no matter what else is going on." No affirmation of client's right to choose or emphasis on his ownership of change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely absent. Operates from "tell and convince" model rather than "ask and explore." Imposes reasons for change ("Smoking is really bad for your health," "you're risking your health and your future") rather than evoking client's own motivations. Minimal change talk ("I've thought about quitting") not amplified or explored. No questions about client's values, what matters to him, or benefits of change. Client's core value (self-respect/strength) revealed but not evoked or connected to change motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Completely absent. Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role and maintains power throughout. Opening explicitly rejects partnership. Client's perspective treated as obstacles to overcome rather than expertise about his own life. Unilateral decisions made about what is important, what client should do, and how client should feel. Client's contributions do not substantially influence session direction. Hierarchical stance maintained throughout with implicit message: "I know what's best for you; you must comply."

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Neither evoked nor cultivated. No strategic effort to encourage client's own language favoring quitting or to build confidence for change. Minimal change talk ("I've thought about quitting," "I know I have to do something about it") not amplified or reinforced. No questions designed to evoke change talk (e.g., "What concerns do you have about smoking?" "How might quitting fit with your goals?"). Fear-based statements and pressure used instead. By session end, only compliance talk produced: "Yeah, I get that I should," not genuine motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Problematic and inconsistent with MI principles. Rather than skillfully softening sustain talk through double-sided reflections or reframing, psychiatrist confronts directly with pressure and criticism. Confrontational questions used ("Why haven't you quit yet?"). Direct confrontation of sustain talk ("That's exactly the kind of thinking you need to change") may trigger psychological reactance. Psychiatrist does redirect focus back to target behavior, demonstrating basic sustain talk management, but executed through pressure rather than skillful reframing. No double-sided reflections or strategic softening techniques employed.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Assessment Level:** Pre-MI competency—below threshold for basic MI practice

**Key Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles**. The psychiatrist operates from an authoritarian, expert-driven model that directly contradicts MI spirit and research on effective behavior change counseling.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Systematic violations of MI principles across all nine domains
- Controlling language and pressure tactics undermine autonomy and trigger psychological reactance
- Absence of empathy and partnership replaced with judgment and dismissal
- Imposition of motivation through fear and authority rather than evocation
- Dismissal of legitimate competing priorities as "excuses"

**Likely Client Impact:**
- Decreased intrinsic motivation for change
- Increased resistance and psychological reactance
- Damaged therapeutic alliance
- Compliance talk without genuine commitment
- Likely disengagement from treatment

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. **Fundamental shift in clinical stance** from expert-driven authority to collaborative partnership
2. **Development of genuine empathy** through reflective listening and validation
3. **Elimination of controlling language** and pressure tactics
4. **Adoption of evocation** as primary change strategy
5. **Integration of competing priorities** rather than dismissal

**Required Action:**
Psychiatrist should **immediately engage in intensive MI training and supervision** before continuing MI sessions, including formal training, regular supervision with MI-specific feedback, session recording and review, and practice with feedback until MI competency achieved.
