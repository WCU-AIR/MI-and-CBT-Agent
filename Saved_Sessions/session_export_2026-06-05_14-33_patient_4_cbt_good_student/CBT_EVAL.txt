## SUPERVISOR NOTES & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Strengths to Reinforce:
- Exemplary empathy and understanding of patient's internal experience
- Excellent use of Socratic questioning and guided discovery
- Strong collaborative stance with explicit agenda-setting and feedback elicitation
- Skillful cognitive restructuring with personalized, balanced alternative thought
- Clear, coherent session structure with appropriate pacing

### Critical Areas for Development (Priority Order):

**1. HIGHEST PRIORITY: Functional Analysis of Reassurance-Seeking**
- Reassurance-seeking (repetitive questioning of nurses/family) is a key maintaining factor in adjustment disorder with anxiety
- This behavior was identified but not analyzed for function or targeted with intervention
- Next session: Explore the anxiety maintenance cycle using functional analysis
- Suggested questions: "When you ask for reassurance, how long does the relief last? What happens after? Would you be willing to try delaying reassurance-seeking and using your coping skills instead?"

**2. SECOND PRIORITY: Behavioral Activation**
- Patient has withdrawn from valued activities (crossword puzzles, presumably others)
- Behavioral activation is a core CBT component for depression and adjustment disorder
- Next session: Identify valued activities and schedule one small activity daily (even 5-10 minutes)
- Track mood before and after activity to demonstrate impact

**3. THIRD PRIORITY: Written Homework Materials**
- Current homework was assigned verbally without written form or tracking sheet
- Reduces likelihood of consistent completion and provides no data for next session
- Create one-page homework sheet with: alternative thought (written), breathing instructions, tracking boxes for each night, anxiety rating scale (0-10 before/after)
- Provide thought record form for independent practice during week

**4. FOURTH PRIORITY: Long-Term Treatment Planning**
- Session focused on this week's homework without outlining longer-term plan
- Patient is awaiting thoracentesis results—a critical moment requiring contingency planning
- Next session: Explicitly outline anticipated number of sessions, what happens if medical news is concerning, transition plan if patient is discharged, long-term goals beyond symptom management

**5. FIFTH PRIORITY: Core Beliefs and Identity Work**
- Patient expressed concern about being "the strong one" her family knows
- This identity disruption is a core feature of her distress but was not developed as treatment target
- Deeper exploration could lead to more lasting change
- Suggested approach: Values-based therapy, exploring how patient can live according to her values even while managing illness

### Recommended Supervision Focus:
- Role-play functional analysis conversation with reassurance-seeking behavior
- Practice behavioral activation planning and motivational interviewing
- Develop competence in values-based therapy and identity-focused CBT
- Review CBT manuals on adjustment disorder and anxiety to deepen case conceptualization skills
- Practice designing behavioral experiments to test anxiety maintenance hypotheses

### Patient Prognosis:
**Short-term (1-2 weeks):** Likely modest improvement in sleep and bedtime anxiety through cognitive restructuring and deep breathing. Patient has good insight, motivation, and family support.

**Medium-term (4-6 weeks):** Risk of incomplete recovery if reassurance-seeking and behavioral withdrawal are not addressed. Patient may plateau without comprehensive intervention.

**Long-term:** Risk of relapse, particularly if thoracentesis results are concerning and patient lacks robust coping strategies and long-term plan.

**Recommendation:** Continue weekly sessions with focus on comprehensive intervention addressing all maintaining factors. Coordinate with medical team regarding test results and patient's psychological response.
