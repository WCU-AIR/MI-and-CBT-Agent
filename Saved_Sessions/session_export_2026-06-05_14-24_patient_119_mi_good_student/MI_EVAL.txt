Sustain talk handling is respectful but passive; MI requires strategic reflection, reframing, and exploration to soften resistance while maintaining autonomy.

## Summary of Areas for Improvement

- **Minimal evocation:** Rarely draws out motivations, values, or resources; critical change talk left undeveloped
- **Severely underdeveloped change talk cultivation:** No strategic amplification or reinforcement of Seamus's own language about desire, reasons, ability, or need for change
- **Passive handling of sustain talk:** Accepted without strategic redirection; no double-sided reflections or reframing
- **Lack of strategic direction:** Reactive rather than strategically guided; accepts compartmentalization without exploration
- **Limited partnership in decision-making:** Expert-driven stance in treatment planning; limited co-creation of solutions
- **Missed opportunity to explore ambivalence:** No systematic exploration of Seamus's mixed feelings about smoking

---

## Key Recommendations

1. **Develop Strategic Evocation of Change Talk (HIGHEST PRIORITY):** When clients provide change talk, strategically explore and amplify rather than moving past. Example: When Seamus says "I got to really want to," respond with "Tell me more about that. What would need to happen for you to really want to quit?"

2. **Use Double-Sided Reflections to Soften Sustain Talk:** Acknowledge both resistance and potential motivation. Example: "On one hand, you're focused on your teeth right now. On the other hand, you mentioned needing to really want to quit—I'm curious what that would look like for you."

3. **Explore Ambivalence Rather Than Accept Compartmentalization:** Gently explore connections between related health issues. Example: "Do you think the smoking might be connected to some of these dental problems?"

4. **Strategically Explore Values and Intrinsic Motivation:** Move beyond surface goals to deeper values. Ask: "What's most important to you in your life right now?" or "What would be different if you were healthier?"

5. **Reframe Past Failures as Learning Opportunities:** When clients cite past failures, explore what they learned and what might be different. Example: "What do you think you learned from that experience? What might be different this time?"

6. **Strengthen Partnership Through Co-Creation:** Invite client's ideas first before offering suggestions. Example: "What have you been thinking about in terms of how to address this?"

7. **Use Affirmations to Highlight Strengths:** Explicitly affirm client strengths and self-efficacy to build confidence and support autonomy.

8. **Maintain Strategic Direction While Respecting Autonomy:** Gently explore whether compartmentalization serves ultimate health goals while fully respecting autonomy to choose.

9. **Develop Curiosity About Client Perspective:** When clients express resistance, respond with genuine curiosity rather than accepting at face value.

10. **Integrate MI Principles Into Compartmentalization:** When clients separate related issues, ask exploratory questions about connections before accepting the boundary.

---

## Clinical Impact

This session maintains therapeutic alliance but fails to shift Seamus's motivation for broader health change. By accepting compartmentalization and focusing exclusively on dental treatment, critical opportunities to explore ambivalence and evoke intrinsic motivation are missed. Seamus will likely follow through with dental treatment (already motivated) but leaves with reinforced resistance to smoking cessation rather than softened ambivalence. The respectful stance prevents harm but represents a missed opportunity to meet Seamus where he is and gently explore his own reasons for change.
