## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Session Competency Level:** General Supportive Counseling (Not Evidence-Based CBT)

**Primary Strengths:**
- Adequate warmth, safety, and professional demeanor
- Non-hostile, respectful communication
- Patient feels comfortable disclosing concerns

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Absence of CBT structure (no agenda, no strategy, no progression)
- Zero application of evidence-based CBT techniques
- Failure to identify and target maintaining factors (rumination, thought suppression, avoidance)
- Misidentification of primary problem (focuses on sleep hygiene instead of identity-related acceptance anxiety)
- Absence of collaboration (expert-advice model rather than partnership)
- Excessive therapist self-disclosure consuming session time
- No homework structure or follow-up

**Impact on Patient Care:**
- Minimal likelihood of symptom improvement without targeting maintaining factors
- Patient may become dependent on therapist for reassurance rather than developing own anxiety management skills
- Continued rumination and thought suppression likely to persist
- Wasted clinical time and resources on non-evidence-based intervention
- Ethical concern: Patient with history of suicidal ideation deserves evidence-based treatment

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. Establish explicit collaborative agenda at beginning of every session
2. Identify and focus on primary anxiety pattern (identity-related acceptance anxiety)
3. Apply specific CBT techniques (thought records, behavioral experiments, cognitive restructuring)
4. Address thought suppression paradox through behavioral experiment
5. Eliminate excessive self-disclosure
6. Create coherent treatment strategy with clear progression
7. Assign specific, structured homework with rationale and follow-up
