# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard demonstrated throughout. Psychiatrist consistently affirms patient's efforts (e.g., "I really appreciate how much you're doing to protect your son"), normalizes struggle without judgment, and validates ambivalence. No labeling or disapproval present. Patient's worth is communicated as independent of smoking status. Warm, nonjudgmental environment creates psychological safety for honest disclosure.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sophisticated, nuanced reflective listening captures emotional complexity and functional meaning of behavior. Psychiatrist reflects double-sided tensions (e.g., "refuge amidst chaos" while aware of harm), uses meaningful metaphors ("release valve"), and demonstrates understanding of unstated concerns. Reflections deepen insight rather than simply parroting content. Accurately captures patient's capacity constraints and validates them throughout.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, strategic focus maintained on target behavior (smoking) while progressively guiding toward change exploration. Well-sequenced questioning moves from general exploration to confidence scaling to past successes to concrete planning. Session stays focused without drift. Psychiatrist establishes collaborative direction-setting in opening ("what's on your mind") and maintains focus throughout without imposing agenda.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing and joint problem-solving demonstrated consistently. Psychiatrist invites patient's input to shape session ("what kind of support do you think would fit best"), affirms patient's own wisdom about needs, and validates patient-generated solutions without override. Inclusive language ("we," "our," "together") communicates equality. Patient designs own support system with psychiatrist as collaborative partner, not prescriber.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support with no pressure, coercion, or guilt-induction. Language consistently emphasizes choice and patient's right to decide ("if you were to imagine," "even if quitting entirely isn't on the table yet," "if you decided"). Psychiatrist explicitly affirms patient's right to choose pace and approach. Patient positioned as decision-maker throughout; fear and capacity constraints are validated rather than minimized.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out patient's own motivations, values, and resources through strategic questioning. Psychiatrist asks rather than tells (e.g., "What helped you succeed then?"), elicits patient's own ideas about solutions, and reinforces past successes. However, exploration of intrinsic values and personal meaning beyond protecting son could be deeper. Could ask more probing questions about patient's own health values, identity, and what change would mean personally. Opportunity to explore meaning beyond external concerns.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Active fostering of power-sharing and co-creation throughout. Psychiatrist positions patient as expert on own life, uses inclusive language, and explicitly affirms patient's wisdom ("you're thoughtful about what you need to succeed"). Respects patient's boundaries without challenge. Genuine curiosity about patient's perspective allows it to substantially shape session. No moments of expert-driven direction; expertise about change positioned as residing within patient.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk throughout (desire: "I don't want him getting sick"; ability: "I could try to cut down"; reasons: past success with inside-house rule; commitment: tracking triggers proposal). Psychiatrist reframes moderate confidence as positive sign of openness. However, could more strategically amplify change talk through follow-up questions ("Tell me more about that," "What gives you that confidence?"). Could ask more questions designed to elicit commitment talk ("What would it mean for you if...?"). Opportunity to explicitly highlight and deepen change talk when it emerges.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses validation and reframing to soften it. Psychiatrist acknowledges complexity without arguing (e.g., "it's not that simple" met with validation of pressure and complexity). Validates fear about quitting by addressing legitimate barrier (lack of support) rather than arguing. Reframes hesitation as wisdom about capacity. However, could use more double-sided reflections to acknowledge both coping need AND concern about harm. Could more directly highlight discrepancies between patient's values (protecting son) and current behavior (smoking) to create productive cognitive dissonance.

---

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 4.6/5**

This is an exemplary motivational interviewing session demonstrating sophisticated, skillful MI practice across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist creates a warm, nonjudgmental environment characterized by genuine partnership, acceptance, and empathy. Patient is consistently positioned as expert on own life with autonomy respected throughout. Psychiatrist skillfully evokes patient's own motivations, past successes, and resources while strategically guiding toward change exploration without pressure or coercion.

**Key Strengths:**
- Exceptional acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- Sophisticated, nuanced empathy capturing emotional complexity
- Clear, strategic direction with logical progression
- Genuine collaboration with consistent power-sharing
- Strong autonomy support with no coercion
- Skillful evocation of patient's ideas and resources
- True partnership positioning patient as expert
- Effective softening of sustain talk through validation

**Areas for Development:**
- Deeper exploration of intrinsic values and personal meaning beyond protecting son
- More strategic amplification of change talk when it emerges
- More direct use of double-sided reflections for sustain talk
- Additional questions designed to elicit commitment talk
- More explicit highlighting of discrepancies between values and behavior

**Clinical Impact:**
By session end, patient has moved from ambivalence and defensiveness to proposing concrete first step (tracking triggers) and identifying needed support. This represents meaningful progress in contemplation-to-preparation transition. Psychiatrist has successfully created conditions for intrinsic motivation to emerge. Patient feels understood, respected, and partnered with—foundation for sustained behavior change.
