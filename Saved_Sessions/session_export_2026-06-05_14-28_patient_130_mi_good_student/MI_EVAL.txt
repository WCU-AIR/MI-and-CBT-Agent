# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment throughout the session. Explicitly validates Tyler's desire not to be judged and affirms his need for autonomy and belonging. Normalizes adolescent developmental needs rather than pathologizing them. Minor opportunity missed to more explicitly validate Tyler's perspective before introducing change considerations. Warm, supportive stance maintained consistently with no judgmental language.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated throughout. Accurate reflections capture Tyler's emotional experience and underlying needs (belonging, acceptance, freedom from judgment). Examples include reflections on relief from being accepted and understanding of his ambivalence. Reflections are competent but remain at basic to intermediate level rather than complex, deeply insightful reflections. Could more consistently capture emotional undertones and underlying anxiety beneath defensiveness.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** General focus on presenting concerns (substance use, family conflict, peer influence) but lacks strategic clarity and consistency. Session drifts toward exploring peer relationships without maintaining clear connection to substance use as primary target behavior. Early direction-setting is collaborative, but follow-through becomes diffuse. Clinician doesn't clarify specifics of substance use (what was in "stash," frequency of use) or maintain strategic focus on target behavior. Final pivot to past successes is skillful but comes late in session.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing throughout most of session. Opening question positions Tyler as co-creator of agenda. Clinician explicitly validates Tyler's desire to shape conversation and incorporates his feedback. Uses collaborative language ("I'm curious," "I'm wondering") that invites input rather than imposing expertise. Minor moments where suggestions introduced somewhat unilaterally (e.g., readiness scale, "similar approach" suggestion) rather than fully co-constructed with client.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent affirmation of Tyler's right to choose and agency in change process. Avoids prescriptive advice and pressure. Explicitly acknowledges Tyler's autonomy when he rates himself 3 on readiness scale. Evocative rather than directive questioning invites Tyler to consider change without mandating it. Subtle moment where autonomy support could be stronger: suggestion to "try a similar approach" is gently directive. Stronger approach would first ask "What do you think might help?" allowing Tyler to generate own ideas.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questioning present, particularly around values and past successes. Excellent late-session question about managing tough situations draws out Tyler's competence and generates change talk. However, evocation is inconsistent and not strategically amplified. Clinician misses critical opportunities to evoke change talk when sustain talk emerges (e.g., "I don't really see what needs changing"). Relies more on acceptance and empathy than actively drawing out and strengthening Tyler's own motivation for change. Change talk that emerges is accepted but not sufficiently amplified or built upon.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance with genuine power-sharing and respect for client's wisdom. Clinician avoids expert role and incorporates client's contributions meaningfully. Explicitly positions Tyler as having valuable knowledge and capability when affirming his successful de-escalation skills. Demonstrates genuine respect for Tyler's perspective and autonomy. Minor moments where clinical tools (readiness scale) or suggestions introduced somewhat unilaterally rather than fully co-constructed. Overall maintains collaborative, non-hierarchical relationship throughout.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. Minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, and reinforce Tyler's change talk. While some change talk emerges naturally ("I don't really want to fight with her all the time," "I guess sometimes just not making it worse helps," "maybe that's something I can do"), clinician accepts it without actively strengthening or building on it. Critical missed opportunities: (1) When Tyler says "I don't really see what needs changing," sustain talk accepted rather than strategically evoking change talk; (2) When Tyler rates 3 on readiness, no follow-up to ask "What would move you from 3 to 4?"; (3) When Tyler describes successful de-escalation, no amplification through follow-up questions; (4) No strategic use of targeted open questions to evoke specific change talk categories (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Clinician relies on natural emergence rather than actively drawing out and strengthening motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflections effectively (e.g., "It sounds like you're feeling pretty sure that things are okay as they are... At the same time, it sounds like you might be open to exploring..."). Skillfully handles initial resistance without confrontation. However, approach is somewhat passive—accepting sustain talk and gently exploring rather than actively reframing or strategically pivoting. Could employ more sophisticated techniques such as amplified reflection, reframing, or strategic questioning to more effectively soften sustain talk. Misses opportunities to gently explore impact on others and consequences when Tyler states "I'm not hurting anyone."

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level: 3.4/5 (Adequate to Good)**

**Key Strengths:**
- Strong acceptance and unconditional positive regard; avoids judgment
- Solid empathic reflections capturing Tyler's emotional experience
- Genuine collaborative stance with true power-sharing
- Consistent autonomy support without prescription or pressure
- Skillful handling of resistance; uses double-sided reflections effectively
- Warm, supportive tone maintaining genuine connection and respect
- Successfully engages resistant adolescent and reduces defensiveness

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Strategic evocation and amplification of change talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) — Core MI skill significantly underutilized
- **Insufficient cultivation of intrinsic motivation** — Session generates behavioral compliance but limited deep personal motivation
- **Diffuse session direction** — Lacks strategic focus on substance use as primary target behavior
- **Passive handling of sustain talk** — Accepts rather than actively reframes
- **Limited exploration of substance use specifics** — Doesn't clarify what, how much, how often, or consequences
- **Missed opportunities for deeper ambivalence exploration** — Could more explicitly name and explore competing values
- **Late introduction of readiness assessment** — Earlier use could have guided more strategic questioning

**Impact on Client Change Motivation:**

Tyler moves from "I don't really see what needs changing" to "maybe that's something I can do," representing genuine reduction in defensiveness and some movement toward change consideration. However, this movement appears driven primarily by desire to reduce family conflict rather than genuine recognition of substance use risks or deep personal motivation for change. Tyler leaves with behavioral commitment ("maybe just tell her I hear her out") but without deeply exploring why change might matter personally or what he stands to gain. This increases risk that commitment will be fragile and dependent on external pressure rather than intrinsic motivation.

**Clinician Demonstrates:**
- Excellent **relational/alliance-building** aspects of MI (acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support)
- Weakness in **change-evoking** aspects (strategic evocation and amplification of change talk)
- Strong **spirit of MI** (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation)
- Incomplete **operationalization of strategic change-evoking techniques**

**Recommendation:** With focused practice on strategic questioning, amplification of change talk, and ambivalence exploration, this clinician could move from adequate to excellent MI practice. Priority should be developing competence in actively evoking, amplifying, and reinforcing client's own reasons for change.
