### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate with solid perspective-taking throughout most of the session. Early reflections capture core concerns effectively ("charging feels like something that might take time away from other important things"). However, empathy lacks emotional depth and complexity. Reflections tend toward the factual rather than exploring underlying fears, ambivalence, and self-protective patterns. When Rowan rates importance at 5/10, the psychiatrist reflects accurately but doesn't explore the emotional experience behind that moderate rating or what annoyance truly feels like for him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains general focus on device charging but lacks strategic progression toward concrete change goals. The session is exploratory but doesn't clearly establish what success looks like or maintain progressive movement toward actionable steps. After Rowan mentions a phone reminder might help, the clinician asks exploratory questions without clarifying whether this is a concrete next step. The conversation drifts into circular territory exploring "why charging matters" without narrowing toward specific action. The session ends without establishing clear direction, commitment level, or concrete planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaborative stance with agenda-setting ("What would you like to focus on?"), explicit power-sharing ("You're in control here"), and regular invitations for Rowan's input. Open-ended questions invite perspective rather than imposing solutions. However, collaboration could be strengthened by inviting Rowan to interpret his own experience rather than offering clinician interpretations. Phrases like "That sense of uncertainty feels like it's holding you back" subtly position the clinician as interpreter rather than co-explorer. Rowan's ideas don't substantially reshape session direction—the clinician maintains gentle steering throughout.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Rowan's right to choose and emphasizes ownership of the change process. Explicit autonomy support is strong: "You're in control here" and "It's completely okay to keep weighing whether it's worth your time." The clinician avoids prescriptive language and doesn't pressure when Rowan expresses hesitation. However, autonomy support could be deepened by more explicitly validating the legitimacy of choosing not to change and exploring genuine benefits of the status quo. The implicit assumption throughout is that charging is the desired outcome, rather than explicitly acknowledging Rowan's right to choose differently.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates inconsistent evocation of Rowan's own motivations and values. Early questions are evocative ("What do you think makes charging feel like a lower priority?"), but evocation weakens as the session progresses. The clinician frequently offers interpretations rather than asking Rowan to discover insights. When Rowan says "Maybe a couple of times when it didn't run out in the middle of something important," this potential change talk is not amplified. The session lacks strategic evocation of Rowan's deeper values—what matters most to him beyond device charging? Opportunities to connect charging to his goals and values are missed.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains collaborative language and invites Rowan's input but subtly assumes the expert role through consistent clinician interpretations. True partnership would involve more co-construction of meaning and deeper honoring of Rowan's expertise about his own life. The clinician frequently frames reflections as fact ("It sounds like you're looking for clear signs...") rather than inviting Rowan to explore what his experience means. While the psychiatrist asks many questions, Rowan's ideas don't substantially reshape direction. The clinician gently steers throughout rather than allowing true co-construction of the change process.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is a significant weakness. The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal strategic cultivation of change talk and misses multiple opportunities to amplify Rowan's own language favoring change. Rowan produces several instances of potential change talk ("Maybe it'd help," "It's annoying when it happens," "Maybe a couple of times when it didn't run out") that go largely unaddressed. When Rowan mentions benefit, the psychiatrist's response actually softens the change talk by adding "even if they felt like just meeting basic expectations." The clinician asks exploratory questions but doesn't strategically target change talk categories (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Some responses inadvertently reinforce sustain talk by asking Rowan to elaborate on how well he manages without charging.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates inconsistent handling of sustain talk. Skillful moments include avoiding confrontation and using exploratory questions to redirect gently. However, some responses inadvertently reinforce sustain talk by asking Rowan to elaborate on reasons to maintain status quo (e.g., "What do you think has helped you manage okay so far?"). When Rowan expresses how well he adapts without charging, the clinician affirms this strength but doesn't redirect toward exploring what would be possible with a charged device. Double-sided reflections acknowledging both sustain talk and change talk are absent. Strategic redirects are inconsistent throughout the session.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Consistent respect, acceptance, and unconditional positive regard
- Explicit autonomy support and collaborative agenda-setting
- Accurate reflective listening with solid perspective-taking
- Skillful avoidance of confrontation and pressure
- Effective identification and affirmation of client strengths
- Warm, nonjudgmental, genuinely collaborative tone

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Weak cultivation of change talk** – Multiple instances of potential change talk are not amplified or strategically developed
- **Inconsistent softening of sustain talk** – Some responses inadvertently reinforce reasons to maintain status quo
- **Clinician interpretations vs. co-construction** – Heavy reliance on clinician interpretations rather than inviting Rowan to discover insights
- **Lack of strategic direction** – Session is exploratory but doesn't progress toward concrete next steps or clear commitment
- **Limited values exploration** – Doesn't deeply connect device charging to what matters most to Rowan
- **Shallow emotional attunement** – Reflections tend toward factual accuracy rather than capturing emotional nuance

### Impact on Client Engagement
While this session is respectful and prevents defensiveness, it is unlikely to significantly shift Rowan's motivation for change. The psychiatrist maintains engagement but doesn't strategically amplify Rowan's own reasons for change or help him discover deeper motivations. Rowan leaves with a vague idea about a phone reminder but no clear commitment, concrete plan, or deepened sense that charging matters to him personally. The session is pleasant but not transformative.

### Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Strategically cultivate and amplify change talk** – Pause when Rowan produces change talk and ask follow-up questions that deepen it
2. **Use strategic questions to evoke rather than clinician interpretations** – Replace interpretations with open-ended questions that invite Rowan's own insights
3. **Softly redirect sustain talk without reinforcing it** – Acknowledge sustain talk without elaborating; use double-sided reflections
4. **Connect charging to deeper values and goals** – Explore what matters most to Rowan and how charging fits into his life vision
5. **Establish clear, concrete next steps and commitment** – Move from exploration to specific, manageable planning with commitment level assessment
6. **Deepen emotional attunement** – Move beyond factual reflections to capture emotional nuance and underlying patterns
7. **Use scaling questions strategically** – Follow up with questions that explore the gap and evoke change talk
8. **Maintain MI spirit while building strategic skill** – Continue warm, collaborative stance while adding more strategic technique

---

**Overall Competence Level:** Solid foundational MI with warm, respectful practice. Requires development of strategic technique in evoking and amplifying change talk to reach advanced competence (4-5 level).
