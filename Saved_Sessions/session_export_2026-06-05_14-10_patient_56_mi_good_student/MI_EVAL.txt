### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance with multiple affirmations of Andrea's strengths ("That ability to manage and push through really speaks to your strength"). Normalizes Andrea's experience throughout. However, misses opportunity to explicitly affirm Andrea's right to not change or her autonomy to decide session direction. Could strengthen acceptance by validating precontemplation stage more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic empathic understanding demonstrated through adequate reflective listening (e.g., "It sounds like you're feeling pretty content with how things are"). However, reflections remain largely surface-level and factual. Clinician reflects *what* Andrea is saying but rarely captures *emotional meaning* beneath statements—such as possible anhedonia or burnout evident in "I'm not like I'm doing it because I'm excited or anything." Lacks complex reflections that deepen insight or explore underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** General focus on exploring Andrea's motivation and potential areas for change maintained, but direction is diffuse and lacks strategic clarity. Session drifts between loosely connected themes (busyness, school stress, motivation, pressure) without progressive narrowing toward specific target behavior. **Critical gap:** Psychiatrist never addresses reason for referral—Andrea's high-risk alcohol screening. Fails to gently introduce this concern collaboratively, representing significant strategic failure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated through opening question ("what's on your mind that you'd like to focus on?") and frequent invitations for Andrea's input. Avoids expert-driven approach and uses inclusive language ("our time together," "I'm curious"). However, subtle tension undermines collaboration: clinician persists with gentle probing for change despite Andrea's repeated clear statements of contentment. True collaboration would more explicitly acknowledge and respect Andrea's position without continued gentle pressure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate autonomy support demonstrated through avoidance of direct advice-giving and maintenance of questioning stance. However, inconsistently applied. Psychiatrist does not explicitly affirm Andrea's *right to choose* whether to engage in change exploration. Repeated gentle probing for areas of change—despite Andrea's clear statements ("I'm fine," "I don't think there's anything I need to change")—subtly communicates that clinician believes Andrea *should* want to explore something. Could be significantly strengthened by explicitly validating precontemplation stance and Andrea's ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significantly underdeveloped. Psychiatrist asks questions but does not strategically elicit Andrea's intrinsic motivation, values, or reasons for change. Misses critical evocation opportunity when Andrea hints at possible change talk ("Maybe just trying to focus on one thing at a time")—clinician simply reflects without amplifying or exploring further. No strategic use of DARN-CAT framework (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Commitment). Session lacks exploration of Andrea's values, strengths, or resources that could connect to motivation for change. Major MI competency gap.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial partnership stance with inclusive language and invitation for Andrea's input, but true power-sharing undermined by several factors. Subtle expert role maintained through repeated variations of "what might you want to change?" despite Andrea's clear contentment. Fails to address elephant in room: referral for alcohol risk. Does not substantially incorporate Andrea's contributions into reshaping session direction. When Andrea states "I just want to get through this meeting," clinician validates but does not truly honor this by collaboratively determining what would be useful.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Critical weakness. Virtually no strategic cultivation of change talk evident. Session dominated entirely by sustain talk from Andrea ("I don't really think there's anything I need to change," "Everything's okay the way it is," "I'm fine," "I don't really see how anything small would make much difference"). Psychiatrist does not employ evocative strategies to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment language. One potential change talk moment ("Maybe just trying to focus on one thing at a time") is not seized upon or amplified. Represents fundamental MI failure: without evoking change talk, clinician cannot build motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal skill demonstrated. While clinician does not reinforce sustain talk (appropriate), psychiatrist also does not skillfully redirect or reframe it. Andrea's repeated statements that she doesn't need change are validated without strategic pivoting toward change. No double-sided reflection used to highlight tension between Andrea's stated contentment and hints of struggle (e.g., "less pressure would be nice"). No amplification of sustain talk to gently highlight limitations. Clinician accepts and validates sustain talk without creating cognitive dissonance that might open space for change exploration.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Establishes warm, supportive, nonjudgmental therapeutic relationship
- Consistent affirmation of Andrea's strengths and resilience
- Uses collaborative, inclusive language throughout
- Opens session by inviting Andrea's agenda
- Avoids confrontation, unsolicited advice, and pressure
- Demonstrates basic empathic understanding through reflective listening
- Maintains respectful tone appropriate for precontemplative client

### Critical Areas for Improvement
1. **Failure to address referral concern:** Complete absence of discussion regarding high-risk alcohol screening—fundamental clinical failure
2. **Minimal evocation of change talk:** No strategic use of MI techniques to draw out Andrea's motivations, values, or reasons for change
3. **Sustain talk not softened:** Andrea's resistance validated but never strategically reframed or redirected
4. **Surface-level empathy:** Reflections lack emotional depth and complexity; misses underlying emotional states
5. **Subtle pressure despite autonomy support:** Repeated gentle probing undermines true autonomy support
6. **Lack of strategic direction:** Session drifts without progressive narrowing toward specific target behavior
7. **No values exploration:** Minimal investigation of what Andrea actually cares about or what brings satisfaction

### Clinical Impact
Session unlikely to evoke meaningful change motivation. While warm relationship is necessary foundation, it is insufficient for MI. Andrea experiences validation of precontemplation stance without exploration of deeper values, ambivalence, or reasons for change. Referral concern remains completely unaddressed. Session represents missed clinical opportunity to move Andrea from precontemplation toward contemplation.

### Recommendation
Psychiatrist demonstrates foundational counseling skills but significant gaps in MI-specific competencies. Formal Motivational Interviewing training and ongoing clinical supervision strongly recommended. Priority actions: (1) directly address referral concern collaboratively, (2) develop skill in strategic evocation of change talk, (3) learn double-sided reflection to soften sustain talk, (4) deepen empathic understanding through complex reflections, (5) explore Andrea's values to build intrinsic motivation.
