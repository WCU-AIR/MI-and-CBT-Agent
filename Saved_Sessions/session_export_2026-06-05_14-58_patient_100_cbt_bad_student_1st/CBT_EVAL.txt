## DETAILED SUMMARY OF CLINICAL CONCERNS

### Critical Gaps in CBT Competence

**1. Structural Deficiencies (Items 1, 6)**
- No session structure, agenda, or time management
- Sessions drift without clinical direction
- Excessive social conversation and tangential material
- Repetitive generic statements across sessions
- Rushed endings without summary or planning

**2. Assessment and Understanding Deficiencies (Items 3, 8)**
- No functional analysis of hair-pulling or skin-picking
- No exploration of triggers, maintaining factors, or relief/reward cycle
- Fundamental misunderstanding of trichotillomania/excoriation disorder
- Belief that positive thinking and wellness practices are primary interventions
- Missed key cognitions (shame, self-criticism) and behaviors (ritualized pulling, avoidance)

**3. Intervention Deficiencies (Items 7, 9, 10)**
- No CBT techniques applied (zero application of HRT, functional analysis, thought records, behavioral experiments, competing response training, stimulus control, cognitive restructuring)
- Generic wellness advice disconnected from specific clinical problems
- No evidence-based approach to body-focused repetitive behaviors
- No coherent treatment strategy or case conceptualization

**4. Collaboration and Engagement Deficiencies (Items 2, 5)**
- Minimal feedback-seeking; no systematic checking for understanding
- Patient positioned as passive recipient rather than active agent
- Excessive therapist self-disclosure (boundary violation)
- No shared decision-making or collaborative empiricism
- Therapist functions as advice-giver, not collaborative partner

**5. Between-Session Work Deficiency (Item 11)**
- No homework assigned in either session
- No review of prior work in second session
- Patient left with vague suggestions, not structured tasks
- Eliminates between-session consolidation essential for CBT

### Impact on Patient Care

This approach is **unlikely to produce meaningful, lasting change**. The patient has already experienced repeated failed attempts to stop pulling/picking. Generic motivational support and wellness advice have not worked in the past and are unlikely to work now. Without targeted, evidence-based interventions addressing the specific mechanisms maintaining her behaviors, the patient will likely continue the cycle of pulling/picking, shame, and avoidance. Risk of further demoralization and treatment dropout is high.

### Immediate Actions Required

1. **Establish structured agenda and goal-setting** in every session
2. **Conduct functional analysis** to understand maintaining factors
3. **Apply evidence-based CBT techniques**, particularly Habit Reversal Training
4. **Assign specific homework** with clear rationale and review plan
5. **Eliminate excessive self-disclosure** and maintain professional boundaries
6. **Implement systematic feedback-seeking** to ensure collaboration
7. **Receive intensive supervision** from CBT-trained supervisor with BFRB expertise
8. **Study evidence-based treatments** for trichotillomania/excoriation disorder before next session

**RECOMMENDATION: Therapist should not continue treating this patient without immediate retraining and supervision.**
