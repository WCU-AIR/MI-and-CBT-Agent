## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5**

**Assessment:** This session demonstrates significant deficiencies in core MI competencies. The psychiatrist operates primarily from a directive, expert-driven stance fundamentally inconsistent with the spirit and practice of Motivational Interviewing. While warm tone and session focus are maintained, core MI principles—partnership, evocation, autonomy support, and strategic amplification of change talk—are largely absent.

**Current MI Practice Level:** Below-average (1.6/5). Session resembles supportive counseling with advice-giving rather than true MI. Psychiatrist tells Amina what to do rather than evoking her own motivations and ideas. This approach risks undermining Amina's intrinsic motivation and may increase resistance or compliance without genuine commitment.

**Critical Strengths:**
- Warm, welcoming opening tone
- Session maintains general focus on employment and childcare
- Client statements acknowledged; input not ignored
- One autonomy affirmation present
- One instance of sustain talk softening

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Shift from advice-giving to evocation** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) - Replace prescriptive suggestions with open-ended questions drawing out Amina's own ideas and motivations
2. **Establish true partnership** - Position Amina as expert on her own life; co-create solutions rather than prescribe them
3. **Amplify change talk strategically** - Amina offers substantial motivation; use follow-up questions and reflections to deepen and strengthen it
4. **Support autonomy through choice-oriented language** - Replace "should" and "would probably help" with language emphasizing Amina's choice and self-determination
5. **Develop complex, emotion-focused reflections** - Move beyond factual reflections to capture emotional nuance and deeper meaning
6. **Explore deeper motivation and values** - Ask questions connecting employment to Amina's deeper values and long-term goals
7. **Validate concerns rather than dismiss them** - When Amina expresses concerns, validate legitimacy before problem-solving
8. **Explore Amina's strengths and past successes** - Ask questions helping Amina recognize and draw on her own resources and resilience
9. **Use double-sided reflections** - Acknowledge both motivation for change and concerns/barriers in same reflection
10. **Establish collaborative goal-setting** - Invite Amina to define success and steps rather than telling her what they should be

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** Currently low to moderate. While Amina demonstrates high intrinsic motivation (10/10 confidence, willingness, clear desire), psychiatrist's directive approach risks converting intrinsic motivation into compliance-based motivation. Over time, this could undermine Amina's sustained commitment to change.

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should engage in MI-specific training focused on: (1) asking open-ended questions, (2) recognizing and amplifying change talk, (3) establishing collaborative partnerships, and (4) supporting client autonomy. Role-play practice with feedback essential for skill development. Consider supervision or consultation with MI expert to review session recordings and receive specific feedback on technique development.
