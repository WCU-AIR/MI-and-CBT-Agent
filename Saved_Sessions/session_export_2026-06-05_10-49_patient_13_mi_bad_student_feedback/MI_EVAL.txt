### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist attempts to address sustain talk but does so through confrontation rather than skillful MI techniques. Direct confrontation ("feeling alone or helpless isn't an excuse") is explicitly contraindicated in MI and typically strengthens rather than softens sustain talk. Evidence of backfiring: after confrontation, patient responds with more entrenched sustain talk ("But it's not about making excuses—it's about feeling like I'm completely alone"). No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic responses to sustain talk. One moment of exploratory questioning ("Can you tell me more about what makes quitting hard for you?") represents minimal attempt to understand sustain talk, but immediately followed by confrontation, negating benefit.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.2/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (drug use)
- Demonstrates awareness that support and isolation are relevant factors
- Asks some exploratory questions in one exchange

### Critical Deficiencies:
- **Fundamental MI spirit violation:** Controlling, authoritarian stance antithetical to MI principles
- **Acceptance:** Consistently judgmental; frames patient distress as personal failure
- **Empathy:** Minimal reflective listening; no genuine effort to understand internal experience
- **Autonomy support:** Controlling language and pressure tactics; no respect for patient choice
- **Evocation:** Tells rather than asks; never explores or amplifies patient's own motivations
- **Partnership:** Entirely expert-driven; zero power-sharing or genuine collaboration
- **Cultivating change talk:** Minimal change talk offered; never amplified or reinforced
- **Softening sustain talk:** Uses confrontation, which strengthens rather than softens sustain talk

### Impact on Client Engagement:
This session is likely to **decrease** rather than increase patient motivation for change. Psychiatrist's controlling, judgmental stance triggers defensiveness and entrenchment of sustain talk. Patient's responses show increasing hopelessness and resignation ("I don't see a way out right now"; "it feels like I'm too far gone"). Repeated dismissal of patient's stated barriers (isolation, helplessness, lack of support) communicates lack of understanding and respect, damaging therapeutic alliance and reducing likelihood of engagement in change efforts.

### Recommendations:
1. Adopt MI spirit: shift from expert-driven to collaborative, client-centered stance
2. Replace confrontation with reflective listening and validation
3. Eliminate controlling language; replace with autonomy-supportive language
4. Ask genuine evocative questions to elicit change talk
5. Amplify and reinforce change talk when it emerges
6. Address barriers collaboratively rather than dismissively
7. Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence
8. Build therapeutic alliance through genuine understanding
9. Explore and normalize patient's experience of hopelessness
10. Seek formal MI training, supervision, and consultation immediately

**This psychiatrist requires comprehensive retraining in motivational interviewing principles and practice. Current approach is inconsistent with evidence-based MI and likely to produce poor outcomes.**
