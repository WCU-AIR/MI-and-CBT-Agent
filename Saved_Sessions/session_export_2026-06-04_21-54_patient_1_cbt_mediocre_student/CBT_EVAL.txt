## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### Strengths:
- Adequate interpersonal warmth and non-judgmental stance
- Basic validation of patient distress
- Professional demeanor and appropriate boundaries
- Willingness to suggest concrete interventions

### Critical Deficiencies:

**1. Absence of Evidence-Based Treatment Strategy (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
No coherent, trauma-informed CBT approach. No clear treatment plan. Exposure therapy—the gold standard for Acute Stress Disorder—not addressed. Generic coping suggestions offered without explaining how they address trauma.

**2. Failure to Address Avoidance (CRITICAL)**
Dylan's primary maintaining factor is avoidance of trauma processing and reminders. Therapist accommodated avoidance rather than systematically addressing it. When Dylan said "I don't want to talk about it too much," this critical clinical moment was not explored.

**3. Inadequate Cognitive Work**
Cognitive restructuring was superficial reassurance. Dylan's core trauma thoughts (catastrophic predictions, threat beliefs, guilt) not systematically identified or processed. Dylan explicitly rejected the "tell yourself you're safe" approach; therapist did not adjust.

**4. No Systematic Behavioral Intervention**
Behavioral activation and exposure not strategically planned. Activity suggestions vague without clear goals, structure, or connection to treatment. No exposure hierarchy created. No plan to return to avoided activities (driving).

**5. Absent Psychoeducation**
Dylan not educated about trauma responses, why symptoms occur, why avoidance maintains symptoms, or how treatment works. Essential for building motivation and understanding rationale for difficult interventions.

**6. Inadequate Homework**
Vague assignments without written instructions or clear rationale. Session two failed to review homework—eliminating opportunity to assess effectiveness and adjust treatment.

**7. No Structured Agenda or Session Planning**
Sessions lacked clear agendas, goals, or prioritization. Identical opening repeated in session two suggests no learning or progression.

**8. Minimal Feedback Elicitation**
No checks for understanding, exploration of ambivalence, or solicitation of reactions. When Dylan expressed doubt, therapist did not explore.

## LIKELIHOOD OF THERAPEUTIC CHANGE: **LOW**

**Why This Session Will Not Produce Meaningful Improvement:**

1. **Avoidance is reinforced, not challenged** – Dylan's primary maintaining factor is not being systematically addressed
2. **Interventions are mismatched to the problem** – Generic coping strategies do not address core trauma
3. **Dylan's ambivalence is unresolved** – Doubt about interventions not explored; unlikely to engage fully
4. **No clear rationale for treatment** – Dylan does not understand why or how interventions address trauma
5. **Homework is vague and unlikely to be completed** – Low specificity and low commitment indicate poor compliance
6. **No systematic progress monitoring** – Without homework review, therapist cannot determine if treatment works
7. **Critical symptoms not addressed** – Intrusive images, hyperarousal, avoidance of driving not systematically targeted

**Risk Factors:**
- Dylan may disengage from treatment if no improvement occurs
- Continued avoidance may lead to increased functional impairment (school failure, social isolation, inability to drive)
- Untreated ASD can develop into chronic PTSD, depression, or anxiety disorders
- Dylan's irritability and anger may escalate

## IMMEDIATE RECOMMENDATIONS

### **HIGHEST PRIORITY ACTIONS:**

1. **Develop a clear, evidence-based treatment conceptualization and plan** that includes exposure therapy and cognitive processing
2. **Directly address avoidance** as the core maintaining factor; explain why gradual exposure is necessary
3. **Implement psychoeducation** about trauma responses and why avoidance maintains symptoms
4. **Create an exposure hierarchy** and begin systematic, gradual exposure to feared situations
5. **Implement rigorous homework review** in every session; adjust based on outcomes
6. **Use guided discovery** (Socratic questioning) instead of directive advice
7. **Establish structured agendas** with specific target problems and time allocation
8. **Elicit genuine feedback** throughout sessions; explore ambivalence collaboratively
9. **Provide specific, written homework** with clear rationale and monitoring instructions
10. **Engage Dylan as a true collaborator** in treatment planning and decision-making

## CLINICAL CONCLUSION

**This session does not qualify as evidence-based CBT for trauma.** It is supportive counseling with generic coping suggestions. Dylan has Acute Stress Disorder requiring trauma-focused CBT with systematic exposure and cognitive processing. Without significant changes in the therapist's approach, Dylan is at risk of chronic symptoms, functional impairment, and development of PTSD or depression.

**The therapist must immediately shift to an evidence-based, trauma-informed CBT approach to help Dylan recover.**
