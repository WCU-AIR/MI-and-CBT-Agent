## Overall Session Summary

### **Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Clinical Impression:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of Motivational Interviewing practice** and demonstrates an approach that is **actively contraindicated for evidence-based behavior change**. The psychiatrist operates from an authoritarian, expert-driven stance that violates the core spirit of MI across all nine dimensions.

**Critical Findings:**

- **Judgment replaces acceptance** throughout the session
- **Dismissal replaces empathy** in every client statement
- **Coercion replaces autonomy support** consistently
- **Prescription replaces evocation** entirely
- **Hierarchy replaces partnership** from opening to close

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

Irina begins with ambivalence and openness to change ("I'm willing to try," "I want to be healthier"). By session end, she is defensive, withdrawn, and explicitly questioning whether change is possible ("I don't know how I can do it at all"). The psychiatrist's confrontational style **strengthens resistance rather than evoking intrinsic motivation**.

**Evidence-Based Concerns:**

This approach violates fundamental principles of:
- Self-determination theory (autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation)
- Behavior change science (collaborative goals increase adherence)
- MI research (validation reduces resistance; coercion increases it)
- Harm reduction principles (gradual change is more sustainable than all-or-nothing demands)

**Recommendation:**

**This psychiatrist requires comprehensive retraining in Motivational Interviewing.** The current approach is contraindicated for clinical practice and likely to produce poor outcomes. Formal MI training (minimum 2-day workshop), supervised practice with feedback, and ongoing fidelity monitoring are essential. Without intervention, this clinician will continue to harm clients' motivation for change.
