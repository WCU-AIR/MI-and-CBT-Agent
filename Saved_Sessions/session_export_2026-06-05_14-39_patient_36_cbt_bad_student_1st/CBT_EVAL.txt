## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Therapist Performance Summary:**

This session demonstrates a **score of 11/66 (16.7%)**, indicating **Poor to Barely Adequate performance** across the CBT Evaluation Rating Scale. The therapist's approach is fundamentally misaligned with evidence-based treatment for Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder in a young child.

**Key Findings:**

- **Diagnostic Misunderstanding:** Therapist treated DSED as a cognitive/mood disorder amenable to positive self-talk, when it is a developmental disorder rooted in attachment dysregulation and impulse control deficits.
- **Inappropriate Interventions:** Cognitive restructuring is developmentally inappropriate for a 4-year-old and has no evidence base for DSED. Patient's own feedback ("hard to make those feelings smaller just by thinking") contradicted this approach, but therapist did not adjust.
- **Absence of Caregiver Involvement:** Complete failure to involve parents/caregivers—a violation of best practices for pediatric treatment and a critical gap for a child with attachment disorder.
- **Safety Concerns Not Addressed:** Patient's willingness to leave with strangers (a serious safety risk) was never directly addressed, explored, or targeted for intervention.
- **No Coherent Treatment Plan:** Disconnected suggestions without unifying framework, evidence base, or theory of change.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Suspend independent practice with this patient** pending intensive supervision
2. **Arrange consultation** with supervisor experienced in attachment disorders and DSED
3. **Conduct comprehensive literature review** on evidence-based DSED treatment
4. **Restructure treatment** to include caregivers as active partners
5. **Eliminate cognitive interventions** and replace with attachment-based approaches
6. **Establish clear behavioral targets** and safety planning
7. **Implement systematic homework** and between-session practice
8. **Receive direct supervision** on all future sessions with this patient

**Prognosis Without Intervention:** Without substantial changes to treatment approach, this case is at high risk of poor outcomes and potential harm to the patient.
