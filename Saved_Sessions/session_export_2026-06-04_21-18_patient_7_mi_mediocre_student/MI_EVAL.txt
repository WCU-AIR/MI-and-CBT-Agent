## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.9/5**

**Clinician MI Competency Level:** Below Average – Significant gaps in core MI skills

**Key Strengths:**
- Basic rapport and respectful, non-hostile tone maintained
- Attempt to link behavior to client's stated goals (soccer, scholarships)
- Recognition of peer pressure as barrier to change
- Client remains engaged despite directive approach

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Prescriptive stance dominates; "should" language pervasive
- Minimal evocation of intrinsic motivation
- Poor handling of ambivalence and sustain talk
- Hierarchical expert-driven model rather than collaborative partnership
- Autonomy and client choice not actively supported

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session likely **reduces intrinsic motivation for sustained change** despite apparent compliance. Alexei may experience psychological reactance to perceived control. Compliance with clinician's plan does not equal genuine commitment. Unaddressed ambivalence about peer pressure may undermine follow-through.

**Likelihood of Sustained Behavior Change:** Moderate to Low. Short-term compliance possible due to external pressure; sustained change unlikely without genuine exploration of Alexei's own motivations and values.

**Recommended Priority Actions:**
1. **Shift from prescriptive to evocative stance** (eliminate "should" language; replace with open-ended questions)
2. **Develop skill in evoking and amplifying change talk** (strategic follow-up on client motivation statements)
3. **Build genuine collaboration and partnership** (position client as expert; actively invite input)
4. **Explicitly support autonomy** (regular affirmation of client choice and control)
5. **Skillfully handle ambivalence** (use double-sided reflections; validate real tensions)

**Clinician Development Recommendation:** Intensive MI training including didactic instruction, observation of expert practitioners, role-play with feedback, and audio/video review of sessions with focus on reducing prescriptive language and increasing evocative questioning.
