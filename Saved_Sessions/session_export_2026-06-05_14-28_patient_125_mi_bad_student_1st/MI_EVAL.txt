### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist establishes authoritarian stance with opening statement "I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do to improve." Pervasive judgment throughout ("You really should quit," "Why haven't you made these changes yet?"). No affirmation of Sofia's strengths, partial engagement in exercise, or awareness of problems. Repeated dismissal of legitimate concerns as consequences of poor choices rather than normalizing struggle. No warmth, support, or communication that Sofia is valued as a person regardless of current behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand Sofia's perspective. When Sofia expresses anxiety concerns ("Weed helps with my anxiety"), psychiatrist responds with prescription rather than exploration. Sofia's fear about withdrawal is minimized ("Poor sleep is just a consequence of your choices"). No open-ended questions to understand Sofia's experience of anxiety, stress sources, family dynamics, or goals. Entire session consists of closed yes/no questions followed by directives—antithesis of empathic exploration. Multiple opportunities for understanding missed entirely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors (marijuana use, eating healthy, exercising, sleeping), preventing session drift. However, focus pursued through coercion rather than collaborative direction. Approach entirely prescriptive ("You have to quit immediately," "No more excuses," "This is non-negotiable"). No exploration of Sofia's own goals, values, or reasons for change. Rigid four-part plan imposed without assessing readiness, preferences, or capacity. Repeated escalation of demands despite Sofia's clear statements of unreadiness demonstrates inflexibility and lack of responsiveness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero collaboration; strict hierarchical, expert-driven model where psychiatrist dictates and Sofia is expected to comply. Opening statement explicitly rejects collaboration. Never solicits Sofia's input on solutions or explores her ideas about managing stress. When Sofia suggests cutting back, psychiatrist dismisses this ("Cutting back is just delaying the problem") with no negotiation or exploration. Repeated yes/no interrogation format allows no space for Sofia's perspective, reasoning, or preferences. Sofia's repeated unreadiness met with escalating demands rather than problem-solving together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Relentless pressure and coercion throughout; explicit denial of Sofia's autonomy. Repeated language strips away choice: "You have to," "You must," "This is non-negotiable," "No more excuses." When Sofia states unreadiness ("I'm not ready for that, not right now"), psychiatrist escalates pressure rather than respecting choice. Sofia explicitly states need for self-paced change ("I need to go at a pace I can handle"); psychiatrist responds with "No more half-measures" and demands immediate total commitment. Word "non-negotiable" explicitly denies Sofia any choice or input. Approach likely to increase resistance and decrease intrinsic motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero evocation; never asks Sofia about her own reasons for change, values, goals, strengths, or ideas about solutions. Multiple missed opportunities: Sofia's statement "I want to be healthier, obviously" could be explored but is dismissed. Sofia's doubt about marijuana's effectiveness ("I guess...") passes unexamined. Sofia mentions stress, anxiety, school pressure, family expectations—all potential sources of intrinsic motivation—but psychiatrist never explores these. Psychiatrist tells Sofia why she should change rather than asking Sofia to articulate her own reasons. Entire approach based on imposing clinician's reasons rather than evoking Sofia's own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete opposite of partnership; psychiatrist assumes exclusive expert role and denies Sofia any expertise about her own life. Opening statement explicitly positions psychiatrist as sole expert. When Sofia expresses concerns about anxiety and sleep, psychiatrist dismisses these as invalid ("Poor sleep is just a consequence of your choices"). Sofia's suggestion of graduated approach rejected without exploration of whether her instinct about needing gradual change might be valid. Repeated yes/no questions and directives position Sofia as someone who needs to be told what to do, not as someone with valuable knowledge. Sofia's contributions consistently overridden; no power-sharing occurs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero cultivation of change talk; approach actively suppresses Sofia's emerging change talk and reinforces sustain talk. Sofia provides multiple instances of change talk: "I get that weed isn't perfect," "I want to be healthier, obviously," "Maybe I could try one or two things," "Maybe someday." Rather than amplifying these, psychiatrist ignores or contradicts them. No questions designed to evoke change talk ("What concerns do you have?" "What would be benefits of cutting back?" "How confident are you?"). Psychiatrist's language reinforces sustain talk by making change feel impossible. Sofia's statements of unreadiness become increasingly firm throughout session, indicating psychiatrist's approach has increased sustain talk rather than cultivating change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Demonstrates opposite of softening sustain talk; directly confronts Sofia's resistance with judgment and pressure, which predictably strengthens her defensiveness. Sofia's sustain talk ("Weed helps me get through stuff," "I need to go at a pace I can handle") met with direct confrontation ("You can't just cut back," "No more half-measures") rather than double-sided reflections or strategic responses. When Sofia expresses fear about withdrawal, psychiatrist responds with shame ("Poor sleep is just a consequence of your choices") rather than validation. Repeated use of "No more excuses" and "You have to take this seriously" directly confronts sustain talk with judgment. By session end, Sofia more firmly entrenched in position than at beginning, demonstrating psychiatrist's approach strengthened commitment to status quo rather than softening resistance.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.0/5**

**MI Competency Level:** No competency demonstrated. Across all nine core competencies, this session scores 1/5, indicating that MI principles are either absent or actively violated.

**Clinical Approach:** Traditional directive medical model fundamentally incompatible with MI principles. Hierarchical, expert-driven, judgmental stance establishes non-collaborative relationship and actively increases client resistance.

**Impact on Client Motivation:** This session is **highly unlikely to evoke Sofia's intrinsic motivation for change** and is likely to be **counterproductive**. Sofia enters with tentative openness and exits with firm refusal and defensiveness. Psychiatrist has moved Sofia backward in stages of change rather than forward.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. Adopt MI spirit and collaborative stance (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. Develop reflective listening skills and demonstrate genuine empathy
3. Explore and validate Sofia's ambivalence rather than imposing false dichotomy
4. Assess and respect Sofia's stage of change and readiness
5. Evoke Sofia's own reasons for change rather than imposing clinician's reasons
6. Soften sustain talk through strategic responses rather than confrontation
7. Support autonomy and respect Sofia's right to choose
8. Assess and address underlying anxiety and sleep issues
9. Develop collaborative treatment plan based on Sofia's input and readiness
10. **Pursue formal MI training immediately** (2-3 day workshop minimum with certification through MINT)

**Clinical Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should not continue conducting MI-based interventions without formal training and supervision. Current approach likely to harm therapeutic relationship and decrease likelihood of positive outcomes.
