--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar II Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Olivia Jacobs was a 22-year-old graduate student in architecture who was referred for urgent psychiatric consultation after telling her roommate she was suicidal.
She had a history of mood symptoms controlled with lithium and sertraline, but depressive symptoms returned 3 months earlier after moving to a new city for school.

She was preoccupied with ways to end her life without inconveniencing others, particularly thoughts of shooting herself while leaning out of a dorm window. She did not have access to a gun but searched online for ways to obtain one.

Her psychiatric history began at age 15 with alcohol and marijuana use in social settings, which she reported as calming and non-problematic. At 17, she experienced brief, intense depressive episodes with tearfulness, guilt, anhedonia, hopelessness, low energy, poor concentration, and oversleeping, followed by 5-day periods of hypomania characterized by increased energy, pressured speech, creativity, and paranoia about friends. She was diagnosed with bipolar II disorder and treated with lithium and sertraline.

Upon evaluation, she was visibly depressed and tearful with psychomotor slowing, difficulty getting out of bed, poor concentration, guilt about family expenses, hopelessness, and persistent suicidal thoughts. She reported occasional superficial self-injury, depersonalization, and panic attacks, but denied current substance use, mood instability outside episodes, identity concerns, or fear of abandonment.

Discussion: Ms. Jacobs presents with major depressive symptoms causing significant distress and impairment for 3 months. She has a history of hypomanic episodes consistent with bipolar II disorder. She also meets criteria for unspecified anxiety disorder. Her suicidal ideation is severe, indicating a high risk of self-harm.
