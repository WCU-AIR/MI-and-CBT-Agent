## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Demonstrates awareness of MI principles in opening framing ("I'm here to explore this with you—not to tell you what to do")
- Eventually recognizes David's concerns as legitimate ("It makes sense that muscle weakness would be a big concern for you")
- Later questions show some shift toward exploration ("Can you tell me more about what worries you most about the medication?")
- Attempts to understand barriers by asking about past experiences with diet and exercise

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Pervasive confrontation:** Session dominated by direct argument with resistance ("you're just making excuses," "you're fooling yourself," "you're risking a heart attack")
- **Authoritarian stance:** Repeated directive language ("you must," "you have to," "you need to") positions clinician as expert and David as non-compliant
- **Failure to evoke:** Imposes reasons for change rather than drawing out David's own motivations and values
- **Lack of autonomy support:** Actively undermines David's autonomy by pressuring compliance and rejecting his right to determine readiness
- **Missed change talk amplification:** Multiple instances of David's change talk go unrecognized and unreinforced
- **Integrity failure:** States "I'm here to explore, not tell you what to do" then immediately tells David what to do repeatedly

### Impact on Client Engagement
This session is likely to **decrease** David's motivation for change rather than increase it. Research demonstrates that confrontation, directive advice, and pressure for compliance activate psychological reactance—clients become more entrenched in resistance when autonomy is threatened. David's explicit statement "I need to feel like I'm making the decision, not being told what to do" indicates the psychiatrist has failed to establish collaborative partnership essential to MI. David is unlikely to follow through on medication adherence or lifestyle changes, and the therapeutic relationship has been damaged, making future engagement more difficult.

### Current MI Competency Level
**Minimal MI competency (1.4/5).** While clinician demonstrates awareness of MI principles, this is vastly outweighed by pervasive confrontation, authoritarian stance, and failure to evoke. Clinician appears to be operating from traditional medical model (expert tells patient what to do) rather than MI model (clinician and client collaborate to evoke motivation).

## Priority Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Establish genuine partnership** by inviting David's perspective and demonstrating his input shapes the session
2. **Stop confronting sustain talk** and start exploring it with genuine curiosity
3. **Evoke David's own reasons** for change rather than imposing reasons
4. **Recognize and amplify change talk** through reflective listening and strategic follow-up
5. **Support autonomy explicitly** and repeatedly throughout session
6. **Validate concerns** without dismissing them; explore with curiosity
7. **Explore David's values** and what matters to him beyond surface-level discussion
8. **Maintain consistency** between stated approach and actual behavior
9. **Use open-ended questions** to invite exploration rather than closed questions and directives
10. **Develop awareness of personal agenda** and genuinely become curious about David's perspective and motivations
