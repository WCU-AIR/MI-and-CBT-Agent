## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Assessment:** The psychiatrist is **not practicing motivational interviewing**. This represents a fundamental failure to embody MI principles. Of 9 competency domains, clinician scores 1/5 in 7 domains and 2/5 in 2 domains. No areas of genuine MI competence demonstrated.

**Session Embodies MI Spirit:** No. The spirit of MI—partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation—is entirely absent. Session embodies the opposite: hierarchy, judgment, coldness, and imposition.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation:** Very Low. Client enters at 5/10 readiness with openness to cutting back. By session end, she is defensive, frustrated, and explicitly rejecting clinician's approach ("pushing me like this makes me feel cornered"). Research consistently shows coercive, authoritarian approaches trigger psychological reactance and decrease motivation. Client unlikely to follow through on change plan, unlikely to return for follow-up, and likely to feel harmed by interaction.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Authoritarian stance with imposed single acceptable outcome
- Coercive pressure through repeated binary demands and threats
- Rejection of client's legitimate barriers and realistic self-assessment
- Suppression of client's own change talk
- Fundamental violation of autonomy and self-determination

**Recommendation:** This clinician requires immediate intervention and formal MI training with ongoing supervision. Continuing current practice will harm patients and undermine their motivation for change.
