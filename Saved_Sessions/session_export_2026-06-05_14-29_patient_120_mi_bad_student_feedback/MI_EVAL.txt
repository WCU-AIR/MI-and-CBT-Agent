### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Change talk cultivation is minimally demonstrated despite multiple instances of client change talk throughout session ("I want to stay healthy," "I do want to get better at it," "I've started adding my meds to my phone checklist and that's helped a bit," "I want to stick with it"). Psychiatrist rarely responds in ways that amplify or reinforce change talk. When Selene offers "I know what you're saying about the risks, and I really don't want those things to happen. No strategic use of open-ended questions designed to evoke change talk (e.g., "What would be the benefits?" "How confident are you?"). No reflections deepening change talk, no follow-up questions, no affirmations celebrating progress. Frequent use of imperatives and lectures actually suppresses change talk by shifting focus to sustain talk and defensiveness. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal understanding of strategic change talk cultivation techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2.5/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is sometimes addressed but often through confrontation rather than MI-consistent techniques. When psychiatrist confronts sustain talk directly (turns 3, 5, 11), sustain talk increases and defensiveness strengthens—classic "righting reflex" pattern. Phrases like "No, you don't get to make excuses" and "You need to stop making excuses and just commit" are confrontational challenges that strengthen client resistance. When psychiatrist uses softer, collaborative approaches (turns 7, 13, 15), sustain talk decreases and engagement increases. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal use of MI-specific techniques for softening sustain talk: no double-sided reflections acknowledging ambivalence, no reframing of sustain talk in positive light, no exploration of discrepancies between values and behavior. Example: when Selene says "I feel fine most of the time," psychiatrist could reflect "On one hand, feeling fine makes it easy to think it's not urgent. On the other hand, you want to stay healthy and avoid complications. How do you make sense of those two things?" Instead, psychiatrist confronts. Pattern suggests psychiatrist may not understand that confrontation typically strengthens rather than softens sustain talk.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.7/5**

**Key Strengths:**
- Occasional collaborative moments (turns 7, 9, 13, 15) demonstrate capacity for MI-consistent practice
- Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (medication adherence)
- Demonstrates some flexibility and willingness to shift approach
- Shows recognition that confrontational approach isn't working by attempting collaborative shifts

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Pervasive imperatives and demands:** "You have to," "you must," "you can't," "no excuses" language violates autonomy support and collaboration principles
- **Confrontational responses to sustain talk:** Directly challenges client resistance, strengthening rather than softening sustain talk
- **Minimal change talk cultivation:** Fails to amplify or reinforce client's own language favoring change
- **Inconsistent empathy:** Frequently negates or dismisses client's emotional experience rather than validating perspective
- **Severe inconsistency in approach:** Oscillates between collaborative and confrontational styles, creating confusion and undermining trust
- **Missed evocation opportunities:** Rarely asks questions designed to access client's intrinsic motivations, values, or resources
- **Judgment and moral language:** Communicates evaluation rather than acceptance and partnership

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Psychiatrist's inconsistent approach significantly undermines stated goals. When confrontational language is used, Selene shows increased defensiveness, sustain talk, and conditional commitment ("I'm willing to try"). When collaborative language is used, Selene shows genuine engagement, specific problem-solving, and authentic change talk. Client explicitly requests collaborative approach ("I'd like us to talk about some ways to make it more manageable for me rather than just being told to 'push through'"), but psychiatrist frequently ignores this and reverts to confrontation. Current approach likely to produce short-term compliance at best; sustained resistance or dropout at worst.

**Recommendations for Development:**
1. Eliminate imperatives; adopt collaborative language throughout entire session
2. Develop consistent collaborative stance from beginning to end
3. Learn to amplify and reinforce change talk strategically
4. Use MI-consistent techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to soften sustain talk rather than confrontation
5. Develop genuine empathic reflections capturing emotional nuance
6. Evoke client's intrinsic motivations and values through open-ended questions
7. Explicitly support autonomy and client choice
8. Replace closed questions and accusations with open-ended exploratory questions
9. Recognize and affirm client strengths and progress
10. Develop awareness of righting reflex and its counterproductive impact

**Supervision Focus:** Psychiatrist requires deliberate practice in collaborative language, strategic change talk cultivation, and MI-consistent techniques for handling sustain talk. Recording and reviewing sessions with focus on these areas essential for skill development.
