## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

This session demonstrates solid motivational interviewing fundamentals with a strong collaborative foundation. The psychiatrist establishes genuine partnership from the opening question, consistently positions Mei Lin as the expert on her own experience, and uses accurate, multi-layered reflective listening that captures her ambivalence and the functional role of drinking in her life. The clinician skillfully avoids confrontation, normalizes her mixed feelings, and maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol use while respecting her autonomy and pacing. By the end of the session, Mei Lin's confidence increases from 4/10 to 7/10, and she commits to a specific, manageable first step (reaching out to a non-drinking friend), suggesting the psychiatrist successfully built her sense of efficacy and readiness for change. The session also demonstrates good scaffolding, moving progressively from exploration of barriers to concrete action planning in a logical, client-centered way.

### Areas for Improvement

The primary deficiency in this session is insufficient strategic evocation and amplification of Mei Lin's change talk. While the psychiatrist asks open-ended questions that invite her perspective, there is minimal deepening of her own motivations, reasons, values, or past successes. When Mei Lin expresses change talk ("I really don't want to go down that path my family has with alcoholism" or "If I drink less, I'll be more reliable with using birth control"), the psychiatrist reflects it but does not follow up with questions designed to strengthen and elaborate on these statements. This represents a critical MI gap: the session explores barriers and develops strategies but does not systematically evoke and amplify the client's intrinsic motivation for change. Additionally, the contraceptive reliability component—a key presenting concern—receives minimal attention after initial exploration, leaving Mei Lin without concrete strategies for remembering birth control use. The psychiatrist also does not explore her values deeply, ask about past successes that could build confidence, or explicitly normalize setbacks and relapses, which could reduce shame and increase autonomy support. Finally, while generally collaborative, the psychiatrist occasionally uses subtle evaluative language that slightly positions the clinician as judge rather than equal partner.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

Despite these gaps, the session appears to have successfully engaged Mei Lin and increased her readiness for change. Her confidence increased from 4/10 to 7/10, she generated her own strategies, and she committed to a specific first step. However, the session's impact on sustained, long-term change is uncertain. Without deeper exploration of her intrinsic motivations and values, Mei Lin may rely primarily on external accountability (checking in with the psychiatrist) rather than internal motivation. The insufficient focus on contraceptive reliability means she may not develop concrete strategies for this equally important target behavior, potentially limiting the session's effectiveness. The lack of explicit discussion about setbacks and relapses may also leave her vulnerable to shame and discouragement if she struggles, which could undermine her confidence and commitment. Overall, the session establishes a good foundation for change but misses opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation and develop comprehensive strategies for both target behaviors.

## Recommendations for Future Sessions

### 1. **Strategically Evoke and Amplify Change Talk**
Follow up when Mei Lin expresses change talk with deepening questions. Example: When she says "I really don't want to go down that path my family has with alcoholism," ask "Tell me more about that. What specifically worries you about following that path? What would it mean for you to break that cycle?"

### 2. **Explore and Strengthen Intrinsic Motivation Through Values Clarification**
Dedicate time to exploring what matters most to Mei Lin. Ask: "If you imagine yourself five years from now, living the life you want, what does that look like? How does your current drinking fit with that vision?"

### 3. **Develop Dual-Target Strategies for Both Alcohol Reduction and Contraceptive Reliability**
Give equal attention to both presenting concerns. After developing drinking reduction strategies, explicitly ask: "What would help you remember to use birth control consistently, especially in moments when you're tempted to drink?"

### 4. **Explicitly Explore and Normalize Setbacks and Relapses**
Discuss what will happen if Mei Lin struggles, framing this as normal and not a sign of failure. Example: "If you find yourself drinking more than planned, that doesn't mean you've failed. We can look at what happened and adjust your plan."

### 5. **Ask About Past Successes to Build Confidence and Identify Strategies**
Explore times Mei Lin has successfully changed behavior. Example: "Tell me about a time in your life when you successfully changed something or managed a challenge. What did you do? What helped you stick with it?"

### 6. **Deepen Reflections to Capture Emotional Nuance and Vulnerability**
When Mei Lin expresses doubt or fear, reflect the underlying emotion. Example: "It sounds like there's real fear underneath that doubt—worry that when the moment comes, you won't be able to choose what you actually want."

### 7. **Explore Barriers to Autonomy: Social Pressure and Identity Conflicts**
Explicitly discuss social pressure from drinking friends and identity conflicts. Example: "How do you see yourself? Are you someone who drinks, or are you someone who's choosing to drink less? How does that feel?"

### 8. **Use Strategic Questioning to Strengthen Commitment Talk**
When Mei Lin expresses commitment, deepen it. Example: When she says "I'd say I'm about a seven now," ask "What does that seven mean? What would it take to move from a seven to an eight?"

### 9. **Maintain True Partnership by Inviting Client Feedback and Critique**
At the end of sessions, ask: "Is there anything I've said or done today that didn't feel right? What would you like to do differently next time?"

## Conclusion

This session demonstrates competent foundational MI practice with strong collaborative stance and good scaffolding toward concrete action. To advance from adequate (3.9/5) to good or excellent MI practice, the psychiatrist should prioritize: (1) strategic amplification of change talk through follow-up questions, (2) deeper values exploration to strengthen intrinsic motivation, (3) equal attention to both target behaviors, (4) explicit normalization of setbacks, and (5) exploration of social and identity barriers to change. These improvements would significantly enhance the likelihood of evoking sustained, intrinsic motivation for change and better prepare Mei Lin for the challenges ahead.
