--- DISORDER ---
Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions (PFAOMC)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Sofia Fredholm was a 22-year-old woman with cystic fibrosis who was hospitalized for respiratory distress. On the fourth day of admission, the ICU requested a psychiatric consultation due to her refusal to comply with nebulizer treatments and noninvasive ventilation. She was consistently hypoxic and hypercarbic.

Ms. Fredholm reported that wearing the mask caused anxiety and worsened her breathlessness. She had been sleeping poorly, waking frequently due to shortness of breath and panic, and wanted to go home. She felt that the hospital staff were not listening to her concerns.

History revealed she grew up with her parents and two older siblings. Her mother, a school nurse, had primarily managed her medical care, making daily treatments an emotionally positive “playtime.” As a child, Ms. Fredholm sometimes resisted her mother’s care, wanting normalcy, but often declined social invitations citing breathing problems.

During the psychiatric interview, Ms. Fredholm repeatedly adjusted her oxygen mask, removed it briefly, and stated she was too short of breath to continue talking. She appeared tremulous, distractible, and fast-breathing. She expressed confidence that she and her mother managed her symptoms better than the medical team. At home, she tolerated nebulizer treatments well, sometimes aided by herbal steam baths.

Later that night, due to rising carbon dioxide levels, Ms. Fredholm was intubated.

Discussion: Ms. Fredholm’s case demonstrates the interaction of physiological and psychological factors affecting treatment outcomes. Anxiety, beliefs about self-management, and behavioral avoidance negatively impacted her medical care. DSM-5 criteria for PFAOMC are met, as her psychological and behavioral factors directly affected the course of her medical condition. This diagnosis highlights the need for empathy, alliance-building, and understanding of developmental and cultural factors influencing adherence.

Reliance on family or culturally endorsed coping strategies

Maladaptive coping affecting disease course
