# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance. Effectively reframes client resistance as strength and validates boundaries. Example: "That respect for your boundaries shows a strong sense of self, and I want to honor that fully." Minor limitation: occasionally frames minimal responses as indicators of deeper emotional work rather than fully accepting current presentation as legitimate.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic reflective listening present but lacks emotional depth and accuracy. Simple reflections are accurate but surface-level ("It sounds like you're open to continuing, even if it feels a bit tough to get started"). Critical gap: fails to capture likely emotional state of defensive, mistrustful adolescent in mandated session. Misses opportunities for complex reflections that would demonstrate understanding of frustration or resentment beneath resistance.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant deficiency in establishing or maintaining clear session direction. After eight exchanges, no clear target behavior or presenting concern is identified or explored. Psychiatrist repeatedly asks open-ended questions without clarifying why client is present or what session should focus on. Fails to gently establish collaborative direction regarding caseworker referral for household conflict.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration demonstrated through respect for boundaries and repeated inquiry about what would feel helpful. However, lacks true co-construction of session agenda and goals. Psychiatrist maintains subtle control over session structure and approach without inviting client input on how to work together effectively.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout session with consistent affirmation of client choice and ownership. Multiple explicit validations: "Your privacy and comfort are important here" and "It's okay to keep things to yourself until you feel ready." Avoids directive language, prescription, or pressure. Minor gap: could more explicitly validate right to disengage entirely from session.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significantly underdeveloped. Psychiatrist asks generic open-ended questions but does not strategically draw out client's own perspective, values, or resources. No evocative questions explore client's understanding of situation, what matters to her, or what she sees as her strengths. Approach is essentially passive, waiting for client to volunteer rather than skillfully inviting exploration.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative language present but true partnership lacking. Psychiatrist maintains subtle expert stance through interpretations ("Sometimes when things feel 'fine,' it can mean there's more beneath the surface") and unilateral session structuring. Does not position client as expert on her own experience or invite input on how to work together. Fails to acknowledge client's expertise about her own life.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Change talk entirely absent from session with zero strategic attempts to evoke it. No questions explore desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment, or taking steps toward change. Psychiatrist focuses entirely on rapport and boundary-setting without advancing core MI goal of evoking and strengthening motivation for change. Critical MI skill entirely absent.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk present ("It's not really any of your business" appears twice), limiting opportunity to demonstrate skill. Psychiatrist's handling is respectful and non-confrontational but lacks strategic MI techniques for softening resistance. Does not employ double-sided reflection, reframing, or strategic redirection to explore ambivalence beneath resistance.

---

## Overall Session Score: 2.8/5

### Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and affirmation of client boundaries
- Strong autonomy support with explicit validation of client choice
- Warm, supportive tone communicating genuine care
- Avoids confrontation, judgment, or pressure
- Creates safe, non-threatening environment

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- No clear session direction or target behavior identified
- Minimal evocation of client's own perspective, values, and resources
- Zero change talk cultivation or strategic attempts to elicit motivation
- Lacks true partnership; maintains subtle expert stance
- Empathy lacks depth; reflections are surface-level
- Passive approach to engagement; waits for client to volunteer rather than skillfully inviting exploration
- No assessment or clarification of presenting concern (household conflict referral)

**Overall Clinical Impression:**

This session demonstrates **below-average MI competence** with significant deficiencies in core MI skills. The psychiatrist excels at creating a safe, accepting environment and demonstrates strong autonomy support. However, the session lacks the strategic direction, evocation, and change talk cultivation that define MI practice. The approach is essentially **supportive counseling focused on rapport-building**, not true MI. After eight exchanges, no clear direction is established, client's perspective is not evoked, and no change talk is cultivated. The client remains in precontemplation with no exploration of her situation or motivation for change. To improve, the psychiatrist must establish collaborative direction, develop deeper empathic reflections, strategically evoke client perspective, introduce change talk exploration early, and position the client as expert on her own experience.
