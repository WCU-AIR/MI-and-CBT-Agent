### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Asks exploratory questions but primarily focused on understanding resistance rather than evoking intrinsic motivation for change. Does not amplify or develop potential change talk when it emerges (e.g., "Sometimes things get heavy, sure, but I just push through"). ### Partnership

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Most critical deficiency. Change talk is virtually absent; session dominated by sustain talk without strategic cultivation of counterbalancing change language. Samir repeatedly expresses sustain talk ("I just don't like taking the pills," "I'm fine on my own," "No meds needed") without psychiatrist strategically evoking change talk. When potential change talk emerges ("Sometimes things get heavy, sure, but I just push through"), psychiatrist reflects and reinforces current coping strategy rather than evoking recognition of limitations. No targeted questions designed to evoke change talk. Samir leaves session having articulated no reasons for considering medication adherence. Session preserves relationship but does not advance readiness for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal skill demonstrated. Psychiatrist's reflections often inadvertently reinforce sustain talk rather than gently redirect it. When Samir says "I'm fine on my own, thanks," psychiatrist validates and reinforces rather than softening ("staying busy really helps you keep your mind focused"). When Samir says "Pills? Nah, not my style," no response provided—missed opportunity to soften. Uses one potential softening transition ("At the same time, I'm curious") but this is not true softening. Explicit softening techniques absent: no double-sided reflection, reframing, amplified reflection, or focus-shifting. Sustain talk largely unchallenged and reinforced through validating reflections.

## **OVERALL ASSESSMENT**

**Overall Session Score: 2.8/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Below Average to Adequate

**Summary:**
This session demonstrates foundational MI competence in creating a safe, collaborative, nonjudgmental space. The psychiatrist successfully establishes acceptance, autonomy support, and collaborative stance—essential MI foundations. However, the session falls significantly short of true MI excellence due to critical deficiencies in strategic evocation of change talk and softening of sustain talk.

**Strengths:**
- Strong opening and collaborative framing
- Consistent autonomy support and respect for client choice
- Nonjudgmental stance and unconditional positive regard
- Reflective listening throughout
- Avoidance of confrontation and argumentation

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Evocation of change talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)—Strategic questions to draw out intrinsic motivation, values, and reasons for change are absent
2. **Softening sustain talk**—Reflections often reinforce rather than gently challenge resistance
3. **Strategic direction**—Session lacks coherent progression toward clarifying ambivalence or assessing readiness
4. **Specific barrier exploration**—Concrete concerns (e.g., "mess with my head") not explored in depth
5. **Values connection**—Client values not systematically connected to potential reasons for change

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
While Samir likely leaves feeling respected and understood, he has not articulated any reasons for considering medication adherence. His resistance has been validated rather than gently challenged. The session has preserved the therapeutic relationship but has not advanced his readiness for change. Likelihood of evoking intrinsic motivation for change is currently **LOW**.

**Recommendation:**
With focused skill development on evocation and sustain talk softening, this psychiatrist could significantly increase effectiveness in moving clients toward contemplation and change. The foundation is strong; strategic MI techniques must be developed to build on it.
