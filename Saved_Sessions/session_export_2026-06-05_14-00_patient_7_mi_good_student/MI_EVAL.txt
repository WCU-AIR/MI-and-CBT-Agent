### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent skill in addressing sustain talk. Client produces several instances: minimization ("only tried once or twice"), discounting relevance, psychological distance ("hard to see the risk like it's really happening to me"). Clinician's approach is generally to gently redirect rather than directly engage sustain talk. While this maintains collaboration, it misses opportunities for more skillful softening. Double-sided reflections would acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting change talk, creating cognitive dissonance that motivates change. Clinician also doesn't explore minimization pattern ("only") by asking what "only" means or whether other contexts increase risk. More direct exploration of personal vulnerability would strengthen this dimension.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 4.0/5**

### Summary

This session demonstrates **solid motivational interviewing competence** with several exemplary elements. The psychiatrist excels at autonomy support and collaboration, consistently positioning Alexei as the decision-maker and owner of the change process. Empathy and reflective listening are accurate and capture both behavioral and emotional dimensions. Most importantly, the clinician effectively evokes and reinforces substantial change talk, successfully moving Alexei from minimization to articulated commitment to change. The consistent positive regard, genuine affirmations, and clear connection between target behavior and meaningful goals create an engaging, respectful therapeutic environment.

### Primary Deficiencies

Three interconnected areas require development:

1. **Premature psychoeducation:** Information about alcohol's effects on athletic performance is provided before fully exploring what Alexei already knows or believes—a missed opportunity for evocation and collaboration.

2. **Insufficient exploration of ambivalence:** When Alexei expresses key ambivalence ("it's hard to see the risk like it's really happening to me"), the clinician redirects rather than deeply exploring this critical moment through double-sided reflections and strategic questioning.

3. **Inadequate softening of sustain talk:** While soft redirection maintains collaboration, more sophisticated techniques like double-sided reflection and exploration of minimization patterns would more skillfully address sustain talk.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

The session successfully engages Alexei and evokes meaningful change talk, resulting in clear articulation of commitment to change. However, the deficiencies represent missed opportunities to deepen and solidify this motivation. By more skillfully exploring ambivalence and asking more strategic follow-up questions, the clinician could have generated even more robust change talk and better prepared Alexei for challenges when peer pressure is high or consequences feel distant. The current approach is likely to result in good engagement and some behavior change, but may not fully develop the depth of intrinsic motivation needed to sustain change over time in high-risk social situations.

---

## Recommendations for Skill Development

1. **Explore client knowledge before providing psychoeducation** – Ask open-ended questions first to elicit existing beliefs, then collaboratively discuss new information.

2. **Use double-sided reflections to address sustain talk** – Acknowledge minimization while highlighting change talk to create cognitive dissonance that motivates change.

3. **Deeply explore ambivalence rather than redirecting** – Ask follow-up questions that help client articulate both sides of mixed feelings more fully.

4. **Ask strategic follow-up questions to deepen change talk** – After change talk, ask about confidence, obstacles, and what would strengthen commitment.

5. **Explore social context and peer dynamics more thoroughly** – For adolescent clients, explicitly explore peer influence patterns, social anxiety around saying no, and specific high-risk contexts.

6. **Use open-ended language for assessment** – Replace scaling questions with more natural, open-ended invitations for self-assessment in client's own language.

7. **Explore minimization patterns to gently challenge sustain talk** – When client uses minimizing language ("only," "just"), gently explore what these words mean and whether other contexts increase risk.

8. **Strengthen acceptance by normalizing adolescent development** – Explicitly normalize adolescent experimentation, peer influence, and developmental challenges of saying no.

9. **Invite consent more explicitly before shifting direction** – Extend consent-seeking practice to other moments where direction is shifting or new topics introduced.

10. **Develop skill in evoking client problem-solving** – Rather than suggesting strategies, ask questions that help client generate their own ideas, strengthening autonomy and ownership.
