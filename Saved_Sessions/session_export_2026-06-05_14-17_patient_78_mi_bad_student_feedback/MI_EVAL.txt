# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1.5/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal unconditional positive regard throughout the session. Judgmental language ("You're not trying hard enough," "stop making excuses," "stop feeling sorry for yourself" repeated four times) communicates conditional regard tied to compliance. Emotional experience is pathologized rather than normalized. Brief moment of validation near end ("I appreciate your honesty") appears performative rather than genuine. Client's worth is never affirmed independent of behavioral compliance. Therapeutic alliance is significantly damaged by repeated invalidation and ultimatums.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is virtually absent. Psychiatrist fails to accurately reflect Liyana's emotional experience, frustration with plateau, sense of isolation, or ambivalence about change. When client states "I don't feel like anyone really gets that or supports me," psychiatrist responds with negation rather than understanding. No exploration of adolescent developmental context or why previous diet attempts failed. Single moment of apparent empathy immediately followed by directive advice, negating genuine empathic stance. Empathy is performed, not embodied.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear focus on diet and weight loss but pursues it through repetitive pressure and predetermined solutions rather than strategic MI guidance. Same question asked repeatedly ("Are you ready to commit to a diet plan today?") without adjusting approach based on client's consistent responses indicating unreadiness. True MI direction would involve understanding client's actual goals, exploring discrepancy between current and desired state, and collaboratively determining realistic next steps. Direction lacks flexibility and responsiveness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Collaboration is entirely absent. Psychiatrist adopts rigid authoritarian expert stance throughout, positioning themselves as sole holder of knowledge. Language consistently uses imperatives and declarations ("you must," "you have to," "you really need to") rather than invitations or collaborative exploration. Client's input is dismissed ("Maybe it's not just about diet?"). Power dynamic is entirely unequal with all power held by clinician. No genuine co-construction of understanding or solutions occurs. Contradicts MI principle that expertise about change resides mostly within the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is fundamentally violated throughout. Psychiatrist repeatedly pressures commitment ("Are you going to commit or not?") and frames hesitation as character defect. Explicit elimination of choice: "There is no room for excuses...No ifs, ands, or buts." Compliance framed as mandatory rather than optional. When client expresses unreadiness, psychiatrist responds with pressure rather than respect for autonomy. Client explicitly states need to "feel like I'm not alone in this, not just being pushed," and psychiatrist responds by pushing harder. No affirmation of client's right to choose.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 1.3/5**

### Strengths
- Maintains consistent focus on health behavior change goal (weight loss and diet modification)
- Gathers basic factual information about client's current behaviors
- Makes one brief attempt at validation near end of session
- Correctly identifies that diet modification is likely necessary for weight loss

### Critical Deficiencies
This session fundamentally violates MI principles across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist adopts a rigid expert stance, uses controlling and judgmental language, dismisses the client's emotional experience, and pressures compliance rather than evoking intrinsic motivation. Repeated use of "you have to," "you must," "stop feeling sorry for yourself," and "no ifs, ands, or buts" directly contradicts MI's core values.

**Failure to understand adolescent development:** Liyana explicitly identifies loneliness and lack of support as central to her struggle. These are dismissed as "excuses" rather than explored as clinically significant factors. For a 16-year-old, peer and family support are developmentally critical.

**Therapeutic alliance rupture:** By session end, client has explicitly stated unreadiness to commit and expressed need to feel supported rather than pushed. Psychiatrist's response is to push harder, damaging the therapeutic relationship and decreasing likelihood of engagement or behavior change.

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change
**This session is likely to significantly decrease motivation for change and damage engagement with treatment.** Research demonstrates that confrontational, directive approaches—particularly combined with dismissal of emotions and pressure for compliance—activate psychological reactance and increase resistance to change. Client enters with genuine ambivalence but real motivation; leaves feeling more discouraged, more alone, and less motivated to engage. **Likelihood of sustained behavior change: Very low.**

### Recommendation
This session requires significant retraining in MI principles and spirit. Recommend: (1) formal MI training or refresher course, (2) supervised practice with feedback, (3) review of MI literature emphasizing spirit and principles, and (4) self-reflection on personal beliefs about client motivation and change.
