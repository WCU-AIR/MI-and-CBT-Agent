### Evocation

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Direct confrontation and escalation in response to every instance of sustain talk. Patient expresses legitimate concerns ("I'm managing okay," "Cutting out alcohol completely feels like a big step," "Three times a week feels like a lot"), and psychiatrist responds with aggressive confrontation: "You're not seeing the bigger picture," "You're not really thinking straight," "That's not good enough." This is opposite of softening sustain talk. Psychiatrist directly argues against every reason patient offers for not changing, likely to entrench resistance and increase sustain talk. No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic responses to minimize sustain talk while maintaining collaborative relationship.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **severe and systematic violation of Motivational Interviewing principles** and fundamental patient-centered care standards. The psychiatrist demonstrates an authoritarian, controlling approach characterized by confrontation, dismissal, and contempt for the patient's perspective. Every core MI principle is violated:

- **Acceptance** replaced with judgment and contempt
- **Empathy** replaced with invalidation and dismissal
- **Collaboration** replaced with unilateral control
- **Autonomy Support** replaced with coercion and pressure
- **Evocation** replaced with imposition
- **Partnership** replaced with rigid hierarchy
- **Change Talk** dismissed rather than amplified
- **Sustain Talk** aggressively confronted rather than skillfully softened

**Clinical Impact:**

The patient begins with openness but progressively withdraws engagement as the psychiatrist escalates pressure and dismissal. By session end, patient explicitly refuses commitment and expresses need for autonomy. This approach is **highly likely to decrease motivation for change and increase resistance**, violating the fundamental MI principle that motivation resides within the client and cannot be imposed externally.

**Critical Deficiencies:**

1. Fundamental misunderstanding of collaborative therapeutic relationship
2. Complete absence of empathic listening and reflective statements
3. Systematic use of coercion and pressure tactics
4. Failure to evoke patient's own motivations
5. Aggressive confrontation of normal ambivalence

**Recommendation:**

This psychiatrist demonstrates **no competence in Motivational Interviewing** and is actively practicing **anti-MI approaches** that are contraindicated for behavior change work. **Immediate, intensive MI training and supervised practice are required** before continuing sessions focused on behavior change. Current approach is likely to harm patients by entrenching resistance and decreasing likelihood of sustained behavior change.
