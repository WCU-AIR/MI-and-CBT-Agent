## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Session Context:** Second session with 40-year-old executive presenting with moderate cocaine use disorder, substance-induced depressive disorder, passive suicidal ideation, insomnia, anhedonia, work stress, marital strain, and alcohol misuse.

**Critical Safety Concern:** Patient presented with explicit indicators of suicidal ideation ("wonder if I'll ever get back to how things used to be," "stuck in this rut with no way out") that were not assessed. No safety plan established. This represents serious clinical and ethical failure requiring immediate remediation.

**Strengths Observed:** (1) Therapist demonstrated basic warmth and was not hostile or demeaning; (2) Therapist was responsive to patient disclosures and did not dismiss concerns; (3) Therapist maintained non-judgmental stance regarding substance use disclosure; (4) Therapist attempted to be supportive and encouraging.

**Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Attention:**
- Complete absence of CBT structure (agenda, conceptualization, strategy, measurement)
- Zero CBT-specific techniques applied
- Failure to assess suicidal ideation
- Failure to address substance use disorder systematically
- Excessive self-disclosure undermining professional credibility
- No collaboration or shared decision-making
- Repetitive, circular session structure without progress
- No homework or between-session tasks

**Recommended Supervision Focus:**
1. Suicide risk assessment and safety planning protocols
2. Case conceptualization development and communication
3. Functional analysis of substance use
4. CBT-specific techniques (cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation)
5. Guided discovery and Socratic questioning
6. Professional boundaries and appropriate self-disclosure
7. Agenda-setting and session structure
8. Homework design and assignment

**Therapist Development Level:** Early-stage training. Requires structured supervision, skill-building, and competency assessment before independent practice with complex cases.
