## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### **Strengths:**
- **Exemplary interpersonal effectiveness (6/6):** Created warm, safe, non-judgmental environment with strong therapeutic alliance and genuine rapport
- **Strong collaboration (5/6):** Invited patient input on agenda, problem-solving, and decision-making; promoted teamwork throughout
- **Consistent feedback-seeking (5/6):** Regularly checked understanding and emotional response; solicited end-of-session feedback
- **Skillful cognitive restructuring (5/6):** Successfully guided patient through evidence examination using Socratic questioning, resulting in measurable anxiety reduction (8→4)
- **Clear communication:** Explained concepts without jargon; patient understood and remained engaged
- **Behavioral focus:** Identified concrete, actionable step rather than remaining abstract
- **Patient engagement:** Patient remained cooperative, motivated, and reported feeling hopeful

### **Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Incomplete case conceptualization (3/6):** Failed to address central stressor (breakup) and its emotional/cognitive impact; no exploration of grief, loss, or relationship-specific cognitions
- **Missed cultural/contextual factors:** Did not explore racial discrimination or first-generation student stress despite these being documented stressors
- **Incomplete symptom coverage:** Addressed only concentration difficulty; ignored depressed mood, social withdrawal, and financial anxiety (which was on agenda but never explored)
- **Limited CBT technique scope (5/6):** Relied primarily on cognitive restructuring and behavioral activation; did not employ behavioral experiments, exposure, activity scheduling, grief work, or other evidence-based techniques
- **No formal CBT tools assigned:** Did not introduce thought records, activity schedules, or structured worksheets for independent skill application
- **Insufficient behavioral planning (4/6):** Homework lacked specificity and comprehensive scope; no backup plans or measurement tools
- **Incomplete agenda (5/6):** Set two-part agenda but never addressed financial worries
- **No explicit treatment strategy (3/6):** Did not articulate coherent plan for addressing adjustment disorder; no explanation of intervention sequencing or rationale
- **Shallow cognitive work:** Examined one automatic thought thoroughly but did not explore core beliefs or deeper cognitive structures
- **Sleep/circadian rhythm ignored:** Despite midnight-shift work being major contributor to concentration difficulties, never explored or addressed

### **Clinical Impact:**
This session will likely produce **short-term symptom relief and increased hope**, but is **unlikely to produce sustained therapeutic change** because it does not address root causes of the adjustment disorder (unprocessed breakup, contextual stressors). Without processing the central stressor and implementing comprehensive interventions, the patient is at risk for symptom recurrence or escalation.

### **Competency Level:**
**Intermediate (4.3/6):** Therapist demonstrates solid foundational skills in general therapeutic practice and competent application of basic CBT techniques, but falls short of evidence-based CBT for complex presentations like adjustment disorder. With recommended changes addressing case conceptualization, cultural competence, and comprehensive treatment planning, this therapist could deliver highly effective CBT.
