--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar I Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Mark Hill was a 34-year-old African American man brought to an urban emergency room by police after neighbors reported him loudly knocking on multiple doors. He referred to himself as the "New Jesus" and declined to give another name. In the ER, he refused to sit, ran through the room, and was placed in restraints while receiving lorazepam 2 mg IM and haloperidol 5 mg IM.

Despite sedation, Mr. Hill remained giddily agitated, claiming he received messages from God and no longer needed sleep. His speech was rapid, disorganized, and difficult to follow. Labs, including blood alcohol and toxicology screens, were normal, aside from elevated blood sugar (210 mg/dL) and blood urea nitrogen.

During evaluation, he was disheveled and malodorous, made poor eye contact, had labile affect, laughed inappropriately, and became frustrated when misunderstood. He exhibited grandiose delusions, rapid speech, distractibility, pressured thoughts, and sexually inappropriate offers. Insight and judgment were poor. Collateral information from his sister revealed a similar episode one week prior and a prior history of hospitalization for schizoaffective disorder two years earlier.

Over 24 hours, he became calmer but continued to have rapid speech, racing thoughts, distractibility, and a strong belief in a divine mission. He denied suicidal or homicidal ideation. Historical and clinical information indicated he met all DSM-5 criteria for a manic episode with mood-congruent psychotic features.
