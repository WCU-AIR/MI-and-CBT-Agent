### Collaboration

Dismissive interruption early in session asserts authority over what Elena actually meant. Rapid-fire questioning designed to move Elena toward clinician's predetermined conclusions rather than inviting collaborative problem-solving. ### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy support is essentially absent. Clinician actively undermines client choice throughout. Soft directive ("Maybe you should start with cutting down smoking first...What do you think about trying that this week?") presents predetermined plan as suggestion while framing it as what Elena "should" do. Word "but" in response to Elena's doubt negates her concern and subtly pressures compliance despite reservations. Conditional autonomy offered ("think about small steps when you feel ready") communicates clinician will decide when client is ready. Fails to affirm Elena's right to choose not to change or to choose her own pace. Never explores Elena's own goals or asks what she wants to work on—assumes smoking cessation is target and directs Elena toward it. No genuine affirmation that "it's completely your choice" or "I respect whatever you decide." Conditional praise ("Keep up the good work") reinforces compliance with clinician's expectations rather than genuine autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Evocation is essentially absent. Clinician demonstrates almost no skill in drawing out client's own motivations, values, and reasons for change. Minimal open-ended questions designed to elicit Elena's thinking; instead relies on leading questions guiding toward predetermined conclusions ("Do you think the smoking might be making things worse?" "Is stress the main reason you smoke?"). When Elena offers change talk ("I've thought about quitting before"), clinician doesn't amplify or explore it—pivots to factual questions about barriers and support without deepening motivation. Fails to evoke Elena's values and what matters to her; tells Elena what should motivate her ("thinking about how it could help your son") rather than asking what she values. Misses opportunities to evoke strengths and resources (resilience, commitment to son, self-awareness). When Elena expresses low confidence ("4 or 5"), clinician doesn't explore what would increase confidence or what she believes about her ability—suggests she "should" try anyway. No questions like "What would be good about quitting?" "How does smoking fit with your values?" "What are your own reasons for change?"

Unilateral problem definition: assumes smoking cessation is problem and solution without asking how Elena defines problem or what she wants to address. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 1.4/5 – Poor Motivational Interviewing Practice**

### Key Findings:

This session demonstrates **significant deficiencies across nearly all MI competencies**. The psychiatrist operates from a directive, expert-driven medical model rather than MI principles, with minimal collaboration, partnership, evocation, or genuine empathic understanding.

**Critical Violations:**
- Opening dismissive interruption ("Sorry, I think you meant to say something else") sets tone of disrespect and undermines acceptance from the start
- Fundamental absence of collaboration and partnership—clinician maintains hierarchical expert stance throughout
- Minimal evocation of client's intrinsic motivation; reliance on leading questions and prescriptive recommendations
- Autonomy actively undermined through soft directives and conditional language
- Multiple missed opportunities to amplify Elena's change talk or skillfully address sustain talk

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
Elena's response trajectory reflects defensive reaction to perceived pressure and lack of genuine partnership. She moves from tentative openness ("Cutting back a little could be possible") to resistance ("I'm not sure it'll really make a difference") to resignation ("I'll just keep doing what I'm doing for now"). By session end, Elena has essentially disengaged and is unlikely to make changes or return for follow-up.

**Strengths (Limited):**
- Basic warmth and politeness in greeting
- Maintains focus on smoking as target behavior
- One accurate reflection ("smoking is your way to get some peace")
- Eventually softens directive stance, reducing confrontation

**Urgent Recommendations:**
1. Eliminate dismissive interruptions and validate client communication
2. Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance
3. Systematically evoke and amplify change talk
4. Explore and respect ambivalence rather than pushing past it
5. Support autonomy by affirming client choice and right to decide
6. Deepen empathic understanding by exploring emotional content
7. Explore client's own goals rather than imposing clinician's agenda
8. Replace leading questions with genuine open-ended questions
9. Validate and build on client strengths and efforts
10. Develop genuine partnership by sharing power and seeking client input

**Conclusion:**
This session does not embody the spirit of MI. Fundamental retraining in MI principles is needed, particularly around collaboration, evocation, autonomy support, and genuine partnership. The most urgent priority is shifting from a directive stance to a collaborative one and learning to evoke and amplify client change talk rather than imposing recommendations.
