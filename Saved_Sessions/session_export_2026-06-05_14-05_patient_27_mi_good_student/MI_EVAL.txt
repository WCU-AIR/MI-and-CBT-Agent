### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Asks some evocative questions early on ("What is it about these options that feels promising?") but quickly shifts to provision. Critically fails to evoke around substance use reduction—client mentions working on cutting back smoking/drinking and difficulty, but psychiatrist affirms without exploring her own reasons for change, barriers, or self-efficacy. Does not evoke client's own problem-solving capacity around childcare. Misses rich opportunities to strengthen client's sense of agency and self-efficacy by drawing out her own ideas and resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantially undermined as session progresses. Psychiatrist increasingly positions self as expert with solutions rather than recognizing client's wisdom and resourcefulness. Client demonstrates existing problem-solving (identified neighbor, heard about Head Start, reduced substance use, thinking strategically about relationships), but psychiatrist provides solutions rather than building on strengths. Language positions psychiatrist as knowledgeable expert and client as recipient. Does not acknowledge limits of clinician expertise or invite client's expertise about her own life and what will work for her family.

Misses opportunities to ask about what made her decide to change, what she's noticed, what would be different with progress. ### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent handling of barriers and concerns. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 3.1/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Strong opening with genuine curiosity and collaborative framing
- Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental tone throughout
- Accurate reflective listening demonstrating good perspective-taking
- Skillful validation of ambivalence and protective concerns
- Practical helpfulness with concrete information and respectful approaches
- Effective strength-spotting and affirmation of resilience

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- **Shift from evocation to provision:** Session transitions from MI-consistent evocation to clinician-directed advice-giving, undermining client autonomy and self-efficacy
- **Minimal change talk cultivation:** Particularly around substance use reduction; affirms but does not strategically evoke or amplify motivation
- **Inadequate ambivalence exploration:** Critical areas not deeply explored; clinician moves quickly to affirmation rather than sitting with mixed feelings
- **Expert positioning over partnership:** Increasingly positions self as expert problem-solver rather than maintaining true partnership
- **Limited autonomy support:** Subtle pressure toward clinician-suggested actions rather than explicit affirmation of client choice
- **Incomplete readiness assessment:** Does not thoroughly explore confidence and commitment regarding proposed next steps

### Clinical Impact:
Session demonstrates tension between supportive counseling and MI principles. While warm and practically helpful, this approach may undermine long-term change motivation and client autonomy. Client leaves with clinician-generated plan rather than client-owned solutions. Most critically, substance use reduction—an area of active ambivalence—receives minimal MI-consistent exploration. Session likely to increase immediate support and reduce acute stress but may not substantially strengthen intrinsic motivation or client confidence in independent problem-solving capacity.

### Recommended Priority Focus:
1. Deepen exploration of substance use ambivalence through evocative questioning
2. Shift from advice-giving to client problem-solving before providing expert input
3. Strengthen partnership by explicitly recognizing client expertise about their own life
4. Strategically cultivate and amplify change talk rather than simple affirmation
5. Maintain consistent autonomy support throughout session
