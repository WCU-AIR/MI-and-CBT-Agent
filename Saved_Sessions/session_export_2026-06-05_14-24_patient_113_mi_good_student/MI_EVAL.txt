# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Strong validation of Rosa's experience and normalization of her fears regarding withdrawal and weight gain. However, missed opportunity to more deeply normalize ambivalence as a healthy part of the contemplation process. Could have sat longer with Rosa's uncertainty before pivoting to problem-solving. Generic affirmation of "holding onto motivation even when confidence feels shaky" could have been more specific and personalized.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated through well-constructed complex reflections capturing both sides of ambivalence (e.g., "carrying a heavy mix of feelings"). Accurate understanding of Rosa's resilience and realistic perspective. However, empathy could deepen by sitting longer with emotional intensity before pivoting to strengths. When Rosa mentions financial strain as a barrier, the response acknowledges but doesn't deeply explore the emotional weight of this concern. Surface-level empathy in moments of high emotional content.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, strategic focus on smoking cessation maintained throughout with logical session progression: ambivalence → core motivation → confidence assessment → past resilience → resources → concrete plan. Collaborative goal-setting from the opening question. However, missed opportunity to more explicitly connect past resilience to current smoking cessation goal. Does not strategically reframe or gently challenge Rosa's limiting belief that she "can't imagine life without smoking." Direction remains somewhat passive in response to this particular barrier.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consistent invitation for Rosa's input and respect for her autonomy in choosing gradual reduction over abstinence. Opening question establishes partnership from the start. Affirms Rosa's choice rather than pushing for abstinence. However, missed opportunities to more explicitly invite co-construction of solutions. When Rosa identifies barriers like weight gain, clinician acknowledges but doesn't ask "What ideas do you have about managing weight while quitting?" Clinician later suggests resources rather than inviting joint problem-solving.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout the session. Consistent choice-affirming language ("If you decided," "would you feel comfortable") emphasizes Rosa's agency and right to choose her own pace. Fully validates her preference for gradual reduction rather than abstinence. Never prescribes or pressures; meets client where she is rather than where clinician thinks she should be. Final affirmation that "honoring your feelings while taking meaningful steps" is a sign of strength reinforces autonomy. Zero instances of directive advice-giving or minimization of client's right to choose.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate evocation with some effective questions drawing out Rosa's core health motivation. However, significant gaps in systematic evocation of change talk across DARN-CAT domains. Does not strategically ask about Desire ("What would be best about quitting?"), Ability ("What strengths could help?"), Reasons (explored but not deeply), Need (largely absent), or Commitment (emerging but not explicitly cultivated). When Rosa's confidence increases from 3-4 to 5, clinician doesn't explore what caused this shift or amplify it. Clinician is responsive to change talk but not proactively evoking it through strategic questioning.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership with genuine power-sharing and respect for Rosa's expertise about her own readiness. Opening question establishes that Rosa's agenda drives the session. Full embrace of her preferred approach (gradual reduction) rather than subtle steering toward abstinence demonstrates true partnership. Consistent use of collaborative language ("It sounds like," "You're recognizing") positions Rosa as source of insight. Minor opportunity: when exploring past resilience, clinician draws connections rather than fully inviting Rosa to co-construct them. Could ask "What do you think from that experience might help you now?" rather than directing the application.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some cultivation of change talk with appropriate affirmation of emerging motivation ("I know I've got to stop," "That made me rethink everything"). However, not systematic or strategic. Using DARN-CAT framework: Desire minimally explored; Ability somewhat explored through past resilience but not deeply; Reasons moderately explored; Need minimally explored; Commitment emerging but not explicitly cultivated; Taking steps well-cultivated. Missed critical opportunity when Rosa's confidence increases from 3-4 to 5—clinician doesn't explicitly explore what caused this shift or amplify it to deepen motivation. Clinician is responsive but not proactively evoking change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal skill in addressing Rosa's sustain talk throughout the session. Rosa articulates clear sustain talk: "I just can't imagine not going through life without smoking," "If I quit smoking, I'm going to gain weight," "Withdrawal… it's awful," "I'm not sure it's going to be worth all the hassle." Clinician acknowledges these concerns without strategically redirecting, reframing, or using double-sided reflections. Limiting beliefs about identity are not gently challenged. Missed opportunities to create cognitive discrepancy (e.g., "You've said your health matters deeply, and you're also saying it might not be worth the effort—help me understand that"). Does not use amplification or shift focus from sustain talk to change talk. Critical MI skill gap that substantially limits session's potential to evoke sustained motivation.

## Summary of Clinical Evaluation

### Strengths
- **Exemplary autonomy support** with consistent choice-affirming language and full validation of Rosa's preferred pace
- **Strong collaborative partnership** with genuine power-sharing and respect for client expertise
- **Solid acceptance and empathy** with consistent unconditional positive regard and well-constructed complex reflections
- **Clear strategic direction** with logical session progression and collaborative goal-setting
- **Emerging change talk** appropriately affirmed; Rosa articulates genuine motivation and concrete next steps
- **Practical planning** with Rosa identifying manageable steps (cutting back, talking to doctor, finding support)

### Areas for Improvement
- **Insufficient systematic evocation of change talk** across DARN-CAT domains; clinician is responsive but not proactively evoking motivation
- **Minimal softening of sustain talk** (most significant gap); limiting beliefs and barriers acknowledged but not strategically reframed or challenged
- **Limited exploration of confidence shift** when Rosa's confidence increases from 3-4 to 5; missed opportunity to amplify and deepen motivation
- **Superficial exploration of barriers** regarding weight gain and financial strain; limited deep exploration or collaborative problem-solving
- **Missed opportunities for co-construction** of connections between past resilience and current change goal

### Clinical Impact
While Rosa leaves with increased confidence (3-4 to 5) and a concrete plan, the session's impact on her intrinsic motivation is likely limited. The psychiatrist has created a safe, collaborative space where Rosa feels heard and respected—genuinely valuable for engagement. However, the session has not deeply cultivated Rosa's own reasons for change, sense of capability, or commitment to action. By not strategically addressing sustain talk, significant barriers and limiting beliefs remain largely unchallenged. Risk of relapse when Rosa encounters first craving, withdrawal symptom, or weight concern is substantial. Session demonstrates supportive counseling but falls short of strategic evocation characteristic of excellent MI practice.
