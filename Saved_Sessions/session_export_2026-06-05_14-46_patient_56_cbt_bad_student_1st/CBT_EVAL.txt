## SUPERVISOR SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Strengths Demonstrated
- Maintains warm, non-judgmental, supportive tone
- Creates safe therapeutic environment
- Displays genuine concern and encouragement
- Does not criticize or blame patient for depression
- Conveys belief that improvement is possible

### Critical Deficiencies
1. **Complete absence of CBT structure and methodology** — No agenda, no guided discovery, no techniques, no homework, no coherent strategy
2. **Fundamental misunderstanding of clinical presentation** — Misses guilt, worthlessness, anhedonia, functional impairment, and peripartum-specific stressors
3. **Ineffective intervention approach** — Generic advice and reassurance disconnected from underlying drivers of depression
4. **Lack of collaboration** — Patient remains passive; therapist adopts expert-advice stance
5. **Excessive therapist self-disclosure** — Shifts focus from patient to therapist; inappropriate in clinical context

### Immediate Actions Required
1. **Implement structured agenda-setting and collaborative goal-definition** at beginning of next session
2. **Learn and apply guided discovery (Socratic questioning)** to replace advice-giving
3. **Identify and target core cognitions** using thought records and cognitive restructuring
4. **Implement behavioral activation** for anhedonia and isolation
5. **Develop coherent, evidence-based treatment strategy** for postpartum depression
6. **Eliminate excessive therapist self-disclosure**
7. **Assign specific, rationale-based homework** with clear instructions
8. **Check for understanding and feedback** throughout sessions
9. **Increase specificity and decrease generic advice**
10. **Pursue urgent CBT training and expert supervision**

### Clinical Concern
**This session is unlikely to produce meaningful therapeutic change.** While Yvonne may feel temporarily supported, she will not develop independent coping skills, will not identify or challenge core beliefs maintaining her depression, and will not engage in behavioral change. If this approach continues, Yvonne will likely remain depressed and may discontinue treatment. For moderate postpartum depression with guilt, worthlessness, anhedonia, and social isolation, evidence-based CBT produces significant improvement. This session does not represent evidence-based CBT.

**Recommendation:** Supervisor should meet with therapist immediately to review CBT fundamentals and establish a structured supervision plan before therapist continues treating patients with depression.
