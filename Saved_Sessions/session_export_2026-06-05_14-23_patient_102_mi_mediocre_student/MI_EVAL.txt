## **SUMMARY OF CLINICAL FINDINGS**

### **Strengths:**
- ✓ Non-confrontational, respectful demeanor maintained throughout
- ✓ Avoids arguing with patient or expressing judgment
- ✓ Appropriate use of scaling questions (importance/confidence)
- ✓ Accepts patient's pace and hesitations without pressure
- ✓ Demonstrates awareness of MI principles in closing statement

### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- ✗ **Pervasive directiveness** (HIGHEST PRIORITY): Consistently tells patient what to do rather than asking what patient thinks
- ✗ **Minimal reflective listening**: Few complex reflections; misses emotional nuance throughout
- ✗ **Failure to evoke**: Provides solutions rather than drawing out patient's own ideas, motivations, resources
- ✗ **Expert-driven stance**: Positions self as authority rather than collaborative partner
- ✗ **Missed exploration of importance-confidence gap**: Critical gap (8 vs. 4) never explored in depth
- ✗ **Insufficient amplification of change talk**: Patient's own motivation never strengthened through strategic questioning
- ✗ **Lack of genuine partnership**: Power remains with psychiatrist; patient contributions don't shape session direction
- ✗ **Generic affirmations**: Lack authenticity after directive advice-giving

### **Impact on Patient Engagement:**
This approach is likely to **reduce intrinsic motivation** rather than strengthen it. While patient leaves with concrete suggestions (tracking drinks, setting limits), has not been helped to access own reasons for change or build confidence in ability to change. Directive approach may **increase resistance** by positioning change as something psychiatrist wants rather than something patient wants for self. Patient's low confidence (4/5) is critical issue; session does nothing to address it and may have inadvertently reinforced doubt.

### **Recommendation:**
**Fundamental shift required from directive, expert-driven advice-giving to collaborative evocation.** This psychiatrist must develop genuine MI competence through focused practice on: (1) asking open questions before offering suggestions, (2) developing reflective listening capturing emotional meaning, (3) exploring ambivalence rather than moving past it, (4) amplifying change talk, (5) building genuine partnership, and (6) using autonomy-supporting language emphasizing choice rather than pressure.
