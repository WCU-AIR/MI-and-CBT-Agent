### Empathy

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Critical deficiency. Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal genuine collaboration and adopts expert-driven, hierarchical stance throughout. Unilateral problem identification through closed questions directing conversation toward predetermined categories. Expert-driven solutions prescribed repeatedly without soliciting client input. No joint problem-solving when client identifies barriers. Scaling question used to justify advice rather than explore client perspective. Power imbalance maintained through language positioning psychiatrist as authority. Final plan entirely psychiatrist-generated with client passively agreeing. Fundamental violation of MI partnership principle.

### Softening Sustain Talk

Attempts to reframe some sustain talk (offering alternatives to relaxation). Acknowledges some barriers, showing recognition of sustain talk. ## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.9/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **significant deficiencies in MI practice** that fundamentally undermine therapeutic effectiveness. While the psychiatrist maintains basic politeness and session structure, the interaction is dominated by an expert-driven, prescriptive stance that violates core MI principles of partnership, collaboration, and evocation.

**Key Strengths:**
- Basic warm opening establishing rapport
- Clear focus on smoking cessation maintained throughout
- Some reflective listening present
- Avoidance of confrontation
- Token acknowledgment of client autonomy at end

**Critical Weaknesses:**
- Expert-driven stance dominates (repeated "it would probably help if..." language)
- Minimal genuine evocation of client's own motivations and resources
- Sustain talk not skillfully addressed; may inadvertently strengthen resistance
- Empathy lacks emotional depth; reflections are factual rather than emotionally resonant
- Autonomy support undermined by prescriptive language and subtle pressure
- Client's expertise about her own life not recognized or valued
- Minimal change talk produced and not amplified
- No genuine partnership or power-sharing

**Impact on Client Engagement:** Client appears cooperative but demonstrates passive agreement rather than genuine commitment. Low confidence (4-5), unresolved ambivalence, and unaddressed concerns about sustain change suggest low likelihood of sustained behavior change. Compliance-based rather than intrinsically motivated.

**MI Spirit Embodied:** No. Session reflects traditional expert-driven, problem-focused approach inconsistent with MI principles of partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation.

**Recommendations Priority:**
1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. Develop strategic evocation skills through targeted questioning
3. Deepen empathy through complex reflections and emotional attunement
4. Explore and amplify change talk systematically
5. Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk and acknowledge ambivalence
