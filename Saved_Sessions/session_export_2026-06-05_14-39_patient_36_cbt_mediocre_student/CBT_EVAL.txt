## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR CORRECTIVE ACTION

### **HIGHEST PRIORITY (Implement Immediately)**

1. **Reconceptualize as attachment disorder requiring parent-child intervention**
   - Shift from individual cognitive therapy to parent-focused, attachment-informed approach
   - Conduct comprehensive assessment of attachment relationship and caregiver checking-in capacity
   - Discontinue cognitive restructuring; replace with behavioral parent training

2. **Involve parents as co-therapists in all future sessions**
   - Schedule session with both adoptive parents present
   - Provide psychoeducation about DSED and neurobiological impact of institutional neglect
   - Collaboratively develop behavioral plan for managing stranger interactions

3. **Replace cognitive restructuring with behavioral parent training and environmental structure**
   - Teach parents: supervised stranger interactions, explicit teaching of "safe adults," reinforcement of caregiver checking-in, environmental structure (keeping child within arm's reach)
   - Discontinue thought-challenging; implement evidence-based behavioral strategies

4. **Establish explicit, prioritized behavioral targets focused on safety**
   - Identify three most dangerous behaviors: (1) indiscriminate stranger approach, (2) willingness to leave with unfamiliar adults, (3) poor boundary recognition
   - Develop specific, measurable goals for each
   - Conduct safety planning with parents

### **HIGH PRIORITY (Implement Within 1-2 Sessions)**

5. **Conduct comprehensive developmental and attachment assessment**
   - Administer standardized measures of attachment and developmental functioning
   - Assess capacity to discriminate safe from unsafe adults and use adoptive mother as secure base
   - Clarify actual developmental level to inform treatment planning

6. **Implement Parent-Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT) or attachment-focused family therapy**
   - Transition to evidence-based treatment model with empirical support for DSED
   - Coach parents in real-time during structured interaction with child
   - Emphasize strengthening parent-child relationship and parental attunement

### **MODERATE PRIORITY (Implement Concurrently)**

7. **Eliminate cognitive restructuring; replace with explicit teaching of safe vs. unsafe adults**
   - Use visual aids, role-play, and repetition for concrete behavioral teaching
   - Avoid abstract thought-challenging; focus on concrete discrimination skills

8. **Set clear, collaborative agenda with explicit prioritization and time allocation**
   - Establish specific agenda at start of each session with parents
   - Allocate time for: psychoeducation, behavioral planning, parent coaching, homework review
   - Prioritize most dangerous behaviors

9. **Elicit and respond to feedback throughout sessions**
   - Regularly check for understanding and barriers to implementation
   - Ask parents and patient: "Does this make sense?" "Do you feel ready?" "What concerns do you have?"
   - Strengthen therapeutic alliance through feedback responsiveness

10. **Assign specific, parent-directed homework with clear rationale and measurement plan**
    - Replace vague homework with specific behavioral assignments (e.g., "Practice checking-in routine 3 times daily")
    - Provide written instructions, visual aids, and tracking sheets
    - Include measurement plan for objective progress tracking

11. **Conduct safety planning with parents to prevent dangerous behavior**
    - Develop specific plan for managing stranger approach and unauthorized leaving
    - Include environmental strategies, behavioral strategies, and emergency protocols
    - Role-play scenarios with parents

### **ONGOING**

12. **Obtain consultation and training in early childhood trauma and attachment disorders**
    - Seek consultation with colleague experienced in DSED and attachment trauma
    - Pursue training in PCIT or attachment-focused family therapy
    - Review current research on DSED and institutional neglect
    - Join consultation groups for early childhood trauma specialists
