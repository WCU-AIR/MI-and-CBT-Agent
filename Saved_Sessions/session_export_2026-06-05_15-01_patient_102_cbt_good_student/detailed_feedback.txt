
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## CRITICAL PRELIMINARY NOTE

**This evaluation identifies a fundamental clinical problem:** The therapist is conducting cognitive-behavioral therapy with a patient who presents with **acute delirium in a medical/surgical setting**. This is clinically inappropriate and potentially harmful.

Victor Alvarez exhibits:
- Acute confusion and disorientation (doesn't know where he is)
- Fluctuating mental status (hypoactive, eyes closed, minimal engagement)
- Severely impaired attention and awareness (cannot sustain focus)
- Cognitive impairment (slowed responses, difficulty tracking)
- Recent physiological insult (hip surgery, anesthesia, medications)
- Elevated BUN, malnutrition, possible dehydration, possible alcohol withdrawal
- Haloperidol administration (antipsychotic for agitation)

**CBT is contraindicated for acute delirium.** Delirium requires medical stabilization, treatment of underlying causes, and supportive care—not cognitive restructuring. The therapist's approach, while technically demonstrating some CBT skills, is fundamentally misapplied to a patient whose cognitive impairment is **organic and medical**, not primarily psychological.

This session represents a **category error in clinical judgment** that supersedes technical CBT competence.

**Explanation:**

## Recommendations

### 1. **URGENT: Recognize and Treat Acute Delirium as the Primary Problem**
- **Action:** Immediately consult with the medical team (internist, surgeon, geriatrician) to conduct a comprehensive assessment of delirium causes. Do not continue CBT until delirium is medically stabilized.
- **Example:** "Mr. Alvarez, I'm concerned that your confusion and tiredness may be related to medical factors from your surgery and hospitalization. Before we continue with our psychological work, I'd like to make sure the medical team has fully evaluated what's causing these symptoms. Let me speak with your doctor about this."
- **Practice:** Learn the DSM-5 criteria for delirium and the common causes in post-surgical patients (pain, dehydration, malnutrition, medications, sleep deprivation, infection). Develop a protocol for assessing delirium before initiating CBT.

### 2. **Assess and Address Medical Drivers of Delirium**
- **Action:** Ensure the following are evaluated and optimized:
  - Pain management (is the patient in pain? Is pain medication adequate?)
  - Hydration status (elevated BUN suggests dehydration; ensure adequate IV/oral fluids)
  - Nutrition (low albumin suggests malnutrition; ensure adequate nutrition)
  - Medication review (is haloperidol necessary? Are there other medications contributing?)
  - Sleep (is the patient getting adequate sleep? Are there sleep disturbances?)
  - Mobilization (is the patient immobilized? Can physical therapy help?)
  - Orientation support (clocks, calendars, reorientation by staff)
- **Example:** "Mr. Alvarez, I'd like to work with your medical team to make sure you're getting enough fluids, food, and pain relief. These things can really affect how clear your thinking is. Let's also make sure you're getting good sleep at night."
- **Practice:** Develop a checklist of medical factors to assess in post-surgical patients with delirium. Collaborate with medical staff on optimization.

### 3. **Defer Intensive CBT Until Delirium Resolves**
- **Action:** Once delirium is medically stabilized and cognitive capacity improves, reassess whether CBT is appropriate. At that time, the patient may benefit from CBT for residual depression, anxiety, or adjustment to hospitalization.
- **Example:** "Once you're feeling more clear-headed and the confusion improves, we can work together on some of the worries you've mentioned about being a burden. Right now, let's focus on getting you medically stable."
- **Practice:** Establish criteria for when CBT can be resumed (e.g., confusion rating <4/10, sustained attention for 20+ minutes, orientation to person/place/time).

### 4. **Provide Supportive, Medically-Informed Psychological Care**
- **Action:** While medical stabilization is underway, provide supportive care:
  - Reassurance about the temporary nature of delirium
  - Validation of fear and confusion
  - Orientation support (reorienting to place, time, situation)
  - Encouragement to engage with medical staff and accept care
  - Coping strategies for managing fear during confusion
- **Example:** "What you're experiencing—the confusion and feeling mixed up—is something that happens to many people after surgery. It's temporary, and we're going to help you feel better. Right now, it's important to accept help from the nurses and doctors, even though it might feel hard."

### 5. **Assess Capacity for CBT Before Initiating Treatment**
- **Action:** Before beginning CBT, assess whether the patient has adequate cognitive capacity:
  - Can the patient sustain attention for 20+ minutes?
  - Can the patient understand and retain information?
