## SUMMARY OF CRITICAL FINDINGS

### Strengths Demonstrated
- **Empathic attunement** (Item 3: 5/6): Genuine warmth, accurate reflections, validation of emotional experience
- **Interpersonal effectiveness** (Item 4: 5/6): Confidence, professionalism, appropriate rapport-building
- **Guided discovery** (Item 7: 4/6): Effective Socratic questioning, excellent perspective-taking technique
- **CBT technique application** (Item 10: 4/6): Competent execution of thought records, cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments
- **Collaboration** (Item 5: 4/6): Collaborative language, solicitation of patient input

### Critical Deficiencies
- **Fundamental misdiagnosis** (Items 8, 9): Treated as depression/anxiety when patient has acute delirium (organic, medical condition)
- **Inappropriate intervention** (Item 9: 2/6): CBT contraindicated for acute delirium; strategy does not address medical drivers
- **Cognitive overload** (Item 6: 3/6): Continued cognitively demanding work despite confusion 7/10 and tiredness 8/10
- **Misaligned agenda** (Item 1: 3/6): Failed to assess capacity for CBT or prioritize medical stabilization
- **Unrealistic homework** (Item 11: 3/6): Cognitively demanding for patient with acute cognitive impairment; does not address medical needs

### Clinical Judgment Alert
**This session should NOT be considered evidence-based CBT.** While technical skills were demonstrated, the fundamental clinical judgment was flawed. Applying CBT to acute delirium is contraindicated and potentially harmful. The therapist must immediately:
1. Recognize acute delirium as the primary problem
2. Consult with medical team
3. Defer intensive CBT until medical stabilization and cognitive capacity improvement
4. Develop competency in delirium recognition and management

**Recommendation:** Do not continue with cognitive therapy. Prioritize medical assessment and stabilization.
