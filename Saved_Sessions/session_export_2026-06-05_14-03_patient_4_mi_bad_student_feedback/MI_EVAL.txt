- Unilateral expert control; explicit rejection of collaboration

### Impact on Client Engagement

**Predicted Outcome: DECREASED motivation and INCREASED resistance.**

Aisha enters with high intrinsic motivation ("I really want to start working out," "I'm ready to make changes"). The psychiatrist's approach systematically undermines this through invalidation, pressure, and disempowerment. Aisha is likely to:
- Leave feeling unheard and pressured
- Experience decreased confidence in her ability to change
- Fail at the prescribed all-or-nothing plan (as she has before)
- Internalize shame rather than recognizing plan misalignment
- Avoid future engagement with this clinician

**This session demonstrates how poor MI practice actively harms client motivation.**

---

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Fundamental reorientation:** Shift from expert-driven to collaborative partnership
2. **Develop reflective listening:** Practice 2:1 ratio of reflections to questions
3. **Evoke and amplify change talk:** Explore client's own reasons for change
4. **Eliminate coercive language:** Replace "must/have to" with collaborative language
5. **Validate barriers:** Respond with curiosity, not dismissal
6. **Support autonomy:** Offer genuine choices; affirm client's right to decide
7. **Use double-sided reflections:** Validate both motivation and concerns
8. **Collaborate on planning:** Involve client in every step
9. **Recognize strengths:** Affirm self-awareness, honesty, and motivation
10. **Seek formal MI training:** Enroll in MINT-certified training and clinical supervision

**Formal MI training and intensive clinical supervision are essential before this clinician continues clinical practice.**
