### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and affirms Amina's commitment throughout the session ("You're carrying a lot, and it's impressive how committed you are despite these challenges"). Warm, supportive tone is maintained with no judgmental language. However, opportunities exist to more explicitly normalize single parenthood challenges and address potential internalized shame about needing support. Acceptance is genuine but could be more proactive in validating worth independent of productivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate, sometimes complex reflections that capture both content and emotional undertone ("The juggling act you're describing feels like it requires constant adjustment and leaves little room for error, which must be exhausting"). Perspective-taking is demonstrated throughout. However, missed opportunities exist to explore emotional nuance more deeply—particularly around the fear of asking for help and the internal conflict between personal goals and parental responsibilities. Empathy is consistent but could penetrate deeper into underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus maintained throughout on the target behavior: attending job interviews by securing reliable childcare. Session does not wander; each exchange builds logically toward concrete action planning. Strategic guiding questions naturally lead toward problem-solving. However, the psychiatrist does not explicitly explore whether employment is truly Amina's autonomous goal or externally motivated (case management, benefits requirements). Deeper clarification of autonomous motivation would strengthen direction and ensure true client-centeredness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration demonstrated with some joint problem-solving and open questions ("What would you like to focus on?"). However, the psychiatrist frequently shifts into a subtle expert or guide role, interpreting and framing Amina's experience rather than co-constructing meaning with her. Power-sharing is uneven; the psychiatrist directs exploration more than partnering equally. Amina's expertise about her own situation is not explicitly invited. True collaborative prioritization of focus areas is absent.

### Autonomy Support

The psychiatrist does not pressure or minimize Amina's concerns. However, opportunities exist to more explicitly explore whether employment is autonomously motivated or driven by external systems. Additionally, the psychiatrist does not invite Amina to set the pace or direction for follow-up, which represents a subtle asymmetry in autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic evocation demonstrated through open questions that invite Amina to articulate her own thinking ("What has helped you manage or find solutions in the past?"). However, significant gaps exist in deeply drawing out intrinsic motivation, values, and reasons for change. The psychiatrist does not ask "What does working mean to you?" or explore what values drive her goal. Past successes and resilience are acknowledged but not deeply explored. Evocation is present but superficial and inconsistent; the psychiatrist accepts stated motivation without helping Amina deepen or clarify it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial partnership demonstrated, but the psychiatrist frequently assumes a subtle guide or expert role. Interpretive statements like "It sounds like having that support could really lift a heavy burden" and "Your organizational skills have been real strengths" position the psychiatrist as the one identifying strengths and framing meaning rather than inviting Amina to do so. True power-sharing and co-creation are limited. The psychiatrist does not explicitly acknowledge Amina's expertise about her own situation, children, and constraints. ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

### Softening Sustain Talk

However, the psychiatrist could more deeply explore ambivalence before redirecting—when Amina expresses uncertainty or fear, the psychiatrist validates but moves quickly past rather than inviting fuller exploration of the underlying emotions and concerns.

## Overall Clinical Summary

### Session Score: **3.7/5** – Adequate to Good MI Practice

**Strengths:**
- Consistent empathic tone with accurate, often complex reflective listening
- Strong autonomy support with explicit affirmation of client choice
- Skillful handling of sustain talk through validation and strategic reframing without confrontation
- Good cultivation of change talk with reinforcement of client confidence
- Clear, appropriate direction maintained throughout without session drift
- Warm, supportive, nonjudgmental stance communicating unconditional positive regard
- Solid movement toward concrete action planning with identification of specific first step

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Limited deep evocation of intrinsic motivation and values** — acceptance of stated motivation without exploration of what work means or why it matters
- **Subtle expert/guide positioning rather than true partnership** — frequent interpretation and framing of experience rather than co-construction
- **Insufficient exploration of ambivalence and emotional nuance** — validation followed by quick redirection rather than deeper exploration
- **Lack of autonomous motivation clarification** — no explicit exploration of whether employment is authentically chosen or externally driven
- **Limited depth in exploring past successes and resilience** — acknowledgment without invitation for deeper reflection
- **Weak specificity in commitment planning** — first step identified but concrete details (who, when, what) not established
- **Absent collaborative prioritization** — psychiatrist does not explicitly invite confirmation that childcare is the right focus

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
The session likely maintains Amina's engagement and provides clear direction. However, sustained motivation for change may be limited without deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation. The subtle expert positioning may reduce Amina's sense of ownership and self-efficacy. Lack of specificity in commitment planning increases risk of non-follow-through given real barriers she faces.

**Recommendation:**
To advance to strong (4.0+) or excellent (4.5+) MI practice, prioritize: (1) deepening evocation of intrinsic motivation through open-ended questioning, (2) strengthening true partnership by inviting client interpretation, (3) exploring ambivalence more thoroughly before redirecting, and (4) establishing concrete, specific behavioral commitments. These changes would significantly increase likelihood of sustained client motivation and behavior change.
