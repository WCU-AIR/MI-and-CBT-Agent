## **SUMMARY ASSESSMENT**

### **Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of motivational interviewing practice** and a **violation of MI spirit across nearly every dimension**. The psychiatrist demonstrates an authoritarian, directive, confrontational approach fundamentally inconsistent with core MI principles.

**Key Deficits:**
- ☐ Zero reflective listening statements in entire session
- ☐ Systematic dismissal of patient's stated priorities and life context
- ☐ Coercive pressure and threat-based persuasion throughout
- ☐ Suppression rather than evocation of patient's genuine change talk
- ☐ Direct confrontation of sustain talk, increasing resistance
- ☐ Dismissal of legitimate clinical priority (medication safety)
- ☐ Rigid, repetitive approach without adaptation or exploration
- ☐ Unilateral decision-making with no collaborative input

### **Clinical Impact:**

**This approach is highly likely to:**
- Decrease patient's motivation for change
- Damage therapeutic relationship
- Reduce likelihood of patient following through on recommendations
- Reduce likelihood of patient returning to this clinician
- Increase patient resistance and defensive responding

The patient is **not resistant to health improvement**; she is resistant to this particular approach and timing. She demonstrates reasonable prioritization, legitimate life constraints, and genuine health motivation. A skilled MI clinician would recognize these as strengths and build on them.

### **Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Eliminate confrontational language** ("You're making excuses," "You have to," "This is non-negotiable")
2. **Develop reflective listening skills** (aim for 2:1 ratio of reflections to questions)
3. **Meet patient where she is** (address stated priorities first; build trust)
4. **Recognize and amplify change talk** (patient demonstrates genuine motivation)
5. **Use autonomy-supportive language** ("It's up to you," "What feels right to you?")
6. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** (this session demonstrates fundamental misalignment with MI principles)

### **Recommendation:**

**Psychiatrist requires immediate, intensive training in MI principles and supervised practice with feedback before continuing to see patients for behavior change counseling.** The most critical priority is developing genuine collaborative stance, reflective listening skills, and ability to recognize and amplify patient's own motivation for change rather than imposing motivation through pressure and threat.
