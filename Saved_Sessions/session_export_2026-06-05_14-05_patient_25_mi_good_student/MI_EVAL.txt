### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Validates Daniel's perspective early ("your evening beers are a way you've found to relax and decompress") and normalizes ambivalence effectively. However, acceptance could be deepened through earlier affirmation of strengths and more explicit celebration of Daniel as a person. Psychoeducation segment is informational rather than collaborative, missing opportunity to invite Daniel's perspective first before offering clinical information.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with several complex, accurate reflections capturing ambivalence and discrepancy (e.g., "You're weighing how these two parts of your life—the need to unwind and your commitment to training—might be in tension right now"). Double-sided reflections are particularly skillful. However, empathy occasionally misses opportunities for deeper emotional attunement—when Daniel expresses difficulty convincing himself change is necessary, clinician could have explored the emotional experience behind this hesitation more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, strategic focus on alcohol consumption and its relationship to marathon training throughout session. Logical progression from validation through discrepancy exploration to concrete planning demonstrates purposeful direction. However, direction could be more precise in earlier exploration of what specific reductions Daniel is considering. Clinician doesn't explicitly revisit Daniel's initial ambivalence about whether drinking is "a big deal" after psychoeducation to assess perspective shift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance established through opening question inviting Daniel to set agenda. Clinician consistently seeks Daniel's input ("What do you think makes your drinking stand out?") rather than imposing direction. However, collaboration weakens during psychoeducation segment where clinician assumes expert role. More collaborative approach would have positioned Daniel as knowledgeable partner by asking what he already suspects about alcohol's effects before offering information.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Daniel's right to choose through autonomy-supportive language ("if you decided," "when you're ready"). Validates ambivalence without pushing ("A 5 or 6 sounds like you're in a space where change feels somewhat important but also not pressing"). Positions Daniel as architect of change plan through questions like "What would be one small, specific step?" However, could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Daniel's right to continue current drinking if he chooses.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates moderate evocation through some good questions ("What are some reasons you might want to think about adjusting your drinking?") but relies too heavily on psychoeducation rather than evoking Daniel's own discovery. Critical gap: provides information about alcohol's effects on recovery without first fully evoking what Daniel already knows or suspects. Misses opportunities to deepen change talk (when Daniel says improvements "sound pretty appealing," clinician could ask "Tell me more about what those improvements might look like"). Doesn't deeply explore how Daniel's discipline could support change or what marathon training represents to him.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Establishes initial partnership through collaborative opening but shifts toward expert role during psychoeducation and planning phases. Framing of information-sharing ("I shared some information") positions clinician as knowledge expert and Daniel as recipient rather than co-constructing understanding. Doesn't deeply explore whether Daniel's proposed plan is truly his idea or subtly guided. Clinician could position Daniel as expert on his own experience by asking "What's your sense of what's causing that?" rather than offering explanations. Partnership is present but inconsistent.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through good questions but doesn't consistently amplify or reinforce Daniel's own language favoring change. When Daniel expresses change talk ("That sounds pretty appealing," surprise at intake level), clinician often validates briefly rather than deepening through follow-up questions. Misses opportunity to ask Daniel to elaborate on his 7/10 confidence ("What makes you feel confident about this plan?"). Doesn't strategically use Daniel's change talk to counter sustain talk. Session would be significantly strengthened by lingering on change talk statements to strengthen Daniel's conviction about change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses gentle reframing to introduce alternative perspectives without confrontation. When Daniel expresses skepticism, clinician validates ambivalence and introduces discrepancy rather than arguing. However, approach is somewhat passive—clinician validates sustain talk but doesn't always actively soften it through strategic reframing. Could use more double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge tension between change and status quo (e.g., "Part of you is waiting to see real problems before making a change, and at the same time, you're noticing that your drinking might already be affecting your recovery").

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 3.7/5 (Intermediate Competence)**

### Key Strengths:
- Strong opening and consistent collaboration establishing genuine partnership
- Excellent reflective listening with complex, accurate reflections capturing ambivalence
- Consistent autonomy support throughout with choice-oriented language
- Skillful handling of sustain talk without confrontation
- Logical, purposeful session progression from validation to concrete planning
- Affirmation of Daniel's discipline and goal-setting as change resources

### Critical Areas for Development:
- **Evocation:** Shift from providing information to evoking Daniel's own discovery through strategic questioning
- **Change Talk Amplification:** Follow up on change talk with deepening questions rather than brief validation
- **Partnership During Information-Sharing:** Ask what Daniel knows first, then co-construct understanding collaboratively
- **Values Exploration:** Deepen understanding of what marathon training represents and how current drinking aligns with Daniel's identity and values
- **Confidence Exploration:** Ask follow-up questions about both numerator and denominator of scaling responses

### Impact on Client Engagement:
Session successfully engages Daniel and moves him from implicit ambivalence to concrete planning with 7/10 confidence—representing meaningful progress. However, Daniel's motivation is somewhat externally driven (by clinician information) rather than internally generated through his own discovery. This creates moderate risk that motivation will wane after session when clinician influence is removed.

### Recommendations for Clinician Development:
1. Practice asking "What do you already know about...?" before offering clinical information
2. Implement strategic follow-up questions for all change talk statements
3. Use double-sided reflections more actively to soften sustain talk
4. Deepen values-clarification exploration in future sessions
5. Record and review sessions, counting change talk statements and follow-up responses
