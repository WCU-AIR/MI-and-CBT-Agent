**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (phone use reduction)
- Explicitly states desired outcome with clarity

**Note:** These minimal strengths do not reflect MI competence. Maintaining focus through coercion and clarity of directive are not MI skills.

---

**Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental rejection of Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist demonstrates:

- **Complete absence of MI spirit:** Confrontation, coercion, and contempt replace partnership, acceptance, and compassion
- **Active harm to therapeutic alliance:** Repeated dismissal, judgment, and pressure create hostile environment and increase client defensiveness
- **Counterproductive clinical approach:** Pressure and coercion are evidence-contraindicated; they increase resistance and decrease likelihood of sustained change
- **Misunderstanding of ambivalence:** Client is in contemplation stage (normal, healthy); psychiatrist attempts to force action-stage behavior through pressure
- **Ignoring client feedback:** Client explicitly states approach is making her "shut down" and "want to hold on even tighter"; psychiatrist escalates pressure in response
- **No collaborative problem-solving:** When client proposes alternatives, psychiatrist refuses engagement

---

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

**Negative impact on motivation and likelihood of change.** Client entered session with stated motivation ("I do want to improve," "I'm open to working on this") and leaves with strengthened resistance ("making me want to hold on even tighter"). This is direct result of confrontational, coercive approach.

---

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Comprehensive MI Training Required:** Complete accredited MI training (minimum 2-day workshop with MINT-certified trainer) plus ongoing supervision
2. **Develop Genuine Acceptance:** Examine personal beliefs about client responsibility; develop capacity for non-judgmental stance
3. **Learn to Recognize Stages of Change:** Study contemplation stage; practice stage-appropriate interventions
4. **Master Reflective Listening:** Develop skill in complex reflections; practice 2:1 ratio of reflections to questions
5. **Shift from Imposing to Evoking:** Learn to ask evocative questions that help clients discover own reasons for change
6. **Develop Sustain Talk Management:** Learn double-sided reflections and reframing techniques
7. **Build Collaborative Skills:** Practice treating client as expert on own life
8. **Support Autonomy:** Present options rather than ultimatums; support client's right to choose
9. **Amplify Change Talk:** Recognize and strategically reinforce client's own motivation statements
10. **Engage Ongoing Supervision:** Monthly supervision with MI-trained clinician; record and review sessions

---

**Clinical Concern:**

This psychiatrist should not continue conducting MI-based interventions without comprehensive retraining. Current approach risks harming clients by damaging therapeutic alliance, increasing defensiveness, and decreasing intrinsic motivation for change. **Immediate intervention (training and supervision) is strongly recommended.**
