Ahmed's primary sustain talk includes: "I don't smoke cigarettes," "I don't think they're as bad as regular cigarettes," "They're just little, mini, little cigars," "I don't really see them as a big problem," "I haven't really thought about quitting because I don't see the point," "I don't feel like it's necessary," "I'm managing fine as it is," and "I'm not convinced this is the problem." Only in final exchange does psychiatrist demonstrate softer approach ("I appreciate you sharing your feelings. It's important that we work together on this. ## Summary of Clinical Findings

### Strengths
- Maintains clear, consistent focus on target behavior (smoking cessation)
- Demonstrates genuine concern for Ahmed's health and wellbeing
- Shows persistence in addressing health concern
- Demonstrates awareness that initial approach is not working and attempts to pivot toward collaboration in final exchange
- Clinician shows some capacity to recognize when different approach is needed

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Fundamental violation of MI spirit:** Pervasive judgment, disapproval, and disrespect for Ahmed's autonomy throughout session
- **Confrontational approach:** Repeated use of direct confrontation, imperatives, and fear-based pressure actively increases client resistance
- **Failure to listen and understand:** Does not accurately reflect Ahmed's perspective, emotional experience, or underlying concerns; fails to adjust when Ahmed explicitly states feeling unheard
- **Minimal evocation:** Tells Ahmed why he should change rather than drawing out Ahmed's own reasons, values, and resources
- **Reinforcement of sustain talk:** Direct confrontation actually strengthens Ahmed's defensive position rather than softening it
- **Dismissal of legitimate concerns:** Ahmed's needs for autonomy, stress management, and readiness treated as excuses rather than valid information
- **Lack of strategic adjustment:** Repeats same confrontational approach multiple times despite clear evidence it is not working

### Impact on Client Engagement and Motivation for Change
**Current likelihood of evoking sustained change motivation: Very Low**

Ahmed is more entrenched in his position by session end and has explicitly stated resistance to clinician's approach ("telling me to just stop without really understanding where I'm coming from"). Unlikely to follow through on any commitments made under pressure. Therapeutic relationship has been damaged through adversarial dynamic rather than collaborative partnership. Ahmed will likely avoid future health discussions with this clinician.

### Recommendations for Improvement
1. Eliminate confrontation and imperatives immediately; replace with collaborative questions
2. Develop genuine reflective listening; reflect Ahmed's perspective accurately before offering information
3. Evoke rather than impose; ask questions designed to draw out Ahmed's own reasons for change
4. Support autonomy explicitly; affirm Ahmed's right to choose and control over process
5. Seek formal MI training and supervision with MINT-certified trainer
6. Develop awareness of own reactions and biases; pause when feeling frustrated rather than intensifying pressure
7. Explore Ahmed's underlying needs and concerns with genuine curiosity rather than dismissing as excuses
8. Use affirmations to build confidence and recognize strengths
9. Assess and respect Ahmed's stage of change; do not push beyond precontemplation/early contemplation
10. Engage in ongoing professional development focused on MI competencies with regular recorded session review

---

**Overall Assessment:** This session represents a critical failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles and demonstrates the exact approach that MI research warns against. Immediate professional development and supervision are essential.
