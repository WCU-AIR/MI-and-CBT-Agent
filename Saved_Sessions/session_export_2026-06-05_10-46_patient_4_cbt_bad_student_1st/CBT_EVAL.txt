## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Context:** Patient is a 65-year-old widow with adjustment disorder presenting with anxiety about medical test results, sleep disturbance with racing thoughts, reassurance-seeking behavior, anhedonia, and concentration difficulties. Primary stressor: pending cancer test results; protective factor: son's upcoming wedding.

**Critical Observations:**

- **Reassurance Reinforcement:** The therapist provided extensive reassurance ("things will get better," "you're stronger than you think") without exploring the patient's catastrophic predictions or teaching uncertainty tolerance. This directly reinforces the reassurance-seeking behavior maintaining the patient's anxiety.

- **Missed Clinical Targets:** Patient explicitly presented key CBT targets (catastrophic thinking, reassurance-seeking, sleep disturbance with cognitive content, behavioral withdrawal). Therapist did not address any of these systematically.

- **Inappropriate Self-Disclosure:** Five personal anecdotes (exam stress, moving anxiety, restlessness, hiking story, sleep difficulties) shifted focus from patient to therapist and blurred professional boundaries. Patient's polite response ("That's a nice story") indicates tolerance rather than therapeutic benefit.

- **Absence of Core CBT Elements:** No agenda, no Socratic questioning, no thought records, no behavioral experiments, no behavioral activation, no homework structure. Session indistinguishable from supportive counseling.

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit:** Low. Patient may feel temporarily supported but will likely return to reassurance-seeking with medical staff. Without cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, or exposure to uncertainty, symptoms will persist or worsen.

**Recommended Supervision Plan:**
1. Weekly individual supervision with CBT-trained supervisor
2. Recording and review of all sessions
3. Role-play practice of Socratic questioning and agenda-setting
4. Completion of CBT training program (40+ hours)
5. Reading of core CBT texts
6. Participation in CBT consultation group
