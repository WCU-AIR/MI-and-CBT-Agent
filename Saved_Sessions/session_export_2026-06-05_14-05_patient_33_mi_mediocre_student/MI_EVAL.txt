**Comments:** Collaboration is virtually absent. Psychiatrist consistently adopts expert, directive stance throughout. Uses performative questions ("What do you think about that?") after already prescribing solutions. Never asks Derrick what *he* thinks should happen, what *he* has tried, or what *he* believes would work. All ideas generated by psychiatrist; Derrick positioned as passive recipient. No genuine co-construction of solutions. Power dynamic heavily skewed toward psychiatrist as expert.

When Derrick expresses hesitation or fear about "rocking the boat," psychiatrist does not respect his autonomy but instead prescribes solutions. When Derrick expresses fear about "rocking the boat" or concern about losing friendships, psychiatrist does not explore underlying fears or use strategic responses. Instead prescribes solutions, which may inadvertently strengthen resistance. Does not use double-sided reflections or reframing. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.7/5**

### Key Strengths
- Warm, respectful opening tone creates initial rapport
- Maintains basic focus on target behavior (drinking and DUI prevention)
- Acknowledges some of Derrick's own ideas (cab fund, tracking drinks, setting limits)
- Derrick remains engaged throughout despite directive approach

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Pervasive directiveness** (HIGHEST PRIORITY): Psychiatrist adopts expert stance, prescribing solutions rather than evoking Derrick's own motivation
- **Minimal empathy**: Reflections are surface-level and factual; emotional nuance and deeper meaning missed
- **Lack of collaboration and partnership**: Psychiatrist generates all ideas; Derrick positioned as passive recipient
- **Failure to support autonomy**: Prescriptive language ("should," "might want to") communicates obligation rather than choice
- **Minimal evocation**: Change talk generated by Derrick but not strategically amplified or reinforced
- **Ineffective handling of sustain talk**: Underlying fears not explored; prescriptive advice may strengthen resistance
- **Scattered direction**: Multiple action items suggested without collaborative prioritization or clear shared goals

### Impact on Client Engagement
While Derrick remains engaged in this session, the directive approach is likely to undermine long-term motivation for change. Derrick leaves with a list of things he "should" do but without deeper understanding of his own motivation or genuine commitment to change. The psychiatrist's failure to explore genuine concerns—fear of losing friendships, experience of isolation during sobriety, difficulty with peer pressure—means critical barriers to change remain unaddressed.

### Primary Recommendation
**Shift from expert to collaborative stance.** This requires fundamental mindset change: reframe role from prescriber of solutions to collaborative partner who helps Derrick discover his own solutions. Replace "Maybe you should..." with "What do you think...?" Ask rather than tell. Evoke rather than impose. Support autonomy rather than prescribe.
