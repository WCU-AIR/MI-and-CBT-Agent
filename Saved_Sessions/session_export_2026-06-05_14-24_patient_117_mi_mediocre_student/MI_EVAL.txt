## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.4/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Minimal MI competence demonstrated. While basic supportive counseling skills present (warm opening, some reflective listening), session lacks strategic use of MI techniques necessary for effective practice. Approach is fundamentally hierarchical and prescriptive rather than collaborative and evocative.

**Critical Clinical Errors:**

1. **Fundamental misalignment with presenting concern:** Psychiatrist focuses on exercise throughout session, but patient's referral reason is medication nonadherence. Patient explicitly attempts to redirect toward medication resistance twice and is ignored both times. This is severe clinical error suggesting psychiatrist either did not review referral information or is not listening to patient's actual agenda.

2. **Expert-driven prescriptive stance:** Repeated use of "you should," "you might want," and "you might want to push yourself" violates MI spirit and undermines autonomy support, collaboration, and partnership.

3. **Absence of evocation:** Psychiatrist provides reasons for change rather than asking about patient's own motivations, values, and reasons. True MI requires drawing out intrinsic motivation, not imposing external reasons.

4. **Ignoring and minimizing sustain talk:** Patient's resistance dismissed or pushed past rather than explored and softened.

5. **Lack of collaboration and partnership:** Unilateral decisions about focus, goals, and interventions. No joint problem-solving or recognition of patient's wisdom about his own life.

**Impact on Patient Engagement:**

Session likely to **decrease** intrinsic motivation and **increase** resistance. Patient's final responses ("Not promising anything," "We'll see how it goes") suggest compliance rather than commitment. Research shows compliance-based change is unstable and unlikely to persist. Without genuine intrinsic motivation, patient unlikely to follow through on walking goal or engage in future sessions.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**

- **CRITICAL:** Clarify actual presenting concern and follow patient's lead regarding medication resistance
- Shift from prescriptive to evocative language
- Develop genuine curiosity about patient's perspective and barriers
- Implement strategic evocation of change talk
- Amplify and reinforce weak change talk
- Skillfully handle sustain talk without confrontation
- Establish genuine collaboration and partnership
- Explicitly support autonomy and emphasize patient choice
- Assess readiness and adjust approach accordingly
- Seek formal MI training or clinical supervision before continuing MI-focused sessions

**Likelihood of Evoking Patient Motivation for Change:** Very low. Current approach risks harm through reduced engagement, increased resistance, and missed opportunities to address patient's actual concerns.
