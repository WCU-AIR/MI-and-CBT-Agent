### Empathy

Session progresses logically from establishing motivation through exploring barriers to collaboratively selecting an approach. Minor gaps: Could more proactively guide exploration of trigger management strategies before moving to medication discussion. Follow-up plan lacks concrete scheduling and timeline specificity.

### Collaboration

Seeks permission and frames decisions as collaborative. Validates David's decision-making around the patch approach. Minor opportunity: During psychoeducation about medications, could more fully honor client expertise by first asking what David already knows or wants to understand, rather than delivering information unilaterally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support demonstrated consistently throughout the session. Establishes client choice from the outset ("What would you like to focus on...?") and repeatedly emphasizes David's role in determining what fits best. Never uses directive language ("you should," "you must"). Skillfully affirms David's decision-making capacity when he chooses the patch over prescription medication, avoiding pressure or persuasion. Frames David as the agent of change, emphasizing his ownership of the process. This is a clear strength of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation of David's own motivations and resources, but inconsistently. Positive moments include asking about past attempts and what makes him want to quit now, which elicits powerful change talk ("I want to be around longer for them...I want to prove to myself that I can do it"). Significant missed opportunities: Insufficient exploration of what worked in past attempts; limited exploration of values beyond health and parenting; premature shift to solutions before exploring David's own trigger management ideas; insufficient exploration of confidence rating (5/10) to evoke ability talk; limited questions about readiness and what would make this attempt different. Change talk is elicited but not sufficiently reinforced or amplified.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent cultivation of change talk. Successfully elicits powerful change talk through strategic questions ("What makes you want to try quitting now despite these challenges?"), which produces meaningful responses about health, parenting, and self-efficacy. However, significant missed opportunities: Insufficient reinforcement of powerful statements—when David says "I want to be around longer for them," psychiatrist acknowledges but doesn't deeply amplify; limited exploration of ability talk and past successes; insufficient exploration of reasons for change beyond health and parenting; limited commitment talk; insufficient amplification of readiness. Change talk is elicited but not strategically reinforced or deepened to maximize internalization of motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent softening of sustain talk. Skillfully handles medication concerns by validating without arguing ("That makes a lot of sense...You're clearly thinking carefully about what fits best"). Gently pivots when David expresses overwhelm. Missed opportunities: Insufficient exploration of social pressure sustain talk—could ask what it would mean to be the one who doesn't smoke; limited reframing of stress as motivation; insufficient exploration of past attempts as learning experiences; limited use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge both concern and motivation; insufficient exploration of confidence rating to shift focus from doubt to hope. More consistent use of strategic reframing would strengthen this area.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.7/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Exceptional autonomy support with consistent reinforcement of client choice and ownership
- Strong collaborative stance with multiple invitations for client input
- Good acceptance and warmth with genuine affirmation and nonjudgmental tone
- Solid reflective listening with several complex reflections capturing barriers and vision
- Logical session structure with appropriate pacing
- Validation of client decision-making and respect for David's preferences

### Primary Areas for Development:
- Insufficient evocation and amplification of change talk—elicit but don't deepen
- Limited exploration of client resources, past successes, and problem-solving capacity
- Inconsistent strategic softening of sustain talk
- Premature shift to solutions before exploring client's own ideas
- Incomplete confidence exploration (5/10 rating accepted without deeper inquiry)
- Unilateral psychoeducation delivery without first exploring client knowledge
- Vague follow-up planning lacking concrete scheduling and accountability

### Clinical Impact:
This session demonstrates solid foundational MI practice that supports client engagement and establishes a concrete plan. David leaves with medication strategy and affirmation of his readiness. However, the session's impact on sustained motivation is limited by insufficient evocation and amplification of change talk. While David articulates powerful reasons for change, these are not deeply explored or internalized. Without more strategic exploration of trigger management and problem-solving, David may struggle when obstacles arise, potentially leading to relapse. The session reads as "supportive counseling with MI elements" rather than "strategic motivational interviewing."

### Recommendations for Advancement:
1. Consistently amplify change talk through follow-up questions that deepen exploration
2. Strengthen confidence building by exploring what's behind existing confidence ratings
3. Explore client's own problem-solving before offering clinical suggestions
4. Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk without confrontation
5. Establish concrete follow-up plans with specific dates, times, and accountability structures
6. Explore values more deeply to strengthen intrinsic motivation
7. Reframe past attempts as learning experiences and sources of resilience
8. Provide psychoeducation collaboratively by inviting client input first
