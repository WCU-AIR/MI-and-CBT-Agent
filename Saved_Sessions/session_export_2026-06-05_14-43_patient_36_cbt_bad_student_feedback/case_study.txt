--- DISORDER ---
Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder (DSED)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Adriana, a 4-year-old girl, was referred due to “dangerous behaviors,” including poor boundaries, impulsivity, and over-familiarity with strangers. She had been adopted from an Eastern European orphanage at 29 months. At adoption, her growth parameters were below the 5th percentile, but no other medical problems were noted. Initially, she approached her adoptive parents without shyness and gave spontaneous hugs.

Following adoption, Adriana formed a selective attachment to her mother but continued to approach and hug strangers indiscriminately, including in public places. She once tried to leave with another family. At school, she had difficulty taking turns, sitting through circle time, and respecting peers’ space; she occasionally hit others. She became distressed with small triggers and had trouble self-soothing, although she calmed with adult support.

Adriana’s development was delayed in language and school readiness skills but average in gross and fine motor abilities. Sleep and appetite were normal. She appeared younger than her age and spoke little during evaluation, yet paid attention to the interviewer and attempted to sit on their lap.

Discussion: Adriana meets DSM-5 criteria for DSED. She exhibits disinhibited social behavior that is developmentally inappropriate and potentially dangerous: reduced reticence with unfamiliar adults, overly familiar behavior, diminished checking back with caregivers, and willingness to leave with strangers. Her early adoption beyond the typical window for selective attachment, coupled with likely early neglect, contributed to this disorder. Other factors such as early malnutrition, prenatal risk, and delayed expressive language may exacerbate the presentation. Comorbidities like ADHD should be considered, though impulsivity here is relational rather than general.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---

Approaches and interacts with unfamiliar adults without hesitation

Overly familiar, including hugging or attempting to leave with strangers

Poor regulation of proximity to peers and adults

Becomes easily distressed; calms with caregiver support

Limited expressive language; attention to interactions preserved

--- DSM-5 SYMPTOMS ---

Reduced or absent reticence with unfamiliar adults

Overly familiar verbal or physical behavior

Diminished checking back with adult caregiver

Willingness to go off with unfamiliar adults with minimal hesitation

Developmentally inappropriate social engagement
