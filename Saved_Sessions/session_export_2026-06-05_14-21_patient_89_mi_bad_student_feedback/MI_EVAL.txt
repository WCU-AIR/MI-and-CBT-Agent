## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.2/5**

### Strengths:
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol use)
- Demonstrates underlying concern for client welfare
- Continues engagement despite poor approach

### Critical Deficiencies:

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, expert-driven, controlling approach that is explicitly contraindicated in MI and actively counterproductive with adolescent clients.

**Core Problems:**
1. Systematic use of confrontation and control language that increases defensiveness
2. Complete absence of empathic listening and reflection
3. Imposition of solutions rather than evocation of client motivation
4. Dismissal of autonomy and choice through pressure tactics
5. Amplification of sustain talk through direct confrontation
6. Failure to recognize or amplify change talk
7. Rupture of therapeutic alliance with no repair attempt
8. Fundamental misalignment with evidence-based practice for adolescents

### Impact on Client Engagement:

**Highly likely to decrease Marcus's motivation for change.** Marcus explicitly states the approach makes him "want to push back more"—a clear indicator of counterproductivity. The confrontational, controlling stance triggers adolescent defensiveness and communicates disrespect for autonomy. By session end, Marcus has not committed to change, has not explored ambivalence, and experiences clinician as dismissive. Likelihood of follow-through or continued engagement is low.

### Immediate Recommendations:

1. **Eliminate controlling language** and adopt collaborative, choice-affirming framing
2. **Develop genuine reflective listening skills** to demonstrate accurate understanding
3. **Repair therapeutic alliance** by acknowledging current approach is not working
4. **Evoke change talk** through strategic open questions rather than imposing reasons
5. **Recognize and amplify** any client statements suggesting openness to change
6. **Use double-sided reflections** to soften sustain talk without confrontation
7. **Explore and respect** Marcus's autonomy and social context
8. **Affirm strengths and values** explicitly throughout session
9. **Adopt genuine MI spirit** of partnership rather than prescription
10. **Receive and respond** to client feedback about therapeutic process

### Competency Assessment:

Psychiatrist is operating at **pre-MI level**, using traditional medical model of directive advice-giving. **Intensive MI training with supervised practice is urgently needed.** Current approach is likely to harm therapeutic relationship and decrease likelihood of positive outcomes.
