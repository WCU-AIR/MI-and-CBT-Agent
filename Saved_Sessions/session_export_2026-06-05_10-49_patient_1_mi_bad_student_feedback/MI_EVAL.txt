Confrontational middle section actively suppresses change talk by triggering defensive sustain talk ("I'm not some junkie," "I'm managing it"). ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is poorly managed throughout session. Psychiatrist's confrontational approach ("You can't just brush it off") actively strengthens Adrian's defensive sustain talk rather than softening it. Demand for immediate cessation triggers resistance and hardening of position. Later exchanges lack strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections, reframing, or amplified reflections to address sustain talk. When Adrian expresses "I don't want to stop completely," clinician does not explore underlying sustain talk or offer collaborative alternatives. Session demonstrates insufficient use of MI techniques to soften resistance.

## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Collaborative opening establishes respectful, curious tone
- Accurate reflective listening captures Adrian's perspective
- Exploration of existing harm-reduction strategies
- Recovery after mid-session lapse
- Consistent focus on meth use and health safety

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Severe mid-session confrontation:** Shift into authoritarian stance ("You can't just brush it off," "This isn't a negotiation," "it could be fatal") represents fundamental breach of MI spirit and actively damages therapeutic alliance
- **Lack of autonomy support:** Explicit denial of Adrian's right to negotiate triggers defensive resistance
- **Insufficient evocation:** Multiple missed opportunities to draw out Adrian's own motivations and values
- **Weak cultivation of change talk:** Adrian's meaningful change talk rarely reinforced or deepened
- **Strengthening of sustain talk:** Confrontational approach triggers stronger defensive resistance rather than softening it
- **Missed exploration of ambivalence:** Clinician does not deeply explore tension between appeal of use and health risks
- **Lack of values exploration:** No inquiry into Adrian's values or what matters to him
- **Insufficient harm-reduction focus:** Adrian's openness to harm reduction not fully explored or supported

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session is unlikely to increase Adrian's motivation for change. Adrian explicitly states "I'm not ready to quit cold turkey," representing hardening of position. Clinician's confrontational approach pushes Adrian away from even modest change goals (harm reduction) he was initially open to exploring. Adrian is likely to leave feeling judged, misunderstood, and defensive—opposite of MI aims.

**Recommendation:**
Psychiatrist must eliminate confrontation, restore genuine curiosity, strategically evoke and amplify change talk, explore Adrian's values and ambivalence without judgment, support harm reduction as legitimate change goal, consistently affirm autonomy, and use MI techniques to soften rather than confront sustain talk.
