# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal acceptance and unconditional positive regard demonstrated. Psychiatrist adopts subtle judgmental stance, framing behavior as problematic requiring correction. Lacks affirmation of Jamal's genuine conflict, self-awareness, or strengths (willingness to engage, loyalty to team). No validation of the real struggle of peer pressure facing a 16-year-old athlete. Treats team loyalty as obstacle rather than meaningful value to understand and integrate.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Superficial empathy present but lacks depth and emotional attunement. Psychiatrist acknowledges facts ("I get that it's hard to be the only one not drinking") but misses opportunities for complex, meaning-centered reflections. Fails to capture emotional core of Jamal's ambivalence between self-care and belonging. Reflections are largely factual restatements rather than explorations of meaning, emotion, or underlying tension. No attempt to reflect what belonging and identity mean to Jamal within the team context.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on target behavior (alcohol use and team dynamics) without significant drift. Logical session progression from exploration to assessment to planning. However, direction is entirely clinician-imposed rather than collaboratively developed. Psychiatrist unilaterally determines session focus without inviting Jamal's input. Increasingly prescriptive as session progresses, culminating in explicit directives about specific goals and behaviors for next weekend.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound lack of collaboration throughout session. Psychiatrist operates from expert, authority-based stance with minimal genuine partnership. Unilateral problem definition without consulting Jamal. Leading questions presume answers rather than invite genuine exploration. Expert-driven assessment of confidence and importance scales without exploring meaning. Repeated prescriptive advice-giving ("you should," "you might want to") positions psychiatrist as authority. No genuine problem-solving partnership when Jamal expresses concerns. Dismisses Jamal's hesitations rather than exploring them collaboratively.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy support nearly absent. Prescriptive language throughout ("you should try," "you might want to," "it would probably help if") communicates psychiatrist's predetermined path rather than options for Jamal's consideration. No explicit affirmation of choice until final intervention, positioned after plan already prescribed. Implicit pressure created through accountability framing ("I gotta give it a shot for real"). Ambivalence treated as problem to overcome rather than legitimate position. Jamal's realistic concerns dismissed rather than validated. No genuine exploration of what Jamal wants or what feels right to him.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation largely absent; psychiatrist relies on telling rather than asking. Minimal exploration of Jamal's own awareness that drinking "is bad for me." Leading questions presume problems exist rather than inviting discovery. No exploration of values, what matters to Jamal, or what kind of athlete/person he wants to be. Prescriptive suggestions replace evocative questions. No exploration of Jamal's strengths or past resources for handling peer pressure. Ambivalence engaged superficially without deep exploration of what each side represents or how Jamal might move through it.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership virtually absent. Psychiatrist operates as expert with answers; Jamal positioned as recipient of advice. Expert-driven framing from opening ("I thought we could talk about..."). Unilateral interpretation of information through lens of "problems" and "barriers" without asking Jamal's perspective. Prescriptive solutions without collaborative exploration. No genuine curiosity—questions are leading, designed to confirm psychiatrist's hypothesis. Dismissal of Jamal's realistic concerns about team dynamics. No acknowledgment that Jamal has valuable expertise about his own life. Power imbalance maintained throughout; Jamal's role is to listen and agree.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk minimally cultivated despite Jamal producing some spontaneously ("I know it's bad for me," "I could try easing up," "I'm down to try that"). Psychiatrist does not deepen, amplify, or reinforce change talk through follow-up questions. No exploration of what concerns Jamal about drinking or why he thinks it's problematic. Commitment statements not reinforced or explored. Prescriptive advice overshadows evocation, positioning psychiatrist's ideas as motivation source rather than inviting Jamal's own generation of reasons for change. Scaling questions used ineffectively—numbers interpreted and plan prescribed rather than used to evoke deeper change talk about what matters.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk inadequately addressed. Psychiatrist does not skillfully soften through double-sided reflections, reframing, or deeper exploration of underlying concerns. When Jamal expresses legitimate barriers ("hard to get everyone on board," "feels weird if I don't join in"), psychiatrist encourages trying anyway, implicitly dismissing barriers. No double-sided reflections validating both desire to change and reasons not to. Insufficient reframing of fear of exclusion. No exploration of whether drinking truly necessary for belonging or whether Jamal's value based on other factors. Genuine difficulty of Jamal's position acknowledged but not deeply validated; psychiatrist moves to problem-solving, which can feel dismissive.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.6/5**

**Key Strengths:**
- Maintains logical session structure and focus on target behavior
- Engages client and creates space for open dialogue
- Recognizes Jamal's ambivalence about change
- Establishes follow-up plan

**Critical Deficiencies:**
This session represents fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles. Psychiatrist operates from directive, expert-driven stance inconsistent with MI spirit. Prescriptive language dominates; genuine collaboration absent; autonomy undermined through implicit pressure; change talk minimally cultivated; sustain talk inadequately addressed.

**Likelihood of Evoking Intrinsic Motivation:**
**Low.** While Jamal may comply with prescribed plan short-term, session does not build genuine intrinsic motivation for change. Ambivalence remains largely unexamined; peer pressure concerns inadequately addressed; autonomy subtly undermined. For sustainable change, Jamal needs to move from contemplation to preparation based on own values and insights, not external compliance. This session moves toward compliance, not genuine motivation.

**Immediate Action Items for Clinician:**
1. Replace prescriptive language with genuine evocative questions
2. Develop true collaborative partnership; invite client expertise
3. Deepen exploration of ambivalence through double-sided reflections
4. Skillfully address sustain talk through curiosity rather than dismissal
5. Actively cultivate and reinforce client's own change talk
6. Strengthen explicit autonomy support; reduce implicit pressure
7. Explore and affirm client values and strengths
8. Increase open-ended questions; decrease leading questions
9. Develop complex, meaning-centered reflections
10. Co-create goals collaboratively rather than prescribe

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Recommend focused MI training with emphasis on evocative stance, collaborative partnership, and autonomy support. Consider recorded practice sessions with feedback to shift from directive to client-centered approach.
