
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

### 1. Agenda
### **Score: 0/6**

**Explanation:**

No agenda was established at any point during this session. The therapist did not collaboratively identify, prioritize, or structure specific treatment targets with the patient. In a first or early session with a patient presenting with Gambling Disorder—a complex condition with acute psychosocial consequences (marital crisis, financial devastation, occupational impairment)—establishing a clear agenda is foundational to evidence-based treatment.

The session drifted without direction. The therapist responded reactively to whatever the patient mentioned (sleep, family relationships, urges) rather than proactively establishing: (1) What are the primary treatment goals? (2) What specific gambling behaviors will be targeted? (3) What is the timeline and priority order? (4) How will we measure progress? The patient presented with multiple acute stressors (wife threatening divorce, $30,000 debt, son's college fund depleted, occupational fatigue), yet no prioritization occurred.

A proper agenda would have included: "Tomás, I can see you're dealing with a lot right now—the gambling urges, the stress with your wife, sleep problems, and work fatigue. Today, I'd like to focus on understanding your gambling patterns and identifying what triggers your urges. We might also talk about what's been happening at home. Does that sound okay, or is there something more pressing you'd like to start with?" This never happened.

**Summary:**

The therapist completely failed to establish session structure or collaborative goals. This is a critical foundational failure that undermines all subsequent clinical work.

## Recommendations

### 1. **Conduct Comprehensive Functional Analysis (URGENT)**
- **Action:** In the next session, systematically explore the gambling behavior using the following framework:
  - **Triggers:** What situations, emotions, or thoughts precede gambling? (e.g., stress at work, conflict with wife, boredom, specific times of day)
  - **Thoughts:** What goes through his mind when he gets the urge? (e.g., "I can win this time," "I need this to relax," "I can control it")
  - **Feelings:** What emotions accompany the urge? (e.g., restlessness, anxiety, excitement, numbness)
  - **Behavior:** What does he do? (e.g., goes to poker game, places bets, plays cards)
  - **Consequences:** What happens after? (e.g., loses money, feels guilty, lies to wife, can't sleep)

- **Example dialogue:**
  "Tomás, I want to understand your gambling better so we can develop a real plan to help you stop. Let's walk through what happens when you get the urge to gamble. Think about the last time you played poker. What was going on that day? What were you thinking and feeling right before you went to play? What happened while you were playing? How did you feel after?"

- **Practice:** Use this framework with every gambling episode the patient describes. Create a written functional analysis that becomes the foundation for all subsequent interventions.

---

### 2. **Teach Urge Management Skills (URGENT)**
- **Action:** Teach specific, concrete strategies for managing gambling urges. These should include:
  - **Urge Surfing:** Explain that urges are like waves—they build, peak, and subside. The goal is to ride them out without acting. Practice in session: "When you feel the urge, notice it without judgment. Observe where you feel it in your body. Watch it intensify, peak, and then decrease. It will pass."
  - **Competing Behaviors:** Develop a list of alternative activities to engage in when urges arise. Include activities of varying duration (5 min, 15 min, 1 hour). Examples: call a friend, go for a walk, play with son, watch a specific show, do pushups, take a cold shower.
  - **Stimulus Control:** Identify and reduce access to gambling triggers. Examples: avoid poker friends, don't check sports scores, delete gambling apps, give wife control of credit cards.
  - **Delay Tactic:** Commit to waiting 15 minutes before gambling. Often the urge will pass.

- **Example dialogue:**
  "Tomás, you mentioned that urges feel overwhelming and hard to resist. Let's develop some concrete strategies. First, I want you to understand that urges are temporary—they build, peak, and then come down. Your job is to ride them out without acting. Here's what we can do: [teach urge surfing]. Second, let's make a list of things you could do instead of gambling when the urge hits. What are some activities you enjoy or that help you relax? [develop competing behaviors list]. Third, let's think about what situations trigger your urges and how we can reduce your exposure to those situations."

- **Practice:** Role-play managing an urge in session. Have the patient practice urge surfing and competing behaviors. Assign homework to practice these skills.
