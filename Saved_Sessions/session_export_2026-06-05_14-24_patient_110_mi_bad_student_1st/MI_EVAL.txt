## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.2/5**

**Assessment Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamentally Deficient

---

### Strengths
- Maintains topical focus on exercise and medical care, providing some structural consistency
- Achieves surface-level compliance with agreed-upon plan by session end

---

### Critical Deficiencies

**1. Complete Absence of MI Spirit**
The session demonstrates none of the four pillars of MI—partnership, acceptance, compassion, or evocation. Instead, it embodies authoritarianism, judgment, contempt, and coercion.

**2. Systematic Invalidation of Client Experience**
Rafael's legitimate concerns about pacing, fear of failure, and need for sustainability are repeatedly labeled as "excuses" and character flaws. This communicates that his perspective is worthless and damages therapeutic alliance.

**3. Coercive Rather Than Collaborative Approach**
Psychiatrist imposes specific plan (3x/week gym, appointment by tomorrow) without exploring Rafael's readiness, constraints, or preferences. When Rafael attempts to negotiate, he is explicitly told "you don't get to decide."

**4. Ignores Spontaneous Change Talk**
Rafael offers multiple instances of motivation and commitment that should be amplified. Instead, they are ignored or contradicted, missing critical opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

**5. Confrontational Approach to Ambivalence**
Rather than exploring Rafael's understandable hesitation about sustainable behavior change, psychiatrist argues against it, which typically strengthens resistance and reactance.

**6. Threat-Based Motivation**
Psychiatrist uses fear and threat language ("your health will get worse," "you'll regret it later") rather than evoking intrinsic motivation, which is less effective and potentially harmful.

**7. Zero Autonomy Support**
Rafael is explicitly told he has no choice in the matter, violating his fundamental right to self-determination and triggering reactance.

---

### Predicted Client Outcomes

**Likelihood of Sustained Behavior Change:** Very Low

While Rafael agrees to the plan at session end, this appears to be **compliance under pressure rather than genuine commitment**. Research on MI and behavior change indicates:

- **Coerced compliance is unsustainable:** Rafael is at high risk of non-compliance once he leaves the session
- **Invalidation reduces intrinsic motivation:** Repeated dismissal of Rafael's concerns undermines self-efficacy
- **Reactance is likely:** Rafael's repeated hesitation suggests he is experiencing reactance to controlling approach
- **Therapeutic relationship is damaged:** Rafael is unlikely to feel heard, understood, or respected; may avoid future mental health care
- **Ambivalence is unresolved:** Rafael's underlying concerns about sustainability remain unaddressed

**Expected outcome:** Rafael will likely not maintain 3x/week gym attendance or follow through on appointment. Risk of treatment dropout and avoidance of future mental health care is high.

---

### Recommendations for Immediate Improvement

**Priority 1: Fundamental Shift in Clinical Stance**
- Transition from expert/authority role to collaborative partnership
- Adopt genuine curiosity about Rafael's perspective and expertise about his own life
- Practice reflective listening before offering any guidance

**Priority 2: Eliminate Harmful Language**
- Remove judgmental phrases ("stop making excuses," "stop feeling sorry for yourself," "you don't get to decide")
- Replace with compassionate, curious alternatives
- Practice validating Rafael's concerns as understandable rather than pathologizing them

**Priority 3: Develop Core MI Skills**
- Enroll in certified MI training program (minimum 2-day workshop)
- Seek ongoing MI supervision with trained supervisor
- Record sessions and review for MI fidelity using MITI coding system

**Priority 4: Evoke Rather Than Impose**
- Ask about Rafael's own motivations, values, and what matters to him
- Amplify spontaneous change talk through reflection and follow-up questions
- Collaborate on goal-setting based on Rafael's input rather than psychiatrist's prescription

**Priority 5: Support Autonomy**
- Explicitly affirm Rafael's right to choose and ownership of change process
- Offer options and genuinely invite his input
- Use language: "What do you think?" "What feels right to you?" "You're the expert on your own life"

---

### Clinical Recommendation

**This psychiatrist should not continue seeing clients using this approach without formal MI training and supervision.** The current practice style demonstrates fundamental violations of evidence-based principles and is likely causing harm to therapeutic relationships and client outcomes. Immediate intervention and retraining are necessary.
