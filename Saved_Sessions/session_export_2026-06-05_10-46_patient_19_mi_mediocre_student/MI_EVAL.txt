## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 1.6/5**

### Summary of Findings

This session demonstrates a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven, prescriptive stance fundamentally inconsistent with MI's core values of partnership, evocation, autonomy support, and genuine collaboration.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Unilateral problem identification without exploring client's perspective
- Persistent prescriptive advice-giving throughout session
- Minimal genuine engagement with client's own perspectives, values, and motivations
- Expert-hierarchical positioning rather than collaborative partnership
- Absence of evocation; clinician tells rather than asks
- Weak change talk not strengthened or cultivated
- Superficial empathy missing emotional depth and complexity
- Minimal autonomy support despite token statement at end

**Likely Clinical Impact:**

This session is unlikely to evoke Anya's intrinsic motivation for change. While she agrees to try suggestions, her language suggests low commitment and low confidence ("Maybe I could try," "It's not a big deal to try, I guess"). The prescriptive approach may actually **undermine her autonomy and self-efficacy** by positioning the psychiatrist as expert and Anya as passive recipient of advice. Without exploration of her values, priorities, or deeper motivations, behavior change suggestions remain generic health recommendations disconnected from her life, identity, and aspirations. The session misses opportunity to build genuine rapport and partnership.

### Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Shift from expert-driven to client-centered approach** – Ask open-ended questions inviting client contribution rather than closed questions narrowing options
2. **Evoke change talk rather than prescribe solutions** – Use DARN-CAT framework to draw out client's own motivations, reasons, and ideas
3. **Explore and honor client ambivalence** – Use double-sided reflections and strategic questioning when client expresses mixed feelings
4. **Build genuine collaboration and partnership** – Actively invite client input into agenda and problem-solving; use collaborative language
5. **Develop accurate empathy through complex reflections** – Move beyond factual reflections to capture emotional nuance, ambivalence, and underlying values
6. **Affirm client strengths and adaptive coping** – Explicitly recognize what client is already doing well before exploring change
7. **Explore values and priorities before suggesting change** – Understand what matters to client before offering recommendations
8. **Use strategic questioning to evoke motivation** – Replace prescriptive statements with open-ended questions inviting client's own thinking
9. **Establish autonomy support as consistent thread** – Weave autonomy affirmation throughout conversation, not just at end
10. **Engage in formal MI training and supervision** – Seek formal MI training through MINT and ongoing supervision with MI-trained clinician

**Training Level:** Trainee-level performance requiring significant skill development in MI practice. With focused training, supervision, and deliberate practice, clinician can develop genuine MI competence and learn to evoke client motivation rather than imposing it.
