## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Fidelity Score: 1.3/5 (Poor)**

### Critical Findings:

**Fundamental Stance Issue:** The psychiatrist operates from an expert-driven, authoritarian position rather than a collaborative partnership. This is the most critical deficiency and undermines all other MI principles.

**Key Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (childcare/employment barriers)
- One genuine evocative question ("What ideas or resources do you think might help?")
- One moment of validation ("It sounds like you're really trying despite the challenges")
- Obtains client compliance by end of session

**Major Areas of Concern:**
- Judgmental language and disapproving stance throughout
- Dismissal of client's legitimate efforts and explanations as "excuses"
- Pressure and ultimatums ("This is non-negotiable," "You have to," "You must")
- Failure to amplify substantial spontaneous change talk
- Overrides client's ideas without collaborative exploration
- Frames structural barriers as personal failings
- Uses threats and fear-based motivation ("If you don't, you'll keep missing opportunities")

### Impact on Client Engagement:

While Amina agrees to take action by session end, compliance appears driven by pressure and exhaustion rather than intrinsic motivation. Client enters with high confidence ("sits about at a 10") and genuine motivation ("I want to move forward"); psychiatrist's approach risks eroding these protective factors. Language shift from "I'm confident" to "I'll do what needs to be done" suggests intrinsic motivation replaced by compliance. Sustained behavior change is unlikely given pressure-based approach.

### Recommendations for Improvement:

1. **Adopt genuine collaborative stance** – Shift from expert-driven to partnership model
2. **Eliminate judgmental language** – Remove "excuses," "have to," "must," "non-negotiable"
3. **Amplify change talk** – Pause and deepen client's own motivation when offered
4. **Explore client ideas collaboratively** – Ask curious questions before prescribing alternatives
5. **Use autonomy-supportive language** – Replace control with choice and client ownership
6. **Develop genuine empathy** – Practice accurate reflective listening capturing emotion and meaning
7. **Validate effort and normalize struggle** – Acknowledge legitimate barriers, not personal failings
8. **Avoid threats and consequences-based motivation** – Focus on client's own values and goals
9. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** – Engage with MI expert for structured learning
10. **Use MI fidelity scales** – Self-evaluate sessions to identify and target lowest-scoring domains
