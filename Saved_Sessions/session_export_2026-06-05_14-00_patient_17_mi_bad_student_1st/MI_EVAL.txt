# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist repeatedly frames client perspective as "wrong" and uses accusatory language ("you're just making excuses," "you're just fooling yourself," "you're choosing to harm yourself"). No affirmation of client strengths (commitment to basketball, self-awareness about performance). Tone escalates to confrontational ultimatums. Client responds with increasing defensiveness, indicating she feels attacked rather than supported. Zero warmth or genuine support demonstrated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand client perspective. When client explains stress triggers vaping urge and cutting off friends would increase stress, psychiatrist dismisses this entirely without reflection. Client's stated basketball goal is reframed as insufficient rather than explored. Client explicitly states "it feels like you're not really hearing me"—objective evidence of empathic failure. No complex reflections capturing emotional nuance or deepening insight. Zero empathic listening present.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear focus on vaping cessation and prevents topic drift; however, direction is maintained through coercion and ultimatum rather than collaborative exploration. Repeated insistence on "quit today" is directional but counterproductive, pushing client away. Fails to recognize and build on client's own identified direction (quitting for basketball season). Direction exists but is accomplished through authoritarian control rather than strategic guidance.

### Collaboration

When client requests partnership ("Can we work on how I can do that?"), psychiatrist responds with rejection and escalation. Assumes exclusive expert role and imposes solutions. No seeking of client input, no co-construction of solutions, no shared decision-making. Power entirely held by psychiatrist; client contributions consistently dismissed or overridden.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1

Psychiatrist frames situation as non-negotiable ("You have to quit now—no more delays"; "There's no in-between"; "This isn't negotiable"). Uses false dichotomies: "Are you going to quit today or keep risking your lungs?" Directly attacks client autonomy: "There is no 'own terms' when it comes to quitting vaping." Uses shame and guilt to pressure compliance. Client explicitly states "That pressure just makes me defensive." No autonomy support present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation present. Uses tell-and-correct approach throughout. Client provides multiple change talk statements (basketball goal, willpower rating of 7/10, desire to be healthy, planning to quit for season) that are never explored or amplified. When client mentions basketball goal, psychiatrist dismisses it: "If you really cared about basketball or your future, you'd stop vaping immediately." Never asks open-ended questions to elicit client's own reasons for change. No inquiry into values, strengths, or resources. Multiple opportunities to amplify change talk are missed or attacked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership entirely absent. Psychiatrist explicitly rejects collaborative stance: "No, no, there is no 'plan' or 'together' here." Language is consistently exclusive and authoritarian ("I'm going to tell you exactly what you need to do"; "You have to"; "This isn't negotiable"). Dismisses client self-knowledge: "You're just fooling yourself." Overrides client's assertion of expertise about her own life. Uses prescriptive directives rather than invitations to explore. Client's repeated requests for partnership ("Can we work on how I can do that?"; "Can you understand that?") are ignored. Rigid expert control maintained throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Change talk actively suppressed and invalidated rather than cultivated. Client provides multiple instances: desire ("I want to be healthy, for real"; "I want to change"), ability ("I'd say my willpower is like a seven out of ten"), reasons ("I play better when I'm not high"), and taking steps ("I'm planning to stop when the season starts"). Psychiatrist attacks rather than amplifies: dismisses basketball goal as insufficient, ignores expressions of desire for change, repeats ultimatums. Never uses reflective listening to amplify change talk. Never asks questions designed to evoke more change talk. By session end, client's change talk has diminished; she is defensive and focused on explaining why she cannot comply.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk met with direct confrontation and judgment rather than skillful softening. When client expresses sustain talk (peer pressure, stress management, social connection), psychiatrist responds with direct argumentation ("No, no, you're wrong about that") and shame ("You're just making excuses"; "You're fooling yourself"). Uses pressure and ultimatum, which predictably increases sustain talk. By final exchanges, client defends position more vigorously. Psychiatrist does maintain some focus on target behavior preventing complete topic drift; however, approach to handling sustain talk is fundamentally confrontational and counterproductive. No double-sided reflection or reframing used.

---

## Overall Assessment Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level:** **Severely Deficient (1.1/5)**

**Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental violation of Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly every domain. The psychiatrist demonstrates an authoritarian, confrontational approach that is directly contraindicated in MI and is likely to **increase client resistance and decrease motivation for change**.

**Core Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation)
- Systematic undermining of autonomy through ultimatums, false dichotomies, shame, and guilt
- Suppression of change talk rather than amplification
- Confrontational handling of sustain talk that predictably strengthens resistance
- Explicit rejection of collaboration and partnership
- Empathic failure with no genuine effort to understand client perspective

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
- Increased defensiveness and resistance
- Explicit disengagement ("it feels like you're not really hearing me")
- Strengthened sustain talk as session progresses
- Reduced perceived autonomy and sense of agency
- **Likelihood of behavior change: Very low**

**Immediate Recommendation:**

The psychiatrist should **not continue seeing clients for behavior change counseling without intensive MI training, supervision, and demonstrated competence**. The current approach is likely to harm clients and reduce the likelihood of positive outcomes. Formal MI training (MINT-certified), supervision with an MI trainer, and recorded session review with feedback are essential before resuming clinical practice in this domain.
