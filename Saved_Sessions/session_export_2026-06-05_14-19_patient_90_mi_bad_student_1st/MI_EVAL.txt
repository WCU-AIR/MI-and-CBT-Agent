### Critical Areas for Improvement

**1. Fundamental Reorientation to MI Philosophy**
- Complete re-training required; this is not MI practice
- Must understand MI evokes client motivation rather than imposing directives
- Recommend: Comprehensive MI training workshop (2-3 days) + foundational texts

**2. Replace Interrogation with Open-Ended Exploration**
- Eliminate yes/no questions that position clinician as interrogator
- Example: Instead of "Do you exercise regularly? Yes or no?" → "Tell me about your current activity level. What does a typical week look like?"
- Goal: 80%+ open-ended questions

**3. Develop Genuine Empathic Listening**
- Use reflective listening as primary intervention
- Demonstrate understanding before offering guidance
- Practice: 5+ reflections per client statement before problem-solving

**4. Recognize and Amplify Change Talk**
- Identify when patient expresses motivation, ability, reasons, commitment
- Use reflective listening to amplify and deepen
- Goal: Amplify 80%+ of change talk

**5. Respect Client Autonomy and Readiness**
- Abandon demands for immediate, maximal commitment
- Collaborate to identify realistic, sustainable steps
- Meet patient where they are, not where clinician thinks they should be

**6. Eliminate Blame and Character Attacks**
- Remove language attributing barriers to character flaws ("laziness," "weak-willed")
- Normalize struggle; validate complexity of behavior change
- Affirm patient's self-knowledge and past learning

**7. Use Collaborative Problem-Solving**
- Ask patient for solutions first before prescribing
- Build on patient's ideas
- Example: "You know your schedule better than anyone. What do you think might work?"

**8. Reframe Incremental Progress as Valuable**
- Recognize small steps build momentum and confidence
- Affirm progress rather than demanding escalation
- Example: "You did walks with the kids—that's great. How did that feel? What would make that sustainable?"

**9. Explore Values and Intrinsic Motivation**
- Move beyond health threats to what matters to patient
- Build change goals around intrinsic motivation (connection, joy, vitality, family)
- Example: "What does being more active connect to for you—your family, your energy, your life?"

**10. Seek Supervision from MI-Certified Trainer**
- This departure from MI requires professional correction
- Commit to monthly supervision for minimum 6 months
- Record all sessions; review with supervisor
- Set specific, measurable goals per session

### Urgent Recommendation

**This psychiatrist requires immediate, comprehensive re-training in Motivational Interviewing before continuing to conduct sessions focused on behavior change. The current approach is harmful to patient outcomes and violates evidence-based practice standards for MI.**

The repeated demand for commitments the patient cannot sustain will reinforce hopelessness and decrease likelihood of sustained behavior change. This approach contradicts decades of research demonstrating that intrinsic motivation, autonomy support, and collaborative goal-setting produce superior outcomes compared to coercive, directive approaches.
