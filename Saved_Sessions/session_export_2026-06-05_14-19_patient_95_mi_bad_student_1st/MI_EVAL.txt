Clinician consistently tells rather than asks. Client's implicit change talk ("I do want to feel better") is not amplified or explored. No open questions designed to draw out client's own reasons for change or values. Directive approach suppresses intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5

Client's contributions are dismissed ("You're just overcomplicating things," "You're just making excuses"). Client's expertise about her own life and constraints is invalidated. #### **Score (1–5):** 1/5

## Overall Assessment

### **Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:**

This session represents a **profound departure from Motivational Interviewing principles and practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no competence across all nine MI domains. The session is characterized by:

- **Authoritarian, expert-driven stance** fundamentally incompatible with MI spirit
- **Active dismissal** of client's perspective, concerns, and autonomy
- **Confrontational responses** to normal ambivalence and resistance
- **Complete absence** of genuine collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support
- **Failure to listen:** Repeated identical opening about smoking despite three clear client corrections

**Critical Clinical Failures:**

1. **Fundamental misunderstanding of MI:** Psychiatrist views MI as a technique for coercing compliance rather than evoking intrinsic motivation
2. **Active undermining of therapeutic alliance:** Judgment, dismissal, and accusation damage the relationship
3. **Failure to attend:** Repeated smoking inquiry despite clear corrections suggests clinician is not genuinely listening
4. **Confrontation increases resistance:** Research demonstrates this approach is counterproductive
5. **Dismissal of legitimate concerns:** Client's concerns about side effects and barriers deserve exploration, not invalidation
6. **Coercion rather than autonomy support:** Implicit threats and demands for immediate commitment
7. **No evocation:** Tells rather than asks; misses opportunities to draw out client's own motivation
8. **Failure to address ambivalence:** Dismisses normal ambivalence as obstinacy

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to **decrease** Maria's motivation for change and damage therapeutic relationship. By session end, client is more entrenched in resistance and explicitly states psychiatrist's approach makes her "want to dig in my heels more." This is the opposite of intended outcome.

7. ☐ Amplify and reinforce change talk when it emerges
8. ☐ Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk

**Recommendation:** **Comprehensive retraining required.** This is not minor skill refinement; it is fundamental reorientation to how behavior change is facilitated. Without these changes, this clinician's practice will continue to harm clients by reducing motivation and damaging trust in healthcare system.
