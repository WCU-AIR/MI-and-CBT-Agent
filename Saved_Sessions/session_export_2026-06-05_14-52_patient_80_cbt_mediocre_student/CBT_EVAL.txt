## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL FEEDBACK

**Strengths Observed:**
- Appropriate tone, warmth, and non-judgmental demeanor
- Age-appropriate language and basic validation
- Patient remained engaged and willing to try suggestions
- No significant interpersonal alienation

**Critical Areas Requiring Immediate Attention:**

1. **Diagnostic Knowledge & Case Conceptualization** — Conduct comprehensive assessment of both Separation Anxiety Disorder and Provisional Tic Disorder before proceeding. Develop explicit, written treatment plan with clear prioritization and evidence-based interventions.

2. **Evidence-Based Intervention Selection** — Implement exposure therapy (gold standard for childhood separation anxiety) and habit reversal training or CBIT (evidence-based for tics). Current generic anxiety management insufficient.

3. **Systematic Assessment** — Thoroughly assess catastrophic thoughts (frequency, triggers, function), avoidance behaviors, ritualistic behaviors, somatic complaints, and tic characteristics before intervening.

4. **Cognitive Restructuring Skill** — Teach systematic cognitive restructuring with evidence examination rather than offering alternative thoughts. Address patient doubt and build credibility of interventions.

5. **Collaboration & Guided Discovery** — Shift from expert-directive mode to collaborative partnership. Use Socratic questioning to enable patient self-discovery rather than direct advice-giving.

6. **Homework Structure** — Assign specific, measurable, prioritized homework with clear rationale. Systematically review in subsequent sessions and adjust based on barriers and successes.

7. **Parent Involvement** — Schedule parent session to explain diagnoses, teach support strategies, and coordinate homework. Parents are essential in treating childhood anxiety and tics.

**Recommended Next Steps:**
- Supervisor-led case consultation to develop evidence-based treatment plan
- Review of exposure therapy protocols for childhood anxiety (e.g., Coping Cat)
- Review of habit reversal training/CBIT manuals for tics
- Observation of supervisor-conducted session as model
- Structured feedback and skill-building on Socratic questioning and guided discovery
