# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard with consistent validation of Anara's fears and uncertainty. Effectively normalizes avoidance as a protective mechanism and reframes ambivalence as understandable rather than pathological. Minor opportunities exist to more explicitly affirm her worth and autonomy, particularly around pacing and choice. Could strengthen with phrases affirming her capacity to manage multiple demands.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistently accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking throughout. Complex reflections capture both content and emotion effectively. Accurately mirrors Anara's fear, confusion, and competing demands. Some reflections are somewhat surface-level; deeper exploration of emotional nuance around her coping mechanisms and fear of "making it real" would strengthen empathic depth. Generally warm and validating tone maintained.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on health concerns but gradually shifts toward specific action goal (making doctor's appointment) without explicitly clarifying whether this aligns with Anara's own priorities. Opening appropriately allows client to set direction; however, presupposes that "taking the next step" toward medical consultation is agreed-upon direction. Lacks explicit exploration of whether Anara's primary concern is the health issue, fear of diagnosis, or life overwhelm. Direction becomes increasingly prescriptive without full collaborative agreement.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial collaboration is present through inviting client input and positioning psychiatrist as partner. However, collaboration becomes increasingly uneven as session progresses. Readiness ruler is clinician-directed assessment rather than collaborative exploration. Resilience question, while skillfully executed, is clinician-directed intervention. Final action planning (role-play) represents substantial shift away from partnership without explicit checking of whether Anara agrees this is best approach. Power-sharing diminishes significantly by session end.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy support present in opening and mid-session with phrases like "we can go at a pace that feels right for you." However, autonomy support weakens significantly as session progresses. Readiness ruler subtly pressures toward change without exploring whether Anara wants to address changes at all. Doesn't adequately explore ambivalence or explicitly affirm her right to choose pace and direction. Doesn't acknowledge her right to not seek medical care. Final action planning feels somewhat prescriptive.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of resilience and coping strategies with skillful use of open-ended questions drawing out client wisdom. Excellent application of past coping to current situation. Anara generates her own solutions (having support, breaking into smaller steps) rather than receiving clinician suggestions. Some opportunities exist to more deeply evoke intrinsic motivation for change—questions about what would be important about understanding her body or what she hopes might happen would strengthen evocation. Could better explore her own ideas about managing fear and overwhelm.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial partnership evident through collaborative language and invitation of client input. However, partnership erodes significantly as session progresses. Psychiatrist increasingly assumes expert role in identifying what needs to happen and how to make it happen. Resilience question represents subtle shift toward expert-driven intervention. Final portion moving toward role-play represents substantial shift away from partnership. True partnership would involve explicit checking of whether Anara wants to make appointment or feels pressured. Doesn't acknowledge that Anara's hesitation might be wise.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid skill in evoking and reinforcing change talk. Effectively reinforces Anara's demonstrated resilience and ability to manage difficulty. Skillfully asks her to apply past coping to current situation, evoking her own change talk about support and breaking tasks into smaller steps. Affirms her "sensible and manageable" first step. Missed opportunities to more strategically cultivate specific change talk domains (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Could ask "What would need to happen to move from 3 to 4?" to evoke change talk about movement.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate empathic validation of sustain talk but misses opportunities to strategically redirect or reframe. Validates avoidance and fear without helping Anara recognize costs of avoidance or tension between stated values and behavior. Uses some effective softening (breaking problem into smaller steps), but doesn't explore function of avoidance or use strategic amplification. Doesn't ask about what worry is doing for her or help her see full picture of avoidance costs. Limited use of double-sided reflections to gently challenge sustain talk.

---

### **Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**

---

## Summary of Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Warm, validating tone with consistent empathic attunement
- Excellent resilience exploration and strength-based questioning
- Good practical problem-solving (breaking overwhelming task into manageable steps)
- Collaborative opening that invites client input
- Solid change talk cultivation and reinforcement
- Appropriate use of pacing language supporting autonomy

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Insufficient exploration of ambivalence before action planning
- Gradual shift toward directiveness without explicit goal clarification
- Limited autonomy support and affirmation of client's right to choose
- Weak strategic management of sustain talk
- Uneven power-sharing that erodes as session progresses
- Insufficient evocation of intrinsic motivation for change
- Presupposes medical consultation is goal without explicit agreement

**Clinical Recommendation:**
This session demonstrates adequate MI competence but falls short of excellent practice. The psychiatrist creates supportive alliance and helps identify concrete steps, but doesn't fully evoke intrinsic motivation or maintain collaborative power-sharing. Recommend deeper exploration of ambivalence, explicit goal clarification before action planning, and more strategic use of MI techniques to help client resolve competing motivations and connect health concerns to personal values.
