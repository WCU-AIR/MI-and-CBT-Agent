# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Explicitly normalizes client defensiveness and validates perspective without criticism. Reassures about judgment ("I'm here to support you, not to judge"). However, affirmations could be more specific and earlier in the session. Surface-level affirmations present; deeper affirmation of autonomy and character strengths would strengthen acceptance further.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking demonstrated. Accurately captures emotional nuance and complexity of client's situation. Reflections mirror client's ambivalence and validate experience. However, empathy occasionally lacks emotional depth—does not fully explore the emotional weight of conflict with mother or the tension of being caught between relationships. Responses sometimes mechanical rather than emotionally sophisticated.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on presenting concern but lacks strategic clarity. Session begins appropriately with open question but drifts from core issue (cannabis use) toward general relationship management and conflict avoidance. Discrepancy exploration is present but not pursued with sufficient depth. Final exchange shifts away from substance use toward coping strategies. Strategic direction toward examining target behavior and its consequences is insufficient.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaborative stance with genuine invitations for client input and permission-seeking before offering suggestions. Uses collaborative language and co-constructs agenda. However, collaboration could be deepened through more genuine back-and-forth exploration of client's own ideas and solutions. Some interpretations presented as clinician observation rather than co-constructed insight. Client expertise about own life could be more explicitly acknowledged.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respects client's perspective but does not actively and explicitly support autonomy and right to self-determination. No affirmation of client's freedom to choose or exploration of what independence means to him. Session subtly moves toward compliance with mother's concerns rather than supporting client's agency. Missing explicit statements affirming client's right to make own decisions. Framing of "managing balance" positions clinician as expert rather than supporting client agency.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant deficiency. Asks some open questions but does not strategically draw out client's own motivations, values, ideas, and resources. Change talk is minimally amplified—when client acknowledges mother gets mad, clinician does not develop this statement. Client's values not systematically explored. Session shifts toward clinician-offered suggestions rather than continuing to evoke client thinking. Questions like "What do you think?" and "What matters most to you?" are underutilized. Strategic evocation of intrinsic motivation is largely absent.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some partnership elements (permission-seeking, open questions) but frequently assumes expert role by making interpretations and offering solutions rather than co-constructing them. Statements like "You've developed some strong skills" presented as clinician observation without exploring whether client agrees. Final exchange represents shift toward expert-driven intervention with specific ideas offered rather than co-constructed. True partnership requires more explicit acknowledgment of client's expertise about own life.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Critical deficiency. Very little change talk evoked or reinforced. Client's limited change talk (concern about upsetting mother, desire to maintain relationships) remains underdeveloped. When client says "I guess sometimes it does make her mad," clinician reflects but does not amplify. No strategic exploration of client's own concerns regarding substance use. When client rates importance at 4/10, clinician accepts without exploring what would increase it. Session ends with clinician offering suggestions rather than continuing to cultivate client's own change talk. Intrinsic motivation remains largely unaddressed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflection ("On one hand...on the other..."), but does not consistently or strategically soften it. When client minimizes ("I don't really think it's that big of a deal"), clinician reflects without exploring discrepancy between peer perspective and parental concern. Limited use of reframing to soften resistance. Double-sided reflection technique underutilized throughout session. Sustain talk is tolerated rather than skillfully redirected toward change perspective.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.2/5**

**Strengths:**
- Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with explicit reassurance about judgment
- Solid reflective listening with reasonable complexity and perspective-taking
- Collaborative approach with genuine invitations for client input and permission-seeking
- Appropriate pacing without rushing or pressuring client
- Validation of experience and normalization of defensiveness

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Minimal change talk cultivation:** Client's limited change talk not adequately amplified or reinforced
- **Insufficient evocation:** Over-reliance on clinician reflections and interpretations rather than drawing out client thinking
- **Weak autonomy support:** No explicit affirmation of right to self-determination; session subtly moves toward compliance with mother's concerns
- **Lack of strategic direction:** Session drifts from core issue (substance use) toward general relationship management
- **Limited discrepancy exploration:** Gap between client's values and behavior not deeply developed
- **Clinician-offered solutions:** Session ends with psychiatrist offering specific ideas rather than continuing to evoke client's own thinking

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

The psychiatrist creates a safe environment but is unlikely to significantly increase client's intrinsic motivation for change. Client's core perspective ("I don't really think it's that big of a deal") remains largely unchallenged. While client shows openness by session end, this appears to be compliance with clinician suggestions rather than genuine movement toward change. Critical opportunities to amplify change talk, explore values and discrepancy, and support autonomy were missed.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**

1. Strategically cultivate and amplify all change talk through deeper follow-up questions
2. Explicitly support autonomy and self-determination with affirming language
3. Evoke client's own ideas and solutions rather than offering suggestions
4. Explore and develop discrepancy between client's values and current behavior
5. Ask directly about consequences and effects of substance use
6. Use double-sided reflection more consistently to soften sustain talk
7. Deepen exploration of client's values and what matters most
8. Maintain strategic focus on target behavior while remaining client-centered
9. Collaborate on solutions rather than presenting clinician ideas as answers
10. Strengthen reflection depth to capture emotional complexity and nuance

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** Moderate to Low

While the session establishes rapport and safety, it does not strategically evoke genuine intrinsic motivation for change. With implementation of recommended improvements—particularly strategic change talk cultivation, deeper discrepancy exploration, and continued evocation—the psychiatrist could substantially increase likelihood of evoking authentic client motivation for behavior change.
