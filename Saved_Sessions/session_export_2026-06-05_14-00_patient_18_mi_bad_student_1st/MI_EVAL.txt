## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### **Critical Clinical Finding:**

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** across nearly all dimensions. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, authoritarian approach that is explicitly contraindicated in MI and empirically associated with *worse* outcomes for behavior change.

### **Core Deficiencies:**

1. **Systematic violation of MI spirit:** Judgment, disapproval, and contempt replace partnership, acceptance, and compassion. Explicit statements ("you don't get to decide," "you're weak," "stop feeling sorry for yourself") violate fundamental MI principles of respecting autonomy and client worth.

2. **Absence of evocation:** Client's clear motivations (love for daughter, long-standing desire to quit, health awareness) are ignored. Psychiatrist attempts to impose motivation through pressure and shame rather than drawing it out.

3. **Confrontation instead of collaboration:** Psychiatrist assumes unilateral expert role, dismisses client perspective, uses ultimatums and threats rather than collaborative problem-solving.

4. **Invalidation of emotional experience:** Fear, shame, and ambivalence—normal responses to difficult change—are labeled as "excuses," "weakness," and character flaws. This amplifies shame and defensiveness rather than reducing it.

5. **Counterproductive pressure:** Research demonstrates confrontation *increases* resistance and sustain talk. By session end, client's change talk has diminished and ambivalence increased—opposite of intended effect.

### **Impact on Client Engagement:**

This session is likely to **decrease** Anisa's motivation for change and damage the therapeutic relationship. The approach increases shame, triggers defensiveness, undermines autonomy, and damages alliance. Client explicitly states the approach makes her "want to shut down" and need "understanding" rather than judgment. Psychiatrist ignores this feedback and intensifies same approach. Likelihood of follow-up engagement is low. Without fundamental shift in approach, sustained behavior change is unlikely.

### **Recommendation:**

**Immediate intervention and retraining required.** This psychiatrist requires formal MI training (e.g., through MINT), clinical supervision with MI-trained supervisor, and review of recordings with fidelity assessment (MITI scale). Continuing current practice will harm patients and reduce treatment effectiveness. MI is learnable; with training and supervised practice, significant improvement in patient outcomes is achievable.
