## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Patient Presentation:**
- 6 days postpartum with severe mania and psychotic features
- Fixed delusional belief: "I've done something awful" / "I've killed my newborn"
- Loss of reality testing: "I don't know what's real anymore"
- Severe sleep deprivation with racing thoughts and flight of ideas
- Catastrophic thoughts about loss of control
- Safety behaviors (avoidance of leaving home)
- Significant risk to self and infant

**Therapist Response:**
- Treated as mild stress/adjustment difficulty rather than psychiatric emergency
- No safety assessment conducted
- Delusional content completely ignored
- Psychotic features not explored or addressed
- Generic self-help advice offered (hydration, positive thinking, animal videos)
- Excessive self-disclosure and inappropriate personal anecdotes
- No CBT conceptualization, strategy, or techniques applied
- No agenda, structure, or prioritization

**Outcome:**
- Patient remains in acute psychiatric crisis without appropriate intervention
- Significant risk of harm to self and infant unaddressed
- Appropriate level of care (psychiatric hospitalization) not discussed
- Medication evaluation not initiated
- Patient likely to experience worsening symptoms without medical treatment

**Recommendation:**
**URGENT PSYCHIATRIC REFERRAL REQUIRED.** This patient requires immediate inpatient psychiatric evaluation, medication management, and possible ECT. Outpatient CBT is inappropriate as primary intervention for acute postpartum psychosis.
