# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance. Validates emotional experience and affirms client's strength and resilience throughout. However, acceptance could be deepened through more explicit normalization of smoking cessation difficulty, deeper affirmation of identity beyond smoking behavior, and warmer personal connection. Minimal use of humor or explicit statements affirming worth as a mother and person independent of change.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and sophisticated understanding of ambivalence. Complex reflections capture both content and emotional nuance effectively. However, missed opportunities for deeper empathic exploration at critical emotional moments—particularly around shame ("leper" metaphor), fear of failure, and identity with smoking. Empathy is accurate but somewhat surface-level when exploring these significant emotional junctures.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, strategic focus on smoking cessation while remaining client-centered. Session progresses logically from exploration of ambivalence through concrete planning. Opening question establishes collaborative direction-setting. However, lacks explicit clarification of client's stage of change (Preparation) and does not establish clear boundaries around what will be addressed in this session versus future sessions. Direction is sound but could benefit from more explicit structuring.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaborative stance with consistent invitation for client input and genuine incorporation of client's suggestions. Opening question invites agenda-setting. However, collaboration occasionally shifts toward clinician-directed problem-solving, particularly in planning phases. When offering to create checklist, psychiatrist positions self as primary architect rather than supporting client's co-creation. Generally genuine but could strengthen through more client-led solution development.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and affirms client's autonomy and right to choose. Language emphasizes client ownership and avoids prescriptive directives. Does not pressure despite readiness score of 7/10. However, autonomy support could be strengthened through explicit naming of client's right to choose, fail, or modify the plan. Does not address what happens if client decides not to quit or wants to pause, which would further reinforce autonomy even around possibility of non-change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic evocation of resources and some exploration of motivations through open questions and effective use of past coping strengths. However, significantly underutilizes evocation of change talk, values, and intrinsic motivation. Does not deeply explore reasons for change, health concerns, or relationship with daughter. Session moves toward planning before fully exploring and amplifying client's own reasons for change. Misses opportunities to deepen motivation statements through targeted follow-up questions.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates collaborative intent but inconsistently maintains true partnership. Frequently assumes expert or guide role, particularly in problem-solving and planning phases. When client identifies stress as barrier, psychiatrist offers suggestions rather than asking what client thinks might help. Positions self as primary architect of plan rather than supporting client as primary owner. Collaborative intent is genuine but defaults to expert positioning, limiting true power-sharing and co-creation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness in session. Minimal strategic effort to evoke, amplify, and reinforce client's change talk. While client provides substantial motivation statements ("I want to quit for my health and for my daughter," "I'm willing to try," "I'm feeling more hopeful"), psychiatrist reflects but does not strategically deepen them. Misses opportunities to explore desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment. Does not use readiness score (7/10) to evoke additional change talk. Session demonstrates reflective listening but lacks intentional cultivation of motivation central to MI practice.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent skill in managing sustain talk. Handles some barriers skillfully through gentle redirection (fear of failure, lingering nerves). However, misses opportunities to more actively soften resistance. When client expresses fear of losing smoking as "a friend," psychiatrist reflects accurately but does not reframe or explore further. Does not address identity-based sustain talk ("part of me now") or explore underlying beliefs about giving in. Adequate management through validation but lacks more active softening techniques such as reframing and alternative perspectives.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**

**Competency Level:** Adequate MI foundational skills with notable gaps in core MI techniques

**Key Strengths:**
- Strong reflective listening with accurate capture of ambivalence
- Consistent collaborative intent and genuine invitation for client input
- Effective use of past coping strengths to build self-efficacy
- Appropriate pacing and logical session structure
- Solid autonomy support with respect for client choice
- Warm, validating tone that reduces shame
- Strategic use of scaling questions (importance 8/10, readiness 7/10)
- Concrete, manageable planning aligned with client preferences

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Insufficient cultivation of change talk:** Client's motivation statements present but underutilized as therapeutic tool
- **Limited evocation of intrinsic motivation:** Surface-level exploration of values, identity, and what matters most
- **Premature shift to problem-solving:** Moves to planning before fully strengthening motivation (readiness 7/10 suggests more motivation-building needed)
- **Expert positioning in planning:** Assumes primary architect role rather than supporting true co-creation
- **Missed empathic opportunities:** Critical emotional moments acknowledged but not deeply explored
- **Insufficient softening of sustain talk:** Lacks active reframing and exploration of underlying beliefs
- **No explicit stage-of-change clarification:** Does not name Preparation stage or provide stage-specific guidance

**Likelihood of Evoking Sustained Client Motivation:**

Session successfully maintains current motivation and creates hope through validation and concrete planning. However, does not significantly strengthen intrinsic motivation or address underlying fears about failure. Client leaves with plan and increased hope but without deeply explored values, strengthened change talk, or addressed identity concerns. May support short-term compliance but unlikely to sustain long-term motivation if barriers emerge or early setbacks occur.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**

1. Strategically cultivate and amplify change talk through targeted follow-up questions
2. Deepen exploration of values and identity before moving to planning
3. Extend motivation-building phase appropriate to readiness level and stated fears
4. Maintain true partnership by positioning client as primary expert in problem-solving
5. Use more active softening techniques (reframing, exploring beliefs, alternative perspectives)
6. Explicitly name stage of change and provide stage-specific guidance
7. Deepen empathic exploration at moments of high emotional significance
8. Establish clear session structure and boundaries
9. Use scaling questions more strategically to deepen motivation
10. Strengthen affirmation and normalization of struggle throughout session

---

**Supervisor Recommendation:** Continue MI training with focus on strategic cultivation of change talk and deeper evocation of intrinsic motivation. Strong foundational skills provide excellent foundation for advancement to good (4/5) and excellent (5/5) MI practice with targeted skill development.
