## CLINICAL COMPETENCY SUMMARY

**Current Competency Level:** Below minimum standard for treating acute psychosis.

**Strengths:**
- Demonstrates warmth, empathy, and non-judgmental stance
- Patient reports feeling "a little less alone"
- Uses collaborative language and invites patient input
- Normalizes nervousness and encourages self-compassion

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental clinical error:** Applying standard CBT to acute-phase schizophrenia (contraindicated)
- **No medication discussion:** Never asks about antipsychotics, psychiatric follow-up, or compliance
- **Misapplied cognitive restructuring:** Assumes patient can reality-test; patient has poor insight and active delusions
- **No coherent strategy:** Ad hoc approach without clear conceptualization or progression
- **Poor homework implementation:** Vague instructions, no written materials, no follow-up
- **No psychiatric assessment:** Hallucinations and delusions minimally explored
- **No safety planning:** Suicidality, self-harm, and risk to others not assessed
- **Repetition without adaptation:** Second session identical to first; no learning evident

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. Establish antipsychotic medication as primary intervention
2. Conduct systematic psychiatric assessment using structured tool (PANSS/BPRS)
3. Develop clear treatment plan prioritizing psychiatric stabilization
4. Refer to specialized first-episode psychosis program or CBT for psychosis (CBTp) specialist
5. Obtain specialized training in first-episode psychosis treatment if continuing with this population

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change:** Very low without medication and specialized intervention.
