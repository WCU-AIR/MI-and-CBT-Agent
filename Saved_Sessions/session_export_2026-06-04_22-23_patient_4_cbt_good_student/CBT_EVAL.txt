## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths

✓ **Strong agenda-setting:** Explicit, collaborative, and maintained throughout session  
✓ **Accurate case formulation:** Correctly identified central automatic thought and connection to feelings/behaviors  
✓ **Skillful cognitive restructuring:** Used Socratic questioning to help patient generate own balanced thought; patient experienced subjective relief  
✓ **Genuine empathy and validation:** Patient felt heard and understood; strong therapeutic alliance established  
✓ **Collaborative approach:** Patient invited to generate solutions; felt like active partner  
✓ **Appropriate pacing:** Logical flow without rushing or dragging  
✓ **Clear homework assignment:** Specific, relevant, collaboratively developed with confidence check  

### Critical Deficiencies

✗ **Limited behavioral intervention:** Reassurance-seeking identified but not targeted; no behavioral activation despite withdrawal; no exposure/anxiety tolerance work  
✗ **Narrow CBT technique application:** Primarily cognitive restructuring; missed opportunities for thought records, behavioral experiments, problem-solving, worry time, exposure  
✗ **Insufficient exploration of core beliefs:** Did not explore meaning of "not being there for wedding" or underlying beliefs about identity, worth, family role  
✗ **No medical/contextual integration:** Did not address reality of medical uncertainty or help patient develop acceptance-based coping alongside cognitive work  
✗ **Incomplete homework structure:** No monitoring/tracking plan, no contingency planning, no behavioral components, limited frequency specification  
✗ **Missed reassurance-seeking intervention:** Critical maintaining factor identified but not explicitly targeted as treatment focus  
✗ **Limited feedback-seeking:** Feedback elicited at beginning/end but not systematically throughout; no probing of resistance or concerns  

### Clinical Impact

**Expected Outcome:** Modest, temporary improvement. Patient will practice cognitive restructuring and may experience relief when using alternative thought. However, without addressing reassurance-seeking behavior, anxiety cycle will likely continue—each reassurance reinforces belief that she cannot tolerate uncertainty, and anxiety returns when reassurance wears off. Without behavioral intervention, depression and withdrawal will persist. Without exploring core beliefs, existential concerns (identity, worth, mortality) remain unaddressed.

**Competency Level:** Intermediate—competent in basic CBT skills (agenda-setting, empathy, cognitive restructuring, homework assignment) but not yet advanced in comprehensive case formulation, multimodal intervention, or integration of medical/contextual factors.

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Target reassurance-seeking as maintaining factor** and develop explicit plans to reduce it while building tolerance for uncertainty
2. **Add behavioral activation** to address withdrawal and depression
3. **Incorporate acceptance-based coping** alongside cognitive work for managing genuine medical uncertainty
4. **Expand CBT techniques** beyond cognitive restructuring to include thought records, behavioral experiments, problem-solving, worry time
5. **Explore core beliefs** to understand deeper meaning of worry and address guilt/helplessness
