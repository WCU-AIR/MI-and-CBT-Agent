## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamental Misunderstanding

**Summary:**

This session represents a **severe failure to practice Motivational Interviewing**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no competence across all nine MI domains. The session is characterized by authoritarianism, coercion, dismissal of legitimate client concerns, and active suppression of the client's own motivation for change.

**Critical Findings:**

- **Harm to Client Engagement:** Client enters with openness ("weight is something I would like to get hold of") and explicit willingness to collaborate ("Can you help me figure this out?"). By session end, client is defensive and disengaged, explicitly stating the approach is counterproductive: "That's pushing me away."

- **Violation of MI Spirit:** Session violates nearly every core MI principle. Psychiatrist operates from expert/authority stance rather than collaborative, client-centered stance.

- **Predicted Outcomes:** Non-adherence, dropout, decreased intrinsic motivation, damaged therapeutic relationship, reinforced helplessness.

**Most Critical Improvements Needed:**

1. Adopt collaborative, client-centered stance rather than expert/authority stance
2. Develop reflective listening skills and use reflections to demonstrate empathy
3. Stop using threats and coercion; replace with autonomy support
4. Learn to evoke rather than impose motivation through skillful questioning
5. Collaborate on realistic solutions rather than imposing unrealistic demands

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist requires fundamental retraining in MI principles and practice. Formal MI training program and ongoing supervision with MI-trained supervisor are essential before continuing to practice MI with clients.
