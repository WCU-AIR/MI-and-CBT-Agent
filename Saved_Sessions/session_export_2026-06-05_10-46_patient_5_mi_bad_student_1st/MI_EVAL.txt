## Overall Summary

### Strengths
**This session contains no identifiable MI strengths.** There are no instances of skillful motivational interviewing practice. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate acceptance, empathy, collaboration, autonomy support, evocation, partnership, cultivation of change talk, or skillful handling of sustain talk. The only consistent element—maintenance of focus on lifestyle change—is pursued through coercion rather than collaboration, which violates MI principles.

---

### Areas for Critical Improvement

**1. Fundamental Reorientation to MI Spirit and Principles**
- Complete retraining in MI fundamentals required before continuing to see patients in MI framework
- Attend certified MI training program (minimum 2-3 days)
- Engage in weekly role-play practice with feedback
- Record and review sessions with MI-trained supervisor

**2. Develop Empathic Attunement and Reflective Listening**
- Learn to recognize and reflect patient's emotional experience
- When patient says "I'm scared," respond with validation, not dismissal
- Practice writing reflective responses; record sessions and count reflections vs. directives (goal: 70% reflections)
- Identify at least three emotional moments per session and practice empathic responses

**3. Eliminate Coercive Language and Threats**
- Replace "you have to," "you must," "no ifs, ands, or buts," and threat-based language
- Use collaborative, autonomy-supporting language: "What feels like a realistic first step for you?"
- Review recordings and rewrite every coercive statement using collaborative language
- Practice new language patterns until natural

**4. Learn to Handle Sustain Talk Without Confrontation**
- Study MI literature on sustain talk (Miller & Rollnick, 2013)
- Develop competence in double-sided reflection and reframing
- Example: Instead of "Stop worrying about cancer," use: "On one hand, you're worried about serious illness—that's understandable. On the other hand, you've noticed stress affects your symptoms. Both are real."
- Aim for zero direct confrontation of sustain talk

**5. Develop Skill in Evoking Client Motivation**
- Replace "telling" with "asking"
- Ask open-ended questions: "What would it mean to feel better?" "What activities do you miss?" "What have you already tried that helped?" "What would make this manageable?"
- Aim for 5-7 evocative questions per session
- Explore patient's own reasons for change rather than imposing them

**6. Establish Genuine Collaboration and Partnership**
- Shift from expert-driven to client-centered approach
- Treat patient as expert on their own experience
- Co-create treatment plans rather than imposing them
- Use inclusive language: "we," "together," "let's explore"
- Explicitly seek patient input: "What do you think about...?" "How could we...?" "What would work for you?"

**7. Address Patient's Actual Presenting Problem and Priorities**
- Conduct thorough assessment of patient's concerns and priorities
- Begin sessions by exploring what brought patient in, not announcing predetermined topic
- Spend first 10-15 minutes exploring patient's perspective on presenting problem, goals, and priorities
- Ensure treatment plan addresses patient's stated priorities, not only clinician's agenda

**8. Develop Competence in Recognizing and Amplifying Change Talk**
- Study DARN-CAT framework (Miller & Rollnick, 2013)
- Identify at least three instances of change talk per session
- Reflect and amplify each instance: "You really do want this. Tell me more about what feeling better would make possible."
- Have supervisor rate skill in recognizing and amplifying change talk

**9. Eliminate Repetitive, Ineffective Confrontation**
- When patient doesn't respond to intervention, change approach rather than repeating same statement
- Repetition suggests either lack of skill or contempt for patient
- If repeating same statement more than twice, stop and change approach
- Explore barriers collaboratively: "Tell me more about what makes this difficult. What might feel more manageable?"

**10. Seek Intensive Supervision and Fidelity Feedback**
- Engage supervisor trained in MI fidelity assessment
- Weekly supervision sessions reviewing recorded sessions
- Use validated MI fidelity scale (e.g., MITI 4.0)
- Complete self-assessment on nine MI domains after each session
- Commit to 12-16 weeks of intensive supervision before resuming independent practice
