# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist uses pejorative language ("you're making excuses," "you're being so weak") and explicit judgment throughout. No affirmation of client's strengths (honors student, athlete) or autonomy. Opening statement establishes confrontational, non-collaborative stance. Systematic invalidation of client's genuine internal conflict demonstrates contempt rather than acceptance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand client's perspective. When client discloses peer pressure concerns, psychiatrist does not explore emotional experience but instead prescribes solutions. No validation of adolescent developmental struggle. Repeated dismissal of client's stated concerns ("No, you're not stuck") signals listening to refute rather than to understand. Zero empathic attunement demonstrated.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear topical focus on alcohol use, preventing complete disorganization. However, direction is imposed coercively rather than collaboratively. Repeated closed yes/no questions cut off exploration. No investigation of client's own goals, values, or readiness. Direction maintained through fear and shame rather than collaborative goal-setting. Approach is authoritarian rather than MI-consistent.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Zero collaboration demonstrated. Opening statement explicitly rejects partnership: "I'll be telling you exactly what you need to do." Commanding language throughout ("You need to," "You have to," "You must"). Client input systematically dismissed ("No, you're not stuck," "That's not acceptable"). No joint problem-solving or power-sharing. Client treated as object to be controlled, not collaborative partner.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Systematic undermining of autonomy at every turn. Coercive language removes choice ("You need to cut down immediately"). False binary pressure tactics ("Are you going to quit completely starting today, yes or no?"). Shaming language ("you have to stop being so weak") attacks client's self-efficacy. Explicit rejection of client's stated readiness ("That's not acceptable"). No acknowledgment of client's right to determine own pace and goals.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist tells rather than asks, prescribes rather than explores. Multiple missed opportunities to evoke client's own motivation: emerging change talk ("feeling off the next day") used as ammunition rather than developed; client's values (academic/athletic achievement) dismissed as rationalization; genuine ambivalence ("stuck between wanting to do well and fitting in") invalidated rather than explored. No open-ended questions designed to elicit client's reasons for change or vision of future.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist assumes exclusive expert role and actively suppresses client contributions. Opening statement positions psychiatrist as sole authority. Client's self-knowledge about readiness explicitly rejected ("That's not acceptable"). No incorporation of client's ideas into collaborative plan. Unequal power throughout—psychiatrist asks questions and makes demands; client responds defensively. Client's expertise about his own life systematically invalidated.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist actively suppresses rather than cultivates change talk. Client's emerging awareness ("feeling off the next day") not amplified. Client's values ("honors student and athlete matters to me") not affirmed or developed. Client's recognition of risk ("drinking can mess things up") dismissed as excuses. Client's change talk ("Maybe I could think about cutting back") explicitly rejected ("just a way to keep making bad choices"). Multiple opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation missed or undermined.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist confronts sustain talk rather than softening it, triggering defensiveness and strengthening resistance. No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or empathic exploration. Client's sustain talk about peer pressure ("trying to fit in") met with prescription ("find new friends") rather than exploration. Confrontational approach backfires—client becomes increasingly defensive and entrenched throughout session. Minimal score reflects attempt to redirect focus, but achieved through coercion rather than skillful MI technique.

## Overall Assessment Summary

**Overall MI Competency Level:** Minimal to None (1.1/5 average)

**Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of Motivational Interviewing practice** and demonstrates departure from evidence-based, client-centered care. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance explicitly inconsistent with MI principles across all nine domains.

**Key Deficiencies:**
- Confrontation and judgment replace collaboration
- Directive advice-giving replaces evocation
- Suppression of change talk replaces cultivation
- Coercive pressure replaces autonomy support
- Systematic invalidation of client perspective

**Clinical Impact:**

This approach is **actively harmful**. Research demonstrates confrontation and coercion *decrease* intrinsic motivation and *increase* defensive resistance. Client explicitly states "I'm not ready to quit completely today"—indicating coercive approach has strengthened resistance rather than motivation. Client likely to leave session feeling judged, misunderstood, and more entrenched in current behavior.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Fundamental philosophical shift** from expert-driven to collaborative stance
2. **Implementation of reflective listening** as primary clinical tool
3. **Elimination of judgmental language** and shame-based messaging
4. **Formal Motivational Interviewing training** with certified trainer (minimum 2-day workshop)
5. **Ongoing MI supervision** with regular session recording review and feedback
6. **Study of MI literature** (Miller & Rollnick foundational texts)

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** Very Low without immediate intervention and retraining.
