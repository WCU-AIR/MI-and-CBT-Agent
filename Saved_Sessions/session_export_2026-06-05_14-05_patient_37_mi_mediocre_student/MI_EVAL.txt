# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal unconditional positive regard demonstrated. Psychiatrist adopts problem-focused stance, repeatedly asking "Which one do you think is the biggest problem?" despite Elena's clear statements that she has no major problems. Fails to affirm Elena's positive functioning, self-awareness, or values-based approach. Expert-driven and prescriptive rather than accepting of client as presented. Limited validation occurs only late in session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Superficial and inconsistent empathic connection. Uses minimal reflective listening and rarely demonstrates genuine effort to understand Elena's emotional experience or perspective. Misses rich opportunities to explore Elena's self-compassion, values-alignment, and realistic self-awareness. Reflections are factual summaries rather than emotionally attuned explorations. No evidence of attempting to understand Elena's internal motivations or what self-respect means to her specifically.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear focus on health behavior change (diet and exercise) is maintained appropriately. However, direction is imposed rather than collaboratively established. Psychiatrist announces agenda rather than exploring what Elena wants to work on. Becomes increasingly prescriptive with specific recommendations (meal prep, journaling, backup plans). Focus is clinician-driven rather than emerging from Elena's priorities. Elena's agreement appears to be compliance rather than genuine co-construction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Profound absence of genuine collaboration. Psychiatrist maintains expert authority throughout, making unilateral decisions about focus, strategy, and goals. Unilateral agenda-setting, prescriptive recommendations without exploration, and dismissal of Elena's perspective that habits are "pretty okay" and not a "big problem." All solutions clinician-generated. Elena's contributions acknowledged but do not substantially influence session direction. Session reads as clinician delivering advice to compliant patient, not as partners collaboratively exploring what matters.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely absent despite lack of explicit coercion. Frames recommendations as suggestions ("Do you think...?" "Maybe you could...?") but these function as directives. Never explicitly affirms Elena's right to choose or ownership of change process. Phrases like "You might want to try" and "It's important to stay focused" position psychiatrist as authority. Elena's explicit autonomy statements ("I want it to feel natural," "I want to keep that balance") are acknowledged but not actively reinforced or used to empower her decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost entirely absent—critical MI failure. Elena provides rich material about motivations and values ("I want to keep feeling good," "I want it to feel natural," "I'm learning to be patient with myself") but psychiatrist largely ignores this and imposes external strategies instead. Never asks clarifying questions to deepen understanding of Elena's perspective, values, or emotional landscape. All solutions come from clinician; Elena never invited to generate her own. Closed questions focus on problems and compliance rather than evoking client wisdom.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Entirely absent. Psychiatrist assumes expert role throughout and maintains this stance consistently. Unilateral knowledge claims ("It would probably help if," "It's important to") position psychiatrist as authority on what matters. Dismisses Elena's expertise about her own situation. Prescriptive problem-solving with no invitation for Elena's input. When Elena contributes ideas, these are acknowledged briefly but do not reshape psychiatrist's approach. Language of authority throughout; no genuine power-sharing or co-construction of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena spontaneously generates substantial change talk ("I feel great," "I want to keep feeling good," "I'd say it's about a 7 for me on importance," "I feel good about giving it a try") but psychiatrist does not strategically cultivate or amplify it. Asks about importance and confidence late in session but does not use responses to deepen exploration. When Elena states preferences, psychiatrist briefly validates but continues with prescriptive advice. Missed opportunities to explore what "improving" means to Elena or how to build on her self-compassion and self-efficacy. Change talk present but underutilized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal and handled reasonably well when present. Psychiatrist avoids amplifying or reinforcing Elena's concerns ("It doesn't always stick," "Sometimes life gets in the way"). Does not argue with sustain talk or use confrontation. Offers practical solutions without pushing back on Elena's realistic perspective. However, could have been more skillful by using double-sided reflections to acknowledge both desire for change and realistic concerns, then inviting Elena to explore bridging that gap. Could have asked Elena to elaborate on sustain talk rather than immediately offering solutions.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.9/5**

### Summary of Key Findings

**Strengths:**
- Maintains clear session focus on health behavior change
- Warm, welcoming opening greeting
- Avoids confrontation and argument with client
- Offers occasional validation of Elena's perspective
- Does not amplify sustain talk
- Asks importance and confidence questions (though underutilized)

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
- **Fundamental MI spirit violation:** Session is expert-driven, prescriptive, and directive rather than collaborative, evocative, and client-centered
- **Absence of genuine collaboration:** Unilateral decision-making about focus, strategy, and goals; Elena's input solicited minimally
- **Failure to evoke:** Imposes solutions rather than drawing out Elena's own motivations and ideas
- **Minimal empathy and reflective listening:** Surface-level acknowledgments rather than deep, complex reflections
- **Lack of autonomy support:** Systematically undermines Elena's sense of choice and self-determination
- **Problem-focused rather than strength-focused:** Frames session around identifying "problems" rather than building on existing strengths and values
- **Missed opportunities:** Elena provides rich material about values, self-compassion, and realistic self-awareness that is not explored

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change

Elena presents as highly motivated, values-aligned, and self-aware. While she is compliant with psychiatrist's suggestions, this compliance appears external rather than internally motivated. Psychiatrist's prescriptive approach risks undermining Elena's intrinsic motivation and autonomy. Elena explicitly stated wanting change to feel "natural" and not like an "extra burden"—the detailed plans and accountability measures may create exactly the pressure she wants to avoid, potentially leading to non-adherence once session ends.

### MI Competency Assessment

**Current Level:** Minimal MI competence. This is supportive counseling with directive, expert-driven stance, not genuine MI practice. Psychiatrist would benefit from comprehensive MI training and supervised practice to develop collaborative, evocative, and client-centered skills.

### Most Critical Recommendations

1. Shift from expert-driven to collaborative problem-solving
2. Evoke rather than impose solutions; ask open questions first
3. Deepen empathic reflections to capture Elena's values and internal experience
4. Actively cultivate and amplify Elena's change talk
5. Support autonomy and explicitly affirm Elena's ownership of change process
6. Build on existing strengths rather than focusing on problems
7. Use double-sided reflections to honor ambivalence
8. Reconnect health behaviors to Elena's values to motivate sustainable change
