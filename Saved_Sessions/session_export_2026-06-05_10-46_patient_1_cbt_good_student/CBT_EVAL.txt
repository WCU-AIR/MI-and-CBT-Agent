## SUMMARY ASSESSMENT

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- ✓ Strong collaborative agenda-setting with explicit invitation for patient priorities
- ✓ Excellent guided discovery and Socratic questioning throughout session
- ✓ Skillful cognitive restructuring with patient-generated balanced thoughts
- ✓ Appropriate behavioral experiment planning targeting avoidance directly
- ✓ Strong interpersonal effectiveness and rapport; patient explicitly appreciated approach
- ✓ Explicit session feedback solicitation and responsiveness to patient input
- ✓ Accurate empathic understanding of multimodal anxiety response
- ✓ Appropriate focus on central cognitions and behavioral patterns for driving anxiety

### Critical Deficiencies:
- ✗ **Incomplete agenda management:** Two priorities identified; only one addressed. Sleep/nightmare work deferred without explicit negotiation. Dylan explicitly stated at session end this should be next priority.
- ✗ **Inadequate trauma-focused intervention:** Patient presents with acute stress disorder two weeks post-trauma. No trauma memory processing, imaginal exposure, or imagery rescripting applied to intrusive images—core ASD symptoms.
- ✗ **No sleep/nightmare intervention:** Patient identified nighttime panic and intrusive thoughts as priority. No sleep hygiene, nightmare management, or grounding techniques introduced.
- ✗ **Insufficient psychoeducation:** No explicit education about acute stress disorder, trauma responses, or rationale for exposure-based treatment—standard CBT component.
- ✗ **Limited emotion regulation/distress tolerance skills:** Patient presents with irritability and emotional dysregulation. No emotion regulation techniques (breathing, grounding, mindfulness) introduced.
- ✗ **No safety planning or crisis protocol:** Given acute distress and sleep disruption, no safety plan or crisis management protocol established.
- ✗ **Incomplete behavioral assessment:** No comprehensive assessment of activity level, concentration difficulties, or academic impairment. Behavioral activation limited to single exposure task.
- ✗ **Inadequate homework implementation:** No explicit rationale, coping strategy planning, commitment assessment, or monitoring instructions provided.
- ✗ **No multi-symptom treatment planning:** Comprehensive treatment plan addressing all acute stress disorder symptoms not established.

## CLINICAL IMPACT ASSESSMENT

**Session Outcome for Driving Anxiety Specifically:**
Dylan demonstrated genuine cognitive progress regarding driving anxiety. His balanced thought ("I'm not ready to drive yet because I'm still scared, but that doesn't mean I never will be") represents meaningful shift from hopelessness. Behavioral experiment is appropriately designed with reasonable likelihood of success, potentially building confidence and breaking avoidance cycle for this specific problem.

**Overall Treatment Adequacy:**
**INADEQUATE for acute stress disorder presentation.** While session demonstrates competent general CBT skills, it represents incomplete treatment for a patient with multiple acute trauma symptoms two weeks post-accident. By addressing only driving anxiety, therapist leaves sleep disturbance, intrusive imagery, hypervigilance, and irritability unaddressed. Without comprehensive trauma-focused intervention, these symptoms likely to persist or worsen, increasing risk of chronic PTSD.

**Likelihood of Full Symptom Resolution:**
**LOW.** Patient will likely experience some improvement in driving anxiety through behavioral experiment and cognitive restructuring. However, overall acute stress disorder symptoms unlikely to resolve without trauma memory processing, sleep intervention, and comprehensive symptom management. Early intervention window (2 weeks post-trauma) represents optimal opportunity for trauma-focused CBT; delay increases chronification risk.

## IMMEDIATE RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Establish comprehensive multi-symptom treatment plan** addressing all acute stress disorder symptoms (intrusive imagery, sleep disturbance, driving anxiety, hypervigilance, irritability)
2. **Introduce trauma memory processing** in next session using imaginal exposure and cognitive processing
3. **Implement sleep/nightmare interventions** including sleep hygiene, imagery rehearsal therapy, and grounding techniques
