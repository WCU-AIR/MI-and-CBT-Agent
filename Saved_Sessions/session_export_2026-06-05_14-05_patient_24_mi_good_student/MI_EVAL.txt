### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, skillful cultivation of change talk across all categories (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Patient generates desire: "I'd like to figure out how to cut back a bit without feeling like I'm giving up something important." Psychiatrist reflects and affirms. Reasons/need emerge: "I don't want to make my depression worse," "I don't like how I feel after drinking a lot." Psychiatrist reinforces: "Noticing how it affects you physically and mentally shows a lot of self-awareness." Ability demonstrated through past successes; psychiatrist affirms resourcefulness. Commitment evident: "I could try two drinks a night," confidence at 7/10. Strategic open-ended questions designed to elicit change talk. Complex reflections amplify and deepen change talk while acknowledging ambivalence.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally skillful handling of sustain talk, though opportunities exist for more proactive, strategic pivoting earlier in session. Psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify sustain talk (e.g., "It's hard because drinking is part of how I relax," "I'm worried about slipping back"). Uses double-sided reflections acknowledging sustain talk while emphasizing change side: "That tension between the comfort and the consequences seems really challenging...but also the hope that cutting back could bring relief." However, could have been more strategic in redirecting earlier. When patient states "drinking is part of how I relax," psychiatrist could ask: "What else helps you relax that might feel just as satisfying?" When patient expresses worry about slipping, could ask: "What strategies have helped you stick with changes when tempted to slip back?" More proactive redirection earlier would strengthen change talk cultivation.

- Sophisticated reflective listening capturing emotional nuance and deeper meaning
- Strategic evocation drawing out patient's own motivations, values, and resources
- Unwavering autonomy support with no pressure, prescription, or external coercion
- Genuine collaboration with permission-seeking and client-centered problem-solving

### Areas for Improvement:
- **Earlier sustain talk redirection:** More proactive strategic pivoting away from sustain talk through targeted questions redirecting toward change talk and client resources
- **Deeper depression-alcohol exploration:** More explicit exploration of how reducing alcohol might specifically improve patient's mood and mental health, given depression as significant driver
- **Explicit follow-up planning:** More concrete discussion of when to check in about progress and what next conversation will focus on to strengthen accountability and continuity

### Clinical Impact:
This session is highly likely to increase patient's intrinsic motivation for change. Psychological safety created through unconditional acceptance reduces shame and defensiveness. Client-centered approach supports intrinsic motivation rather than external pressure. Patient moves from ambivalence (6/10 importance) to concrete planning with 7/10 confidence—meaningful shift toward readiness. Patient generates own goal, identifies own strategies, and recognizes own resources, increasing ownership of change process critical for sustained behavior change.
