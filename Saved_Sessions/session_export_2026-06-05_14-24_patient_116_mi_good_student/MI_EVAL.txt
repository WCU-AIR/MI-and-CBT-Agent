When Samir identifies children as motivator, clinician affirms but does not evoke deeper exploration. Shift toward providing information represents move away from evocation toward installation of ideas. Spontaneous change talk not systematically amplified through strategic questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inconsistent partnership demonstrated. Early collaborative language ("I want to make sure this time is as helpful as possible for you") present, but gradual shift toward expert-evaluator role occurs. Clinician positions self as evaluator of Samir's approach rather than as equal co-explorer. True partnership—where client's contributions substantially shape direction and clinician positions self as co-explorer—inconsistently demonstrated throughout session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal strategic cultivation of change talk. Samir spontaneously offers multiple instances (problem recognition, ability, commitment, values), but clinician does not strategically evoke or amplify. When Samir identifies children as motivator or states asthma is "out of control," clinician reflects but does not ask follow-up questions to deepen. Change talk emerges spontaneously but is not systematically reinforced through strategic questioning and reflection.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant deficiency in addressing sustain talk. Samir expresses multiple instances of resistance ("not ready to quit completely," "not convinced quitting will help," "worry about slipping back"). Rather than skillfully softening or redirecting, clinician validates and reinforces sustain talk, potentially strengthening resistance. Double-sided reflections highlighting tension between concerns and values absent. Clinician does not explore doubt about efficacy or fear of failure strategically.

## Overall MI Competency Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 3.4/5**

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates competent, supportive counseling with genuine warmth, acceptance, and respect for the client's perspective. The psychiatrist creates a safe, nonjudgmental environment and demonstrates accurate reflective listening. However, the session falls short of exemplary motivational interviewing practice in several critical areas.

**Strengths:**
- Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard
- Accurate, empathic reflective listening
- Appropriate pacing and respect for client readiness
- Clear affirmation of progress and effort
- Logical session structure with appropriate progression
- Awareness of real-world constraints and context

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Insufficient strategic evocation of change talk
- Reinforcement rather than softening of sustain talk
- Gradual shift from collaborative to expert-driven stance
- Limited explicit autonomy support
- Surface-level rather than complex empathic reflections
- Missed opportunities for deeper exploration of values and goals

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

While the supportive environment likely increases Samir's comfort and engagement, the session is unlikely to significantly increase intrinsic motivation for substantial lifestyle change. By reinforcing sustain talk rather than skillfully addressing ambivalence, the clinician may inadvertently strengthen resistance. The modest action plan (cutting one cigarette per day) emerges more from clinician guidance than from Samir's own discovery of reasons for change, reducing ownership and sustainability.

**Recommendations for Development:**

1. Develop systematic questioning to strategically evoke and amplify change talk
2. Learn to use double-sided reflections to skillfully address sustain talk and ambivalence
3. Maintain true collaborative partnership; resist shift toward expert-driven guidance
4. Explicitly affirm client autonomy and choice throughout session
5. Deepen empathic understanding through complex reflections capturing emotional nuance
6. Connect behavior change to deeper values and life goals
7. Use confidence scaling as collaborative exploration tool
8. Address occupational triggers more strategically

**Recommendation for Development:**

The psychiatrist should prioritize training in strategic evocation of change talk and skillful handling of ambivalence. Supervision with MI-focused feedback, role-play practice, and review of recorded sessions with attention to missed opportunities for change talk amplification would significantly strengthen MI practice. With focused development, this clinician could move from competent supportive counseling to exemplary motivational interviewing.
