### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1/5
**Comments:** Minimal cultivation of Elena's significant change talk throughout session: "I'm so relieved I'm not pregnant," "That was really scary," "I don't want that to happen again," "I want to prevent pregnancy," "I want to do better." Psychiatrist does not strategically evoke or amplify this language. When Elena expresses desire to prevent pregnancy, psychiatrist demands immediate action on unrelated topic rather than exploring her motivations. Psychiatrist provides arguments for change rather than inviting client to discover own motivations. Research demonstrates client-generated change talk more predictive of behavior change than clinician-provided arguments. Fails to cultivate confidence by exploring past successes and strengths. No questions like "What would it mean to you to prevent another scare?" or "What strengths have helped you make other important decisions?" Psychiatrist's arguments replace client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Poor handling of sustain talk using confrontation that strengthens resistance rather than skillful softening. Elena's sustain talk ("I'm not ready," "Maybe later," "It just feels like too much") met with confrontation: "Don't let feelings like stress or fear stop you—that's just an excuse." Research demonstrates confrontation typically strengthens sustain talk; Elena becomes more defensive and entrenched after psychiatrist's responses. No double-sided reflections validating both Elena's desire to prevent pregnancy and her hesitation about methods. No reframing of hesitation as strength (thoughtfulness about body). No strategic questioning ("What would need to happen for you to feel more ready?"). Elena's sustain talk often reflects legitimate concerns rather than resistance, but psychiatrist frames concerns as obstacles to overcome. Slight improvement in exchange 4 with collaborative question ("What would be a manageable first step?"), but occurs only once after multiple confrontational responses. Legitimate concerns about side effects not validated as worthy of exploration.

## Overall Session Assessment

### **Overall Score: 1.3/5**

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates **severe and systematic violations of Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit**. The psychiatrist employs an authoritarian, expert-driven approach fundamentally incompatible with MI. The session begins with **agenda imposition** (smoking cessation rather than client's stated concern about contraception), continues with **confrontational responses** to client's ambivalence, and maintains a **coercive dynamic** throughout most of interaction.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Acceptance undermined by judgmental language and conditional regard
- Empathy limited to brief superficial moments
- Direction severely misaligned with client's stated goals
- Collaboration absent; client input does not shape session
- Autonomy systematically undermined through pressure and demands
- Evocation minimal; psychiatrist provides arguments rather than evoking client's own motivations
- Partnership not established; expert role actively assumed
- Change talk not cultivated or amplified despite significant client expressions
- Sustain talk confronted rather than softened; resistance strengthened

**Impact on Client Outcomes:**

Elena enters session with significant **intrinsic motivation** to prevent another pregnancy scare. Psychiatrist's approach **systematically undermines this motivation** through pressure, dismissal of concerns, and failure to support autonomy. By session end, Elena is **complying with pressure rather than committing to genuine change**. This approach significantly **reduces likelihood of treatment adherence** and sustained behavior change.

**Therapeutic Relationship:**

Psychiatrist has **damaged therapeutic relationship** through repeated MI violations. Elena explicitly identifies lack of being heard. This rupture makes it **less likely Elena will return for follow-up care** or trust psychiatrist's recommendations.

**Recommendation:**

Psychiatrist requires **comprehensive MI training and clinical supervision** to develop competence in this evidence-based approach. Current approach is not only ineffective but **potentially harmful to client outcomes**. Session would likely result in poor treatment adherence, continued client ambivalence, and potential therapeutic rupture.
