### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Poor handling of sustain talk. Psychiatrist uses direct confrontation and accusation ("You need to stop making excuses," "You're just making excuses again") rather than MI techniques to soften resistance. This approach amplifies defensiveness and entrenches patient's sustain talk rather than softening it. Patient's responses become increasingly defensive: "I'm not making excuses, I'm just saying what's true for me." ## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI. This psychiatrist is operating at a fundamental level incompatible with motivational interviewing principles.

**Key Findings:**

This session represents a severe failure to implement motivational interviewing across nearly all core domains. The psychiatrist employs a directive, confrontational, shame-based approach that violates MI spirit and actively undermines patient motivation for change.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Authoritarian stance established from opening statement
- Repeated accusations and shame-inducing language
- Systematic dismissal of patient's perspective and barriers
- Escalation of demands in response to resistance
- Complete failure to evoke or amplify change talk
- Violation of patient autonomy through ultimatums and emotional manipulation
- Absence of genuine empathy and reflective listening

**Impact on Patient Engagement:**

Elena's responses demonstrate the predictable consequences of directive, confrontational practice:
- **Increasing defensiveness:** Moves from acknowledgment ("I know it's not good for me") to defensive positioning ("I'm not making excuses")
- **Withdrawal of commitment:** Begins with openness ("I've thought about quitting") and ends with clear refusal ("I'm not ready to commit right now")
- **Stated desire to disengage:** "Pushing me like this just makes me want to shut down, not change"
- **Repeated refusal:** Multiple refusals by session end; psychiatrist cycles back to opening statement, indicating session is stuck

**Likelihood of Behavior Change:** Very low. Session has likely decreased patient motivation and damaged therapeutic relationship.

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Adopt collaborative, partnership-based stance
2. Eliminate accusatory language and shame-based framing
3. Develop genuine empathy through reflective listening
4. Recognize that escalating demands in response to resistance is counterproductive
5. Enroll in formal MI training and supervision before continuing clinical practice
