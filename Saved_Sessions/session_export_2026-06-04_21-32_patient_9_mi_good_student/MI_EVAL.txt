### Collaboration

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects and reinforces Amina's autonomy throughout. Avoids prescriptive advice-giving, instead asking "What do you think would need to happen?" Explicitly affirms her right to choose and adjust ("It's completely okay to take it step by step"). Does not pressure despite ambivalence. Frames one-hour-before-bed limit as her idea and asks permission rather than assuming compliance. Minor opportunity: Could explicitly acknowledge her right to *not* change and verify that stated confidence increase reflects her genuine assessment rather than response to clinician enthusiasm.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid evocation by drawing out Amina's own motivations, reasons for change, and resources. Directly asks "What do you think makes you want to think about changing?" and she responds with change talk (grades slipped, tired in class, distraction). Strategically evokes past successes and Amina generates her own first step. Amplifies change talk through reflections. However, does not explicitly explore core values or ask about vision of changed future ("What would your life look like if you successfully managed phone use?"). Does not strategically evoke competing values when sustain talk emerges.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership stance by consistently positioning Amina as expert and co-constructor of solutions. Affirms her competence and past successes. Frames her ideas as central rather than clinician-driven. Uses inclusive language and avoids expert role. However, some subtle expert-driven framing remains—e.g., asking about past successes presupposes they exist and subtly directs her toward identifying them. Does not explicitly invite feedback on the collaborative process itself or ask "How is this conversation feeling for you?"

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid cultivation of change talk by strategically evoking and amplifying Amina's own language favoring change. Asks questions that elicit change talk ("What makes you want to think about changing?") and reflects it back with emphasis. Reinforces commitment language and builds on it. However, does not systematically explore all dimensions of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Does not strategically deepen qualified change talk statements ("maybe," "I guess," "I'm not sure"). Missing explicit questions about desire, need, and commitment strength.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** **Weakest area of MI practice.** Sustain talk emerges multiple times ("helps me relax," "stay in touch with friends," "might miss out," "don't want to lose that part of my day") but is not strategically addressed. Psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk through reflections but does not reframe, explore, or challenge it. Does not use strategic techniques such as: exploring whether fears are proportional to reality, evoking competing coping strategies, or amplifying discrepancy between values. When Amina expresses low confidence (sustain talk about inability), psychiatrist highlights past successes but does not explore whether low confidence is realistic. Sustain talk remains relatively unchallenged and potentially reinforced.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 4.0/5**

### Key Strengths:
- ✓ Strong collaborative stance with genuine respect for client expertise
- ✓ Accurate, well-constructed reflective listening
- ✓ Effective evocation of change talk and past successes
- ✓ Consistent autonomy support and nonjudgmental acceptance
- ✓ Clear direction with logical session flow
- ✓ Concrete action planning with identified supports
- ✓ Genuine warmth and affirmation of strengths

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ **Weak softening of sustain talk** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) – sustain talk acknowledged but not strategically reframed or explored
- ✗ Surface-level empathy – misses deeper emotional exploration and affective nuance
- ✗ Limited values exploration – does not explicitly connect phone use to core values
- ✗ Incomplete goal clarification – does not define what "success" means or how progress will be measured
- ✗ Non-systematic change talk cultivation – does not comprehensively explore all five dimensions
- ✗ Absent future-oriented evocation – does not ask client to envision changed life
- ✗ Subtle leading questions – some questions guide rather than purely evoke
- ✗ No explicit process feedback – does not invite client feedback on collaborative approach

### Impact on Client Engagement:
Session successfully increases Amina's confidence (4-5 → 6-7) and generates commitment to concrete first step. Collaborative, respectful stance likely makes her feel heard and supported. However, unchallenged sustain talk may undermine follow-through when implementation becomes difficult. Lack of explicit values connection means Amina may not have deeply linked change goal to what matters most, potentially weakening sustained motivation.

### Recommendation:
With focused attention to strategically softening sustain talk, deepening emotional empathy, systematically cultivating all change talk dimensions, and explicitly exploring values, this clinician would advance from solid competence (4/5) to exemplary MI practice (5/5).
