## Overall Summary

### **Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Critical Findings:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure in Motivational Interviewing practice**. The psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no competence across all nine MI domains. The session is characterized by:

- **Authoritarian stance** rather than partnership and collaboration
- **Judgment and dismissal** rather than acceptance and empathy
- **Demands and pressure** rather than evocation and autonomy support
- **Misdiagnosis of presenting problem** (focusing on smoking rather than alcohol use)
- **Rejection of client's emotional reality** and legitimate concerns
- **Failure to evoke or reinforce change talk** despite Sofia's substantial spontaneous motivation
- **Likely harm to therapeutic relationship** and Sofia's intrinsic motivation

**Impact on Client:**

Sofia enters with genuine concern for her baby and some intrinsic motivation to change. By session end, she has nominally agreed to quit but expresses loneliness and lack of support ("I just wish it wasn't so damn lonely"), suggesting her commitment is fragile and externally motivated rather than intrinsically motivated. This approach is empirically associated with decreased motivation and increased resistance to change.

**Critical Recommendation:**

This clinician requires **immediate, intensive training in Motivational Interviewing principles and practice**. The current approach is likely to harm patients rather than help them. Before seeing additional patients for substance use issues, this clinician should complete formal MI training and receive supervision from an MI-trained clinician. Consider referral to MINT (Motivational Interviewing Network of Trainers) for formal training and certification.
