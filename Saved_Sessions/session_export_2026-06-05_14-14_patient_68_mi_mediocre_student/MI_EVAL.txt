### Evocation

## Overall Assessment

**Total Score: 2.0/5**

**Summary:**

This session demonstrates minimal competence in Motivational Interviewing. While the psychiatrist maintains appropriate focus on alcohol reduction and avoids overt confrontation, the fundamental approach violates core MI principles by prioritizing expert advice-giving over collaborative exploration and evocation. The psychiatrist positions himself as the authority with predetermined solutions rather than as a genuine partner in Kenji's discovery process.

**Key Strengths:**
- Maintains clear focus on alcohol reduction throughout
- Avoids directly reinforcing sustain talk
- Demonstrates basic respect and willingness to engage
- Makes one explicit statement about client autonomy
- Shows awareness that small goals may be more achievable than large ones

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Shift from advice-giving to evocation** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) – Replace prescriptive statements with open-ended questions that invite Kenji to generate his own ideas
2. **Develop deeper empathic reflections** – Move beyond simple content reflections to capture emotional nuance and underlying needs
3. **Normalize ambivalence** – Recognize that Kenji's uncertainty is healthy and explore both sides without judgment
4. **Build genuine partnership** – Defer to client expertise and invite collaborative problem-solving
5. **Strategically evoke change talk** – Use open questions to draw out reasons for change and explore emerging change talk more deeply

**Impact on Client Motivation:**

This approach is likely to reduce Kenji's intrinsic motivation for change. While Kenji demonstrates some willingness to comply with suggestions, this is not genuine internal motivation. Research consistently shows that client-generated change talk predicts actual behavior change better than therapist-prescribed solutions. By failing to evoke Kenji's own reasons for change, the psychiatrist has missed the critical opportunity to build sustainable motivation. Kenji is likely to leave with a list of things he "should" do rather than genuine internal drive to change. Without significant improvement in MI skillfulness, likelihood of Kenji following through and maintaining change is low.
