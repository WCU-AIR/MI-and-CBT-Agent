## **Recommendations for Clinical Improvement**

### **1. Develop Strategic Responses to Sustain Talk (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
When clients express barriers or reasons for maintaining status quo, use double-sided reflections and strategic exploration rather than simple acknowledgment. Explore the sustain talk to understand its roots, then help the client problem-solve.

**Example:** When Camila says "some days the motivation just isn't there," respond: "So on some days motivation is harder to find. I'm curious—what's different about those days? And when motivation has been low in the past, what's helped you push through or adjust your approach?"

### **2. Deepen Evocation Through Gap Exploration**
After clients provide importance or confidence ratings, ask follow-up questions that explore the gap and evoke additional change talk.

**Example:** When Camila rates importance at 7/10, ask: "What would need to happen for this to feel like an 8 or 9? What would make it even more important to you?"

### **3. Draw Out Client Ideas Before Introducing Strategies**
Before offering external strategies, ask the client what ideas they've already considered. This maintains collaboration and honors the client's expertise.

**Example:** Instead of offering strategies, ask: "You've been thinking about how to add activity without feeling overwhelmed. What ideas have come to mind for you?"

### **4. Develop Contingency Plans for Anticipated Obstacles**
When clients identify potential barriers, explicitly explore these and develop concrete contingency plans using if-then statements.

**Example:** "If you have a particularly busy day, then you could do a 5-minute walk instead of 15 minutes."

### **5. Connect Past Successes to Current Change Efforts**
When clients identify past strengths or successes, explicitly link these to their current change goals to strengthen self-efficacy.

**Example:** "How have you used that discipline in the past to make other changes? How could you use that same approach with these walks?"

### **6. Strengthen Specificity of Behavioral Plans**
Make plans as specific as possible: exact days, exact times, specific location, how to use reminders, what to do if barriers arise.

### **7. Use Scaling Questions More Strategically**
After asking importance or confidence scales, use follow-up questions that explore what contributes to the current number and what would move it higher.
