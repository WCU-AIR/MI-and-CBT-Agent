### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance with genuine, specific affirmations grounded in Sofia's statements. Normalizes ambivalence early ("That's a really common place to be"). However, could deepen acceptance by more explicitly normalizing fear of relapse and validating stress as a normal human experience rather than personal failing. Overall tone is warm and supportive but could strengthen affirmation of Sofia's inherent worth independent of ability to change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and emotional nuance. Reflections capture cyclical nature of struggle and validate emotional weight Sofia carries. However, misses opportunities for deeper emotional exploration—when Sofia expresses worry about giving up again, clinician reflects ambivalence but doesn't explore underlying shame, disappointment, or self-doubt. Could use more complex reflections that demonstrate deeper attunement to Sofia's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains appropriate focus on sustainable lifestyle change with logical session progression: concerns → barriers → past success → principles → current context → concrete steps. However, path to first step is somewhat meandering. Could be more strategically directive through targeted questioning earlier. Additionally, doesn't explicitly link identified first step back to Sofia's core values and reasons for change, which would strengthen directional coherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes strong collaborative stance from opening ("What's on your mind right now that you'd like us to focus on?"). Frequently invites Sofia's input and validates her autonomy. Positions Sofia as active contributor to problem-solving. However, occasionally slips into expert stance by providing substantial interpretation before inviting Sofia's perspective. Could position Sofia's wisdom as primary earlier in process and invite her solutions before offering suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Sofia's right to choose with explicit affirmation of her pace and autonomy. Uses exploratory, invitational language rather than prescriptive directives. However, could more explicitly emphasize that Sofia's confidence level and internal experience are valid regardless of external expectations. When discussing reminders, could more explicitly ask what feels supportive versus pressuring. Could directly address fear of failure as valid concern Sofia gets to manage.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation with good questions about past success (yoga experience), but misses multiple opportunities to strategically amplify Sofia's change talk. Primarily reflects rather than deepens through follow-up questions. Minimal exploration of Sofia's reasons, ability, need, or commitment to change. Doesn't ask evocative questions about values ("What does having more energy mean to you?") or self-efficacy ("What gives you that 7?"). Limited strategic amplification of Sofia's own motivation throughout session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership with consistent positioning of Sofia as co-expert and frequent validation of her contributions. Uses inclusive language suggesting shared problem-solving. However, occasionally slips into expert stance by providing interpretations without first inviting Sofia's perspective. Could more consistently position Sofia's wisdom as primary earlier in process. Language in responses could be more genuinely collaborative rather than perfunctory ("What do you think?").

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia provides multiple instances of change talk (desire, ability, commitment), but clinician primarily reflects rather than strategically amplifies. Misses follow-up questions to deepen change talk: "What makes you feel that way? Tell me more about why that matters?" Doesn't strategically evoke reasons, ability, need, or commitment. When Sofia reports 7/10 confidence, clinician assesses but doesn't ask "What gives you that 7?" to amplify self-efficacy. Limited strategic deployment of evocation throughout session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some skill in managing sustain talk through reframing past failures as learning, normalizing barriers, and focusing on past success. However, inconsistent application with multiple missed opportunities. Doesn't use double-sided reflections to address fear of relapse. Minimal exploration of "I'm not sure" statements or overwhelm ("a lot to figure out"). Could strategically reframe overwhelm by highlighting what Sofia already knows. Limited exploration of how to interrupt stress-eating-inactivity cycle.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 3.7/5**

**Competency Level:** Adequate to Good MI Competence

**Key Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative opening and partnership stance
- Consistent affirmation and acceptance without judgment
- Excellent use of past success (yoga experience) to identify supporting principles
- Solid reflective listening with emotional nuance
- Concrete, manageable action planning with appropriate confidence assessment
- Warm, supportive therapeutic environment

**Primary Areas for Development:**
- Strategic evocation and amplification of change talk through follow-up questions
- Deeper exploration of Sofia's core values and intrinsic motivations
- More consistent use of double-sided reflections to address sustain talk and ambivalence
- Explicit linking of identified actions to values and past success
- Deeper exploration of self-efficacy and what strengthens confidence
- More direct strategic addressing of relapse fears

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
This session likely increases Sofia's engagement and readiness for the identified first step (looking up local groups). Sofia leaves with reasonable confidence (7/10) and a plan for support. However, the depth of intrinsic motivation for sustained change through obstacles is moderate rather than high. Sofia may successfully take the first step but may lack the deeper motivational foundation needed when stress hits or obstacles arise.

**Recommendations for Growth:**
1. Strategically amplify change talk through follow-up questions that deepen and strengthen Sofia's own motivation
2. Explore and evoke core values and deeper meanings behind behavioral goals
3. Use double-sided reflections to address sustain talk and ambivalence more strategically
4. Explicitly connect identified actions to values and past success
5. Deepen exploration of self-efficacy by asking what gives Sofia confidence and what would strengthen it
6. Address relapse fears more directly by exploring what's different this time and developing alternative strategies
7. Invite Sofia's perspective before offering interpretations to strengthen true partnership
8. Use evocative questions to help Sofia generate her own solutions
