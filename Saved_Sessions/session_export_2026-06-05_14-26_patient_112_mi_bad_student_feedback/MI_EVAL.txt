### Empathy

Two adequate but surface-level reflections appear ("It sounds like there's a lot tied up in your smoking"). One moment of exploratory direction appears ("I wonder, what parts of smoking feel most important to you right now?"), but quickly abandoned in favor of directive pressure. When Richard expresses resistance or offers perspective, it is overridden rather than explored collaboratively. One brief moment of collaborative language ("Let's explore that together") immediately undermined when clinician reverts to directive control. ## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.8/5**

### Strengths
- One genuinely curious, open-ended evocative question demonstrates awareness of MI principles
- Brief moment of acceptance and warmth ("I appreciate you sharing that with me")
- Two basic reflections demonstrate awareness that reflection is part of MI
- Recognition that behavior change is multifaceted ("not just about willpower")

### Critical Areas for Improvement
- **Fundamental MI Spirit Violations:** Expert, authoritarian stance prioritizes telling over asking, control over collaboration, external pressure over internal motivation
- **Absence of Acceptance and Empathy:** Unconditional positive regard absent; labeling of client statements as "excuses"; minimal empathic understanding
- **Missed Evocation Opportunities:** Change talk not amplified; relational motivation not explored; tells rather than asks
- **Directiveness and Pressure:** Direction imposed through repeated demands; client autonomy undermined; small steps dismissed as insufficient
- **Reinforcement of Resistance:** Arguing against sustain talk strengthens resistance; client explicitly states pressure makes him "dig heels in more"; clinician does not adjust course
- **Stage of Change Misalignment:** Client in pre-contemplation; premature action planning inappropriate; goal should be rapport-building and exploration, not commitment

### Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation
**Low.** This session is unlikely to evoke Richard's intrinsic motivation for change and likely to strengthen resistance and damage therapeutic alliance. Only relational motivation identified ("I don't want to lose her") is not explored or amplified. Approach relies on external pressure and health statistics, less effective for pre-contemplation clients. If continued, Richard likely to disengage from treatment.

### Most Critical Recommendations
1. Eliminate directive language; adopt genuine collaboration
2. Develop authentic empathy and unconditional positive regard
3. Evoke rather than impose motivation; amplify change talk
4. Respect stage of change; focus on exploration, not action planning
5. Adjust approach when encountering resistance; use client feedback to course-correct
6. Develop genuine curiosity about client's world and perspective
7. Explore and connect to relational motivation (marriage/wife)
8. Support autonomy; frame change as client's decision
9. Use strategic responses (not confrontation) to soften sustain talk
10. Recognize that current approach is counterproductive; commit to fundamental shift toward partnership, acceptance, and evocation

---

**Clinical Impression:**

The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal MI competency. While awareness exists that MI involves reflective listening and open-ended questions, execution is inconsistent and contradicted by directive pressure. The fundamental MI spirit—partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation—is not embodied. This session would likely harm rather than help Richard's motivation for change. With focused training on MI spirit, collaborative stance, and strategic evocation and reflection, genuine competency is achievable. The first critical step is recognizing that the current approach is counterproductive.
