## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.9/5 – Poor Adherence to MI Principles**

### Key Findings

This session demonstrates **poor adherence to Motivational Interviewing principles** and reflects a traditional expert-driven, prescriptive counseling approach rather than an MI approach. While the psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior and demonstrates basic politeness, the session fundamentally violates MI spirit.

### Critical Issues

1. **Prescriptive Stance Dominates:** Repeated use of "you should," "it would probably help," and "maybe you should try" tells Rafael what to do rather than asking what he thinks might work.

2. **Passive Client Engagement:** Rafael ends with "I'm not promising much, but I'll try"—indicating compliance rather than genuine commitment. This is a red flag for poor follow-through.

3. **Missed Evocation Opportunities:** Rafael's spontaneous change talk is not amplified or deepened. Psychiatrist installs motivation rather than evoking it.

4. **Sustain Talk Overridden:** Rather than skillfully exploring Rafael's doubts and barriers, psychiatrist responds with more prescriptions, potentially reinforcing resistance.

5. **No Genuine Collaboration:** Unilateral decision-making throughout. Rafael's ideas, preferences, and resources are minimized.

6. **Autonomy Undermined:** Controlling language and lack of choice affirmation create obligation rather than self-determination.

### Impact on Client

Rafael is likely to experience **decreased intrinsic motivation** and **increased ambivalence**. His low confidence (4/10) combined with hedged commitment suggests high risk of non-adherence. If he doesn't follow through, he may interpret this as personal failure rather than recognizing the plan wasn't right for him.

### Immediate Priorities for Improvement

1. **Replace prescriptive language with genuine curiosity** – Ask Rafael what he thinks before offering suggestions
2. **Strategically amplify change talk** – Deepen Rafael's own motivations when he offers them
3. **Explore sustain talk with validation** – Use double-sided reflections; don't override doubt with solutions
4. **Build genuine collaboration** – Ask Rafael for input before problem-solving
5. **Affirm existing strengths** – Recognize gym membership and willingness to engage as evidence of commitment

### Recommendation

The psychiatrist must fundamentally shift from an expert-driven, directive stance to a collaborative, evocative one. This requires intentional practice with open-ended questions, strategic amplification of change talk, and genuine curiosity about Rafael's perspective. With these changes, future sessions can transform from compliance-focused interventions into genuine MI conversations that evoke intrinsic motivation for sustainable behavior change.
