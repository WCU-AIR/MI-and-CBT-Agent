# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Strong validation of Jordan's exhaustion and discouragement without minimization. Affirms courage ("the fact that you're here and sharing this shows a lot of courage") and reframes survival efforts as resilience. Minor gap: could more explicitly normalize Jordan's hesitation about asking for help as a common, understandable response to past disappointment rather than a personal failing.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening with accurate capture of emotional content and underlying patterns. Strong complex reflection: "your resilience comes from a deep, fundamental desire to simply survive and keep yourself intact...shows a quiet strength." Accurately captures ambivalence and the push-pull dynamic. Limitation: occasionally blends reflection with subtle coaching ("shows your willingness to explore options") rather than maintaining pure empathic understanding. Could explore emotional experience behind low confidence (4/10) more deeply before moving to problem-solving.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on Jordan's presenting concerns (financial stress, employment instability, feeling stuck) but lacks strategic clarity about primary change goal. Focus broadens considerably across exhaustion, survival mode, dreams of school, and support systems without explicit prioritization. Part-time work emerges organically from Jordan's suggestion rather than from collaboratively set therapeutic direction. Session would benefit from early explicit question: "Of all we've discussed, what feels like the most important place to start?" and maintaining consistent focus on that target throughout.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consistent power-sharing. Opening question ("what's on your mind right now?") immediately establishes partnership. Positions Jordan as expert on his own experience through questions like "What do you think would need to happen...?" and "What would feel like the best way...?" Affirms Jordan's suggestions (part-time work) as his own thinking rather than clinician's prescription. Demonstrates genuine curiosity. Minor limitation: occasional subtle coaching ("sometimes just taking the first step helps build that") slightly shifts from pure collaboration toward gentle direction.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Jordan's right to choose and emphasizes his agency. Frames part-time work as "something that respects where you are right now." Avoids pressure when Jordan expresses low confidence; validates rather than pushes ("It's okay that confidence isn't higher yet"). Respects Jordan's pace and right to move cautiously. Minor opportunity: could more explicitly acknowledge Jordan's freedom *not* to change. For example, when asking "What would help you move from 4 to 5 or 6?" could first ask "Do you want to increase confidence, or would you prefer to try and see what happens?"

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation through open-ended questions ("What do you think keeps you going?" "What do you imagine life might look like?") but evocation is inconsistent and relatively shallow. When Jordan offers change talk—"Maybe I'd try to do something different, like go back to school"—clinician acknowledges but does not strategically amplify or deepen. Significant gap: minimal evocation of ability talk. Missing questions like "Tell me about a time you've overcome a setback—what did you do?" or "What strengths do you have that might help you succeed?" No strategic evocation of commitment language or exploration of exceptions to global negative beliefs.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership with active power-sharing and genuine incorporation of Jordan's contributions. Opening establishes Jordan's agenda as central. Clinician builds on Jordan's ideas rather than redirecting. Uses inclusive language and demonstrates respect for Jordan's expertise about what might work for him. Asks follow-up questions showing genuine interest in his thinking rather than moving quickly to solutions. Minor limitation: occasional interpretive statements ("That shows a lot of self-awareness and care for yourself") subtly position clinician as having insight into Jordan's motivations. Could replace with more collaborative language: "How do you see that?" or "What does that mean to you?"

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. While some change talk is evoked, it is not strategically amplified, reinforced, or deepened. Jordan offers multiple instances of change talk that go largely unaddressed: "I just wish something would go right," "Maybe I'd try to do something different, like go back to school," "Maybe if someone could help me with the applications...That might help a little," "Maybe it's worth trying if it helps me not feel so stuck." Each is acknowledged but not explored further. No strategic questioning to deepen reasons for change or build confidence. Absent: ability talk, commitment language exploration, and scaling questions to track movement. This substantially limits session's potential to build Jordan's intrinsic motivation for change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some skill in gently redirecting away from sustain talk through validation, but inconsistent and could be significantly strengthened. Jordan offers substantial sustain talk ("Every time I try, something screws up," "I can't just snap my fingers and fix all that," "Getting that kind of job feels like a dream," "I've tried job applications and it just feels like wasting time"). Psychiatrist's approach is primarily validation rather than strategic redirection. One effective double-sided reflection used: "It's understandable you're questioning whether more effort will make a difference...At the same time, the fact that you're here shows a lot of courage and desire for things to improve." Could more consistently use this technique. Missing: gentle challenging of global negative statements ("Is *everything* outside your control, or are there some things you do have some say in?") and exploration of exceptions to sustain talk.

---

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 3.6/5 (Adequate MI Competence)**

### Strengths:
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation
- Solid empathic reflections capturing emotional nuance and reframing survival as strength
- Excellent collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing
- Strong autonomy support respecting Jordan's pace and right to choose
- Warm, genuine therapeutic presence communicating respect and care
- Effective use of open-ended questions inviting Jordan's perspective

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- **Insufficient cultivation of change talk** — Change talk is evoked but not amplified or deepened; missed opportunities to explore Jordan's reasons, values, and vision
- **Absent ability talk** — No strategic questioning about strengths, past successes, or capacity to overcome obstacles; this limits confidence-building
- **Inconsistent softening of sustain talk** — Validation is strong but lacks strategic double-sided reflections and gentle reframing of global negative beliefs
- **Lack of strategic direction** — No explicit collaboration about primary change goal; focus drifts between employment, support, and broader life improvement
- **Limited commitment language** — No exploration of commitment level or concrete action planning with specific timelines and obstacle-handling

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change:
The psychiatrist creates psychological safety and genuine partnership, likely increasing Jordan's willingness to engage. However, impact on *intrinsic motivation for change* is modest. Jordan moves from "I can't get a break" to identifying one concrete step (looking up community programs), but this movement is not deeply rooted in his own articulated reasons or confidence. Confidence remains fragile at 4/10; commitment remains tentative ("I guess"). A more skillfully executed MI session would strategically amplify change talk, evoke ability talk, explore values, and use double-sided reflections to challenge sustain talk—resulting in higher confidence, clearer commitment, and stronger intrinsic motivation substantially increasing likelihood of follow-through.

### Key Recommendations (Priority Order):
1. Strategically amplify and deepen every instance of change talk with follow-up questions
2. Evoke ability talk through questions about past successes and strengths
3. Use double-sided reflections to validate sustain talk while reflecting change talk
4. Establish and maintain strategic focus on primary change goal
5. Move from "maybe" to concrete commitment and action planning
6. Explore values and vision to strengthen intrinsic motivation
7. Use scaling questions to track importance, readiness, and commitment
8. Explore exceptions to challenge global negative beliefs gently
9. Reduce subtle coaching language in favor of pure reflection
10. Connect emerging topics back to primary change goal

---

**Overall Assessment:** This session demonstrates adequate MI foundational competence with strong alliance-building skills. The psychiatrist creates safety and partnership essential for engaging a discouraged client. However, execution of core MI techniques for evoking and strengthening intrinsic motivation requires refinement. With focused attention to amplifying change talk, evoking ability, and strategically softening sustain talk, this clinician can move from adequate to excellent MI practice.
