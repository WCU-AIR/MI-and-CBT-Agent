### Direction

The pivot to asking about past successes, while valid, represents a subtle directional shift away from current barriers. Most significantly, the transition to expert-driven problem-solving ("Would it be okay if I shared some information?") represents a move away from client-centered direction toward clinician-directed solutions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates early collaborative stance through open-ended opening and permission-seeking language ("Would it be okay if I shared...?"). However, collaboration deteriorates when transitioning to expert mode. Clinician provides specific strategies (stretching, heat therapy, pacing) without first exploring what Mateo has already tried, what barriers exist, or what Mateo's own ideas might be. ### Autonomy Support

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates minimal strategic cultivation of change talk. While Mateo produces change talk ("I'm doing the rehab," "I'd like to have less pain," "I'm willing to try"), clinician does not consistently evoke, amplify, or reinforce it. When Mateo describes past motivation from daily walks, clinician does not explore how this could be rekindled. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates minimal skill in softening sustain talk. Mateo expresses considerable discouragement ("My progress is kind of slow," "nothing seems to change much," "I don't want to get my hopes up"), and while clinician validates these feelings, there is little strategic use of double-sided reflection, reframing, or amplification. Clinician does not use techniques like: "You're discouraged by the pace, and at the same time, you're still showing up and doing the rehab—that takes real commitment." When Mateo expresses protective skepticism, clinician does not explore or reframe it as wisdom. Opportunities to explore small changes, reframe pain as part of healing, and strengthen hope are missed.

## Summary of Clinical Evaluation

### Strengths:
- ✓ Strong collaborative opening establishing client-centered direction
- ✓ Accurate reflective listening capturing ambivalence and emotion
- ✓ Consistent validation of frustration without judgment
- ✓ Skillful exploration of past motivation and values (daily walks)
- ✓ Concrete planning with specific, manageable first steps
- ✓ Appropriate use of affirmation regarding Mateo's careful approach

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ Premature shift to expert-driven problem-solving without adequate evocation
- ✗ Insufficient amplification of change talk through follow-up questions
- ✗ Minimal softening of sustain talk using double-sided reflection or reframing
- ✗ Uneven autonomy support with subtle pressure toward action
- ✗ Inadequate validation of protective skepticism before moving to solutions
- ✗ Clinician-generated strategies rather than co-created solutions
- ✗ Limited exploration of importance, readiness, and commitment depth

### Clinical Impact:
This session demonstrates **adequate MI competence with significant gaps in core MI techniques**. While Mateo agrees to try new strategies (confidence 6/10), this agreement appears somewhat reactive to clinician suggestions rather than emerging from deep exploration of intrinsic motivation. The premature shift to expert mode may inadvertently reinforce passive stance. Without strategic amplification of change talk and softening of sustain talk, long-term engagement and sustained behavior change are uncertain. Session likely to result in short-term compliance but uncertain long-term follow-through.

### Recommended Focus Areas:
1. **Prioritize evocation over information-provision** – Ask client's ideas before offering suggestions
2. **Strategically amplify change talk** – Follow up with questions about importance, readiness, commitment
3. **Use double-sided reflection** – Acknowledge barriers while highlighting client effort and commitment
4. **Deepen exploration of values** – Connect past motivation to current change goals
5. **Strengthen autonomy support** – Remain neutral about direction; honor client's right to choose pace
6. **Validate protective skepticism** – Sit with and explore protective mechanisms before problem-solving
7. **Co-create solutions** – Involve client in generating strategies rather than offering expert advice
