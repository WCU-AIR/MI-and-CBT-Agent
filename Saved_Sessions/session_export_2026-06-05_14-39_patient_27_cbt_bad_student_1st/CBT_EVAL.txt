## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Quality Assessment:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure to deliver evidence-based CBT**. While the therapist displays warmth and apparent concern, the clinical approach is ineffective and potentially counterproductive for a patient with:
- Cannabis-induced psychotic disorder with active paranoid ideation
- Prior suicidal and homicidal threats
- Cannabis use disorder with withdrawal anxiety
- Bipolar I disorder with 4 hospitalizations in one year
- Sleep disruption and fear of psychotic recurrence

**Most Critical Issues:**

1. **Safety Not Addressed:** Patient has history of suicidal threats ("threatening to jump out of hotel window") and homicidal threats ("threatening harm to wife"). Patient explicitly states worry about hurting himself or wife "if things get out of control." No safety assessment conducted. No safety plan developed.

2. **Psychotic Symptoms Mismanaged:** Therapist treats paranoid delusions as ordinary worry amenable to positive thinking. This demonstrates fundamental misunderstanding of psychosis. Paranoid delusions require specific cognitive interventions (reality testing, thought records, behavioral experiments), not cheerleading.

3. **No CBT Delivered:** Zero CBT-specific techniques applied. No thought records, behavioral experiments, exposure, activity scheduling, relapse prevention, psychoeducation, or safety planning. Session is supportive counseling, not CBT.

4. **Patient Feedback Ignored:** Patient explicitly stated positive thinking feels "empty" and "almost impossible." Therapist did not explore this feedback or adjust approach. Instead, therapist continued offering same ineffective suggestion.

5. **Excessive Self-Disclosure:** Therapist repeatedly shared personal experiences ("When I was stressed..."). This violates professional boundaries, shifts focus from patient, and may be particularly problematic for paranoid patient.

**Recommendation:**

This therapist requires **intensive training in CBT fundamentals** before continuing to see patients with psychotic disorders or substance use disorders. Recommend: (1) CBT training workshop, (2) regular supervision from CBT-trained supervisor, (3) practice with simpler cases before returning to complex cases, (4) review of CBT manuals and protocols, (5) recording and reviewing sessions with supervisor feedback.

**Immediate Action Required:**

Therapist must conduct comprehensive safety assessment and develop written safety plan at next session with this patient. This is non-negotiable.
