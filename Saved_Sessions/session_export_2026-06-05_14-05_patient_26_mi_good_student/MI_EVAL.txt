### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate autonomy support with notable gaps representing significant opportunity for improvement. Opening question respects patient's right to set agenda; psychiatrist avoids direct prescription. However, critical gap: when patient expresses ambivalence ("I wouldn't say I'm ready to cut it out completely"), psychiatrist does not explicitly affirm his autonomy and right to make own choices. Explicit statements like "This is your decision to make" or "You're the expert on what will work for your life" are largely absent. Session lacks repeated emphasis on patient's fundamental right to choose and ownership of change process. Opportunity to explicitly affirm autonomy within supportive relationships: "You're making your own decision about what you want to work on—that's a good balance." This represents a significant gap in MI spirit and opportunity to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid cultivation of change talk through strategic questioning and reinforcement of patient's own language favoring change. Psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces pride in accomplishment ("It sounds like you've been doing a great job"), desire for control ("maintaining control without feeling deprived is a key part of what matters most to you"), and ability talk by asking about smoking cessation strategies. Confidence scaling strategically evokes ability talk; psychiatrist reinforces patient's 6-7 response: "That six or seven really reflects a solid foundation." Commitment talk reinforced when patient commits to talking with wife. Opportunities to cultivate more comprehensively: when patient expresses ambivalence, could ask "What would feel like a realistic and meaningful change for you?" to evoke patient's own articulation. Could explicitly ask about values and meaning: "What would it mean to you if you were able to follow through on this?" Could ask deeper questions about identity: "What kind of father and partner do you want to be?"

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid skill in softening sustain talk, though relatively little sustain talk emerges in this session, suggesting effective early management. When patient expresses mild sustain talk ("I don't think it's a huge problem" and "I wouldn't say I'm ready to cut it out completely"), psychiatrist handles skillfully using double-sided reflection that acknowledges both minimization and concern without arguing. Patient's desire for enjoyment is reframed as part of change goal rather than obstacle: "balancing act between enjoying social moments and staying true to your values." Psychiatrist avoids amplifying sustain talk by not asking questions that would elicit more reasons against change. Opportunities: could use more explicit reframing when patient says "I still want to have a good time occasionally"—reframe as "You're looking for a way to enjoy yourself socially while also being more in control—that's not about giving up fun, it's about finding a balance that works for you." Could explore "catch me off guard" comment more deeply to shift from sustain talk to change talk.

### Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 4.0/5 – Competent Motivational Interviewing**

#### **Key Strengths:**
- Strong collaborative stance with consistent invitation of patient input and co-construction of solutions
- Solid reflective listening with generally accurate reflections capturing patient perspective
- Effective, specific affirmations of accomplishments and self-awareness
- Strategic session progression from exploration through values clarification to concrete action planning
- Good evocation through open-ended questions drawing out patient's own motivations and strategies
- Effective handling of ambivalence by framing as meaningful tension rather than problem
- Solid cultivation of change talk with strategic reinforcement of patient's own language about change

#### **Primary Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Autonomy Support (Most Significant Gap):** Inconsistent emphasis on patient's fundamental right to choose and ownership of change process. Explicit statements affirming autonomy are largely absent. This represents notable gap in MI spirit.

2. **Empathic Depth:** Reflections occasionally remain at behavioral description level rather than consistently capturing emotional nuance and underlying motivations. Opportunity to reflect emotional experience underneath behavior.

3. **Direction Sharpness:** Could more explicitly explore whether proposed alternatives genuinely address alcohol's function. Follow-up accountability mechanisms are vague—no explicit plan for check-in timing or success metrics.

4. **Change Talk Comprehensiveness:** While existing change talk is reinforced, opportunities exist to more systematically evoke reasons, ability, and commitment through deeper questioning about values and meaning.

5. **Subtle Expert Positioning:** Several moments where psychiatrist edges toward expert stance through slightly leading questions or prescriptive language. More purely collaborative approaches would allow patient self-discovery.

#### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**
Session demonstrates solid MI practice likely to increase patient engagement and motivation for change. Patient expresses genuine commitment to action (talking with wife, setting reminders, planning strategies) and demonstrates increased confidence (8/10 about conversation). Collaborative stance and affirmation likely contribute to patient feeling heard and supported rather than judged. However, inconsistent autonomy support represents missed opportunity to deepen intrinsic motivation. Deeper exploration of underlying values and what patient wants his life to look like would strengthen motivation. Session likely to result in initial action steps, but depth of motivation for sustained change could be stronger with more explicit autonomy support and deeper values exploration.

#### **Recommendation Priority:**
1. Explicitly and repeatedly affirm autonomy and client ownership throughout sessions
2. Deepen empathic reflections to capture emotional nuance and underlying motivations
3. Establish clear, explicit follow-up accountability and success metrics
4. More comprehensively evoke reasons, ability, and commitment for change
5. Reduce subtle leading questions and allow more patient self-discovery
