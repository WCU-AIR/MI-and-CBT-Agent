--- DISORDER ---
Illness Anxiety Disorder (care-seeking type) with comorbid Panic Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Oscar Capek, a 43-year-old man, was brought to the emergency room by his wife due to fatigue and confusion, describing a “relapse” of chronic Lyme disease. He requested the ER contact his psychiatrist, who had treated him for over two decades. Mr. Capek’s psychiatric history included treatment for a panic attack in the past, which resolved with small doses of clonazepam.

Mr. Capek had a long-standing preoccupation with chronic Lyme disease, organizing his life to avoid perceived triggers, limiting work, and relying on his wife for support. He often brought literature on Lyme disease to psychiatric sessions and participated in a local support group. His symptoms worsened occasionally, typically in response to psychosocial stressors, such as an affair that occurred one year prior.

He generally felt physically well, occasionally experiencing mild fatigue, anxiety, or concentration difficulties, which he considered manageable. He preferred homeopathic and lifestyle approaches over psychotropic medications. Despite negative Lyme testing, he continued to insist on having the illness and became alarmed at minor lab abnormalities (slightly low hemoglobin), requesting additional investigations.

On examination, Mr. Capek was a healthy, well-developed male, anxious, speaking quietly with eyes closed, occasionally losing his train of thought, but able to provide a detailed history with support. Physical examination and standard laboratory results were largely unremarkable.

Discussion: Mr. Capek demonstrates persistent worry about having a serious illness despite minimal or mild physical symptoms and repeated reassurance, consistent with DSM-5 illness anxiety disorder (IAD), care-seeking type. His history of recurrent panic attacks meets criteria for comorbid panic disorder. While his preoccupation is distressing and disabling, it does not meet criteria for somatic delusions or psychosis. Collateral history over decades confirms the chronicity and maladaptive nature of his illness anxiety.
