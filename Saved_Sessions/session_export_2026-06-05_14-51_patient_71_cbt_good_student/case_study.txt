--- DISORDER ---
Other Specified Depressive Disorder (Depressive Episode with Insufficient Symptoms)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ka Fang, a 59-year-old widowed Hmong woman, was referred for psychiatric evaluation due to persistent fatigue, chronic back pain, and insomnia over the preceding 11 months. These complaints had been managed medically with clonazepam and hydrocodone, which improved her sleep and pain but not her daytime fatigue.

Ms. Fang immigrated from Thailand 10 years earlier, having spent almost two decades in a Thai refugee camp following the Vietnam War. She lives with her son and his family on a small farm near Sacramento. She reported no depressed mood, anhedonia, or suicidal thoughts, but expressed feelings of worthlessness and embarrassment over her perceived inactivity.

On mental status examination, she appeared alert but sad and constricted. Speech was slow and careful, and insight appeared limited. Cognitive testing revealed normal attention and concentration. She wore white strings on her wrists from recent Hmong shamanistic soul-calling ceremonies intended to reunite with distant relatives.

Discussion: Ms. Fang demonstrates somatic symptoms commonly associated with depressive presentations in Hmong culture, including fatigue and insomnia. She meets only three of the nine DSM-5 criteria for major depressive disorder; five are required for diagnosis. Cultural factors—including functional illiteracy, loss of her husband, geographic separation from family, and traditional shamanistic practices—may contribute to her presentation. Collateral information from her children could help clarify symptom impact. Given the insufficient symptom count and cultural considerations, her presentation is best categorized as an other specified depressive disorder (depressive episode with insufficient symptoms).
