## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Rating: 1.8/6 (Poor)**

**Key Findings:**

This session demonstrates fundamental gaps in clinical competence for treating patients with serious mental illness in forensic contexts. While the therapist displays basic interpersonal warmth and avoids hostility, these foundational skills are insufficient to compensate for critical clinical deficiencies.

**Most Critical Issues:**

1. **Psychotic Symptoms Mishandled:** Patient explicitly reports auditory hallucinations ("voices in my head") and persecutory delusions ("they're telling me that everyone's plotting"). Therapist applies standard cognitive restructuring designed for anxiety/depression, not psychosis. This represents a fundamental misunderstanding of the patient's presentation.

2. **Risk Assessment Absent:** Patient has documented history of violence (pushing stranger onto subway tracks), current psychotic symptoms, substance use disorders, and antisocial personality disorder. No risk assessment conducted; no safety plan developed. This is clinically negligent and potentially dangerous.

3. **Medication and Substance Use Not Addressed:** Patient has documented nonadherence to psychiatric medications and chronic cocaine/alcohol use. Neither is assessed or discussed. These directly impact symptom management and treatment engagement.

4. **No Case Conceptualization:** Therapist does not articulate understanding of how patient's multiple diagnoses interact or guide treatment. Interventions are ad hoc and disconnected.

5. **Minimal Collaboration:** Patient positioned as passive recipient of advice rather than active partner. Feedback not systematically elicited. Homework vague and unlikely to be completed.

**Immediate Actions Required:**

- Therapist must not continue treating patients with psychotic disorders without intensive training and supervision.
- Recommend completion of specialized training in: (1) CBT for psychosis, (2) Forensic risk assessment, (3) Case conceptualization for complex presentations.
- Assign experienced supervisor specializing in psychosis or forensic mental health.
- Recommend consultation with psychiatrist and multidisciplinary team for this patient.

**Strengths to Build Upon:**

- Non-judgmental stance and basic empathy provide foundation for rapport.
- Patient remained engaged, suggesting therapist did not alienate him.
- Therapist accurately identified presenting symptoms (feeling watched/judged).

**Overall Assessment:**

This session does not meet standards for evidence-based CBT. Immediate intervention and intensive training are required.
