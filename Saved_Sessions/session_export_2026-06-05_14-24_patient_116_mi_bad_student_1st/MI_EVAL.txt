## Summary Assessment

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **fundamental departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates a **confrontational, authoritarian approach that is contraindicated for behavior change and harmful to the therapeutic relationship.**

The psychiatrist violates virtually every core MI principle:
- **Acceptance:** Judgment, shame, and contempt permeate the interaction
- **Empathy:** No reflective listening or perspective-taking
- **Autonomy Support:** Systematic undermining through ultimatums and coercion
- **Evocation:** Entirely advice-giving with no effort to draw out client motivation
- **Partnership:** Explicit denial of client expertise and refusal to share power
- **Change Talk Cultivation:** Client motivation is ignored and extinguished
- **Sustain Talk Management:** Confrontational approach amplifies rather than softens resistance

### Impact on Client Engagement

The client's responses indicate **increasing defensiveness, entrenchment, and resistance:**
- "It makes me want to dig in my heels even more"
- "I'm scared of failing and letting everyone down"
- "Hearing only 'quit now or else' makes me shut down"

These statements demonstrate that the psychiatrist's coercive approach is triggering **psychological reactance**—the tendency to resist when autonomy is threatened. Research consistently shows this approach is **ineffective and often counterproductive.**

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Cease current approach immediately** – This method violates MI principles and is harmful to therapeutic alliance
2. **Enroll in formal MI training** – Comprehensive retraining required before continuing behavior change counseling
3. **Shift from confrontation to collaboration** – Replace all ultimatums with genuine questions and partnership
4. **Develop reflective listening skills** – Practice empathic responding and perspective-taking
5. **Amplify client change talk** – Actively listen for and reinforce client's own motivations and strengths
6. **Support autonomy** – Communicate that change must come from client's own motivation, not external pressure

### MI Competency Assessment

**Current Level:** Minimal MI competency. This is not "MI with gaps"—this is a fundamentally non-MI approach requiring comprehensive retraining before continued use with clients.
