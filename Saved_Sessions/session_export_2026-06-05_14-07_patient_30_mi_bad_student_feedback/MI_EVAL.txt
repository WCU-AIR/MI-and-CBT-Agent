## Overall Session Summary

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

### Critical Findings:

This session represents a **fundamental failure to implement Motivational Interviewing principles**. The psychiatrist demonstrates authoritarian, confrontational counseling directly contradictory to MI spirit and evidence-based behavior change practice.

**Pervasive Deficiencies:**
- Repeated accusations of "making excuses" despite clear evidence of David's motivation and honesty about barriers
- Coercive language ("must," "have to," "you really need to") creating pressure rather than support
- Failure to listen—asking "Why haven't you started yet?" 6+ times despite David providing clear answers each time
- Invalidation of legitimate occupational barriers; insisting David "must avoid" client dinners despite repeated explanation these are non-negotiable
- Suppression of change talk and triggering of defensive sustain talk
- Rigid repetition of identical ineffective directives without modification despite clear client feedback
- No evocation, collaboration, or genuine partnership

### Impact on Client Engagement:

This session is **likely to decrease David's intrinsic motivation for change** despite his initial high readiness. The confrontational approach triggers psychological reactance—David becomes defensive, emphasizes barriers, and shifts from commitment to conditional willingness. If David attempts to quit, he will likely do so with low confidence and high ambivalence, increasing relapse risk. The therapeutic relationship is damaged, reducing the psychiatrist's future influence.

### Immediate Recommendations:

1. **Eliminate confrontational language immediately** (accusations of "making excuses," "not being committed")
2. **Implement reflective listening as foundation**—reflect before directing
3. **Explore and validate barriers collaboratively** rather than dismissing them
4. **Seek formal MI training and supervision** (MINT-certified training, MITI assessment)
5. **Respond to client feedback by adjusting approach** rather than rigid repetition
6. **Develop genuine curiosity** about client's world, barriers, and motivations
7. **Build collaborative problem-solving** into every session
8. **Use autonomy-supportive language consistently** ("could," "might," emphasizing client choice)

**This session requires immediate intervention and retraining. Continued practice without modification will harm patient outcomes.**
