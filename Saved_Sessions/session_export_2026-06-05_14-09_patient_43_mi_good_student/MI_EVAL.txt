Positive examples include normalizing her pace of change ("It sounds like you're open to thinking about it but also cautious, which makes a lot of sense") and affirming her right to hesitation ("It's great that you recognize it's okay to feel hesitant"). ### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This represents a significant weakness. Psychiatrist asks about reasons for change but doesn't strategically evoke, amplify, or reinforce Elise's change talk once it emerges. When Elise offers change talk ("I guess I do want to be healthier...I think about how it might be easier to breathe or have more energy"), clinician reflects back without amplifying through follow-up questions. No strategic use of open-ended questions to evoke different types of change talk (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment). Scaling questions measure motivation but don't evoke it. When Elise identifies concrete step (talking to close friends), clinician doesn't strengthen commitment language. Critical gap in MI practice: change talk remains largely uncultivated and unstrengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates minimal skill in softening sustain talk. Significant sustain talk offered throughout ("You bond with other smokers"; "Instant friends"; "If we just sat around without smoking, it wouldn't feel the same"; "I don't want to lose those bonds"; "I don't want to seem like I'm judging them"). Clinician primarily validates these concerns without strategically redirecting toward change or exploring alternatives. When Elise expresses fear about social consequences, clinician validates worry but doesn't gently explore whether fear is realistic or whether friends might be supportive. No double-sided reflections acknowledging both sustain talk and change talk simultaneously. Sustain talk remains largely unchallenged, potentially reinforcing Elise's reasons for maintaining smoking.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall Score: 3.2/5**

**Summary:**

The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate foundational MI competence with solid reflective listening, consistent validation, and appropriate session structure. However, the session exhibits critical gaps in core MI competencies that significantly limit effectiveness. Most critically, the clinician fails to strategically evoke, amplify, and reinforce change talk—a fundamental MI skill. Sustain talk is validated but not strategically reframed or explored for alternatives. The clinician operates primarily from an expert stance rather than true collaboration, and autonomy support, while present, lacks explicit affirmation of Elise's right to choose.

**Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

While Elise remains engaged and cooperative, reflecting the psychiatrist's respectful stance, the session is unlikely to significantly shift her ambivalence or strengthen intrinsic motivation for change. Elise rates importance at 5-6 and confidence at 6—moderate levels indicating genuine ambivalence. The identified action step (talking to close friends) may be followed through due to clinician suggestion and Elise's cooperativeness rather than developed intrinsic motivation, increasing risk of abandonment if social pressure remains strong.

**Recommendations for Development:**

1. **Strategically evoke and amplify change talk** through follow-up questions deepening Elise's own reasons for change
2. **Soften sustain talk** through reframing and exploration of alternative ways to meet underlying needs
3. **Deepen exploration of intrinsic motivation and values** independent of smoking
4. **Strengthen true collaboration** by presenting interpretations as hypotheses and inviting client expertise
5. **Explicitly affirm autonomy** and client's right to choose
6. **Use scaling questions strategically** with follow-up open-ended questions evoking commitment
7. **Deepen emotional attunement** exploring affective dimensions of concerns
8. **Strengthen commitment language** when action planning
9. **Explore function of smoking** more deeply to identify alternative need-meeting strategies
10. **Tailor interventions to contemplation stage** by focusing on ambivalence exploration rather than rushing to action
