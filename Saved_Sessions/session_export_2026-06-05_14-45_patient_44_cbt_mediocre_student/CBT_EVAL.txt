## SUPERVISOR NOTES & RECOMMENDATIONS

### Strengths Demonstrated
- Warm, professional demeanor with appropriate boundaries
- Patient remained engaged and cooperative throughout
- Basic session structure (beginning, middle, end)
- Use of validating language
- No evidence of harmful or dismissive behavior

### Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Attention

**1. Agenda-Setting (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** Vague opening without collaborative identification of target problems
- **Impact:** Session lacked structure and explicit prioritization
- **Recommendation:** Practice explicit agenda-setting: "What would be most helpful to focus on today? Are there other concerns we should address?"

**2. Feedback Elicitation (Score: 1/6)**
- **Issue:** No systematic checking for understanding or patient satisfaction
- **Impact:** Therapist cannot assess whether interventions are perceived as relevant or helpful
- **Recommendation:** Establish feedback loops at three points: after understanding, after suggesting intervention, at session end

**3. Understanding of Gender-Related Concerns (Score: 3/6)**
- **Issue:** Patient explicitly linked anxiety to gender journey; therapist acknowledged but did not explore
- **Impact:** Missed opportunity to address core, identity-related drivers of anxiety
- **Recommendation:** Use guided discovery to explore: "Tell me more about how your gender journey connects to these worries. What specifically are you concerned people might think?"

**4. Collaboration (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** Therapist-directed recommendations; patient compliance rather than partnership
- **Impact:** Patient not engaged as active agent in treatment planning
- **Recommendation:** Shift language: "What do you think would be most helpful? Which approach feels most relevant to you?"

**5. Guided Discovery (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** Reliance on suggestion and advice rather than Socratic questioning
- **Impact:** Patient did not independently reach insights; therapist provided them
- **Recommendation:** Practice Socratic method: ask questions that help patient examine evidence, generate alternatives, and reach conclusions

**6. Case Conceptualization (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** No explicit formulation linking anxiety to gender transition and identity concerns
- **Impact:** Interventions are generic and unlikely to address underlying drivers
- **Recommendation:** Before next session, write one-paragraph formulation explicitly connecting anxiety to gender identity and identity-related beliefs about legitimacy

**7. Application of CBT Techniques (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** Techniques mentioned but not practiced, explained, or structured
- **Impact:** Interventions lack rigor and are unlikely to be effective
- **Recommendation:** Practice cognitive restructuring as discovery process; use thought records; design behavioral experiments

**8. Homework (Score: 2/6)**
- **Issue:** Vague assignments without specificity, rationale, or feasibility assessment
- **Impact:** Patient unlikely to complete homework consistently or effectively
- **Recommendation:** Provide specific instructions, clear rationale, and practice during session

### Supervision Plan

**Immediate (Next 2 Sessions):**
1. Bring session recordings for review
2. Practice agenda-setting and feedback elicitation with role-play
3. Develop explicit case formulation for this patient
4. Practice Socratic questioning with supervisor

**Short-Term (Next 4 Weeks):**
1. Implement guided discovery in all sessions
2. Establish collaborative agenda-setting as standard practice
3. Create structured homework assignments with clear rationale
4. Develop case conceptualizations for all active patients

**Ongoing:**
1. Weekly supervision with CBT-trained supervisor
2. Training in core CBT competencies (guided discovery, cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments)
3. Regular session recording review
4. Consultation on gender-affirming CBT principles

### Qualification as Evidence-Based CBT

**Current Status:** This session does NOT qualify as evidence-based CBT. While CBT-adjacent elements are present, they lack the systematic rigor, collaborative process, and technical skill required of competent CBT practice.

**Path to Competence:** With intensive supervision, training, and deliberate practice, this therapist can develop competence in evidence-based CBT. The foundational interpersonal skills are present; technical CBT skills must be developed.
