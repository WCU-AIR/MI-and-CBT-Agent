--- DISORDER ---
Adjustment Disorder with Mixed Anxiety and Depressed Mood

--- CASE STUDY ---

Gabriela Trentino, a 65-year-old widowed Italian American homemaker, was referred for psychiatric evaluation to "rule out depression" during her hospitalization for recurrent lung cancer. She had been admitted for shortness of breath and found to have a unilateral pleural effusion. Ms. Trentino had undergone several rounds of chemotherapy and was awaiting thoracentesis results to assess for pulmonary metastases.

Prior to this hospitalization, she had never experienced significant depression or anxiety and had been considered the family's "rock." She had no prior psychiatric treatment, medications, or substance use. Family history was notable for a father with alcohol use disorder.

During evaluation, she appeared well-groomed but visibly dysphoric, wringing her hands and tearful. She expressed worry about her thoracentesis and the possibility of disease progression, saying, "I want to be alive for my son's marriage this year." She reported poor sleep, impaired concentration, decreased appetite, and lack of interest in usual activities, but denied suicidal ideation. Cognitive function was intact, with a Montreal Cognitive Assessment score of 29/30. Vital signs and neurological exam were unremarkable aside from hypoxemia corrected with oxygen.

Collateral reports from nursing staff indicated frequent requests for information and reassurance. The family noted her increased emotional dependency and repetitive questioning.

Discussion: Ms. Trentino’s depressive and anxious symptoms developed in clear temporal relation to a major stressor (hospitalization for potentially life-threatening illness). Symptoms affect her daily functioning but do not yet meet DSM-5 criteria for major depressive disorder. There is no evidence of a primary anxiety disorder, psychosis, or delirium. Her presentation is consistent with adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood. Monitoring for symptom intensification and consideration of medical contributions (hypoxemia, medications, paraneoplastic or neurological complications) is warranted.
