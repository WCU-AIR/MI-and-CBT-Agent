## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL CONCERNS

### Critical Deficiencies

**1. Fundamental Misunderstanding of FNSD**
The therapist's entire approach is predicated on a cognitive model (maladaptive thoughts → negative emotions → symptoms) that does not fit Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder. Functional neurological symptoms are not caused by thinking the wrong way; they are neurobiologically real symptoms arising from complex interplay of psychological, neurological, and social factors. By focusing on "changing thoughts," the therapist implicitly communicates that the patient's problem is psychological weakness or distorted thinking—precisely the invalidation causing her distress.

**2. Failure to Address Core Invalidation**
Paulina's central concern is being disbelieved and having symptoms dismissed as "all in her head." The therapist never directly addresses this fear or validates the reality of her symptoms. Instead, the therapist proceeds with interventions that subtly reinforce the invalidation. A competent therapist would explicitly state: "Your symptoms are real. They're not something you're choosing or faking. The fact that they don't show up on an EEG doesn't make them less real."

**3. Absence of Psychoeducation**
The therapist never explains FNSD to the patient. Paulina does not understand her diagnosis, the neurobiology of functional symptoms, or why she is having episodes. Psychoeducation is a cornerstone of FNSD treatment and should have been a primary focus of early sessions.

**4. No Assessment of Underlying Stressors**
The patient recently moved for graduate school—a significant life stressor. The therapist never explores whether this is related to symptom onset or what other stressors or traumas might be contributing. For FNSD, understanding underlying psychological factors is essential.

**5. Superficial Cognitive Restructuring**
The cognitive work is poorly executed. The therapist tells the patient what to think rather than guiding her to discover alternative perspectives. When the patient indicates the reframe doesn't feel authentic, the therapist does not adjust but instead encourages continued practice of an inauthentic thought.

**6. Inadequate Homework and No Follow-Up**
Homework is vague, lacks specificity, and is not reviewed in subsequent sessions. This represents a significant missed opportunity for between-session progress and accountability.

**7. No Agenda or Treatment Planning**
The therapist never establishes clear treatment goals, priorities, or a coherent strategy for change. Sessions drift without clear direction.

**8. Minimal Feedback Elicitation**
The therapist does not systematically check whether the patient understands, agrees with, or finds the approach helpful. When the patient expresses doubt, this is not explored or addressed.

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMMEDIATE IMPLEMENTATION

1. **Develop accurate understanding of FNSD** as a real neurobiological condition, not a psychological disorder.
2. **Provide psychoeducation to the patient** explaining her diagnosis and validating the reality of her symptoms.
3. **Shift from cognitive restructuring to evidence-based behavioral interventions** (psychoeducation, behavioral activation, graded exposure).
4. **Directly address the patient's fear of invalidation** and repeatedly validate her symptoms.
5. **Establish a coherent treatment plan** with clear goals, strategy, and measurement of progress.
6. **Implement systematic feedback elicitation** throughout sessions.
7. **Assign specific, structured homework** with clear rationale and monitoring tools.
8. **Review prior homework** at the beginning of each session.
9. **Seek supervision and training on FNSD** from experienced clinicians.

## LIKELIHOOD OF THERAPEUTIC BENEFIT

**Current Approach:** In its current form, this treatment is unlikely to produce meaningful therapeutic change and carries risk of harm. The patient may experience temporary relief from the warm therapeutic relationship, but underlying symptoms and distress are unlikely to improve. The implicit message that symptoms are caused by thinking the wrong way may deepen her sense of invalidation and shame.

**With Recommended Changes:** With significant modifications to the therapeutic approach, there is potential for meaningful improvement in the patient's symptoms, functioning, and quality of life. However, without these changes, the current approach does not qualify as evidence-based treatment for FNSD.
