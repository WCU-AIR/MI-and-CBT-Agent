### Direction

**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use and potential change throughout session. Logical progression from exploration to change readiness to concrete steps. Collaborative approach to establishing direction. Opportunity for improvement: could more strategically introduce discrepancy by gently providing health guidelines information; could connect Evelyn's stated concerns (fall, son's worry) to drinking behavior more directly and earlier in session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with frequent invitations for Evelyn's input and affirmation of her expertise. Opening question establishes partnership. Uses inclusive language positioning Evelyn as equal contributor. Respects her stated preference for autonomy. Minor opportunity: could invite Evelyn's evaluation of ideas rather than subtly steering toward clinician-preferred approaches (e.g., "middle ground" framing). Could invite Evelyn to suggest assessment scales rather than imposing 0-10 framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout session. Consistently emphasizes Evelyn's right to choose and ownership of change process. Never uses prescriptive language; instead employs permissive, choice-oriented language ("could," "might," "open to"). Immediately validates her concern about pressure. Positions Evelyn as expert on her own life. No instances of pressure, prescription, or minimization of client choice. Outstanding demonstration of respecting client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic evocation with some open questions and affirmation of past successes, but inconsistent and relatively surface-level. Significant missed opportunities: doesn't deeply explore emotional connection to risks (fall, son's concern); doesn't ask about values or what matters most; doesn't strategically amplify change talk when it emerges. When Evelyn mentions scary fall or son's worry, psychiatrist reflects but doesn't evoke deeper meaning or personal significance. Limited exploration of intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership demonstrated through consistent invitations for Evelyn's input and affirmation of her expertise. Positions clinician as supporter rather than expert. Uses collaborative language and asks what Evelyn thinks throughout. Minor opportunities: could remain more neutral about specific approaches rather than subtly affirming particular direction; could more explicitly co-construct ongoing therapeutic relationship and discuss how decisions will be made together going forward.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic cultivation with some affirmation of Evelyn's change-favoring language, but inconsistent and relatively passive. Significant missed opportunities: doesn't ask follow-up questions to deepen or elaborate on change talk; lacks strategic questions designed to elicit specific types (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment); doesn't amplify change talk when it emerges. When Evelyn identifies practical steps or expresses openness to change, psychiatrist affirms but doesn't ask "tell me more" questions to strengthen her own reasoning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic softening through validation and gentle redirection without confrontation. Affirms her autonomy when she resists major change. Significant missed opportunities: doesn't introduce discrepancy by gently challenging minimization (e.g., "seven drinks a week isn't much"); doesn't use double-sided reflections acknowledging both sustain and change talk simultaneously; doesn't strategically explore what would increase motivation. Handling of sustain talk is present but relatively passive and could be more strategic.

## Overall Session Summary

**Overall Score: 3.7/5**

### Key Strengths:
- Exemplary autonomy support with consistent emphasis on Evelyn's choice and ownership
- Strong collaborative stance with frequent invitations for input
- Consistent acceptance and warmth with no judgment or labeling
- Solid reflective listening that accurately captures perspective
- Logical session structure with appropriate pacing
- Practical focus on concrete, manageable first steps

### Primary Areas for Development:
- **Insufficient evocation of intrinsic motivation:** Limited exploration of emotional connection to risks, values, and what matters most to Evelyn
- **Inconsistent cultivation of change talk:** Lacks strategic follow-up questions to deepen, elaborate, or amplify Evelyn's own language favoring change
- **Missed opportunities for discrepancy:** Doesn't gently challenge minimization of drinking with health guidelines or reality-testing questions
- **Limited use of double-sided reflections:** Doesn't explicitly acknowledge both sustain and change talk simultaneously
- **Surface-level emotional exploration:** When significant events mentioned (fall, son's worry), reflects but doesn't explore emotional weight or personal meaning
- **Passive handling of sustain talk:** While non-confrontational, lacks strategic effort to soften resistance through reframing or exploration

### Clinical Impact:
While Evelyn leaves with concrete first step (tracking drinks) and moderate confidence (7/10), her intrinsic motivation for change remains modest (4-5/10 importance). Session demonstrates competent foundational MI practice but falls short of strategic evocation and amplification of change talk that characterizes excellent MI. With recommended improvements—particularly deeper evocation of intrinsic motivation and strategic cultivation of change talk—clinician could advance from solid to excellent MI practice.
