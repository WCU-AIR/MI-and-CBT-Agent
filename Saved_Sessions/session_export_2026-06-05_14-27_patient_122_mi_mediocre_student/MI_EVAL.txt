### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Collaboration is almost entirely absent—the cornerstone deficiency of this session. Clinician explicitly positions self as leader ("I'll lead the way for now") and consistently assumes expert role making unilateral decisions about what Sofia should do. Prescriptive advice disguised as suggestions ("Maybe you should try to open up to someone," "You might want to think about ways to handle the anger"). When Sofia expresses skepticism about breathing exercises, clinician explains why they work rather than exploring her concerns collaboratively. Sofia's repeated signals of hesitation ("I don't know if I'm really into all that," "I'm not sure if I'm ready") are pushed past without genuine exploration. Unilateral goal-setting throughout. Power imbalance evident in language ("You might want to...," "It would probably help if..."). True collaboration would invite Sofia's ideas and respect her expertise about her own life. Sofia's input solicited superficially but not genuinely integrated into decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy support virtually absent. Clinician repeatedly pressures Sofia toward predetermined interventions while minimizing her agency and choice. Prescriptive framing ("You might want to start by talking to that friend...") frames specific actions as necessary rather than optional. Implicit judgment of Sofia's avoidance coping ("Avoiding might help short-term, but it probably makes things worse in the long run") communicates her choice is wrong. Pushes past explicit hesitation ("Maybe you should try to face it a little bit" contradicts Sofia's stated "It just feels safer not to"). Conditional support ("It's really important to take steps to feel better") creates pressure and removes genuine choice. Legitimate fears about counselor confidentiality dismissed rather than validated. Sofia's autonomy treated as obstacle to overcome rather than fundamental right to respect. Clinician's predetermined agenda drives session, not Sofia's readiness or preferences.

### Evocation

Psychiatrist predominantly tells Sofia what to do rather than asking questions that help her discover her own reasons for change. When Sofia mentions her friend ("Maybe if my real friends actually stuck around, it'd be different"), clinician misses opportunity to explore this rich material revealing Sofia's deep desire for connection. When Sofia expresses ambivalence (rates importance as "5"), clinician prescribes solutions rather than exploring with curiosity. Sofia's change talk ("Yeah, maybe talking to my friend first is okay") acknowledged but not amplified or deepened. Session lacks values exploration—never asks what matters to Sofia, what kind of person she wants to be, or what "better" looks like. Predominantly tells rather than asks. One genuine moment of evocation occurs when Sofia spontaneously generates her own idea (texting friend), but clinician doesn't deepen by exploring confidence, plan, or hopes for reconnection. Multiple missed opportunities to help Sofia discover her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Partnership—belief that wisdom about change resides mostly within client—is fundamentally absent. Clinician operates from traditional medical model where clinician is expert and Sofia is passive recipient of advice. Explicitly claims leadership and direction-setting authority ("I'll lead the way for now"). Unilateral problem definition and solution generation without inviting Sofia to define problem or generate solutions. When Sofia says breathing exercises sound "dumb," clinician explains why they work rather than exploring her assessment—communicates Sofia's self-knowledge is incorrect and clinician's is correct. Prescriptive language throughout ("You might want to...," "It would probably help if...," "You should try..."). No genuine power-sharing; Sofia's contributions solicited but not integrated into decision-making. Focus on what Sofia needs to change rather than understanding her perspective and co-constructing solutions. Treats Sofia as the problem rather than as expert on her own life. Violates MI's collaborative spirit and reduces Sofia's sense of agency and ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled poorly. Rather than skillfully redirecting or reframing, clinician either ignores sustain talk or directly confronts it, which can strengthen resistance. Sofia's hopelessness ("I don't see how talking about it is gonna help much") not explored or reframed. When Sofia explains avoidance ("I skip school once in a while"), clinician directly challenges ("Do you think avoiding school is a good way to handle it?")—confrontational approach can entrench resistance. When Sofia dismisses breathing exercises as "dumb," clinician explains why they work rather than exploring skepticism—can entrench her resistance. When Sofia expresses hopelessness ("It feels like it won't change anything anyway"), clinician doesn't explore or use double-sided reflection. When Sofia expresses fear about counselor confidentiality, clinician doesn't validate concern or explore it. When Sofia says "Avoiding stuff sucks, but sometimes it feels like the only way to stop the drama," clinician directly confronts logic ("Avoiding makes things worse in the long run") rather than collaborative exploration. Missing: double-sided reflections acknowledging both desire to avoid pain and desire for connection; reframing that honors underlying values; strategic non-response; curiosity rather than confrontation. Sustain talk confrontation can strengthen Sofia's resistance and entrench ambivalence.

## Overall Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.6/5 – BELOW AVERAGE**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor motivational interviewing practice** with fundamental deficiencies across nearly all domains. The psychiatrist operates from a traditional medical/expert model rather than an MI collaborative model. Core problems include: expert-driven stance, prescriptive advice-giving, minimal empathy, sustain talk confrontation, and missed change talk amplification. While basic supportive counseling skills are present (warmth, focus, concrete planning), the session lacks strategic evocation, collaboration, and autonomy support that define MI.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Collaboration and autonomy support nearly absent (scores of 1/5)
- Partnership model fundamentally absent (score of 1/5)
- Acceptance and empathy superficial (scores of 2/5)
- Change talk evocation and sustain talk management poorly executed (scores of 2/5)

**Impact on Client Motivation:** This session is likely to **decrease Sofia's intrinsic motivation** rather than increase it. Sofia enters with ambivalence and skepticism; the prescriptive, expert-driven approach risks triggering reactance and reinforcing her distrust of authority. While Sofia leaves with a concrete plan (text her friend), this is fragile motivation that could easily collapse without deeper exploration of her values and genuine collaboration.

**Recommendations for Improvement:**
1. **Shift from expert-driven to collaborative stance** (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
2. **Develop deeper empathic reflections** (HIGH PRIORITY)
3. **Evoke rather than prescribe** (HIGH PRIORITY)
4. **Amplify and reinforce change talk** (HIGH PRIORITY)
5. **Validate Sofia's concerns and explore with curiosity** (HIGH PRIORITY)
6. **Use double-sided reflections to address ambivalence** (MEDIUM PRIORITY)
7. **Soften sustain talk through strategic redirection** (MEDIUM PRIORITY)
8. **Respect autonomy and emphasize Sofia's choice** (MEDIUM PRIORITY)
9. **Explore Sofia's values and vision for change** (MEDIUM PRIORITY)
10. **Establish true collaboration from the start** (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

**To become an effective MI practitioner, this clinician must fundamentally reconceptualize their role from expert/authority to collaborative partner, and from advice-giver to evocative questioner.** This requires significant practice and likely supervision or training in MI-specific techniques.
