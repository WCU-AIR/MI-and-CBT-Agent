
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## CRITICAL PRELIMINARY NOTE

**This evaluation reveals a fundamentally flawed therapeutic approach for the patient's clinical presentation.** Mark Hill presents with **Bipolar I Disorder, manic episode, severe, with mood-congruent psychotic features**—a psychiatric emergency requiring medical stabilization and pharmacological intervention as the primary treatment modality. The therapist's approach of applying standard CBT cognitive restructuring techniques to psychotic delusions is **clinically inappropriate and potentially harmful**. This session demonstrates a critical misunderstanding of when CBT is indicated and when it is contraindicated.

**Key Clinical Concern:** Psychotic symptoms (grandiose delusions with religious content, racing thoughts, pressured speech, decreased need for sleep, psychomotor agitation) require antipsychotic medication and psychiatric stabilization *before* cognitive work. Attempting to "reality-test" delusions during acute mania with psychosis can:
- Reinforce the patient's sense that his beliefs are being taken seriously as negotiable
- Delay necessary psychiatric intervention
- Create false hope that cognitive techniques alone will manage psychotic symptoms
- Undermine medication compliance by suggesting thoughts can be managed through willpower

### 1. Agenda
### **Score: 3/6**

**Explanation:**

The therapist did establish a collaborative agenda at the session's outset, asking Mark what he wanted to focus on and inviting him to identify priorities. This represents basic agenda-setting. However, the agenda is fundamentally **misaligned with the clinical presentation and diagnostic requirements**. 

The therapist framed the session as: "focus on understanding these thoughts and how they influence your feelings and behaviors, and see what we might do to manage them better." This agenda treats Mark's grandiose delusions and racing thoughts as *cognitive distortions amenable to CBT restructuring*—a critical clinical error. The agenda should have included: (1) assessment of current medication status and compliance, (2) psychiatric stabilization as the primary goal, (3) safety planning given recent police involvement and impulsive behavior, (4) psychoeducation about bipolar disorder and the role of medication, and (5) referral to psychiatry for medication management.

The therapist did not establish time boundaries, did not prioritize among competing needs (psychotic symptoms vs. thought patterns), and did not address the acute nature of Mark's presentation (recent hospitalization, current psychotic features, impulsive door-knocking behavior leading to police involvement). A proper agenda would have acknowledged: "Mark, I want to help you, and I'm noticing you're experiencing some very intense thoughts and feelings right now. Before we work on managing thoughts, I want to make sure you're getting the right medical support. Have you seen a psychiatrist recently? Are you on any medications?"

**Summary:**
Basic agenda-setting was attempted, but the agenda itself is clinically inappropriate for acute mania with psychosis. The therapist failed to prioritize psychiatric stabilization and medication management over cognitive restructuring.
