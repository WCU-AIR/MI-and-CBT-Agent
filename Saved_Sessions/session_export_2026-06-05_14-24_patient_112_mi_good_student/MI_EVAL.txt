# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard throughout, consistently validating Richard's autonomy and reframing resistance as strength ("That speaks to your autonomy and strength"). Explicit affirmation of his experience is present ("It's completely understandable to feel that way"). However, acceptance could be deepened by more explicit normalization of his internal conflict between independence and marital harmony, and fuller affirmation of his personhood beyond the smoking behavior context.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening demonstrated with accurate capture of both behavioral and emotional dimensions ("It sounds like smoking has been a long-standing part of your daily routine, something familiar and comforting"). Clinician accurately reflects core tension between marital harmony and independence. However, empathy remains somewhat surface-level; deeper reflections could capture emotional nuance beneath statements (e.g., the fear and vulnerability when Richard says "I don't want to lose her"). Clinician misses opportunities to reflect frustration and anger underlying defensiveness.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some focus on smoking behavior and readiness to change is maintained, with appropriate use of the readiness ruler. However, direction is inconsistent and somewhat diffuse. After establishing Richard is at a "2," clinician pivots to exploring past successes rather than deepening exploration of current ambivalence or clarifying what would shift his readiness. Movement toward action planning appears premature given Richard's low readiness and external motivation. Strategic progression toward clarifying change goals is lacking.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance demonstrated throughout most of session. Opening question explicitly establishes partnership ("Before we get started, I want to make sure this time is really about what matters most to you"). Clinician consistently seeks Richard's perspective and co-constructs solutions rather than prescribing them. However, collaboration subtly weakens as session progresses toward action planning; repeated affirmations of Richard's proposed step contain gentle directive elements that could undermine full partnership and may reflect clinician's preference for action rather than true neutrality.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist consistently affirms Richard's right to choose and emphasizes his ownership of change process ("You're expressing a strong sense of personal agency"). Explicit validation of his autonomy when expressing resistance is present throughout. Clinician respects Richard's stated readiness level without persuasion. However, opportunity missed to explicitly affirm Richard's right to *not* follow through with proposed step or to change his mind without judgment. True autonomy support would include permission to abandon the small step without consequences.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questioning present (e.g., "What would need to be different for you to feel ready to explore any changes?"). Readiness ruler is used effectively. However, evocation becomes significantly weaker after Richard identifies family as motivator. Critical change talk ("I don't want to lose her") is reflected but not strategically amplified or deepened. Missed opportunities to explore intrinsic motivation beyond relational concerns. Follow-up questions could have drawn out Richard's own observations about health rather than relying solely on wife's concerns.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance with consistent positioning of Richard as expert on his own life ("You've really pinpointed some key factors that would support you"). Clinician avoids expert role in discussing change strategies, instead inviting Richard's input. Opening establishes genuine power-sharing. However, partnership subtly shifts as session progresses; repeated affirmations of Richard's small step could be experienced as clinician assuming quasi-expert role in validating his choices. True partnership would involve more curiosity about whether proposed step is genuinely what Richard wants or accommodation of perceived expectations.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness identified. While Richard offers multiple instances of change talk (desire to not lose wife, family motivation, ability to cut back), psychiatrist fails to strategically evoke, amplify, or reinforce these statements. Responses are validating but not strategically designed to strengthen motivation. When Richard says "I don't want to lose her," clinician reflects factually rather than deepening emotional exploration. Missed opportunity to strategically use readiness ruler to explore what would shift his readiness ("What would move you from a 2 to a 4?"). Change talk is present but underutilized.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist handles some resistance well with non-confrontational warmth and reframing ("Feeling pushed can understandably make the idea of change feel like a loss of that control"). However, multiple strategic opportunities missed. When Richard minimizes smoking concerns, clinician doesn't explore this or gently introduce other perspective. Double-sided reflections acknowledging both resistance and underlying concerns are insufficient. Clinician doesn't strategically reframe Richard's need for control as potentially compatible with choosing to cut back. Responses are validating but not strategically designed to minimize sustain talk or amplify the other side.

---

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 3.4/5**

**Competency Level:** Basic to Intermediate

---

### Key Strengths:
- ✓ Consistent acceptance and unconditional positive regard
- ✓ Solid reflective listening with accurate empathic reflections
- ✓ Strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing
- ✓ Explicit autonomy support throughout session
- ✓ Non-confrontational handling of resistance
- ✓ Warm, authentic, and respectful tone

---

### Critical Areas for Improvement:
- ✗ **Weak change talk cultivation** – Fails to strategically amplify or reinforce Richard's change talk, particularly around fear of losing wife
- ✗ **Insufficient strategic use of readiness ruler** – Does not explore what would shift Richard's readiness after establishing he is at "2"
- ✗ **Premature action planning** – Moves toward small steps without establishing genuine readiness or authentic motivation
- ✗ **Limited exploration of ambivalence** – Insufficient depth in exploring core tension between independence and marital harmony
- ✗ **Missed emotional depth** – Reflections remain factual rather than capturing vulnerability and fear beneath resistance
- ✗ **Inadequate double-sided reflections** – Insufficient use of reflections acknowledging both resistance and underlying concerns
- ✗ **Limited intrinsic motivation exploration** – Relies heavily on relational motivation without exploring client's own health values

---

### Clinical Impact:

Richard leaves the session having agreed to cut back during reading time, but this agreement appears based more on accommodation to gentle clinician guidance and desire to reduce marital conflict than on genuine personal motivation. **Likelihood of follow-through: Moderate to Low.** Without strategic cultivation of intrinsic motivation, Richard is vulnerable to abandoning the small step once he leaves the session, particularly if his wife "backs off" as he requested.

The psychiatrist has built an excellent foundation of trust and rapport but has not sufficiently shifted Richard's internal motivation toward change. The session demonstrates solid relational MI skills but insufficient strategic technique application to achieve the core MI objective of evoking and strengthening intrinsic motivation for change.

---

### Priority Recommendations:

1. **Strategically amplify change talk** through follow-up questions and reflections rather than simple validation
2. **Use readiness ruler strategically** to explore what would shift readiness and evoke client thinking
3. **Deepen exploration of ambivalence** before moving toward action planning
4. **Explore intrinsic motivation** beyond external pressure and relational concerns
5. **Use double-sided reflections** to acknowledge both resistance and underlying concerns
6. **Establish genuine readiness** before action planning through explicit authenticity checks
