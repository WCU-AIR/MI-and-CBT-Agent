### **CRITICAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT**

#### **1. Fundamental Reorientation to MI Spirit and Principles**
- Immediately discontinue confrontational, expert-driven approach
- Engage in intensive training focused on four pillars of MI: partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation
- Replace opening statement from "I'm here to tell you what you need to do" to "I'd like to understand what's most important to you right now and how I can be helpful"

#### **2. Develop Genuine Empathic Listening and Reflective Responses**
- Practice reflective listening as primary response to client statements
- Reflect back both content and emotion before offering guidance
- Never dismiss or invalidate client's concerns
- Record sessions and review specifically for reflective listening; aim for ratio of at least 2 reflections per 1 question

#### **3. Establish Collaborative Agenda-Setting**
- Begin every session by asking what client wants to talk about and what matters most
- Collaboratively establish session agenda rather than imposing it
- Explore discrepancies between client priorities and clinical concerns collaboratively
- Document client-identified priorities and reflect on how this shapes session dynamics

#### **4. Eliminate Confrontational Language and Threats**
- Remove from clinical vocabulary: "You're making excuses," "You're avoiding the issue," "You're not listening," "You don't care," "You have to," "This isn't optional," and all threats
- Replace with collaborative, curious language
- Before next session, write 10 confrontational statements commonly used and develop collaborative alternatives
- Have supervisor listen to session and provide feedback specifically on confrontational language

#### **5. Develop Autonomy-Supportive Language and Stance**
- Consistently communicate that client has choice and control over decisions and change process
- Use language emphasizing client ownership: "It's your choice," "You get to decide," "What do you think would work for you?" "What feels right to you?"
- In next 5 sessions, count autonomy-supportive vs. controlling language; aim for 80% autonomy-supportive
- Record session and review with supervisor focusing on consistent communication of respect for client choice

#### **6. Explore and Build on Client Values and Strengths**
- Actively seek to understand what matters most to client and what strengths they possess
- Build clinical work around values and strengths rather than focusing primarily on problems and deficits
- When client expresses values (family, successful release), respond with: "Family is really important to you, and you want to be successful when you get out. Those are real strengths. Tell me more about what that looks like for you."
- In next session, identify at least 3 client values or strengths and explicitly build clinical work around these

#### **7. Develop Skill in Evoking Change Talk**
- Learn to ask open-ended questions designed to evoke client's own reasons for change, confidence in ability to change, and commitment to change
- Replace telling with asking
- Study DARN-CAT framework for change talk (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Commitment, Taking steps, Activation, Talking)
- In next 5 sessions, specifically ask questions designed to evoke each type of change talk
- Record yourself and count how many times you evoke vs. impose change talk

#### **8. Learn to Skillfully Handle Resistance and Sustain Talk**
- When client expresses sustain talk, respond with curiosity and reflection rather than confrontation
- Use techniques: double-sided reflections, reframing, strategic redirection
- Study MI techniques for handling resistance
- Practice in role-play with supervisor
- Record session and identify instances where you confronted sustain talk; rewrite those responses using MI techniques

#### **9. Conduct Thorough Assessment of Client Priorities and Readiness**
- Before deciding what health behaviors to address, assess: (1) what client identifies as primary concerns, (2) what client is ready to change, (3) what client values and cares about, (4) what client sees as relevant to their goals
- Use assessment to collaboratively identify appropriate clinical targets
- Spend first 15-20 minutes of session conducting open-ended assessment of client priorities and readiness
- Do not introduce clinical agenda until thoroughly understanding client's perspective
- Document what you learned and how it shaped clinical approach

#### **10. Engage in Intensive Supervision and Training Focused on MI Competence**
- Seek formal MI training through MINT (Motivational Interviewing Network of Trainers)
- Engage in regular clinical supervision with MI-trained supervisor
- Enroll in 2-day MI training workshop
- Engage in weekly supervision with recorded sessions reviewed for MI adherence and spirit
- Use MITI (Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity) scale to assess MI competence
- Set specific goals for improvement in each MI domain

The confrontational, controlling approach demonstrated in this session is contraindicated for MI practice and particularly harmful when working with adolescents, who are sensitive to perceived threats to autonomy.
