## Overall Clinical Summary

**Session Score: 1.1/5**

**MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI / Fundamental Misalignment with MI Principles

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **fundamental violation of Motivational Interviewing principles** across all nine dimensions. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, authoritarian, directive approach that is explicitly contraindicated in MI and demonstrably counterproductive.

**Core Deficiencies:**
- Systematic invalidation of client's lived experience and perspective
- Explicit rejection of partnership and collaboration
- Suppression rather than cultivation of change talk
- Coercive pressure through threat, shame, and control
- Absence of empathic listening and genuine curiosity
- Imposition of external motivation rather than evocation of intrinsic motivation

### Impact on Client Engagement

Despite entering the session with genuine openness ("I'm open to doing more," "I'm willing to try"), Camila demonstrates increasing assertion of her own perspective and gentle but firm resistance to directives. This is a predictable psychological response to autonomy violation and invalidation, not evidence of client resistance to change.

**Likelihood of sustained behavior change:** Very low. Research demonstrates that confrontational, directive approaches produce inferior long-term outcomes compared to collaborative, evocative approaches. This session may result in short-term compliance but is unlikely to produce sustained behavior change and may damage therapeutic relationship and client trust in healthcare providers.

### Recommendations for Improvement

1. **Fundamental reorientation to MI spirit:** Shift from directive advice-giving to collaborative exploration of client's own motivations
2. **Comprehensive MI training:** Attend formal MI workshop and engage in ongoing clinical supervision with MI-certified trainer
3. **Development of empathic listening:** Practice reflective listening that demonstrates accurate understanding and captures ambivalence
4. **Recognition of client strengths:** Explicitly affirm existing accomplishments and build upon them
5. **Replacement of directives with open-ended questions:** Ask rather than tell; evoke rather than impose
6. **Support for autonomy:** Replace coercive language with genuine support for client choice and self-determination
7. **Clinical supervision:** Use MITI (Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity) scale for ongoing assessment and feedback

**Recommendation:** This psychiatrist requires comprehensive MI retraining and ongoing supervision before continuing MI-based interventions. Current approach is misaligned with MI principles and potentially harmful to client outcomes.
