Repeated demands for commitment ("Are you going to commit to taking your inhaler daily now?" turn 2; "Are you willing to commit to taking your medication every day?" turn 3; "Will you commit to following the medication plan strictly?" turn 5) are not invitations to choose but demands for compliance framed as questions. ### Partnership

Language consistently positions expertise with clinician: "you really need to take your asthma seriously," "you must understand," "you can't just," "you have to be disciplined," "you have to decide now." These statements communicate "I know what you need to do, and you need to do what I say"—opposite of partnership. When Luis offers expertise about his situation ("I don't think you really get my situation," "I'm managing okay so far," "I do what I can"), psychiatrist dismisses this rather than treating it as valuable knowledge. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates virtually no skill in softening sustain talk and instead actively reinforces it. Luis offers sustain talk throughout: "it's just a pain," "I don't see what's so urgent," "I'm tired of all the fuss," "Oscar hasn't made me die yet," "I don't want to be locked into this routine where I'm a walking pharmacy," "I'm honestly tired of keeping track of it all," "I'm not gonna promise you something I can't realistically stick to." When Luis expresses sustain talk, psychiatrist's response is to argue against it, escalate fear, and demand commitment. When Luis says "Feels like too much to handle," psychiatrist responds "you really need to take your asthma seriously"—direct confrontation, not softening. When Luis says "Oscar hasn't made me die yet," psychiatrist escalates: "you're risking serious attacks or even hospitalization"—fear-escalation, not softening. When Luis expresses overwhelm, psychiatrist demands discipline rather than acknowledging legitimate barrier. Skilled approach would use double-sided reflections acknowledging both sides without argument. Psychiatrist's approach actively reinforces rather than softens Luis's resistance. One genuine reflection (turn 9) acknowledges concern but is not developed. Argument against sustain talk is fundamental MI error that strengthens defensiveness.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.9/5**

**Current MI Competency Level:** Below-average with significant gaps in fundamental MI principles.

**Key Strengths:**
- Opening statement establishes collaborative intent and invites Luis's perspective
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (asthma management) throughout session
- Mid-session shift (turns 9-11) demonstrates awareness that directive approach is not working
- Final exchanges (turns 13-15) show genuine collaborative stance where Luis responds with engagement
- Demonstrates capacity for accurate reflection (turn 9)

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Fundamental MI spirit violation:** Pervasive controlling language ("you must," "you have to," "this isn't optional") directly contradicts MI principles of autonomy support and partnership
- **Argument against sustain talk:** Rather than softening resistance, psychiatrist argues against it, escalates fear, and demands commitment—all strengthen defensiveness
- **Dismissal of client input:** When Luis identifies concrete strategies, psychiatrist rejects them as "not enough" rather than building on them
- **Inconsistent approach:** Oscillation between directive and collaborative creates confusion about whether psychiatrist genuinely accepts and respects Luis
- **Minimal evocation:** Imposes reasons for change rather than drawing out Luis's own motivations, values, and resources
- **Failure to amplify change talk:** Undermines rather than amplifies client-identified steps

**Impact on Client Engagement:**
The psychiatrist's approach actively undermines Luis's motivation for change. Early exchanges trigger defensiveness and sustain talk through controlling language and fear-based messaging. Mid-session shift demonstrates that collaborative approach is possible and effective. Final exchanges show Luis responds positively to genuine collaboration ("Yeah, that sounds better," "I'm willing to try a plan that doesn't feel like it's taking over my whole life"). However, this represents only final two exchanges after extensive damage to therapeutic relationship. Luis explicitly states "I don't think you really get my situation," indicating psychiatrist has failed to establish trust necessary for MI effectiveness.

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation for Change:** **Low.** Current approach likely decreases intrinsic motivation and increases defensiveness. While final exchanges show improvement, overall session demonstrates psychiatrist does not yet embody MI spirit and is at risk of harming therapeutic relationship and Luis's willingness to engage in treatment.

**Recommendation:** Focused training and practice on eliminating controlling language, developing genuine empathy for client's overwhelm, stopping argument against sustain talk, amplifying change talk, and maintaining consistent collaborative stance throughout sessions. With deliberate practice on these specific competencies, psychiatrist can develop genuine MI effectiveness.
