### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Despite client's spontaneous change talk (desire for change, ability statements, reasons for change, commitment language), psychiatrist fails to cultivate, amplify, or reinforce any of it. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled through direct confrontation, shaming, and repetitive demands rather than skillful reframing. Responds to "I'm not ready to quit cold turkey" with "No, you can't just pick and choose." Uses shame ("you're lazy," "you're irresponsible," "you're selfish") rather than exploring legitimacy of concerns. Repeats demand ("Are you ready to quit completely today or not?") multiple times without strategic redirection. Invalidates client's time constraints ("everyone has busy lives"). Creates adversarial dynamic where client must defend current behavior. Approach increases defensiveness and reinforces sustain talk rather than softening it.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.2/5**

**Summary:** This session represents a **critical failure of motivational interviewing practice** across virtually every dimension. The psychiatrist operates from an expert-driven, coercive model fundamentally inconsistent with MI principles. Rather than evoking client motivation, the psychiatrist imposes demands and uses shame and guilt to attempt coercion.

**Key Failures:**
- Judgmental language and moral criticism communicating disapproval of client as person
- Dismissal of legitimate barriers as character flaws
- Coercive pressure using shame and guilt
- Systematic rejection of client autonomy
- Failure to evoke or amplify change talk
- Confrontational handling of sustain talk increasing defensiveness
- Complete absence of genuine collaboration or partnership

**Impact on Client Engagement:** This session is likely to **decrease** Aisha's intrinsic motivation for change and increase defensiveness. Client entered with emerging motivation (concern about cancer, desire to be healthy for son); she is leaving more entrenched in position and less likely to engage with future health recommendations. Therapeutic relationship damaged by violation of client's explicit boundary about not wanting to be lectured.

**Critical Recommendations:**
1. Fundamental shift from expert-driven to collaborative partnership
2. Elimination of judgmental, shaming, and coercive language
3. Development of genuine empathic listening and reflective responding
4. Recognition and amplification of client change talk
5. Support for client autonomy and respect for client expertise
6. Formal MI training and ongoing supervision required before continuing health behavior change conversations

**Current Competency Level:** Minimal to no MI competency. Approach is in direct opposition to MI principles and likely counterproductive to behavior change outcomes.
