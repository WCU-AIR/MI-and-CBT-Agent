# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete absence of unconditional positive regard. Psychiatrist demonstrates explicit judgment and disapproval throughout, using shaming language ("You're just making excuses," "Stop feeling sorry for yourself"). No affirmation of patient strengths or warmth. Repeated dismissals ("No, that's not acceptable") and contemptuous tone violate the foundation of MI. Patient's autonomy and perspective are consistently rejected rather than respected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening present. Psychiatrist actively negates patient's lived experience ("It's not about relaxing or friends; it's about your health") rather than seeking to understand it. No exploration of why patient drinks, social context, or ambivalence. When patient clarifies he doesn't smoke (twice), psychiatrist ignores correction and repeats same opening, suggesting inattention to actual concerns. Fundamental failure of empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear focus on reducing alcohol consumption is maintained, and specific targets are identified (three beers or less). However, direction is pursued through coercion and repetition rather than strategic collaboration. No exploration of patient's own goals or values. Goals are imposed unilaterally rather than co-created. Method of maintaining direction is fundamentally non-MI and represents directive counseling rather than collaborative goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Explicitly hierarchical, expert-driven stance throughout. Psychiatrist opens with "I'll be telling you what you need to do," establishing unilateral authority. Repeated "Yes or no?" demands eliminate collaborative exploration. Patient's attempts to share perspective ("I need to handle this on my own terms") are consistently dismissed or overridden. No genuine joint problem-solving or co-construction of solutions. Psychiatrist operates as sole expert; patient positioned as non-compliant.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy actively undermined at every turn through commands ("You have to stop immediately"), prescriptive language ("You need to quit now"), and moral framing ("You have to be disciplined"). Binary "Yes or no?" demands eliminate nuanced choice. When patient states need to "handle this on my own terms," psychiatrist responds "No, that's not acceptable"—direct rejection of autonomy. Patient's right to deliberate and choose is explicitly denied throughout.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No genuine evocation occurs. Psychiatrist imposes reasons for change ("Drinking is ruining your health," "You're going to end up with serious health problems") rather than drawing out patient's own motivations. Patient offers emerging change talk ("Now that I know about the health risk, I have something I gotta think about"), but psychiatrist responds with "No, you can't just think about it"—actively suppressing emerging motivation. No exploration of patient's values, concerns, or self-efficacy.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Complete rejection of partnership model. Psychiatrist explicitly assumes total expert authority ("I'll be telling you what you need to do") and actively suppresses patient input. Patient's contributions systematically rejected: autonomy needs met with "No, that's not acceptable"; social context negated with "It's not about relaxing or friends." No genuine power-sharing. Patient's perspective treated as invalid rather than valuable. Antithesis of MI partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Minimal change talk emerges, and what does is never amplified or reinforced. When patient expresses "I have something I gotta think about," psychiatrist responds with "No, you can't just think about it"—actively suppressing emerging change talk. No reflective listening to strengthen change talk. No open questions to deepen motivation. Confrontational approach actually suppresses change talk; patient becomes increasingly defensive and entrenched in sustain talk by session end.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist directly confronts sustain talk, which strengthens it. When patient minimizes ("I don't consider my drinking binge drinking"), psychiatrist argues directly ("You definitely drink too much"). Uses shaming ("You're making excuses") and catastrophizing ("Your liver and your life") to counter sustain talk. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic non-response. Confrontational approach creates predictable reactance: patient becomes more defensive and entrenched. Sustain talk increases throughout session, demonstrating iatrogenic effect of confrontation.

## Overall Assessment

**Overall Session Score: 1.0/5**

**MI Spirit Embodiment:** ☐ Present | ☒ Absent

**Likelihood of Evoking Client Motivation:** ☐ High | ☐ Moderate | ☒ Low/Counterproductive

**Summary:**

This session represents a **fundamental failure of motivational interviewing** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist violates core MI principles through explicit confrontation, authoritarian stance, shaming language, and active suppression of client autonomy and emerging motivation. The approach is antithetical to MI and demonstrates active harm to client engagement.

**Evidence of Counterproductive Impact:**
- Session opening: Patient ambivalent but cooperative
- Mid-session: Patient defensive ("That sounds extreme")
- End-session: Patient entrenched in resistance ("I'm not ready," "I need time to think")

Patient's motivation has **decreased** rather than increased. Confrontational approach created reactance effect, strengthening resistance and sustain talk. Research demonstrates this approach predicts worse outcomes and damages therapeutic alliance.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
1. No MI spirit present (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation absent)
2. Explicit judgment and shaming throughout
3. Autonomy actively undermined
4. No genuine reflective listening or empathy
5. Goals imposed rather than co-created
6. Emerging change talk suppressed rather than amplified
7. Sustain talk confronted rather than skillfully softened

**Recommendation:** Immediate retraining in MI fundamentals required. Psychiatrist demonstrates no competence in MI and operates from beliefs fundamentally incompatible with MI principles. Supervision and formal MI training essential before continued practice with this modality.
