## SUPERVISOR RECOMMENDATIONS

### **URGENT (Implement Immediately)**

1. **Conduct Comprehensive Suicide Risk Assessment**
   - Use structured tool (e.g., Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale adapted for children)
   - Assess: frequency, intensity, intent, plan, means, protective factors, past attempts
   - Develop written safety plan with patient and family
   - Establish crisis protocols and resources

2. **Eliminate Therapist Self-Disclosure**
   - Stop sharing personal anecdotes about childhood, past experiences, or possessions
   - Redirect focus to patient's experience: "Let's focus on your experience. What was that like for you?"
   - Record sessions and review for self-disclosure; aim for zero personal anecdotes in next 5 sessions

3. **Establish Clear, Collaborative Agenda and Treatment Plan**
   - At start of each session, explicitly set agenda with patient input
   - Prioritize problems collaboratively
   - Explain treatment plan in concrete, age-appropriate language
   - Use visual supports (written agenda, treatment plan handout)

### **HIGH PRIORITY (Implement Within 2-4 Sessions)**

4. **Learn and Apply Core CBT Techniques**
   - Cognitive restructuring with visual supports (Thought Detective worksheets)
   - Behavioral experiments to test negative beliefs
   - Thought records adapted for ASD (concrete, visual)
   - Behavioral activation and exposure planning
   - Practice with supervisor before implementing with patient

5. **Assign Specific, Concrete Homework Every Session**
   - Make homework specific, achievable, and concrete
   - Explain rationale clearly
   - Check understanding before session ends
   - Review homework at start of next session
   - Examples: thought records, behavioral experiments, prediction logs, sensory accommodation planning

6. **Adapt All Interventions for ASD**
   - Use concrete language; avoid metaphors and abstractions
   - Provide visual supports (worksheets, diagrams, written instructions)
   - Explain concepts explicitly and literally
   - Recognize special interests as core to identity and coping, not mere hobbies
   - Coordinate with school for sensory accommodations (not just "think positive")

### **ONGOING (Every Session)**

7. **Elicit and Respond to Feedback**
   - Ask: "Did that make sense?" "How does that land with you?" "What was most helpful today?"
   - Encourage expression of confusion or disagreement
   - Adjust approach based on patient feedback
   - Summarize session content and ask for takeaways

8. **Focus on Key Cognitions and Behaviors**
   - Identify central automatic thoughts: "Everyone hates me," "I'm weird," "I can't handle this"
   - Target behavioral patterns: avoidance, rumination, social withdrawal
   - Use guided discovery to help patient examine evidence and develop alternative thoughts
   - Connect cognitions to emotions and behaviors

9. **Maintain Appropriate Therapeutic Focus**
   - Keep focus on patient's experience, not therapist's
   - Use empathic reflection: "That sounds really painful" rather than "I know how you feel"
   - Validate patient's unique struggles
   - Avoid cheerleading and platitudes; use genuine empathy
