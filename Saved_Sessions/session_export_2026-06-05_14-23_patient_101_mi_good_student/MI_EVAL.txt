# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard and genuine warmth throughout the session. Opening establishes welcoming tone and affirms client autonomy. Consistently validates Maya's experiences without judgment, particularly when she discloses drinking. Skillfully affirms her strengths and coping abilities (e.g., "That's impressive that you recognized what helped you feel less stressed"). Minor opportunity: could more explicitly normalize ambivalence about change as a natural part of the change process rather than moving directly to scaling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate early reflections capturing emotional experience. Uses complex reflections that go beyond surface content to capture underlying meaning and ambivalence (e.g., "That balance between needing a break and staying on track seems tricky"). Shows nuanced understanding of social dimension of drinking behavior. However, misses opportunities to deepen empathy by exploring hesitations more fully before problem-solving. When Maya expresses doubt about non-drinking socializing being fun, clinician does not explicitly explore this significant barrier.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains some focus on presenting concerns but direction becomes increasingly diffuse as session progresses. Opening is appropriately open-ended, allowing Maya to set direction. However, conversation gradually shifts away from alcohol use concern (the referral reason) toward strengths-building and academic planning. Critical clinical questions about drinking frequency, quantity, and consequences remain unaddressed. Session ends with focus on task list and accountability support—positive but represents narrowing of direction away from dual focus on both school stress AND alcohol use patterns.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaborative stance with consistent invitations for Maya's input and perspective. Opening explicitly positions Maya as expert on her own priorities. Uses language that invites problem-solving partnership ("What do you think makes it hard to find that balance?"). Collaboratively explores past success rather than explaining why strategy worked. Accepts Maya's stated priority (school stress) over referral concern (alcohol use), respecting her autonomy. Minor opportunity: could more transparently address tension between referral concerns and stated priorities while maintaining partnership through explicit dialogue.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Maya's right to choose and emphasizes her agency throughout session. Opening explicitly frames session as Maya's choice about content and direction. When Maya expresses ambivalence about readiness, psychiatrist responds without pressure, validating her right to be uncertain. Uses language emphasizing her choice ("what fits for you right now without pressure"). Reinforces her autonomy to set pace of change. Minor opportunity: could more fully explore her doubts about change possibilities (e.g., whether non-drinking socializing could be fun) while maintaining autonomy support rather than implicitly suggesting she move forward despite doubts.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates inconsistent evocation with some skillful moments but significant gaps. Most effective evocation occurs when asking about past success ("Can you tell me about a time when you managed stress...?"), which successfully draws out Maya's resources and capabilities. Builds on this with follow-up questions about motivation. However, misses critical opportunities to evoke Maya's own perspective on alcohol use—does not ask about concerns, reasons for change, or impact on goals. When Maya discloses drinking, clinician reflects and normalizes without exploratory questioning. Does not evoke what might move her from 5 to higher importance of change. Fails to systematically elicit change talk specifically about drinking behavior.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership with consistent power-sharing and genuine incorporation of Maya's ideas into conversation. Opening explicitly invites Maya to shape agenda. When Maya identifies school stress rather than alcohol use, psychiatrist accepts this direction without redirecting, respecting her autonomy and true partnership. Uses inclusive language and affirms Maya's own thinking rather than imposing clinician-generated plans. Incorporates her suggestion about accountability support. Minor opportunity: could more transparently address tension between referral concerns and stated priorities by explicitly discussing how they connect while maintaining collaborative stance.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates minimal and inconsistent cultivation of change talk, particularly regarding alcohol use—a significant deficiency in MI practice. When Maya discloses drinking, psychiatrist does not ask questions designed to elicit change talk about concerns, reasons, ability, or need for change. Does not explore how drinking affects her goals or what concerns she has. Successfully cultivates change talk regarding academics ("I felt less stressed and kinda proud"; "Knowing the project was really important"). However, fails to explicitly connect this academic change talk to drinking concern. When Maya states she's at 5 on importance of change, clinician does not use this as opportunity to evoke change talk through questions like "What makes it a 5 and not a 3?" Systematic evocation of Maya's own reasons for change regarding alcohol is absent.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate but inconsistent skill in responding to sustain talk. When Maya expresses ambivalence about readiness, psychiatrist skillfully uses double-sided reflection and normalizes ambivalence without confrontation ("That makes a lot of sense given how complex this feels"). Does not argue or confront when Maya states easier to keep things as they are. However, misses opportunities to more actively explore specific barriers. When Maya expresses doubt about whether non-drinking socializing could be fun, clinician does not address this significant barrier statement. Does not explore whether belief about peer pressure ("I'd feel left out") is actually true or whether alternative ways to maintain connection exist. Sustain talk softening present but could be more proactive and targeted.

---

## Overall Session Summary

### Strengths
- Strong opening and collaborative stance establishing genuine partnership
- Consistent acceptance and warmth with unconditional positive regard
- Skillful exploration of past success with effective evocation of academic coping strategy
- Solid reflective listening capturing emotional nuance and ambivalence
- Consistent autonomy support affirming Maya's right to choose and pace of change
- Effective softening of ambivalence without pressure or confrontation

### Areas for Improvement
- **Insufficient focus on alcohol use concern:** Session gradually loses focus on referral concern; dual attention to both school stress and drinking not maintained
- **Minimal evocation of change talk regarding drinking:** Critical opportunities to ask about Maya's perspective on alcohol use, concerns, and reasons for change are missed
- **Lack of assessment of drinking severity:** Frequency, quantity, consequences, and escalation of drinking remain unaddressed
- **Missed opportunity to bridge past success to current concern:** Does not explicitly connect successful academic coping strategy to drinking behavior
- **Insufficient exploration of barriers:** Doubt about non-drinking socializing and fear of social exclusion not explored with curiosity
- **Weak cultivation of change talk overall:** Systematic evocation of Maya's own reasons, desire, ability, and need for change regarding alcohol is minimal

---

### **Overall Session Score: 3.6/5**

**Clinical Recommendation:** This session demonstrates solid foundational MI skills in partnership, acceptance, and empathy with a warm, collaborative relational foundation. However, it falls short of true MI competence due to insufficient evocation of change talk regarding alcohol use, loss of focus on the referral concern, and missed opportunities to explore barriers. The psychiatrist's strong relational skills create engagement but do not translate into systematic evocation of intrinsic motivation for change regarding drinking. To improve, the clinician must develop more targeted questioning to draw out Maya's own perspective on alcohol use, maintain explicit dual focus on both school stress and drinking, and strategically use past success as a bridge to change.
