--- DISORDER ---
Intermittent Explosive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Lucas Sandahl, a 32-year-old landscape architect, presented to a psychiatrist with his wife for help managing anger. His wife described him as "almost impossible to live with," noting increasing frequency and intensity of outbursts and fear of potential violence.

The most recent incident occurred when Lucas returned home to find dinner not prepared. He verbally lashed out at his wife, cursed, and broke glassware and a chair. His wife fled with their two young children.

Lucas reported that temper outbursts began in childhood but became problematic around age 13, including fights with classmates. In recent years, he experienced approximately four verbal outbursts per week and physically aggressive episodes about every two months, including throwing objects or damaging property. He denied recent fights but acknowledged near-conflicts with neighbors and strangers. Between outbursts, he felt "fine."

He described periods of brief depressed mood and anxiety that resolved spontaneously. He drank socially and had experimented with drugs in the past but not recently.

Family history revealed significant anger issues: his father was emotionally abusive and perfectionistic, and his older sister had repeated divorces linked to temper problems.

On examination, Lucas was casually dressed, cooperative, coherent, contrite toward his wife, cognitively intact, and demonstrated good insight and judgment. He denied depression, psychosis, suicidal or homicidal ideation.

Discussion: Lucas displays recurrent, disproportionate, and impulsive verbal and physical outbursts causing significant distress and impairment. Symptoms are not better explained by another psychiatric or medical disorder. He meets DSM-5 criteria for intermittent explosive disorder (IED). Associated features include hostile attribution bias, early onset, chronic course, and potential personality disorder traits. His behavior also includes intimate partner abuse, emphasizing the need for intervention.
