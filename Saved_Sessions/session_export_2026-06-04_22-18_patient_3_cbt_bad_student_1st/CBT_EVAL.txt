## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL CONCERNS

### Critical Safety Issue
**No safety assessment or safety planning occurred.** For a 16-year-old in early recovery from a high-lethality suicide attempt (requiring intubation), this is a mandatory clinical element—not optional. Therapist did not ask about current suicidal ideation, warning signs, coping strategies, or crisis resources. This represents a serious clinical failure.

### Core CBT Deficiencies
- ☐ No agenda-setting or session structure
- ☐ No guided discovery or Socratic questioning
- ☐ No CBT technique application
- ☐ No homework assignment
- ☐ Minimal focus on key cognitions (rumination, hopelessness) or behaviors (withdrawal, avoidance)
- ☐ No coherent change strategy or case conceptualization
- ☐ Excessive therapist self-disclosure (shifted focus from patient's experience)
- ☐ Minimal feedback-seeking
- ☐ Poor time allocation (generic wellness vs. CBT work)

### Strengths (Limited)
- ✓ Therapist was warm and not hostile
- ✓ Basic rapport established
- ✓ Therapist was responsive to patient's statements
- ✓ Attempted to normalize some experiences
- ✓ Encouraged effort and persistence

### Impact on Patient Care
Hannah leaves session without:
- Clear understanding of treatment goals or plan
- Structured CBT interventions targeting rumination, hopelessness, or behavioral withdrawal
- Safety plan or crisis resources
- Between-session homework or practice
- Explicit assessment of current suicide risk

**Risk Level:** HIGH. Patient lacks tools to manage future acute stressors or return of suicidal ideation.

---

## IMMEDIATE RECOMMENDATIONS

### Priority 1: Implement Safety Assessment and Safety Planning
- Conduct systematic safety assessment at every session
- Develop written safety plan identifying warning signs, coping strategies, crisis contacts, and resources
- Practice asking directly about suicidal ideation without hesitation

### Priority 2: Establish Collaborative Agenda-Setting
- Begin each session by asking what Hannah wants to work on
- Collaboratively identify 1-3 priorities and allocate time
- Check in at end about whether session addressed her goals

### Priority 3: Replace Therapist Self-Disclosure with Guided Discovery
- Eliminate personal anecdotes as primary intervention
- Use Socratic questions to help Hannah discover her own insights
- Practice ratio of 1 personal statement per 3 questions

### Priority 4: Identify and Focus on Key Cognitions
- Systematically explore rumination about boyfriend and hopeless thinking
- Use thought records to identify automatic thoughts and core beliefs
- Practice cognitive restructuring with supervisor

### Priority 5: Apply Behavioral Activation
- Create structured activity schedule to counteract withdrawal
- Start small and build incrementally
- Track mood before and after activities

### Priority 6: Teach Sleep-Focused CBT Techniques
- Implement sleep hygiene, stimulus control, worry time scheduling
- Teach cognitive techniques for racing thoughts
- Track sleep patterns and identify interventions

### Priority 7: Assign Structured Homework
- End every session with 1-2 specific assignments with clear rationale
- Explain why each assignment matters
- Plan to review homework at next session

### Priority 8: Develop Coherent Treatment Conceptualization
- Create written case formulation linking symptoms to interventions
- Share formulation with Hannah in clear, jargon-free language
- Ensure strategy is coherent and patient understands it

## COMPETENCY ASSESSMENT

**Current Level:** Basic interpersonal skills present; CBT competence inadequate

**Critical Gap:** Therapist appears to confuse supportive counseling with cognitive-behavioral therapy. This is a fundamental distinction: supportive counseling builds rapport but is insufficient for treating adjustment disorder and suicidal behavior disorder.

**Recommendation:** Therapist should not continue treating Hannah without immediate supervision, training, and skill development in CBT. Consider:
1. Intensive supervision with CBT specialist
2. Referral to therapist with demonstrated CBT competence
3. Formal CBT training/certification program
4. Deliberate practice with recorded sessions and feedback

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Change (Current Approach):** LOW. Hannah may feel supported but will not develop specific cognitive and behavioral skills needed for sustained recovery. Risk of relapse or recurrence of suicidal behavior is elevated.
