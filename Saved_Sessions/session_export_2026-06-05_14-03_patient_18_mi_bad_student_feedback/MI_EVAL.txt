## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.3/5**

**Fundamental Assessment:** This session represents a severe violation of Motivational Interviewing principles and spirit. The psychiatrist operates from a confrontational, expert-driven model characterized by judgment, shame, pressure, and directives—an approach research demonstrates *decreases* intrinsic motivation and *increases* resistance to change.

**Critical Findings:**

- **Shame as Primary Tool:** Despite patient's explicit statement of existing shame ("I feel gross and ashamed enough already"), clinician intensifies shame through repeated judgmental language, directly contradicting evidence-based practice.

- **Counterproductive Outcome:** Patient enters session with genuine motivation ("I've been wanting to quit for a long, long time now") and exits more defensive and less confident ("I'm not ready to fully commit today"). Session likely *decreased* rather than increased motivation for change.

- **Ignored Client Feedback:** Patient provides explicit feedback that approach is harmful ("all these demands and ultimatums, it just makes me feel more stressed and less sure"). Clinician does not recognize or adjust.

- **Absence of Core MI Skills:** No reflective listening, no evocation of patient's own reasons for change, no exploration of ambivalence, no genuine partnership.

**Competency Level:** Psychiatrist is **not practicing Motivational Interviewing**. While occasional statements suggest awareness that collaboration is important, this awareness is not translated into consistent behavior. Gap between stated collaborative intent and actual confrontational practice is stark.

**Likelihood of Evoking Motivation for Change:** Very low. Session actively undermines patient's intrinsic motivation and increases defensiveness.

**Recommendation:** Comprehensive retraining in MI principles and spirit required. Basic workshop attendance insufficient; ongoing supervision, role-play practice, and genuine reflection on clinician's beliefs about behavior change and client capacity for self-direction essential. Without retraining, clinician will continue to harm clients by intensifying shame, increasing resistance, and decreasing intrinsic motivation for change.
