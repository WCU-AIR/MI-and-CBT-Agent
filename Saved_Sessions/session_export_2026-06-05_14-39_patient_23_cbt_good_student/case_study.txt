--- DISORDER ---
Borderline Personality Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Juanita Delgado, a 33-year-old single unemployed Hispanic woman, sought therapy for depressed mood, chronic suicidal thoughts, social isolation, and poor personal hygiene. She had spent the prior 6 months largely isolated in her apartment, lying in bed, eating junk food, watching television, and engaging in excessive online shopping beyond her means. Previous treatments had little effect.

Ms. Delgado was the middle of three children in an upper-middle-class immigrant family in which her father valued professional achievement above all else. She experienced social isolation throughout school and recurrent periods of depressed mood. She had angry outbursts within her family. She performed well academically in high school but dropped out of college due to conflicts with a roommate and a professor.

She attempted internships and entry-level jobs but repeatedly quit, citing that “bosses are idiots” and left her feeling both inadequate and angry. She had dated men in her younger years but avoided intimacy due to anxiety. She maintained distant connections with friends from college but felt disconnected from most neighborhood peers.

Ms. Delgado reported dozens of 1- to 2-day episodes of elevated energy, edginess, and all-nighters followed by “crashes” with extended sleep. She occasionally engaged in superficial self-harm and had persistent thoughts that she would be better off dead. She had been hospitalized three times after overdoses. Psychotherapy and multiple medication trials—including mood stabilizers, antidepressants, and low-dose antipsychotics—had not been effective.

Mental status examination: casually groomed, somewhat unkempt, cooperative, coherent, goal-directed. Affect was generally dysphoric and constricted but smiled appropriately several times. She expressed shame over poor performance and simultaneous belief in her potential for greatness. Toward the end of the session, she became angry when the interviewer glanced at the clock.

Discussion: Ms. Delgado demonstrates affective instability, anger dysregulation, unstable interpersonal relationships, identity disturbance, self-mutilation, feelings of emptiness, and transient stress-related paranoia. She meets criteria for DSM-5 borderline personality disorder. She also presents with depressive symptoms (persistent depressed mood, diminished interests, anergia, overeating, chronic suicidal ideation) meeting criteria for major depressive disorder. Her brief “manic” episodes do not meet DSM-5 criteria for bipolar disorder. The instability and affective storms of BPD can mimic mania, potentially leading to underdiagnosis.

Diagnosis: Borderline Personality Disorder
