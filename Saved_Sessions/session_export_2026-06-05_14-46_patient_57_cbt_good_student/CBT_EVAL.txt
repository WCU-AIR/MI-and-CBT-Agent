### OVERALL EVALUATION

| **Total Score (Sum of Items 1–11)** | **40/66** | **CRITICAL CLINICAL CONCERN:** This session demonstrates competent application of basic CBT techniques within a well-structured, empathic therapeutic relationship. The therapist demonstrated genuine warmth, skillful guided discovery, appropriate pacing, and collaborative engagement. However, these strengths are fundamentally undermined by a critical failure in diagnostic formulation and treatment planning. **The therapist applied standard CBT to a patient with major neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease without acknowledging, assessing, or adapting for the patient's documented cognitive deficits and progressive neurological decline.** This represents a serious clinical error that violates the principle of matching treatment to diagnosis and pathology. The patient's primary symptoms—apathy, withdrawal, cognitive decline, and functional deterioration—are direct neurological manifestations of Alzheimer's disease, not secondary to depression or distorted thinking. Cognitive restructuring cannot reverse neurological decline. Behavioral activation cannot overcome neurological apathy. The therapist's well-executed CBT techniques are being applied to the wrong problem. **Immediate actions required:** (1) Reassess diagnosis and treatment appropriateness; (2) Assess cognitive capacity for CBT; (3) Involve wife as co-therapist; (4) Provide written materials and memory supports; (5) Establish realistic goals acknowledging disease progression; (6) Consider alternative approaches (behavioral activation adapted for cognitive limitations, family-based interventions, supportive psychotherapy, coordination with neurology). **Likelihood of therapeutic benefit:** Low to moderate. While therapist's interpersonal skills and CBT technique application are competent, the fundamental mismatch between diagnosis and treatment significantly reduces likelihood of meaningful therapeutic benefit. Substantial risk of homework failure, disappointment, and loss of trust when patient's cognitive decline continues despite therapy. **This session does not qualify as evidence-based treatment for major neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease.** |

## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL REVIEW

**Strengths Demonstrated:**
- Excellent interpersonal effectiveness and rapport-building
- Skillful use of Socratic questioning and guided discovery
- Clear session structure and appropriate pacing
- Collaborative engagement and patient-centered language
- Competent execution of standard CBT techniques
- Specific, achievable behavioral goals

**Critical Deficiencies Requiring Immediate Attention:**

1. **Fundamental Diagnostic Mismatch (MOST CRITICAL):** Standard CBT applied to patient with progressive neurological disease without acknowledgment of cognitive deficits or disease progression.

2. **Failure to Assess Cognitive Capacity:** No evaluation of whether patient can engage in metacognitive work required for CBT; no adaptation of techniques for documented memory, attention, and executive function deficits.

3. **Misattribution of Causation:** Treated neurological apathy and withdrawal as secondary to depression and negative thoughts rather than as direct manifestations of Alzheimer's disease.

4. **Incomplete Behavioral Analysis:** Focused on one specific situation rather than global behavioral withdrawal; did not assess daily activity level or identify core beliefs about identity loss.

5. **Inadequate Homework Support:** No written materials provided; no explicit wife involvement; no contingency planning; insufficient follow-up plan; unrealistic assessment of patient's independent implementation capacity.

**Recommended Supervisor Actions:**
- Schedule clinical consultation to review diagnostic formulation and treatment appropriateness
- Provide training on cognitive assessment and treatment adaptation for neurocognitive disorders
- Discuss family-based interventions and realistic goal-setting for progressive illness
- Review evidence-based approaches for Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
- Establish supervision plan to monitor next session and ensure appropriate treatment modifications
