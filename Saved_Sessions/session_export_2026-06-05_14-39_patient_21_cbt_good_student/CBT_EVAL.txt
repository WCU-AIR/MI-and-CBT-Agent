## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Strengths Demonstrated:**
- Collaborative agenda-setting with explicit patient input
- Accurate empathic reflection and listening
- Effective use of Socratic questioning for self-discovery
- Successful cognitive restructuring (thought from "I'm cured" to "I feel good, but meds still important")
- Warm, professional therapeutic stance
- Concrete, time-limited homework with feasibility check

**Critical Areas for Improvement:**
1. **Clinical Judgment & Risk Assessment:** Failed to recognize manic presentation and prioritize medication refusal as urgent clinical issue. No risk assessment conducted regarding medication nonadherence, substance use relapse, or impulsive behavior.

2. **Case Conceptualization:** Treated presentation as standard cognitive distortion case rather than recognizing mania with poor insight. Did not integrate substance use into formulation or treatment planning.

3. **Treatment Strategy:** Cognitive restructuring alone is insufficient for patient with poor insight and mania. Strategy should include psychoeducation, behavioral interventions, substance use relapse prevention, and psychiatric collaboration.

4. **Addressing Resistance:** Did not explore patient's expressed irritation at being told what to do or hesitation about homework as therapeutic resistance. Missed opportunity to address ambivalence about treatment.

5. **Homework Rationale & Specificity:** Assignment lacked clear explanation of therapeutic purpose and did not target most critical behaviors (medication adherence, sleep regulation, substance use avoidance).

**Recommended Actions:**
- Seek clinical supervision focused on bipolar disorder treatment
- Complete training in recognizing mania and assessing psychiatric risk
- Develop competency in integrating medication management with psychotherapy
- Learn motivational interviewing techniques for addressing ambivalence and resistance
- Practice conducting comprehensive risk assessments for bipolar disorder
- Establish protocols for collaborating with prescribing psychiatrists and medical teams
