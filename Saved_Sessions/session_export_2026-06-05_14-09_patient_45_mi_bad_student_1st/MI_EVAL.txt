# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates profound absence of unconditional positive regard. Repeatedly labels client's emotions as "weakness" and dismisses legitimate psychological experience. Explicitly judges Erik's ambivalence and self-compassion as character flaws. Zero affirmation of genuine progress (pants fitting better, reduced back pain, gym attendance). Communicates disapproval and blame throughout ("you're just making excuses," "you have to stop that right now"). Fails to normalize the struggle of behavior change—a core MI competency. This approach violates fundamental MI principle of unconditional positive regard and actively demoralize the client.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains virtually no reflective listening or genuine effort to understand client's perspective. When Erik explains discouragement from comparing himself to younger gym-goers, psychiatrist dismisses it as an "excuse" rather than exploring it. Makes no attempt to understand Erik's fear of burnout or concern that rigid approaches have failed him. Zero complex reflections present. Uses only closed questions and declarative statements rather than attempting to understand Erik's internal world. Never captures emotional nuance, explores ambivalence, or demonstrates curiosity about what drives behavior or barriers faced. Actively resists understanding Erik's experience.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist maintains clear focus on weight loss and lifestyle change, preventing aimlessness. However, direction is maintained through authoritarian prescription and threat rather than collaborative exploration. Approach is rigid and punitive ("You have to quit candy bars completely," "You have to go to the gym regularly without fail," "This isn't optional"). Lacks progressive clarification—no exploration of Erik's values, priorities, or personal meaning of "getting healthier." Direction without collaboration constitutes coercion, not MI-consistent guidance. Minimal credit given for maintaining behavioral focus despite poor methodology.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session demonstrates rigid expert-driven, hierarchical stance with zero power-sharing. Opening statement explicitly positions psychiatrist as sole authority: "I'll be telling you exactly how to do it." Throughout session, makes unilateral declarations without soliciting Erik's input or co-constructing solutions. When Erik offers perspective ("I'm trying to balance wanting to take this seriously with not beating myself up"), psychiatrist dismisses it rather than incorporating it. Repeatedly overrides client contributions with "No, you're just making excuses again." This is domination, not partnership. Zero collaborative problem-solving present.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session characterized by coercion, pressure, and explicit denial of choice. Psychiatrist repeatedly uses language eliminating autonomy: "You have to," "You can't," "This isn't optional," "You have to make the change today." When Erik expresses preference for gradual, self-compassionate approach, psychiatrist responds with dismissal and demands. Uses threat and fear-mongering to pressure compliance ("consequences will catch up with you, and it won't be pretty"). Presents false binary choices ("Are you going to keep sabotaging yourself or are you ready to commit?"). Zero affirmation of Erik's freedom to choose or emphasis on his ownership of change process. Complete failure in autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session contains virtually no evocation. Psychiatrist does not ask open questions designed to elicit Erik's intrinsic motivation. Makes declarative statements and demands compliance instead. Multiple instances of spontaneous change talk are completely ignored: "My pants feel a bit better," "My back doesn't hurt nearly as much," "I've been to the gym a couple of times." Skilled MI clinician would recognize these as valuable and ask follow-up questions to deepen motivation. Instead, psychiatrist ignores all change talk and focuses exclusively on what Erik is *not* doing. Never asks: "What would help you get to the gym more consistently?" or "What do you value most about your health?" or "What strengths have helped you make changes?" These fundamental evocation questions are completely absent. Critical failure in MI technique.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session demonstrates opposite of partnership. Psychiatrist actively assumes expert role for entire interaction; client contributions are systematically dismissed and overridden. Opening statement explicitly anti-partnership: "I'll be telling you exactly how to do it." When Erik offers perspective, it is dismissed: "No, you're just making excuses again," "Feeling discouraged is just weakness talking." These responses communicate that Erik's contributions are not valued and will not influence session direction. Zero instances where client's ideas substantially influence direction or content. Psychiatrist makes all decisions unilaterally. Fundamental violation of MI principle that expertise and wisdom about change reside mostly within the client.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Catastrophic failure to recognize, evoke, or amplify Erik's substantial change talk. Client spontaneously offers multiple instances: "My pants feel a bit better," "My back doesn't hurt nearly as much," "I've been to the gym a couple of times," "I want to commit—I really do," "I'm trying to make changes." Skilled MI clinician would reflect and deepen each instance. Instead, psychiatrist ignores all change talk and focuses exclusively on deficits and demands. Actively undermines confidence through doubt and criticism: "If you can't handle a little discomfort, you'll never get anywhere," "Are you going to keep sabotaging yourself?" These statements reduce self-efficacy rather than cultivating it. Missed opportunity to strengthen intrinsic motivation represents catastrophic failure in change talk cultivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Psychiatrist avoids extensively exploring or reinforcing sustain talk (which would entrench it), preventing some reinforcement of ambivalence. However, technique used is confrontational and likely counterproductive. When Erik expresses sustain talk ("It's hard to make it a regular thing," "I don't like it because younger guys lift more"), psychiatrist responds with direct confrontation: "No, you're just making excuses," "Feeling discouraged is just weakness talking," "You have to push through that." This approach is likely to trigger defensiveness and entrenchment rather than skillfully softening ambivalence. MI-consistent approach would use reframing and double-sided reflections. Minimal credit given for not extensively reinforcing sustain talk, but technique used is poor and likely counterproductive.

## Overall Clinical Summary

**Overall Session Score: 1.1/5**

**Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (weight loss, exercise, diet)
- Does not extensively explore or reinforce sustain talk
- Session stays on topic and does not wander aimlessly

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- **Complete absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard** — Judges Erik's emotions as "weakness," dismisses concerns, communicates disapproval throughout
- **Zero empathic understanding** — No reflective listening, no exploration of perspective, no attempt to understand internal experience
- **Authoritarian, expert-driven stance** — Makes unilateral decisions, dismisses client input, explicitly states "I'll be telling you exactly how to do it"
- **Coercion and pressure** — Uses threats, demands, and false binaries to pressure compliance
- **Complete failure to evoke or amplify change talk** — Ignores multiple instances of Erik's progress and motivation
- **Undermining of autonomy and self-efficacy** — Denies choice, pressures compliance, communicates doubt in client's ability
- **No collaboration or partnership** — Entirely clinician-driven with zero power-sharing

**Current MI Competency Level:** Pre-MI. This represents fundamental misunderstanding of MI principles, not basic competence with room for improvement. Psychiatrist requires comprehensive retraining.

**Impact on Client Engagement:** This session is likely to *decrease* intrinsic motivation and increase dropout risk. Research demonstrates confrontational, authoritarian approaches reduce motivation and increase relapse rates. Erik's final response ("it kind of feels like you're not hearing me") indicates therapeutic alliance damage.

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist should not continue MI sessions without formal training and supervision. Refer client to MI-trained clinician while psychiatrist completes comprehensive MI training and ongoing clinical supervision.
