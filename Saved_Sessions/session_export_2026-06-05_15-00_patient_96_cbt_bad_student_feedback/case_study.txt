--- DISORDER ---
Specific Phobia, Situational (Flying); Specific Phobia, Animals

--- CASE STUDY ---

Olaf Hendricks, a 51-year-old married businessman, presented due to intense anxiety about flying. He reported feeling fine in most areas of life except when confronted with air travel. His fear began 3 years prior during a flight that landed amid an ice storm. He last flew a year ago but cried during takeoff and landing and subsequently avoided flying, taking trains instead. Attempts to board a plane for his daughter’s wedding failed despite alcohol use to reduce anxiety. His avoidance has prevented him from meeting his granddaughter and attending other family events. Anxiety about business travel led him to decline promotions and job offers.

Mr. Hendricks denied other symptoms of depression or anxiety and reported functioning well at work, including public speaking. He increased alcohol use (three glasses of wine nightly) to relax but denied complications or withdrawal. He described a childhood fear of wild animals that led to avoidance of camping and hiking. He has no family history of psychiatric illness.

On examination, he appeared motivated, cooperative, and alert, with anxiety specifically around flying but otherwise no impairments. Cognition was intact, and insight and judgment were appropriate.

Discussion: Mr. Hendricks demonstrates a persistent, excessive, and distressing fear of flying, which significantly impairs his social and occupational functioning. DSM-5 criteria for specific phobia are met, with a situational specifier for airplanes. His childhood fear of wild animals represents a second specific phobia. Comorbidities such as other anxiety, mood, or substance use disorders should be evaluated, though his alcohol use does not currently meet diagnostic criteria. Functional impairment due to avoidance of air travel underscores the clinical significance of his phobias.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---
