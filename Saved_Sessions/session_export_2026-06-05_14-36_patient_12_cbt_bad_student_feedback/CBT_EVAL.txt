## CRITICAL CLINICAL SUMMARY

**Strengths:**
- Basic interpersonal warmth and non-threatening demeanor
- Non-judgmental tone; did not dismiss Ashley's concerns
- Encouraging language used throughout

**Critical Deficiencies:**
1. **Fundamental mismatch:** CBT contraindicated; abstract cognitive interventions applied to patient with severe intellectual disability
2. **No assessment:** No functional analysis of self-injury; no exploration of communication barriers, sensory needs, or environmental factors
3. **Lack of structure:** No agenda, no coherent strategy, no prioritization, no measurement
4. **Boundary violations:** Excessive self-disclosure; therapist positioned as peer rather than expert
5. **Failed adaptation:** No simplification of language; no visual supports; no verification of comprehension despite clear communication deficits
6. **No caregiver involvement:** Parents/guardians not mentioned or consulted despite patient's severe disability requiring partnership
7. **Ineffective intervention:** Generic "think positive" advice repeated 4+ times; no specific skills taught; no behavioral planning; no environmental modification

**Immediate Actions Required:**
- Recognize CBT is contraindicated; discontinue cognitive interventions
- Conduct functional behavior assessment of self-injury
- Involve parents and school staff as central treatment partners
- Adapt communication to developmental level (simple, concrete, visual)
- Teach concrete coping strategies through demonstration and practice
- Establish clear, measurable goals with monitoring system
- Consult with BCBA or ABA specialist
- Consult with developmental pediatrician or psychiatrist specializing in autism/intellectual disability

**Recommendation:** This therapist should not continue treating Ashley without significant training and supervision in autism, intellectual disability, and behavioral intervention. Treatment approach must be fundamentally redesigned.
