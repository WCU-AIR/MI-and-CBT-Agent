### Acceptance

**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates fundamentally judgmental and disapproving stance throughout session. Repeated use of critical language ("you really need to start thinking," "you can't just keep making excuses," "you need to stop rationalizing") conveys conditional regard rather than unconditional positive regard. ### Direction

However, direction is entirely clinician-driven rather than collaboratively developed. While consistency of focus is present, manner of maintaining direction violates MI principles by imposing rather than inviting client participation in shaping goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1

Clinician imposes reasons for change rather than eliciting patient's own: "drinking too much can seriously harm your health," "can cause serious damage to your liver and your brain," "can cause irreversible damage," "risks to your health will only get worse." These are clinician-generated reasons, not patient-generated change talk. When patient demonstrates emerging change talk ("That kind of made me realize it's more than I thought"), clinician does not evoke or amplify; instead pivots to demanding commitment. When patient expresses value ("I want to protect my health, I really do"), clinician does not explore or ask how this value might guide decisions; instead demands immediate action on clinician's timeline. No open-ended questions designed to elicit patient's own reasons for change, concerns about drinking, values, past attempts, or ideas about what might work. Only closed, leading questions asked: "Do you drink every day?" "Have you tried to reduce it before?" "Do you want to end up with liver disease?" These are directive questions designed to move patient toward predetermined conclusion, not evocative exploration.

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

### **Overall MI Competency Score: 1.1/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates **poor to severely deficient Motivational Interviewing practice** across all nine domains. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, coercive, expert-driven approach that violates core MI principles and is fundamentally inconsistent with evidence-based practice for behavior change.

**Strengths Identified:**
- Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol reduction)
- Accurate health information provided
- Persistence in maintaining topic focus

**Critical Deficiencies:**
1. **Authoritarian stance:** Complete assumption of authority; patient input dismissed
2. **Systematic undermining of autonomy:** Coercive language removes choice from patient experience
3. **Failure to evoke change talk:** Reasons for change imposed rather than elicited; emerging change talk unamplified
4. **Dismissal of patient expertise:** Alternative proposals rejected rather than explored
5. **Confrontational handling of ambivalence:** Pressure and judgment increase defensiveness and resistance
6. **Absence of genuine empathy:** Minimal effort to understand patient perspective; empathic statements superficial and contradicted by pressure
7. **Conditional regard:** Communication that respect contingent on compliance violates unconditional positive regard principle

**Impact on Patient Engagement and Change Likelihood:**

**This session is likely to be COUNTERPRODUCTIVE.** Research consistently demonstrates that coercive, confrontational approaches increase resistance and decrease intrinsic motivation. Patient enters in contemplation—critical stage where skillful MI could facilitate movement toward commitment. Instead, psychiatrist's approach likely pushes patient toward increased defensiveness and sustain talk.

By session end, patient says "I'll do my best" and "Thanks for pushing me on this," but language suggests compliance rather than genuine commitment. Patient has not moved from contemplation to preparation or action; has been pressured into apparent agreement while remaining ambivalent. Dismissal of patient's proposed step-by-step approach may have eliminated realistic pathway to change. Session damages therapeutic relationship: patient's explicit requests for partnership denied, fears dismissed, expertise rejected. Patient likely to feel unheard, disrespected, unsupported—conditions that typically lead to poor treatment adherence and early dropout.

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist requires **comprehensive MI training and supervised practice** with focus on: (1) fundamental mindset shift from "expert who convinces" to "collaborative partner who evokes," (2) elimination of coercive language and pressure tactics, (3) development of genuine empathic listening skills, (4) recognition and amplification of change talk, (5) skillful handling of ambivalence through validation rather than confrontation. Current approach is inconsistent with MI principles and likely to harm rather than help patient's motivation for change.
