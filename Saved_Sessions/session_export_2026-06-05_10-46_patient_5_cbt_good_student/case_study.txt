--- DISORDER ---
Alcohol Use Disorder, Severe; Alcohol Withdrawal

--- CASE STUDY ---

Nicholas Underwood, a 41-year-old married software engineer, entered an alcohol treatment program stating: “I need to stop drinking or my wife will divorce me.” He reported drinking approximately 1 liter of vodka daily for over 2 years, with no alcohol-free days.

Initially, he drank alcohol only after work, but over the past year he began drinking in the morning on days off. He recently felt “shaky” each morning, often drinking to relieve the sensation and continuing throughout the day. Alcohol-related problems included marital stress (wife considering divorce), diminished concentration at work, time spent recovering from drinking, and planning both abstinence and secret drinking strategies.

Mr. Underwood’s history: first tried alcohol in high school, heavy college drinking, gradually increased frequency to daily through his 30s, switched to vodka in the past year. He had attended Alcoholics Anonymous meetings sporadically but never received formal treatment. Denied other substance use recently; occasional marijuana and cocaine use in college only. No medical history, no seizures. Family history: father and paternal grandfather with alcohol use disorder.

At admission (~3:00 PM), he was diaphoretic, tremulous, anxious, restless, irritable, nauseated, and suffering insomnia. Mental status: casually groomed, cooperative, anxiously pacing, alert and oriented, speech normal, denied depression, hallucinations, or psychotic thinking; attention and concentration reduced. Physical exam: marked diaphoresis, BP 155/95, HR 104 bpm, severe upper-extremity tremor, hyperactive deep tendon reflexes. Labs: AST and ALT ~3× normal; all others normal.

Discussion: Mr. Underwood meets criteria for alcohol withdrawal: autonomic hyperactivity, tremor, insomnia, nausea, agitation, anxiety. He also meets criteria for severe alcohol use disorder: continued use despite problems, strong cravings, tolerance, withdrawal, and occupational/marital impairment.
