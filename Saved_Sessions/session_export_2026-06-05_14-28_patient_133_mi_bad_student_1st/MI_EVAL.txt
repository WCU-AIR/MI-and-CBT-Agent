## Summary Assessment

### Critical Findings

This session represents a **near-complete failure of Motivational Interviewing practice.** The psychiatrist demonstrates an authoritarian, expert-driven approach that violates MI principles at every level. The clinician explicitly rejects collaboration ("No, we're not going to talk about what you want"), uses coercive pressure and fear-based motivation, demonstrates zero empathy or reflective listening, and completely ignores substantial spontaneous change talk from the patient.

### Impact on Therapeutic Outcome

**This session is likely to produce the opposite of intended outcomes.** Patient enters with genuine motivation for sustainable change ("I want to find a way to be healthier that actually works for me in the long run") and openness to exploration. By session end, she explicitly states she is "not ready to commit" and that the approach makes her "want to shut down" and "resist even more." The coercive approach activates psychological reactance, damages therapeutic alliance, and undermines intrinsic motivation—the very factors that predict sustained behavior change.

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Fundamental reorientation to MI spirit and principles** — Comprehensive MI training focused on partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation
2. **Replace interrogation with dialogue** — Shift from yes/no questions to open-ended exploration
3. **Develop genuine empathy and reflective listening** — Make reflections central to practice
4. **Recognize and amplify change talk** — Build on patient's own motivation rather than ignoring it
5. **Establish true partnership** — Explicitly invite patient input and co-construct treatment plans
6. **Eliminate coercion and fear-based pressure** — Replace ultimatums with autonomy support
7. **Explore function of current behavior** — Understand what needs are being met before suggesting change
8. **Use double-sided reflections** — Soften sustain talk through curiosity rather than confrontation
9. **Build on patient strengths** — Recognize and amplify what patient is already doing well
10. **Develop humility about clinician role** — Acknowledge limits of expert knowledge and patient's wisdom about their own experience
