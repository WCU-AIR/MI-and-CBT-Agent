6. **Modest explicit affirmation of strengths:** While respectful, there are limited explicit affirmations of Margaret's strengths (her willingness to engage, her self-awareness, her capacity for self-reflection).

## Key Recommendations for Enhanced Practice

### 1. Strategically Address Minimization Through Discrepancy Exploration
When clients minimize their behavior, explicitly highlight the discrepancy between their minimization and evidence they've already provided. Use gentle, curious language rather than confrontation.

**Example:** "I hear you—seven drinks doesn't feel like much to you. And I'm also noticing that you mentioned your fall earlier this year, and your son has expressed concern, and you yourself said your drinking has increased since retirement. Help me understand how you're making sense of all that together."

### 2. Deepen Emotional Exploration Around Key Events
When clients mention significant events (like Margaret's fall), explore the emotional and personal impact more deeply to create emotional connection to the change goal.

**Example:** "That must have been a scary experience. Tell me what that was like for you. How did it affect you?"

### 3. Use Readiness Scaling Strategically
When clients rate their readiness at a midpoint, ask what would move them to the next level. This is a high-yield technique for evoking commitment language.

**Example:** "You're right in the middle at a 5. What would it take for you to move from a 5 to a 6 or 7? What would need to shift?"

### 4. Introduce Normative Data and Health Context Early
Introduce health guidelines or normative information in a collaborative, curious way that helps clients understand the clinical context for the conversation.

**Example:** "I'm curious—do you know what health guidelines suggest for safe drinking? Let me share what the research shows, and then I'd like to hear what you think about how that compares to your current drinking."

### 5. Deeply Explore Values to Strengthen Intrinsic Motivation
Beyond affirming Margaret's value for social connection, explore her deeper values around health, independence, longevity, and quality of life.

**Example:** "You mentioned wanting to feel steady on your feet and avoid another fall. Tell me more about what independence and safety mean to you. What does it look like to be able to do the things you want to do without worry?"
