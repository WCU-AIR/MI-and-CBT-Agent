# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Early validation of Kevin's embarrassment and normalization of his ambivalence as a natural tension between competing values shows strong acceptance. Genuine affirmations tied to observable behavior ("That kind of thoughtful reflection shows you're really tuning into what matters to you") strengthen authenticity. Minor limitation: slight shift toward directiveness when introducing strategies before fully exploring Kevin's own ideas, which slightly compromises pure acceptance of his autonomy.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid empathic understanding with well-executed complex reflections that capture both content and emotional nuance. Strong opening reflection accurately captures Kevin's ambivalence and uses it to deepen exploration. Later reflections accurately reflect the tension between social belonging and safety. However, opportunities for deeper empathic exploration are missed—emotional weight of regret and social anxiety about peer pressure could be explored more fully. Empathy is consistent but could be deepened in moments where emotional nuance is present but not fully explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early direction is clear—exploring Kevin's ambivalence about drinking and understanding what change might look like. However, direction becomes increasingly unclear and prescriptive as the session progresses. After Kevin rates importance at 5-6, the psychiatrist pivots away from exploring this critical ambivalence rather than deepening it. Most significantly, introduction of specific strategies (drink limits, pacing with water, planning ahead) shifts direction from exploratory to prescriptive, guiding Kevin toward predetermined solutions rather than evoking his own ideas about what might work.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Kevin's input throughout ("What's on your mind that you'd like to focus on?"). However, true collaboration requires that the client's contributions substantially shape session direction and content. When the psychiatrist introduces predetermined strategies, this is framed as collaborative but represents a unilateral decision. A more collaborative approach would have asked Kevin to generate his own ideas first. Collaboration becomes one-directional: psychiatrist suggests, Kevin responds positively, psychiatrist affirms. True collaboration would involve Kevin's counter-proposals and alternative ideas equally shaping the session.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Kevin's right to choose and avoids pressuring or directive language in most instances. Permission-seeking ("If it's okay with you, I can share some ideas...") and affirmation of Kevin's pace ("respects your pace and social life") demonstrate strong autonomy support. However, subtle limitation exists: while strategies are framed as optional, their presentation implicitly suggests they are the "right" approaches. Stronger autonomy support would involve asking Kevin to generate his own ideas first ("What do you think might help?") to ensure his autonomy is truly centered rather than constrained by psychiatrist's suggestions.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates some evocative skill, particularly in exploring past successes ("What are some things you've done in the past...?") and asking open questions ("What do you imagine might be some reasons or motivations...?"). Kevin skillfully applies past experience to current situation. However, evocation is significantly limited by introduction of predetermined strategies before Kevin's own ideas are fully explored. The psychiatrist tells rather than evokes: "One approach some people find helpful is setting a personal limit... Others try pacing themselves..." A more evocative approach would ask Kevin to generate strategies first before introducing external suggestions. Overall pattern shows premature shift from exploration to solution-provision.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates partial partnership but falls short of true collaborative power-sharing. Statements like "You're showing a lot of self-awareness and practical wisdom" acknowledge Kevin's expertise. However, introduction of predetermined strategies subtly shifts power dynamics from true partnership to "collaborative expert." True partnership would involve Kevin generating solutions first, Kevin's disagreement or alternative ideas substantially shaping the session, and shared decision-making about when to shift from exploration to strategy-sharing. The psychiatrist maintains subtle authority over session direction and content rather than genuinely sharing power with Kevin.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges throughout the session ("Maybe I'm not as in control as I thought," "I do want to avoid more stupid injuries," "I'd say maybe a 7"), but the psychiatrist does not consistently cultivate or strategically amplify it. Missed opportunities include: not deepening the 5-6 importance rating with "What would move you to a 7?"; not exploring emotional statements like "waking up feeling like crap" more deeply; not asking follow-up questions after change talk statements. The psychiatrist affirms change talk but rarely uses strategic questions to evoke more or strengthen existing change talk. Limited use of open questions to draw out commitment language and deepen motivation represents a significant gap in MI practice.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal in this session, and the psychiatrist handles it skillfully when it does emerge. The clinician validates concerns without reinforcing them, uses double-sided reflections ("You're caught between wanting to keep those good parts..."), and reframes sustain talk as part of natural ambivalence rather than resistance. Strategies are presented as preserving values ("These strategies aren't about giving up fun"). One missed opportunity: when Kevin expresses worry about sticking to limits with peer pressure, the psychiatrist could have explored this concern more deeply to convert sustain talk into change talk through problem-solving rather than simple reassurance.

---

### Overall Session Score: **3.4/5**

---

## Summary Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Consistent unconditional positive regard and genuine validation of ambivalence
- Well-constructed empathic reflections demonstrating accurate perspective-taking
- Skillful handling of sustain talk through validation and reframing
- Strong autonomy support and collaborative framing
- Effective evocation of past resources and client strengths
- Clear focus on harm reduction and referral concern

**Areas for Improvement:**
- Premature introduction of predetermined strategies before exploring Kevin's own ideas
- Insufficient cultivation and amplification of change talk
- Limited exploration of ambivalence when Kevin rates importance at 5-6
- Reduced partnership in latter half of session due to strategy introduction
- Missed opportunities for deepening emotional exploration (regret, social anxiety, worry)
- Limited use of open questions to generate commitment language

**Clinical Impact:**
This session demonstrates competent, respectful MI practice with solid foundational skills. However, it falls short of skilled MI practice due to prioritizing solution-provision over evocation of the client's own motivation and wisdom. Kevin leaves with a concrete plan and moderate confidence (7/10), but his intrinsic motivation and sense of ownership could be significantly stronger. The premature shift to predetermined strategies may reduce his sense of ownership and limit sustained behavior change when facing social pressure or challenging situations. With deeper exploration of Kevin's own ideas, more strategic amplification of change talk, and fuller engagement with his ambivalence before moving to solutions, the psychiatrist could substantially increase the likelihood of sustained behavior change and deeper client engagement.
