
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

## Recommendations

### 1. **Conduct Comprehensive Safety Assessment (URGENT)**
- **Action:** In the next session, explicitly assess suicidality. Use direct questions: "Have you thought about harming yourself?" "Do you have a plan?" "What's stopping you?" "Do you have access to means?" Document responses clearly.
- **Example:** "Betty, you mentioned feeling hopeless. I want to understand this better. When you feel that way, do you ever think about not wanting to be here anymore? Have you thought about harming yourself?"
- **Practice:** Review suicide risk assessment protocols. Practice asking these questions with colleagues until they feel natural. Understand that asking about suicide does not increase risk; it decreases it by opening dialogue.

### 2. **Establish Structured, Collaborative Agenda in Every Session**
- **Action:** Begin each session with explicit agenda-setting. Identify 2-3 target problems, prioritize collaboratively, and allocate time.
- **Example:** "Betty, I want to make sure we use our time well today. I'm hearing that you're struggling with low mood, worry about money, and not doing activities you used to enjoy. Which of these feels most important to work on today? How much time should we spend on each?"
- **Practice:** Write out agendas before sessions. Practice saying: "Here's what I'm hearing are your main concerns. How would you prioritize these?" Develop the habit of time-checking: "We have 15 minutes left. Should we continue with this or shift to something else?"

### 3. **Assess Cognitive Capacity and Adapt Interventions Accordingly**
- **Action:** Explicitly assess Betty's executive function, attention, and memory. Recognize that cognitive restructuring may be poorly matched to her profile. Prioritize behavioral activation and graded exposure instead.
- **Example:** "Betty, I notice you sometimes have difficulty with word-finding and concentration. I want to make sure the strategies we use work with your brain, not against it. Rather than focusing on changing thoughts right now, let's focus on getting you back to activities you value—like your card games. Does that sound like a good place to start?"
- **Practice:** Review literature on depression-executive dysfunction syndrome. Learn how to adapt CBT for patients with cognitive impairment. Practice explaining this to patients: "Your executive function is affected by depression, so we'll start with concrete behavioral changes rather than abstract thought work."

### 4. **Implement Systematic Behavioral Activation, Not Cognitive Restructuring First**
- **Action:** Create a graded hierarchy of valued activities. Schedule specific activities with external support. Monitor engagement and mood. Only introduce cognitive work after behavioral engagement improves.
- **Example:** "Let's think about the activities you used to enjoy. You mentioned card games and the senior center. Let's start with one card game this week. I'll help you plan how to make that happen. Your daughter could drive you, or we could arrange transportation. What day works best?"
- **Practice:** Learn the behavioral activation protocol: (1) Assess current activity, (2) Identify valued activities, (3) Create hierarchy, (4) Schedule systematically, (5) Monitor, (6) Adjust. Practice this with colleagues until it becomes automatic.

### 5. **Systematically Elicit and Respond to Feedback Throughout Sessions**
- **Action:** Check understanding regularly. Ask: "Does this make sense?" "What are your thoughts?" "How does this connect to what we've been discussing?" "What was most helpful today?" Adjust based on feedback.
- **Example:** After explaining behavioral activation, ask: "I want to make sure I explained that clearly. Can you tell me back what you understand about why we're focusing on activities first?" If Betty doesn't understand, re-explain differently.
- **Practice:** Set a goal to elicit feedback at least 3 times per session. Practice phrases: "I'm curious what you think about that." "How does that land for you?" "What questions do you have?" Make feedback-seeking habitual.

### 6. **Review Homework Systematically and Adapt Based on Outcomes**
- **Action:** Always begin sessions by reviewing prior homework. Ask what worked, what didn't, and why. Adapt interventions based on outcomes. Never repeat the same intervention without modification if it didn't work.
- **Example:** "Last week we talked about trying small activities. How did that go? Were you able to do anything? What got in the way? What would make it easier this week?"
- **Practice:** Create a homework review template: (1) What was assigned? (2) What did the patient do? (3) What worked? (4) What didn't? (5) Why? (6) What's next? Use this template in every session.

### 7. **Assign Specific, Structured, Written Homework with Clear Rationale**
- **Action:** Provide written homework instructions. Explain rationale. Assess feasibility. Check commitment. Plan for barriers.
- **Example:** "Here's your homework for this week: I want you to attend one card game. Your daughter will pick you up on Thursday at 1 PM and bring you home by 4 PM. The purpose is to help your mood by doing something you value. I know it might feel hard to get ready, so your daughter will help. Before you go, I want you to notice: (1) Did I go? (2) How did I feel before, during, and after? Write these down so we can talk about it next week. Do you think you can do this? What might get in the way?"
- **Practice:** Write out homework assignments before sessions. Include: (1) Specific task, (2) When/how often, (3) Rationale, (4) Format for recording, (5) Feasibility check, (6) Barrier planning. Practice explaining homework clearly and checking understanding.

### 8. **Use Guided Discovery and Socratic Questioning, Not Didactic Advice**
- **Action:** Replace "You should think..." with "What do you think...?" Ask questions that help Betty discover insights rather than providing pre-packaged answers.
- **Example:** Instead of "You've managed before, so you can manage again," ask: "Tell me about a time you faced a difficult situation. How did you handle it? What did you do that helped? How could that apply now?"
