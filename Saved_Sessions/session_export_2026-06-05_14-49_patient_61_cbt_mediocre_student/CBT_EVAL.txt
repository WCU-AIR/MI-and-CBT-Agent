## CLINICAL SUMMARY FOR SUPERVISOR REVIEW

**Patient:** Betty (76-year-old female with late-life depression, mild neurocognitive disorder, executive dysfunction, psychomotor retardation, and multiple medical comorbidities)

**Sessions Evaluated:** Two consecutive sessions with identical structure and interventions

**Overall Rating:** 1.9/6 (Inadequate)

### Key Findings:

**Strengths:**
- Consistent warmth and non-judgmental demeanor
- Basic empathic validation present
- No hostile or destructive behavior
- Gentle, non-threatening approach

**Critical Deficiencies:**
1. **No structured CBT fundamentals** – Missing collaborative agenda, systematic feedback, clear conceptualization
2. **Mismatched interventions** – Cognitive restructuring prioritized despite executive dysfunction; behavioral activation vague and unsystematic
3. **Safety failure** – Hopelessness statement ignored; no suicidality assessment
4. **Poor homework practice** – Vague assignment, no review in Session 2, no adaptation
5. **Absence of guided discovery** – Didactic advice rather than Socratic questioning
6. **No treatment strategy** – Interventions appear ad hoc; Session 2 repeats Session 1 identically

### Immediate Actions Required:
- Conduct comprehensive safety assessment (suicidality screening)
- Establish structured agenda and feedback loops
- Shift to behavioral activation as primary intervention
- Assign specific, written homework with clear rationale
- Review homework systematically in next session
- Involve family support given cognitive impairment
- Discuss medication optimization with prescribing physician
- Provide therapist with additional training in geriatric CBT and neurocognitive adaptation

**Recommendation:** Therapist should not continue treating this patient independently without significant additional supervision and training.
