--- DISORDER ---
Cyclothymic Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Rachel was a 15-year-old girl referred for psychiatric evaluation due to worsening difficulties at home and school over the prior year. Her mother expressed concern that “Rachel's meds aren't working.” Rachel herself had no particular complaints.

Over the past year, her grades dropped from As and Bs to Cs and Ds, she lost long-standing friends, and conflicts at home escalated. She was described by her mother as “nasty and mean.” Rachel had been treated for ADHD since age 8 with methylphenidate, which had been effective until age 14 when mood changes emerged.

Since age 14, she experienced periods of persistent sadness, social withdrawal, hypersomnia, and loss of interest, alternating with irritability and occasional brief hypomanic episodes lasting 1–2 weeks, during which she was giddy, energetic, talkative, and unusually helpful around the house. These mood fluctuations were spontaneous, without clear external triggers. She denied alcohol or illicit substance use.

On examination, she was casually groomed, coherent, goal-directed, wary and sad, with some affective constriction. She described her mood changes as unpredictable and distressing. No psychotic symptoms, confusion, or suicidal/homicidal ideation were present. Cognition was intact.

Discussion: Rachel exhibits recurrent, spontaneous mood fluctuations including multiple brief hypomanic and subsyndromal depressive episodes over at least 1 year. These symptoms significantly impact her functioning at school, home, and socially. She does not meet criteria for mania, major depressive disorder, or a schizophrenia spectrum disorder. The presentation is consistent with cyclothymic disorder.
