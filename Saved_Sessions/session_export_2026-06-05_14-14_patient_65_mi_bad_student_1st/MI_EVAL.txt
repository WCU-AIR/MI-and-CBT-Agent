### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk present and strengthening throughout ("I'm young and healthy," "I don't think I need extreme changes," "it doesn't work for me," "I'm not ready"). Psychiatrist uses confrontation rather than skillful softening: "No, you're just making excuses again," "you're not seeing the big picture," "Stop downplaying the risks." Confrontational approach typically strengthens sustain talk—well-documented phenomenon in MI research. Evidence of backfiring: client's resistance becomes increasingly entrenched as session progresses. By end, client states "I need to work things out on my own terms"—more defensive than beginning. Double-sided reflections never used. Sustain talk addressed but ineffectively, likely reinforcing rather than reducing resistance.

## Overall Session Assessment

**Overall MI Competency Score: 1.2/5**

**Clinical Summary:**

This session represents a **complete departure from Motivational Interviewing principles** and demonstrates what MI explicitly teaches clinicians NOT to do. The psychiatrist employs a confrontational, expert-driven, authoritarian approach that violates nearly every core MI competency.

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Systematic rejection of MI spirit (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation absent)
- Authoritarian expert stance dismissing all client input
- Repeated pressure and ultimatums removing autonomy
- Dismissal of legitimate client concerns as "excuses"
- Strengthening of resistance through confrontation
- No evocation of intrinsic motivation
- Complete absence of reflective listening
- Unilateral imposition of goals without collaboration

**Impact on Client Engagement:**

**This session is likely counterproductive.** Research consistently shows confrontation, pressure, and autonomy removal increase resistance and decrease behavior change likelihood. Jordan's responses demonstrate this pattern:
- Early: Mild resistance, some openness ("I'm willing to try")
- Mid: Increased defensiveness ("feels like you're pushing me hard without really hearing")
- Late: Entrenched resistance ("I'm not about to just flip a switch," "I need to work things out on my own terms")

Client is moving away from change, not toward it. Therapeutic alliance damaged. Trust eroded. Likelihood of behavior change **decreased, not increased.**

**Current MI Competency Level: Novice/Pre-competent**

**Recommendation:** Psychiatrist requires formal MI training and supervision before continuing this approach. Without reorientation to MI principles, this clinician will continue strengthening client resistance rather than evoking motivation for change.
