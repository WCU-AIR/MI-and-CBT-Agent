### Cultivating Change Talk

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist appropriately avoids confrontation with sustain talk, which is foundational to MI. However, the clinician does not consistently use strategic techniques to soften resistance. Double-sided reflections are largely absent. When Anya expresses doubt about willpower ("I don't even know if I've got the willpower"), the clinician highlights past success—helpful but does not deeply explore the underlying belief that she lacks ability to change. When Anya expresses ambivalence about losing her social life, the clinician does not help her problem-solve alternatives. Most significantly, the clinician does not address the critical sustain talk about her doubt regarding follow-through. More skillful responses would involve: "You're expressing real doubt, and that's understandable. But I'm also hearing that you've succeeded before. What would help you feel more confident this time?"

## Summary of Clinical Evaluation

### Key Strengths
- **Consistent collaborative stance** with genuine invitation for client to set agenda and identify solutions
- **Accurate, sophisticated reflective listening** that captures emotional nuance and internal conflict
- **Strategic evocation of values** linking dreams (travel, financial stability) to current behavior—a core MI strength
- **Skillful affirmation** of strengths and past successes without dismissiveness
- **Clear directional focus** on gambling, debt, and lifestyle change with logical session progression
- **Appropriate use of scaling questions** to assess and track confidence level

### Critical Areas for Improvement
1. **Insufficient exploration of gambling's function** – Does not ask why Anya gambles or what need it meets (excitement, social connection, escape, stress relief). This limits targeted problem-solving.
2. **Weak barrier planning** – Minimal exploration of high-risk situations, triggers, or coping strategies. Questions about "What will you do if you slip?" are largely absent.
3. **Limited strategic handling of sustain talk** – While avoiding confrontation, the clinician does not use double-sided reflections or skillfully reframe resistance. Sustain talk is often bypassed rather than softened.
4. **Inadequate exploration of costs of change** – Focuses primarily on benefits (travel, financial stability) without deeply exploring genuine losses (social connection, fun, identity). This limits autonomy support and realistic planning.
5. **Subtle expert positioning** – Occasional directive language ("Remember, it's okay to have ups and downs; what matters is you're moving forward") subtly suggests the clinician knows what's best.

### Impact on Client Engagement
Despite these gaps, the session successfully engaged Anya and moved her toward change. Her confidence increased from initial ambivalence to 6/10, she identified concrete steps (cutting up a card, being honest with friends), and she expressed willingness to follow up. However, without deeper exploration of barriers and underlying motivations, the durability of change is uncertain.

### Overall Assessment
This session represents **competent but not exemplary MI practice**. The clinician demonstrates solid foundational skills in collaboration, reflective listening, and values-based evocation. With focused attention to exploring gambling's function, developing comprehensive barrier plans, and skillfully handling sustain talk, this clinician could move to a 4.5–4.7/5 level and significantly increase the likelihood of sustained client change.
