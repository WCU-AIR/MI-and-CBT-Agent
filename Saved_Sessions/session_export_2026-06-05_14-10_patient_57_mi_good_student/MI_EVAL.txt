### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client ownership of change process. Respects Isolde's autonomy in rating commitment at 9/10 rather than pushing for 10/10. Language emphasizes readiness and choice throughout ("What do you think might be some small, manageable steps...," "What do you think would be the first small step you'd feel ready to try?"). ### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary skill in drawing out client's own motivations, values, and resources rather than providing them. Strategic questions evoke powerful change talk: "What do you think might be some reasons or motivations...?" Client articulates own reasons (lifelong commitment, children's security, marriage strength). Clinician asks about past successes and what helped, inviting Isolde to identify her own strengths. Client generates all behavioral ideas; clinician affirms and amplifies rather than prescribes.

**Comments:** Strategically evokes and reinforces change talk at multiple points. Client articulates powerful change talk about commitment ("lifelong commitment"), desire ("I want my kids to grow up feeling loved and secure"), ability ("I want to be the kind of person who shows up"), and reasons ("If I let work or stress take over, it could really damage those things I care about most"). Psychiatrist recognizes and amplifies through reflections: "You have a deeply rooted commitment... That's a powerful motivator." Scaling questions (9/10, 8/10) evoke commitment language. Past success exploration elicits rich change talk about values and motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles minimal sustain talk that emerges using double-sided reflections and normalization. When Isolde acknowledges barriers (work stress, distractions), clinician reflects: "You're really attuned to the ways your work and stress can sometimes create distance, even though your intention is to be present." When rating 9/10 instead of 10/10, psychiatrist affirms realistic acknowledgment: "That balance between high importance and the practical difficulties really highlights your thoughtful approach." However, session presents low ambivalence and high intrinsic motivation, limiting opportunities to demonstrate advanced sustain talk management techniques. Score reflects competent handling of minimal sustain talk rather than demonstration of full range of advanced softening techniques.

## Overall Session Score: **4.8/5**

### Summary of Strengths:
- Exemplary empathic accuracy with complex, nuanced reflections
- Consistent strategic evocation of client's own motivations and resources
- Authentic partnership with true power-sharing
- Exceptional autonomy support with no pressure or prescription
- Skillful cultivation of powerful change talk throughout
- Warm, genuine acceptance with specific, earned affirmations
- Clear, strategic direction without rigidity
- Realistic confidence assessment affirming client's honest self-evaluation

### Areas for Development:
- Limited opportunity to demonstrate advanced sustain talk management with higher-ambivalence clients
- Could deepen proactive barrier exploration and problem-solving
- Could increase specificity in behavioral planning (when, where, how)
- Could develop relapse prevention strategies for long-term maintenance
- Minor opportunities to use more genuinely open-ended language in final action planning

### Clinical Recommendation:
This session demonstrates sophisticated MI competence. Psychiatrist would benefit from continued practice with higher-ambivalence clients and more complex clinical presentations to further refine advanced MI skills, particularly in sustain talk management and relapse prevention planning.
