# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Strong validation of overwhelm ("That makes a lot of sense") and affirmation of insight ("You're already showing a lot of insight"). Normalizes struggle effectively. Minor opportunity: could deepen affirmation of Jonathan's inherent worth independent of compliance or insight production. Maintains warmth and supportive tone even during resistance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking. Excellent emotional attunement in reflections such as "That sense of diabetes running your life rather than you being in the driver's seat must feel really heavy and restrictive." Captures both external pressure and internal conflict. Opportunity for improvement: could use simpler, more emotionally attuned reflections in moments of high distress (e.g., "It feels like too much to carry"). Limited use of complex, double-sided reflections that capture ambivalence. Generally strong but could deepen emotional exploration.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains appropriate focus on diabetes self-management with flexibility. Opening question establishes collaborative direction-setting. Logical progression from barriers (overwhelm, complexity) to resources (coping skills) to action planning. Strategic guidance without imposing predetermined agenda. Minor opportunity: could more explicitly explore whether Jonathan sees work strategy as applicable to diabetes before moving forward. Maintains focus naturally without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consistent power-sharing. Opening question invites Jonathan to set agenda. Frequent use of collaborative language positioning Jonathan as equal contributor ("That's a really valuable insight," "You're wondering if"). Positions Jonathan as someone with wisdom and agency. Minor opportunity: could more explicitly invite Jonathan's ideas about solutions rather than asking what "needs to change." Could check preferences before introducing assessment frameworks.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect for Jonathan's autonomy and choice throughout. Explicitly acknowledges his right to choose and avoids pressuring toward change. Accepts ambivalence without pressure ("Even if you're not fully ready yet"). Emphasizes Jonathan's agency in choosing what feels manageable. Minor opportunity: subtle shift toward end of session when introducing time frame ("in the next few days") that implies expectation of action. More autonomy-supportive phrasing would be "If you were to try this" rather than "How confident do you feel about trying that step in the next few days."

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some evocation through open questions and effective elicitation of coping skills. Excellent work asking about past successes ("Can you tell me about a time when you managed to handle something challenging?"). However, significant gaps in strategic evocation. Misses critical opportunities to deepen change talk—when Jonathan says "maybe trying one small thing at a time wouldn't hurt," psychiatrist moves quickly to problem-solving rather than exploring and amplifying. Does not ask about Jonathan's values, what matters most to him, or what he wants his life to look like—all central to MI evocation. Limited exploration of deeper motivation beyond problem-reduction.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership with consistent power-sharing and collaborative language throughout most of session. Opening question establishes true partnership. Uses inclusive language and positions Jonathan's contributions as shaping direction. Avoids expert role; treats Jonathan's experience as legitimate and transferable. Accepts Jonathan's pace and ambivalence without rushing. Minor opportunity: when introducing 0-10 confidence scale, this is clinician-driven rather than co-created. Could invite Jonathan to suggest how to measure confidence or readiness rather than imposing structured tool.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant weakness. Jonathan produces multiple instances of change talk that are not strategically amplified or reinforced: "Maybe not getting worse," "Taking it slow sometimes helps me at work," "Maybe trying one small thing at a time wouldn't hurt," and "I guess maybe I could try to check my blood sugar a little more regularly." Psychiatrist acknowledges but does not deepen any of these statements. Most critically, when Jonathan produces strongest change talk about blood sugar checking, psychiatrist immediately moves to confidence assessment rather than exploring and amplifying commitment. Session focuses on problem-solving rather than evoking intrinsic motivation. Minimal use of follow-up questions to strengthen Jonathan's own language about change. This represents fundamental MI gap limiting sustained motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate but inconsistent skill in softening sustain talk. Handles some sustain talk skillfully through validation and reframing (e.g., when Jonathan says "It doesn't really feel like I have much choice," psychiatrist reframes as addressable through autonomy support). Validates difficulty while implicitly suggesting manageability. Missed opportunities: when Jonathan expresses hopelessness ("I don't see how to get there"), could use double-sided reflection addressing both overwhelm and existing coping skills. When Jonathan doubts work strategy applies to diabetes, could explore ambivalence more directly. Relies primarily on validation and gentle redirection rather than sophisticated techniques like strategic amplification, reframing, or humor. Limited active addressing of hopelessness.

## Summary Assessment

### Strengths
- Consistent acceptance and unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental stance
- Good reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and emotional attunement
- Excellent use of open questions inviting client exploration and resources
- Effective elicitation of coping skills through past success exploration
- Strong collaborative stance with power-sharing and positioning of Jonathan as knowledgeable partner
- Consistent autonomy support with respect for ambivalence and client choice
- Clear, strategic direction progressing logically from barriers to resources to action
- Avoidance of expert stance and prescription of solutions

### Areas for Improvement
- **Minimal cultivation of change talk:** Change talk produced by Jonathan is not strategically amplified, reinforced, or deepened
- **Limited exploration of values and deeper motivation:** Session lacks exploration of what Jonathan values, what matters most, or what he wants his life to look like
- **Missed opportunities to deepen ambivalence:** Limited complex reflections capturing both sides of Jonathan's mixed feelings
- **Premature movement to action planning:** Relatively quick shift from exploration to identifying specific behavior before intrinsic motivation fully developed
- **Limited use of advanced MI techniques:** Relies primarily on validation and gentle redirection rather than double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, or sophisticated sustain talk softening
- **Clinician-driven assessment frameworks:** 0-10 confidence scale introduced without co-creation, representing subtle shift in power

### Impact on Client Engagement and Change
While respectful and collaborative, session is unlikely to produce sustained motivation for change. Jonathan leaves with tentative commitment (confidence 5/10) but underlying ambivalence and sense of external control remain unaddressed. Motivation likely to remain externally driven (doctor's orders) rather than internally motivated (personal values). Session demonstrates good MI spirit but falls short of core MI goal: **evoking intrinsic motivation for change.**

---

## Clinical Competency Level

**Current Assessment:** Basic to Intermediate MI Competence

The psychiatrist demonstrates solid foundational MI skills with good interpersonal abilities but incomplete application of core MI principles. Session embodies MI spirit of partnership and acceptance but underutilizes strategic change talk amplification and values exploration—the primary mechanisms for evoking sustained intrinsic motivation.

**Likelihood of Sustained Behavior Change:** Moderate to Low

Jonathan may attempt blood sugar checking as compliance gesture, but without deeper exploration of personal reasons for change and connection to values, sustained behavior change is unlikely.
