Maintains this expert stance throughout by telling client what he must do, dismissing client input, and refusing to incorporate client perspective into session. ### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client does express sustain talk: "I'm torn between hanging on to some freedom," "I'm not ready to quit drinking completely today." Psychiatrist attempts to address this sustain talk but in highly unskillful and counterproductive way. Rather than using MI-consistent techniques like reflection, reframing, or exploring ambivalence, psychiatrist uses confrontation and pressure: "No, you're not getting it," "you're just not trying hard enough," "you don't get to pick and choose." Psychiatrist does attempt to address sustain talk (why score is 2 rather than 1), but execution so poor it likely increases rather than decreases sustain talk.

## Summary of Clinical Findings

### Strengths
- Maintains some consistency in topical focus on health behaviors (alcohol, exercise, diet)
- Attempts to address client resistance to change
- Clearly identifies target behaviors

### Critical Deficiencies

This session represents a **fundamental failure to practice Motivational Interviewing**. The psychiatrist demonstrates:

- **Complete absence of acceptance** — Judgment, dismissal, and shaming throughout
- **Zero empathic understanding** — No reflective listening, active dismissal of client explanations
- **Authoritarian expert stance** — Eliminates all collaboration and partnership
- **Active undermining of autonomy** — Controlling language, false dichotomies, pressure tactics
- **Complete failure to evoke** — No open-ended questions, dismissal of spontaneous change talk
- **Counterproductive handling of sustain talk** — Confrontation strengthens rather than softens resistance

### Impact on Client Engagement

**This session is highly likely to harm therapeutic relationship and reduce client motivation for change.** Client enters with genuine commitment ("I'll do anything to get my kids back"), concrete progress (reduced alcohol, violence program enrollment, family contact), and clear values (his children). By session end, client is defensive, entrenched, and likely to disengage from treatment.

Client explicitly states that pressure makes him "shut down and give up on everything altogether," yet psychiatrist continues applying pressure. This is likely to result in treatment dropout, continued alcohol use, and failure to achieve stated goals of reuniting with children.

---

## Priority Recommendations

1. **Adopt collaborative, partnership-based stance** — Shift from "I tell you what to do" to "Let's explore this together"
2. **Develop reflective listening skills** — Practice reflecting back client statements before responding
3. **Link behavior change to client values** — Connect health behaviors to client's core motivation (his children)
4. **Eliminate confrontational pressure tactics** — Replace demands with collaborative, evocative approaches
5. **Amplify rather than dismiss change talk** — Recognize and reinforce client's spontaneous change talk
6. **Receive formal MI training** — Enroll in certified MI training program with ongoing supervision

---

**CLINICAL CONCLUSION:** This session does **NOT** embody the MI spirit. It lacks partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. It is characterized by judgment, control, and confrontation. **Immediate, comprehensive retraining in MI principles and practice is essential.**
