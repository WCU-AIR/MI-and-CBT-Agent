## Summary of Clinical Findings

### **Critical Strengths:**
- Psychiatrist maintains consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol use)
- Does not completely abandon session topic

### **Major Areas Requiring Immediate Improvement:**

**1. Fundamental Violation of MI Spirit**
This session represents a complete departure from Motivational Interviewing principles. The opening statement—"I'll be telling you exactly what you need to do"—explicitly rejects the collaborative, client-centered foundation of MI. The psychiatrist assumes an authoritarian expert role that communicates contempt for the client's perspective and autonomy.

**2. Confrontational Approach Strengthens Resistance**
Research demonstrates that confrontation and pressure actually strengthen client resistance and sustain talk. By the end of this session, the client is more entrenched in his position ("Maybe down the road, but not today") than at the beginning. The psychiatrist's aggressive counter-arguments to sustain talk have backfired.

**3. Suppression of Emerging Change Talk**
The client provides spontaneous change talk throughout ("I get the risks," "I'm willing to listen and maybe cut back a little," "I'm not saying I want to keep drinking forever"), but the psychiatrist does not amplify or reinforce any of it. Instead, confrontation suppresses this emerging motivation.

**4. Complete Absence of Empathy and Reflective Listening**
There are zero complex reflections in this session. The psychiatrist never demonstrates genuine understanding of the client's perspective, values, or ambivalence. When the client expresses that social connection is important, the psychiatrist dismisses this rather than exploring it.

**5. Active Undermining of Autonomy**
Explicit statements ("No, you don't get to decide that," "This isn't optional") communicate that the client's autonomy is not respected. This violates a core MI principle and damages the therapeutic alliance.

### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

This session is likely to **decrease** the client's motivation for change and **increase** his resistance. The client is unlikely to follow through on any recommendations because he does not feel heard, respected, or understood. If this approach continues, the client will likely disengage from treatment entirely and view the psychiatrist as punitive and disrespectful.

### **Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Enroll in formal MI training** through a MINT-certified program
2. **Seek ongoing supervision** with an MI-trained clinician
3. **Replace confrontational language** with curious, open questions
4. **Develop genuine reflective listening skills** through deliberate practice
5. **Explicitly communicate respect for client autonomy** in every session
6. **Evoke rather than impose** client motivation for change
7. **Amplify spontaneous change talk** rather than suppressing it
8. **Use double-sided reflections** to soften resistance without confrontation
9. **Record and review sessions** with supervisor feedback
10. **Do not see clients independently** until MI competency is demonstrated

---

**Overall Assessment:** This clinician requires immediate remedial training in Motivational Interviewing before continuing independent clinical practice. The current approach violates evidence-based standards and is harmful to client engagement and outcomes.
