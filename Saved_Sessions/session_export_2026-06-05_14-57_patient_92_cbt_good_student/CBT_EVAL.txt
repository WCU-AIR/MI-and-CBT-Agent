## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths Demonstrated:
- ✓ Strong collaborative agenda-setting with baseline measurement
- ✓ Accurate empathic reflection and validation of patient's experience
- ✓ Effective use of Socratic questioning to facilitate self-discovery
- ✓ Appropriate cognitive restructuring (evidence examination, alternative thought generation)
- ✓ Consistent collaborative approach with shared decision-making
- ✓ Warm, validating, professional therapeutic environment
- ✓ Relevant focus on central cognitions and avoidance behaviors
- ✓ Well-calibrated behavioral experiment aligned with patient confidence level

### Critical Deficiencies:
- ✗ **URGENT: No safety assessment or planning despite documented suicidal ideation and self-injury**
- ✗ Minimal feedback elicitation (only 2 checks across entire session)
- ✗ Incomplete case formulation lacking integration of trauma, family dynamics, and maintaining factors
- ✗ Superficial cognitive work (no exploration of catastrophic predictions or core beliefs)
- ✗ Narrow treatment strategy addressing only one situation without broader agoraphobia or PTSD focus
- ✗ Underutilization of CBT techniques (missing activity scheduling, coping skills training, thought records, exposure hierarchy)
- ✗ Inadequate homework structure (no written instructions, rationale, contingency planning, or follow-up protocol)
- ✗ No trauma-focused interventions despite moderate PTSD diagnosis
- ✗ Limited time management and no explicit session structure

### Impact on Patient Care:
This session would likely produce **modest, incremental improvement** in agoraphobia if homework is completed. However, without addressing safety, trauma processing, family dynamics, and systematic exposure, progress will be slow and incomplete. Risk of dropout is moderate due to minimal feedback and narrow focus. **Safety is the most critical concern—without a safety plan, patient is vulnerable in crisis.**

### Recommendation for Supervision:
**Immediate priorities:** (1) Integrate safety assessment and planning into every session; (2) Develop comprehensive case formulation; (3) Increase feedback elicitation; (4) Deepen cognitive work; (5) Establish systematic exposure hierarchies. Recommend regular supervision with trauma-informed CBT specialist and training in adolescent-specific and trauma-focused approaches.
