## DETAILED COMMENTS BY DOMAIN

### Strengths
- **Warm, Non-Hostile Demeanor:** Therapist maintained supportive, friendly tone throughout without hostility or criticism.
- **Willingness to Listen:** Therapist allowed patient extended speaking time and did not interrupt excessively.
- **Genuine Concern:** Therapist appeared to care about patient's wellbeing.

### Critical Deficiencies

**Structural Failures:**
- No agenda established; session lacked direction and purpose
- No feedback elicited; therapist did not check understanding or adjust based on patient responses
- No homework assigned; no between-session learning or skill practice

**Conceptualization Failures:**
- No explicit understanding of FNSD or its maintaining mechanisms
- Therapist treated FNSD as equivalent to normal academic stress
- Did not recognize that reassurance and positive thinking reinforce anxiety in FNSD

**Strategy Failures:**
- No coherent treatment plan or logical progression toward goals
- Interventions were generic wellness suggestions, not targeted CBT strategies
- No prioritization of concerns or systematic focus on core issues

**Technique Failures:**
- No CBT techniques applied (no cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, attention training)
- Suggested interventions (positive thinking, distraction, reassurance) are contraindicated in FNSD
- No psychoeducation about FNSD to validate symptoms or address fear of disbelief

**Collaboration Failures:**
- Therapist offered unsolicited directives rather than collaborative exploration
- Patient remained passive throughout; never actively engaged in problem-solving
- Patient's clinical input not incorporated into treatment planning

**Guided Discovery Failures:**
- No Socratic questioning employed
- No exploration of patient's beliefs, fears, or assumptions
- No behavioral experiments designed to test hypotheses
- Missed opportunity to address hypervigilance, anticipatory anxiety, and catastrophic thinking

### Impact on Patient Care

This session is unlikely to produce therapeutic change and may worsen outcomes. By repeatedly offering reassurance despite patient's explicit feedback that it was ineffective, therapist reinforced anxiety and hypervigilance. By failing to address core fear of invalidation and disbelief, therapist missed critical therapeutic opportunity. Patient left session without clear treatment plan, specific skills, or homework—significantly reducing likelihood of recovery.

### Recommendations for Immediate Action

1. **Seek supervision from CBT specialist with FNSD expertise**
2. **Eliminate reassurance and positive thinking; replace with validation and acceptance**
3. **Establish clear agenda and treatment plan at next session**
4. **Learn and apply specific CBT techniques for FNSD**
5. **Use guided discovery and Socratic questioning instead of directive advice**
6. **Assign specific, tailored homework with clear rationale**
7. **Provide psychoeducation about FNSD to validate symptoms and address invalidation fears**
8. **Reduce self-disclosure; increase demonstration of professional expertise**
9. **Develop competence in behavioral activation and exposure for FNSD**
10. **Attend training on functional neurological disorders**
