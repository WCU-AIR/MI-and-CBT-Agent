--- CASE STUDY ---

Wallace Pickering was a 29-year-old man who presented for outpatient therapy with a chief complaint: "I've never been to a shrink before, but I just read DSM-5, and I have ten diagnoses, including six paraphilias, two personality disorders, a substance use disorder, and maybe something else."

Mr. Pickering described himself as a cisgender gay man with a few sexual "kinks." He had been dating a former college classmate for 2 years. Raised in a politically and religiously conservative Midwestern household, he attended elite college and law school and worked as an attorney at a large law firm. His interest in psychiatric diagnoses was partially motivated by his boyfriend, who was training to be a psychiatrist.

He reported multiple sexual interests potentially linked to paraphilias, including voyeurism, exhibitionism, bondage, sadistic role-play, fetishistic interest in rubber, and occasional cross-dressing. He enjoyed both dominant and submissive sexual roles. He had engaged in occasional anonymous sexual encounters since age 20, timed to coincide with his boyfriend being away. He reported using cocaine and alcohol during these excursions but noted careful planning and limited frequency. He also smoked tobacco, with difficulty reducing below half a pack per day.

Mr. Pickering described himself as highly invested in work performance, tracking billable hours, and maintaining top performance at his firm. He expressed feelings of sadness and loneliness, reporting that his family knew little about him aside from a lesbian cousin aware of his sexual orientation.

On examination, he was well-groomed, coherent, goal-directed, and cognitively bright, with intact insight and judgment. Affect was appropriate, and he denied suicidality, confusion, or psychosis.

Discussion: Mr. Pickering presents a range of behaviors and sexual interests, many of which might be considered paraphilic. However, none of these behaviors caused distress, impairment, or harm to others, meaning they do not meet DSM-5 criteria for paraphilic disorders. His substance use is occasional and controlled, with the exception of tobacco use, which qualifies as a mild tobacco use disorder. Personality and mood features (narcissism, obsessive-compulsiveness, dysphoria) do not meet criteria for DSM-5 disorders, though exploration of his social isolation, ambivalence about sexual orientation, and lifelong sadness may be appropriate in psychotherapy.
