## SUPERVISOR NOTES

**Session Quality Summary:**

This session represents a fundamental departure from evidence-based CBT. The therapist demonstrated interpersonal warmth but failed to deliver structured, technique-driven intervention. The session functioned as supportive counseling rather than cognitive-behavioral therapy.

**Key Observations:**

- **Agenda:** Completely absent. Session lacked direction and collaborative goal-setting.
- **Feedback:** Minimal. No systematic checking for understanding or satisfaction.
- **Understanding:** Surface-level empathy present but critical clinical nuances missed (separation anxiety vs. generalized worry, panic cycle, safety behaviors, functional impairment).
- **Interpersonal Effectiveness:** Warm and approachable but undermined by excessive self-disclosure and boundary violations.
- **Collaboration:** Minimal. Therapist functioned as advice-giver; patient remained passive.
- **Pacing:** Poor. Session drifted into peripheral conversations without clinical focus.
- **Guided Discovery:** Absent. Therapist relied on direct advice rather than Socratic questioning.
- **Focus on Key Cognitions/Behaviors:** Minimal. Central anxiety mechanisms (catastrophic thoughts, checking, reassurance-seeking, avoidance) not targeted.
- **Strategy for Change:** Absent. No coherent treatment plan or evidence-based approach articulated.
- **CBT Techniques:** None applied. Session was supportive counseling, not CBT.
- **Homework:** None assigned. No between-session work to extend learning.

**Clinical Concern:**

For a 12-year-old with separation anxiety disorder and panic attacks—a condition with strong empirical support for exposure-based CBT—this represents a significant clinical failure. The patient deserves evidence-based treatment. Without immediate intervention and training, this therapist will continue to provide ineffective care.

**Supervisor Recommendation:**

Schedule immediate meeting with therapist to discuss findings. Require completion of formal CBT training (e.g., CBT for Anxiety Disorders in Children certification program). Implement weekly supervision with session recording review. Reassess competency after 8 weeks of intensive training and supervised practice. Consider temporary case reassignment if therapist is unwilling to commit to training.
