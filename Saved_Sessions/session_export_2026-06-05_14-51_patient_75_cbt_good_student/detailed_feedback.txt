
# CBT Session Evaluation Prompt

## Role
You are evaluating a Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) session. Your goal is to provide rigorous, evidence-based feedback to help trainees become excellent CBT therapists. Be strict and thorough—lenient feedback harms future patients.

## Input
You will receive a conversation transcript with:
- **Therapist:** [response]
- **Patient:** [response]

Base evaluation ONLY on transcript content. Ignore typos.

## Scoring Scale (0-6)
- **0:** Not addressed
- **1:** Minimal/inadequate
- **2:** Poor with significant gaps
- **3:** Below average; inconsistent
- **4:** Adequate; basic requirements met
- **5:** Good; consistent with minor gaps
- **6:** Excellent; exemplary throughout

**Key Standards:**
- Score based on evidence-based CBT, not general counseling
- Scores 5-6 reserved for truly excellent technique
- Basic empathy alone doesn't warrant high scores
- Always cite specific transcript examples
- Identify what was NOT done that should have been
- Be rigorous—this prevents patient harm

## Evaluation Criteria (11 Categories)

### 1. Agenda
Setting agenda for session, establishing goals, prioritizing topics collaboratively, maintaining session structure

### 2. Feedback
Eliciting patient feedback about session, checking understanding, asking for patient's takeaways, soliciting input on helpfulness

### 3. Understanding
Therapist's comprehension of patient's concerns, accurate reflection, demonstrating grasp of patient's experience and perspective

### 4. Interpersonal Effectiveness
Warmth, empathy, genuineness, appropriate professional boundaries, creating safe therapeutic environment, rapport building

### 5. Collaboration
Working as partners, shared decision-making, soliciting patient input, collaborative empiricism, patient actively engaged in process

### 6. Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Session flow, time management, appropriate depth on topics, avoiding tangents, covering important material without rushing or dragging

### 7. Guided Discovery
Using Socratic questions, facilitating self-discovery, asking rather than telling, helping patient reach insights independently

### 8. Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Identifying and exploring central automatic thoughts, core beliefs, or critical behavioral patterns relevant to presenting problems

### 9. Strategy for Change
Clear plan for symptom improvement, explicit treatment approach, logical progression toward goals, conceptualization guiding interventions

### 10. Application of CBT Techniques
Using specific CBT methods (cognitive restructuring, behavioral experiments, thought records, exposure, activity scheduling, etc.)

### 11. Homework
Assigning between-session tasks, providing clear rationale, ensuring specificity, checking patient understanding and commitment

## Score Requirements Examples

**For 6/6:** Explicit collaborative agenda with time allocation, 3+ instances of feedback elicitation, systematic application of CBT techniques, specific homework with clear rationale

**For 4/6:** Some collaborative language, 1-2 instances of technique use, basic implementation with room for improvement

**For 2/6:** Technique touched on but not systematically applied, significant gaps in execution

**For 0/6:** Technique completely absent from session

Odd numbers (1, 3, 5) represent intermediate ratings between descriptors. Ratings are based on the therapist’s skill level while accounting for patient difficulty.

## Recommendations

### 1. **Conduct Comprehensive Forensic Psychiatric Assessment (HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
- **Action**: Before proceeding with CBT interventions, complete a full forensic psychiatric evaluation including: (a) current psychotic symptoms (delusions, hallucinations, command hallucinations), (b) medication status and adherence, (c) substance use state and triggers, (d) violence risk assessment using validated instruments (e.g., HCR-20, STATIC-99R for sexual risk), (e) sexual risk assessment, (f) mental state at time of offense, (g) capacity to conform behavior to law.
- **Example**: "Before we work on coping strategies, I need to understand your current symptoms better. Are you still hearing voices? Do you still believe people are trying to harm you? Are you taking any medications? When was the last time you used cocaine or alcohol?"
- **Practice**: Review forensic assessment protocols and violence risk assessment instruments. Consult with forensic psychiatry colleagues on appropriate assessment procedures for this patient population.

### 2. **Distinguish Between Cognitive Distortions and Psychotic Delusions**
- **Action**: Assess the patient's insight into his thoughts. Use reality testing questions to determine whether thoughts are amenable to cognitive restructuring or represent fixed delusions requiring different intervention.
- **Example**: "You mentioned that you know 'maybe it wasn't' real that the man was going to expose you. Tell me more about that. How do you know when a thought is real versus when your mind might be playing tricks on you? Have there been times when you thought someone was out to get you, but it turned out they weren't?"
- **Practice**: Study the distinction between paranoid ideation and psychotic delusions. Learn reality testing techniques for working with delusions. Consult with psychosis specialists on appropriate interventions.

### 3. **Prioritize Medication Optimization and Adherence**
- **Action**: Assess current medication status, adherence barriers, and side effects. Collaborate with prescribing psychiatrist on medication optimization. Make medication adherence a primary treatment goal.
- **Example**: "I see from your chart that you were on risperidone before. Are you taking any medications now? What made it hard to take them in the past? What would help you take them more consistently?"
- **Practice**: Learn about antipsychotic medications, their efficacy for different psychotic symptoms, and common adherence barriers. Develop motivational interviewing skills for addressing medication nonadherence.

### 4. **Conduct Comprehensive Violence Risk Assessment and Develop Safety Plan**
- **Action**: Use validated violence risk assessment instruments (HCR-20, VRAG). Identify specific warning signs, triggers, and protective factors. Develop a detailed safety plan including: (a) warning signs for escalation, (b) de-escalation techniques, (c) crisis contacts, (d) what to do if urges to harm emerge, (e) environmental modifications.
- **Example**: "Let's talk about what happens right before you feel like you might hurt someone. What are the warning signs? What triggers you? What helps you calm down? If you feel like you might hurt someone again, what should you do?"
- **Practice**: Complete training in violence risk assessment. Learn de-escalation and safety planning techniques. Develop protocols for monitoring and follow-up.

### 5. **Assess and Address Substance Use as a Maintaining Factor**
- **Action**: Conduct comprehensive substance use assessment including: (a) current use patterns, (b) triggers for use, (c) relationship between substance use and psychotic symptoms/aggression, (d) treatment history and barriers. Integrate substance use treatment into the overall treatment plan.
- **Example**: "When you use cocaine or alcohol, do you notice your thoughts about people being out to get you get stronger? Do you feel more likely to act on those thoughts? What would help you stay away from drugs and alcohol?"
- **Practice**: Learn about substance use disorders and their relationship to psychosis and aggression. Develop motivational interviewing and relapse prevention skills. Consult with addiction specialists.

### 6. **Assess Sexual Risk and Develop Sexual Safety Planning**
- **Action**: Conduct comprehensive sexual risk assessment including: (a) current sexual urges and fantasies, (b) risk factors for sexual offending, (c) access to potential victims, (d) previous sexual offenses and patterns. Develop a detailed sexual safety plan.
- **Example**: "I know from your history that you've had sexual contact with children. Are you still having those urges? What situations put you at risk? What would help you stay safe and not act on those urges?"
- **Practice**: Complete training in sexual risk assessment. Learn about evidence-based interventions for sexual offenders (e.g., relapse prevention, offense chain analysis). Consult with sexual offender specialists.
