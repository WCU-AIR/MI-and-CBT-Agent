## CRITICAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMMEDIATE IMPLEMENTATION

**1. Establish Structured Agenda-Setting** – Begin every session with explicit collaborative agenda. Ask: "What would be most helpful to focus on today?"

**2. Eliminate Personal Self-Disclosure** – Stop sharing personal anecdotes. Replace with Socratic questions about patient's experience.

**3. Learn and Apply CBT Techniques** – Develop competence in thought records, behavioral activation, cognitive restructuring, and rumination interruption. Start with these foundational techniques.

**4. Implement Guided Discovery** – Replace advice-giving with skillful Socratic questioning that helps patient reach own insights.

**5. Assign Structured Homework** – Assign 1-2 specific, tailored homework tasks with clear rationale at end of each session. Review at start of next session.

**6. Develop Treatment Conceptualization** – Create written case formulation explaining how patient's thoughts, behaviors, and emotions interact to maintain depression. Use this to guide all interventions.

**7. Address Trauma-Related Beliefs** – Recognize social anxiety and avoidance as trauma-rooted, not simple shyness. Use trauma-informed CBT approaches.

**8. Maintain Session Continuity** – Review previous session content and homework at start of each session. Build progressively rather than starting fresh.

**9. Pursue Formal CBT Training** – Complete accredited CBT training (Beck Institute, ABCT). Attend supervision focused on CBT technique development.

**10. Distinguish CBT from Supportive Counseling** – Understand that while supportive counseling is valuable, it is not evidence-based CBT. Commit to learning and implementing specific CBT methodology.

**OVERALL COMPETENCY ASSESSMENT:** Therapist demonstrates basic counseling skills but lacks CBT competence. This is not evidence-based treatment for persistent depressive disorder. Immediate comprehensive training and intensive supervision required.
