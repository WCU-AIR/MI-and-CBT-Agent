## CRITICAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMMEDIATE ACTION

### 1. **Coordinate Medical Intervention (URGENT)**
Explicitly discuss medication adjustment options with prescribing psychiatrist in next session. Offer to facilitate communication or provide patient with specific talking points about dose reduction, medication change (e.g., bupropion), or adjunctive agents (sildenafil, buspirone augmentation).

### 2. **Provide Psychoeducation**
Educate patient that sexual dysfunction is a predictable, reversible side effect of clomipramine—not a sign of psychological pathology, not permanent, and not a reflection of masculinity or relationship quality.

### 3. **Establish Structured, Collaborative Agenda**
At start of each session, explicitly set 2-3 specific priorities with patient input. Allocate time and follow agenda. Review prior session goals and homework.

### 4. **Systematically Elicit Feedback**
Ask for feedback at least 5 times per session: "Does this make sense?" "How are you feeling about this?" "Is this something you can actually do?" "What's your reaction?" "What's the most important thing you're taking away?"

### 5. **Involve Patient's Wife**
With patient's permission, conduct at least one conjoint session to discuss medication-side-effect relationship, reduce shame, and develop joint problem-solving approach.

### 6. **Reframe Catastrophic Thoughts as Rational Concerns**
Stop treating thoughts ("What if this is permanent?") as cognitive distortions. Instead, acknowledge as reasonable concerns and address through medical intervention and reassurance based on accurate information.

### 7. **Develop Competence in Medication-Induced Sexual Dysfunction**
Seek training on: (a) which medications cause sexual side effects, (b) mechanisms, (c) medical management options, (d) psychoeducation strategies, (e) behavioral management. Consult with psychiatrist about this patient's medication options.
