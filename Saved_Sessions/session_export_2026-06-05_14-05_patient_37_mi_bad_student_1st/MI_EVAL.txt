### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Psychiatrist does attempt to address sustain talk present in client speech ("I'm not ready," "I haven't set a quit date," "I'm not ready to commit to patches tomorrow"). Attempts to address through reframing urgency ("You can't just wait around"), highlighting consequences ("Your health will decline," "Your life depends on it"), and challenging premises ("You don't get to pick and choose"). Fails to explore underlying concerns driving sustain talk—client's hesitation stems from autonomy concerns, not lack of motivation. Better approach would address this: "It sounds like you want to make sure this is your decision and you're truly ready. What would help you feel ready?" Somewhat effective at redirecting but misses opportunity to understand and address root concerns.

## **OVERALL SESSION ASSESSMENT**

### **Overall Score: 1.2/5**

---

### **Strengths:**
- Maintains consistent focus on target behavior (smoking cessation)
- Asks some factual questions about smoking frequency
- Attempts to address sustain talk (though poorly executed)
- Expresses concern for client's health (though expressed in harmful ways)

---

### **Critical Areas for Improvement:**

1. **Complete absence of acceptance and unconditional positive regard** — Judgment, shame, and dismissal pervasive throughout
2. **Zero empathic listening** — Not a single reflective listening statement in entire session
3. **Authoritarian stance** — Positions psychiatrist as sole expert; client as passive recipient of orders
4. **Active suppression of autonomy** — Explicitly removes choice; uses coercive language
5. **Failure to evoke** — Substantial change talk completely ignored and unamplified
6. **Mechanical repetition** — Identical opening repeated three times, suggesting contempt for client input
7. **Counterproductive approach** — Coercive strategy triggers psychological reactance; client becomes increasingly resistant
8. **Missed opportunities** — Multiple chances to build on client motivation, explore values, and collaboratively problem-solve completely missed

---

### **Impact on Client Engagement and Change:**

**This session is actively harmful to client motivation.** Client enters with genuine change talk and openness to exploration. By session end, client has become increasingly defensive and resistant, explicitly stating "I dig in my heels," "it makes me shut down," and "I pull back." This is textbook psychological reactance: when autonomy is threatened, people resist even changes they support. Likelihood of client following through on smoking cessation is extremely low—not because client lacks motivation, but because change has been imposed rather than self-directed.

---

### **Key Recommendations:**

1. **Complete formal MI training** (MINT-certified) focused on MI spirit and genuine acceptance
2. **Master reflective listening** as primary response mode; implement reflect-then-ask structure
3. **Eliminate coercive language** systematically; replace with collaborative exploration
4. **Develop empathic accuracy** to distinguish between "I don't want to change" and "I want to change on my own terms"
5. **Cultivate change talk** by recognizing and amplifying client motivation
6. **Support autonomy** by emphasizing client choice and self-direction
7. **Establish true partnership** by seeking and incorporating client input
8. **Recognize psychological reactance** and respond by backing off and supporting autonomy
9. **Eliminate mechanical repetition** by adjusting approach based on client feedback
10. **Engage in regular clinical supervision** with MI-trained supervisor; use MITI scale for objective assessment

---

### **Conclusion:**

This session represents profound departure from MI principles and spirit. Psychiatrist operates from authoritarian, coercive stance that actively undermines client's intrinsic motivation. Rather than evoking client's own reasons for change, psychiatrist imposes reasons. Rather than supporting autonomy, psychiatrist suppresses it. Rather than building partnership, psychiatrist establishes hierarchy.

**Without foundational shift in worldview—from "I know what's best" to "Client has wisdom about their own life"—no amount of technique training will be effective.** Psychiatrist must genuinely believe in client's capacity for self-direction and respect client's autonomy, even when disagreeing with choices. This is essence of MI spirit, and it is completely absent in this session.

**Expected outcome: Poor treatment adherence and continued resistance to change.**
