## SUPERVISOR NOTES FOR CLINICAL CONFERENCE

**Session Quality Assessment:** This session does not meet standards for evidence-based CBT for a patient with moderate cocaine use disorder and substance-induced depressive disorder.

**Immediate Safety Concerns:**
- Passive suicidal ideation documented but never assessed in therapy
- No safety plan established
- No crisis resources provided
- Therapist unaware of safety assessment requirement

**Clinical Conceptualization Gaps:**
- No understanding of temporal relationship between cocaine use (9 months) and depression onset
- No recognition that substance-induced depression requires different treatment approach than primary depression
- No functional analysis of substance use triggers or consequences
- No case formulation developed or shared with patient

**Treatment Mismatch:**
- Diagnosis: Moderate cocaine use disorder + substance-induced depressive disorder
- Treatment provided: Generic cognitive restructuring
- Evidence-based treatment required: Substance use intervention (motivational interviewing, contingency management), safety planning, behavioral activation, substance use monitoring

**Specific Skill Deficits:**
- Minimal use of Socratic questioning; relies on directives
- Poor application of cognitive restructuring; does not address patient's explicit resistance
- No systematic homework review (critical failure in session 2)
- Vague, unmeasurable homework assignments
- No behavioral activation planning
- No substance use assessment or intervention

**Positive Elements:**
- Basic empathy and warmth demonstrated
- Patient remains engaged
- Non-judgmental tone established
- Some identification of relevant cognitions

**Recommendations for Supervision:**
1. Require immediate training in substance use disorder assessment and treatment
2. Require training in suicide risk assessment and safety planning
3. Require training in case conceptualization for comorbid conditions
4. Require training in motivational interviewing and contingency management
5. Require training in proper application of CBT techniques with skill and adaptation
6. Require review of CBT protocol standards, particularly homework review procedures
7. Recommend intensive supervision (weekly) until competency demonstrated
8. Consider reassignment of substance use cases until training completed

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit:** Low. Without addressing underlying cocaine use, patient's depression unlikely to remit significantly. Poor application of cognitive restructuring and patient's explicit resistance suggest low homework compliance. Mismatch between treatment offered and treatment needed may lead to dropout or continued substance use escalation.
