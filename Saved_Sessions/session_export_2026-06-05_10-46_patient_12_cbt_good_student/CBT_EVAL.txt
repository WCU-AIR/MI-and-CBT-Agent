## SUMMARY OF CLINICAL COMPETENCIES

### **Strengths:**
- ✓ Strong agenda-setting with explicit establishment of clear, collaborative priorities
- ✓ Accurate understanding and empathic reflection of patient's thoughts, feelings, and behaviors
- ✓ Genuine warmth, patience, and appropriate professional boundaries throughout session
- ✓ Effective guided discovery using Socratic questioning and perspective-taking
- ✓ Coherent, evidence-based CBT strategy with logical sequencing
- ✓ Skillful application of core cognitive restructuring techniques (thought records, evidence examination)
- ✓ Collaborative stance with genuine invitation for patient input
- ✓ Concrete, relevant homework assignment directly testing alternative thought

### **Critical Deficiencies:**

**1. Insufficient Tailoring to Neurodevelopmental Profile**
- Therapist treated patient largely as typical adolescent with anxiety, missing opportunity to address autism-specific rigidity, need for predictability, and social communication deficits
- Did not explore how autism (distinct from intellectual disability) drives distress or leverage patient's strengths

**2. Narrow Focus on Surface Cognitions Rather Than Core Beliefs**
- Focused on specific thought about coloring rather than underlying rigid beliefs about predictability ("things must stay the same")
- Did not address social anxiety cognitions ("I will bother people") or learned helplessness
- These deeper cognitions likely drive distress more fundamentally

**3. Insufficient Behavioral Component**
- Minimal behavioral interventions; no graded exposure to schedule changes
- No behavioral activation for reported apathy
- No formal coping skills training or environmental modifications (advance notice, visual schedules)
- For patient with severe autism and intellectual disability, behavioral and environmental interventions often more powerful than cognitive work alone

**4. Missing Systems Approach (CRITICAL)**
- Did not involve parents, teachers, or school team in session or treatment planning
- For patient with severe intellectual disability relying on external support systems, this is critical omission
- No coordination of environmental modifications or support system involvement in homework monitoring

**5. Insufficient Scaffolding and Support for Homework**
- Homework lacks specificity, written instructions, visual supports, and explicit support system involvement
- No written plan provided; no involvement of parents/teachers in monitoring
- No clear success criteria established; no concrete strategies developed to overcome identified barriers

**6. Limited Exploration of Self-Injury Function and Alternatives**
- Identified biting as coping mechanism but did not explore function (emotion regulation vs. self-stimulation vs. communication)
- No explicit alternatives developed
- Important treatment target for patient engaging in self-injury

**7. No Measurement or Progress Monitoring Plan**
- Did not establish specific, measurable goals beyond initial mood rating
- No plan for tracking progress or adjusting treatment based on outcomes
- Essential for ongoing treatment with this population

**8. Insufficient Feedback Elicitation and Consolidation**
- No systematic checking of comprehension of CBT concepts
- No end-of-session consolidation of learning
- No explicit request for patient to summarize takeaways
- For patient with intellectual disability, this reinforcement is critical

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

**HIGHEST PRIORITY (Implement Immediately):**

1. **Conduct comprehensive functional assessment** of anxiety and rigidity to distinguish autism-related rigidity from anxiety from learned helplessness
2. **Involve parents and school team** in treatment planning and coordination; establish system for monitoring homework and progress
3. **Develop graded exposure hierarchy** for schedule changes, starting with small, predictable changes and gradually increasing unpredictability
4. **Provide explicit scaffolding and visual supports** for homework (laminated cards, written steps, tracking sheets)
5. **Establish environmental modifications** through school coordination (advance notice of changes, visual schedules, structured transitions)

**IMPORTANT (Implement Within 1-2 Sessions):**

6. Address social anxiety and learned helplessness directly through behavioral experiments and role-play
7. Explore and address function of self-injury; develop explicit alternatives based on function
8. Establish measurement system to track progress (frequency of anxiety, self-injury, advocacy attempts, tolerance duration)
9. Develop structured homework monitoring system with parents/teachers; include troubleshooting plan for barriers
10. Provide psychoeducation about autism, rigidity, and anxiety to patient and family

**ONGOING:**

11. Plan for generalization and maintenance of gains; gradually reduce environmental supports as patient becomes more independent
12. Expand treatment goals to include broader functioning (tolerance for unpredictability, confidence in self-advocacy, reduced self-injury, increased engagement in non-preferred activities)

## CLINICAL IMPRESSION

**Competency Level:** Therapist demonstrates **solid foundational CBT skills** appropriate for higher-functioning patients or less complex presentations. However, **additional training is needed** for patients with severe intellectual disability and autism in: (a) neurodevelopmental assessment and formulation, (b) systems-based intervention, (c) behavioral and environmental modification, (d) scaffolding and support for implementation, (e) measurement and progress monitoring.

**Likelihood of Therapeutic Benefit:** Session demonstrates competent, evidence-based CBT that would likely produce **some short-term benefit**. Patient developed more balanced perspective and identified concrete coping strategy. However, **sustainability and generalization are at risk** without support system involvement, explicit scaffolding, and attention to underlying autism-related rigidity and social anxiety.

**Long-Term Outcomes:** **Limited by narrow scope of treatment.** Session addresses only immediate anxiety response to schedule changes, not broader pattern of rigidity, social withdrawal, and learned helplessness. Without addressing deeper patterns and involving support system in coordinated intervention, treatment gains may be temporary.

**Critical Concern:** For patient with severe intellectual disability and autism, **individual cognitive therapy alone is insufficient.** Most effective treatment would integrate: (1) cognitive work (provided), (2) behavioral interventions (exposure, skills training), (3) environmental modifications (advance notice, visual supports), and (4) family/school coordination. Current session provides only component 1, limiting impact.
