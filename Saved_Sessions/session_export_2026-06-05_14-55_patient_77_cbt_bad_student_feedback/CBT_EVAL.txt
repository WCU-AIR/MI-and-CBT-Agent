**Patient:** Thomas, 8-year-old with mild-to-moderate intellectual developmental disorder, pica (recent emergency endoscopy for lead ingestion), constipation, abdominal pain, behavioral isolation, anxiety, and depression. Recent family move 4 months prior correlating with symptom onset.

**Session Quality:** Inadequate. This session does not meet standards for evidence-based CBT and is unlikely to produce therapeutic change.

**Strengths Identified:**

- Therapist acknowledged patient's pain and distress
- Therapist attempted to be warm and supportive

**Critical Deficiencies:**
- Complete absence of CBT framework (no agenda, no conceptualization, no strategy, no techniques)
- Failure to assess pica—the presenting problem was never explored
- Missed clinical presentation (depression, anxiety, behavioral withdrawal, stressor impact)
- Non-specific, generic support repeated ineffectively across sessions
- No collaboration or shared decision-making
- Inappropriate self-disclosure undermining professional boundaries
- No parent engagement
- No homework or between-session accountability

**Risk Assessment:** Without appropriate intervention, Thomas is at risk for escalating pica behavior (medical complications), worsening anxiety and depression, deepening behavioral withdrawal, and functional decline.

**Supervision Plan:** Intensive weekly supervision required. Recommend pairing with experienced CBT supervisor to review sessions, provide feedback, and model appropriate technique. Focus on: (1) functional assessment skills, (2) treatment conceptualization, (3) CBT technique application, (4) parent engagement, (5) adaptation for intellectual disability.
