## Recommendations

### 1. Master Session Structure
- **Begin every session with collaborative agenda-setting:** "What would you like to focus on today? I was thinking we might also discuss X. Does that sound good?"
- **Bridge from previous session:** Review homework, recap previous insights
- **Manage time explicitly:** Allocate time to agenda items and track progress
- **End with summary and feedback:** Collaboratively recap and elicit patient's takeaways

### 2. Teach and Apply the CBT Model
- **Explicitly teach the cognitive model:** Use diagrams to show how situations trigger thoughts, which influence feelings and behaviors
- **Apply the model to patient's examples:** "Let's map out what happened using the CBT model"
- **Develop a case conceptualization:** Share your understanding of maintaining cycles and patterns
- **Check patient's understanding:** "Can you explain back to me how thoughts and feelings are connected?"

### 3. Systematically Identify and Evaluate Automatic Thoughts
- **Elicit hot thoughts:** "What went through your mind right at that moment? What was the worst part about that?"
- **Use thought records:** Introduce and practice the 7-column thought record
- **Examine evidence:** "What evidence supports this thought? What evidence goes against it?"
- **Generate alternatives:** "What's another way of seeing this? What would you tell a friend?"
- **Develop balanced thoughts:** Help patient create realistic, evidence-based perspectives

### 4. Identify and Label Cognitive Distortions
- **Teach common thinking traps:** Provide handouts on cognitive distortions
- **Label patterns when they arise:** "That sounds like catastrophizing - expecting the worst-case scenario"
- **Help patient self-identify:** "Do you notice any thinking patterns here? Which distortion might this be?"

### 5. Incorporate Behavioral Interventions
- **Activity monitoring and scheduling:** Help patient track and plan activities
- **Behavioral experiments:** Design tests of predictions and beliefs
- **Graded task assignments:** Break overwhelming tasks into manageable steps
- **Exposure hierarchies:** For anxiety, develop gradual exposure plans

### 6. Assign and Review Homework Every Session
- **Collaboratively develop homework:** "What would be helpful to practice this week?"
- **Explain rationale:** "Homework helps you practice new skills and see what works"
- **Make it specific and manageable:** Clear, concrete assignments with written summary
- **Review in next session:** Start every session with homework review and problem-solving

### 7. Enhance Guided Discovery
- **Use Socratic questioning:** Ask questions that lead patient to insights rather than providing answers
- **Ask for evidence and examples:** "What makes you think that? Can you give me a specific example?"
- **Explore implications:** "If that thought is true, what does it mean? How does that affect you?"
- **Facilitate self-correction:** Lead patient to discover contradictions or alternatives themselves

### 8. Provide Comprehensive Psychoeducation
- **Normalize experiences:** "Many people with anxiety have thoughts like this"
- **Explain diagnoses and symptoms:** Use patient-friendly language and analogies
- **Teach specific skills:** Introduce techniques with clear rationale and demonstration
- **Use written materials:** Provide handouts, diagrams, and resources

### 9. Work at Multiple Levels
- **Automatic thoughts:** Day-to-day thinking in specific situations
- **Intermediate beliefs:** Rules, assumptions, attitudes (e.g., "If I'm not perfect, I'm a failure")
- **Core beliefs:** Deep schemas about self, others, world (e.g., "I'm unlovable")
- **Use downward arrow:** "If that's true, what does it mean about you?"

### 10. Strengthen Collaboration
- **Explicitly share power:** "You're the expert on your life; I'm the expert on CBT. Together we'll figure this out"
- **Check in frequently:** "Does this make sense? Are you with me?"
- **Invite feedback:** "What's working? What's not? How can I be more helpful?"
- **Adjust based on input:** Demonstrate flexibility and responsiveness

### 11. Develop Emotional Awareness and Regulation
- **Identify and label emotions:** Help patient expand emotional vocabulary
- **Teach emotion regulation skills:** Grounding, mindfulness, distress tolerance
- **Connect emotions to thoughts and behaviors:** Make explicit links in the moment
- **Practice coping strategies:** Role-play or rehearse techniques in session

### 12. Ensure Session Summary and Feedback
- **Collaboratively summarize:** "What are your main takeaways from today?"
- **Elicit patient's perspective:** "What was most helpful? What wasn't?"
- **Recap homework:** Review specific assignments and rationale
- **Address concerns:** "Is there anything about today's session that confused you or bothered you?"

### 13. Continuous Self-Evaluation
- **Record and review sessions:** Identify missed opportunities and areas for improvement
- **Use CBT competency scales:** Regularly assess your adherence to CBT model
- **Seek supervision:** Get feedback from experienced CBT supervisors
- **Study CBT manuals and research:** Stay current with evidence-based protocols
