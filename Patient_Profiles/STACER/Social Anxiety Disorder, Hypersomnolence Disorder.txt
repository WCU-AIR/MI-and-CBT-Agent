--- DISORDER ---
Social Anxiety Disorder, Hypersomnolence Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Bernadette Kleber was a 34-year-old divorced, unemployed white mother of three school-age children, living with a new companion. She presented to a psychiatrist for anxiety and excessive sleepiness.

Ms. Kleber had experienced anxiety much of her life, worsening since the birth of her first child 10 years ago. She was "okay at home" but anxious in social situations, avoiding new people for fear of embarrassment or judgment. She wanted to lose weight (current BMI 27.7) but feared ridicule at the gym. She had withdrawn from social and professional interactions, making it almost impossible to interview or maintain a job. She had been successfully treated for social anxiety disorder 5 years earlier with psychotherapy, an SSRI, and clonazepam 0.25 mg twice daily, but her symptoms had recurred over the prior year. She denied changing her medication dose or taking other medications.

She denied periods of significant depression or manic symptoms but reported feeling frustrated with her limited effectiveness. She was concerned about maintaining her new romantic relationship.

Ms. Kleber reported sleeping excessively: at least 9 hours per night plus two 2.5-hour naps daily. She had difficulty functioning in the mornings, feeling disoriented for at least 30 minutes after waking, and often fell asleep during quiet social situations, while reading, or watching TV. She quit driving alone due to sleepiness. Late afternoon naps were nonrestorative, and hypnopompic hallucinations occurred several times per year since adolescence.

On examination, she was overweight, cooperative, and coherent, preoccupied with her sleep problems, denying depression, suicidality, psychosis, and memory complaints. Insight and judgment were intact. Physical exam was noncontributory. Medical history included hypercholesterolemia, occasional migraines, and exertion-related musculoskeletal complaints. She used marijuana occasionally for pain and clonazepam for anxiety. No family history of sleep or mood disorders, though multiple relatives were anxious.

Sleep studies: polysomnography showed apnea hypopnea index of 3 events/hour. MSLT indicated mean sleep latency of 7 minutes with one sleep-onset REM period. CSF hypocretin-1 levels were normal. Findings were consistent with hypersomnolence disorder. Social anxiety disorder was recurrent.

--- SYMPTOMS from DSM-5 ---

- Hypersomnolence: excessive sleep duration, multiple naps, daytime sleepiness, sleep inertia, nonrestorative sleep, brief sleep latency
