--- DISORDER ---
Bulimia Nervosa; Major Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Wanda Hoffman was a 24-year-old woman referred to a psychiatrist by her internist for feelings of sadness, worthlessness, and loneliness. She reported low energy, poor sleep, stomachaches, and difficulty exercising. She had been living alone since college graduation 2 years earlier and recently received a promotion as an accountant. She maintained some social contacts but felt too fatigued and sad to engage in activities beyond brief dinners with friends.

Ms. Hoffman expressed concern about weight gain and requested an antidepressant that would not cause weight gain, hoping for one that might promote weight loss. She disclosed that she was a "failed anorexic," describing a long-standing pattern of dieting since age 13, followed by episodes of binge eating and self-induced vomiting that began in college. Her typical eating pattern included skipping breakfast, light lunch, average dinner, followed by a late-night binge and purging.

Discussion: Ms. Hoffman exhibits symptoms of major depressive disorder, including depressed mood, anhedonia, poor sleep, low energy, somatic complaints, feelings of worthlessness, and diminished concentration. She denies suicidal intent but has thoughts of death. Her eating history is consistent with bulimia nervosa (BN), characterized by recurrent binge eating, inappropriate compensatory behaviors (self-induced vomiting), and undue influence of body weight and shape on self-evaluation. The binge/purge cycle occurs at least weekly and has persisted for several years. BN is commonly comorbid with MDD, which is reflected in Ms. Hoffman's presentation.
