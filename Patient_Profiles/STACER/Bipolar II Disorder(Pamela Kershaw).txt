--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar II Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Pamela Kershaw was a 43-year-old married librarian who presented to an outpatient mental health clinic with a long history of episodic depressions.
Most recently, she reported depressed mood since starting a new job one month earlier, with preoccupation about her work performance and social interactions. She had no energy or enthusiasm at home, preferring to watch television, overeat, and oversleep, leading to a 6-pound weight gain. She began crying several times per week and thought often of death, though she had never attempted suicide.

Her depressive history began in her teens, with at least five adult depressive episodes characterized by depressed mood, low energy, hypersomnia, hyperphagia, guilt, decreased libido, and mild to moderate suicidal ideation without plan. These episodes were punctuated by brief periods of elevated energy, irritability, pressured speech, and flight of ideas, lasting hours to days, sometimes up to a week. During these periods, she appeared unusually happy, talkative, self-confident, productive, and needed little sleep—consistent with hypomania.

Ms. Kershaw denied alcohol or substance misuse. Family history included a maternal aunt and grandfather with recurrent mania. Previous psychotherapy provided short-term relief but was interrupted by depressive episodes.

On examination, she was well-groomed, overweight, soft-spoken, often averting her eyes. Movements were constrained, affect was sad and constricted, thought processes fluid but possibly slowed, thought content depressive with passive suicidal ideation, no hallucinations, delusions, or paranoia. Insight and judgment intact.

Discussion: Ms. Kershaw’s recurrent depressive episodes and history of hypomanic periods support a bipolar II disorder diagnosis. Her hypomanic episodes are often brief but, with a prior 6-day episode, meet criteria for bipolar II. Accurate diagnosis is essential for effective management, including monitoring suicide risk and functional impairment.
