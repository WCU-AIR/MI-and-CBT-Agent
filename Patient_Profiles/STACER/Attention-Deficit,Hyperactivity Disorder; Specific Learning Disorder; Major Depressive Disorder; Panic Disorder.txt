--- DISORDER ---
Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder; Specific Learning Disorder; Major Depressive Disorder; Panic Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Carlos was a 19-year-old Hispanic college freshman referred by his guidance counselor for academic difficulties. Since starting college 6 months earlier, he had struggled with test performance, managing his study schedule, and maintaining concentration, resulting in anxiety, insomnia, poor concentration, and hopelessness. After a difficult week, he told his family he thought he should quit college.

Carlos had a childhood diagnosis of ADHD, combined type, at age 9. He had difficulty following instructions, completing homework, remaining seated, and concentrating except for video games. Psychoeducational testing at that time revealed reading fluency and comprehension difficulties, but he did not meet the criteria for a learning disability. His family supported him with tutoring rather than medication.

Since starting college, Carlos reported restlessness, agitation, and worry. He had trouble sleeping, low energy, and reduced enjoyment in activities. His depressive symptoms fluctuated during the week and were compounded by social stressors, including being the only Dominican student on campus and questions about sexual orientation. He denied substance use.

Collateral history revealed a childhood history of ataques de nervios, with trembling, agitation, palpitations, shortness of breath, dread of harm, and fear of separation from caregivers, treated with supportive prayer and herbal remedies. Family history is positive for ADHD in a younger brother and a cousin with dyslexia.

On examination, Carlos was well-groomed, polite, soft-spoken, hunched, and avoided eye contact. He tapped his fingers and shuffled in his seat but responded appropriately. He spoke English fluently with a slight Spanish accent and occasional mispronunciations. He denied suicidal thoughts and appeared motivated to improve academically.

Updated psychoeducational testing revealed moderately severe reading and writing difficulties, confirming a Specific Learning Disorder.

Discussion: Carlos has a history of childhood ADHD, current academic difficulties, mild major depressive episode, and panic symptoms in the context of transition stress and acculturation. He meets DSM-5 criteria for ADHD (inattentive type), SLD, and mild depression. Academic accommodations are indicated to support his college performance.
