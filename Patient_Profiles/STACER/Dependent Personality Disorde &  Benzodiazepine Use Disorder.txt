--- DISORDER ---
Dependent Personality Disorder; Benzodiazepine Use Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Nate Irvin was a 31-year-old single white man who sought outpatient psychiatric services for "lack of self-confidence."
He reported lifelong difficulties with assertiveness and was frustrated by being "stuck" for 2 years at his current administrative assistant job. He wished someone would tell him what to do next to avoid making independent decisions. At work, he followed directions easily but struggled with even minor independent choices. He described the situation as "depressing," though not new.

Mr. Irvin also reported dissatisfaction in his relationships with women. Over the past 10 years, he had multiple short-term relationships that ended despite his efforts to please and remain calm. His most recent relationship was with an opera singer, which ended for unclear reasons. He linked his mood and self-confidence to his dating life, describing a cycle of desperation that made forming relationships difficult. Since the breakup, he had experienced sadness and frequent crying spells but denied other depressive symptoms, including sleep, appetite, energy changes, suicidality, or anhedonia.

He initially denied medications but later reported that he had been prescribed alprazolam 0.5 mg/day for anxiety one year earlier. Over time, his dose escalated to 5 mg/day, with prescriptions obtained from three different physicians. Attempting to reduce his dose caused anxiety and tremors.

Mr. Irvin denied any prior personal or family psychiatric history. The evaluating psychiatrist was concerned about his escalating benzodiazepine use and chronic difficulties with independence. For clinical documentation, however, his official diagnosis was recorded as "adjustment disorder with depressed mood," though he met criteria for dependent personality disorder and benzodiazepine use disorder.

Discussion: Mr. Irvin demonstrates an excessive need for care and decision-making by others, difficulty initiating tasks independently, discomfort being alone, and reluctance to disagree on minor matters. These behaviors meet at least six of eight DSM-5 criteria for dependent personality disorder, are enduring, inflexible, pervasive, and cause significant distress and impairment. His benzodiazepine use meets criteria for a substance use disorder due to tolerance, withdrawal, and impairment. Though depressive symptoms are present, he does not meet full criteria for a depressive disorder.

Diagnoses:

Dependent Personality Disorder

Benzodiazepine Use Disorder
