--- DISORDER ---
Somatic Symptom Disorder (with Predominant Pain), Cannabis Use Disorder, Major Depressive Disorder, Single Episode, Chronic

--- CASE STUDY ---

Michelle Adams, a 51-year-old former hairdresser, was referred to psychiatry by her primary care physician due to persistent back pain and associated depressive symptoms. She sustained multiple fractures (pelvis, coccyx, right elbow, and ribs) after a fall while attempting to enter her apartment via a fire escape. Despite several months of rehabilitation, medical procedures, and daily opioid use, her pain remained severe and disabling.

Ms. Adams reported being socially isolated, unable to work, and experiencing disrupted sleep, poor self-care, and lack of motivation. She described daily cannabis use to manage pain and anxiety. She denied current suicidal ideation and had no prior psychiatric history, though she acknowledged a family history of temper. Her social history included one emotionally abusive romantic relationship and past minor legal issues.

Discussion: Ms. Adams presents with persistent, medically unexplained pain that significantly impairs her functioning, meeting criteria for somatic symptom disorder (predominant pain). She developed secondary chronic depression and behaviorally dependent cannabis use. The interplay of persistent pain, mood disturbance, and substance use constitutes a “tri-morbidity,” with SSD as primary and depressive and substance use disorders as secondary.
