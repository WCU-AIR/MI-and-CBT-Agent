--- DISORDER ---
Cannabis-Induced Psychotic Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Kevin Foster was a 32-year-old married white male with a history of bipolar I disorder who was brought to the ER by police after threatening to jump out of a hotel window during a vacation with his wife. The episode followed smoking a marijuana cigarette after getting tattoos. He became delusionally paranoid about the tattoo symbols, the tattoo artist, and his wife’s fidelity, and threatened self-harm and harm to his wife.

Mr. Foster had a history of sporadic cannabis use since age 18, progressing to daily use over the past 5 years, with a recent increase to three times daily. He had four psychiatric hospitalizations in the past year: two for mania, one for suicidal depression, and one for a 6-week episode of cannabis-induced psychosis. He had been adherent to lithium monotherapy for 3 months prior to this episode. No other substances were used, and alcohol use was minimal.

During hospitalization, his psychotic symptoms resolved spontaneously within 3 days, and he gained insight that the delusions were caused by cannabis intoxication. He declined further antipsychotic medication and continued lithium.

Discussion: Psychotic symptoms emerged rapidly after cannabis use, without concurrent mood symptoms, and resolved within days. This pattern is consistent with a DSM-5 cannabis-induced psychotic disorder. Bipolar I disorder is in remission. Cannabis use disorder, moderate, is evident due to recurrent use despite adverse consequences and withdrawal-like anxiety when not using. Schizophrenia spectrum disorders and other psychotic disorders are unlikely due to rapid resolution of symptoms and limited affected domains.
