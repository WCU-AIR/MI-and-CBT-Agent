--- DISORDER ---
Major Depressive Disorder (in remission), Restless Legs Syndrome

--- CASE STUDY ---

Dingxiang Meng was a 63-year-old Chinese-born man referred for psychiatric consultation for depression and excessive somatic complaints. He had a history of psychotic depression with two prior hospitalizations over the past decade. At the time of evaluation, he was an outpatient in the renal unit during routine hemodialysis.

Mr. Meng was diagnosed with diabetes soon after immigrating to the United States 15 years earlier, which progressed to renal insufficiency; he began hemodialysis 7 years ago. He was divorced, with two adult children and three young grandchildren, living with one of his children. Before dialysis, he worked intermittently in a local grocery store; prior to emigrating, he had been a pharmacist. Interviews were conducted with a Mandarin interpreter due to limited English.

At evaluation, he described sensations of feeling "itchy, creepy, and crawly," like "worms crawling under his skin," worsening in recent weeks. Physical symptoms were mostly in the legs, relieved by movement or pacing, worsened by sedentary positions like dialysis. He also reported daytime sleepiness and fatigue. He did not display a thought disorder and cheered up when interacting with grandchildren or old friends. Past physical complaints had been conceptualized as akathisia, peripheral neuropathy, or psychosomatic/psychotically ruminative symptoms. He had been off psychiatric medications for 2 years and euthymic prior to this evaluation.

During the interview, he frequently stood up during dialysis, showing an intense urge to move. Nurses noted tiredness, nonadherence, and restlessness. His symptoms—worsening at night, improved by movement, frequent, chronic, and distressing—were consistent with restless legs syndrome (RLS), particularly common in patients with end-stage renal disease on dialysis. Sleep quality was reduced, contributing to daytime sleepiness. Other potential contributors such as anemia, folate deficiency, and uremia should be considered.
