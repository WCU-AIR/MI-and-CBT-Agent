--- DISORDER ---
Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD), with absent insight/delusional beliefs, with muscle dysmorphia

--- CASE STUDY ---

Vincent Mancini, a 26-year-old single white man, was brought for outpatient evaluation by his parents due to distressing preoccupations with his appearance. Since age 13, he had been excessively concerned with perceived defects in his skin, hair, ears, and muscularity, believing he looked “ugly and hideous” despite appearing normal. He felt certain that others ridiculed him for his appearance.

Mr. Mancini spent 5–6 hours daily performing compulsive behaviors, including mirror checking, hair styling, ear pulling, skin picking, weight lifting, and layering clothes to appear larger. He avoided social situations, did not attend college, and had frequent suicidal ideation. He used alcohol and marijuana to cope with distress related to his appearance. His mother has a history of OCD.

During evaluation, he was neatly dressed, wore a baseball cap, had poor eye contact, and was depressed and irritable. He was cognitively intact and denied psychotic symptoms aside from delusional beliefs about his appearance. He reported passive suicidal ideation.

Discussion: Mr. Mancini meets DSM-5 criteria for BDD due to preoccupation with perceived appearance defects, time-consuming repetitive behaviors, and significant functional impairment. He has absent insight/delusional beliefs because he is completely convinced of his perceived flaws. Muscle dysmorphia is also present due to preoccupation with insufficient muscularity. Co-occurring major depressive disorder is evident, secondary to BDD-related distress. Differential diagnoses such as social anxiety disorder, OCD, or psychotic disorders were considered and excluded.
