--- DISORDER ---
Hoarding Disorder, with excessive acquisition, with good or fair insight
Unspecified Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Wendy Nichols, a 47-year-old single white woman, was referred to a community mental health team for management of low mood and generalized anxiety. She had a history of a depressive episode 5 years earlier treated with cognitive-behavioral therapy but had never taken psychiatric medication. Ms. Nichols lived alone, had no nearby family or friends, and worked part-time as a sales assistant at a charity thrift shop.

She had long-standing difficulty discarding possessions, beginning in childhood. Over the years, her apartment became severely cluttered with books, papers, boxes, and miscellaneous items, leaving little functional space. Ms. Nichols continued to acquire items, including free newspapers and goods from work, and avoided inviting others into her home for at least 15 years. She experienced distress and anxiety when attempting to discard items or when others tried to intervene.

Despite her social isolation, she was articulate, coherent, and cooperative during evaluation. She denied psychotic symptoms, obsessions, or compulsions. Photographs confirmed extensive clutter filling her living spaces. She reported long-standing sadness, loneliness, and anxiety.

Discussion: Ms. Nichols meets DSM-5 criteria for hoarding disorder due to difficulty discarding possessions, distress related to discarding, and accumulation resulting in severely cluttered living spaces. The “excessive acquisition” specifier applies because she continues to obtain items unnecessarily. Her insight is good or fair as she recognizes the problem and seeks help. Her depressive symptoms are insufficiently detailed to diagnose major depressive disorder; therefore, unspecified depressive disorder is included. Other disorders, including OCD, neurocognitive, and psychotic disorders, were ruled out.
