--- DISORDER ---
Gender Dysphoria, Posttransition

--- CASE STUDY ---

Irene Rohmer, a 25-year-old salesperson, presented for psychiatric evaluation due to anxiety and depression, emphasizing that any assessment of mood needed to consider her gender journey.

Born male and assigned male at birth, she was teased as a child and preferred the company of girls. During adolescence, she considered herself a gay male and dated a few gay teens but felt like an outsider. At age 19, during a romantic relationship with a man, she recognized an intense desire to be a woman, evolving into a strong sense of being born the wrong gender. She experienced hopelessness and one nonlethal suicide attempt at age 21.

At age 22, she began living full-time as a woman, changing her name, clothing, and later starting gender-affirming hormone therapy at age 24. She gradually became more comfortable in the female role, dating both gay and straight men, and her anxiety diminished. At 25, she legally changed her gender markers and underwent gender-affirming surgeries including vaginoplasty and breast augmentation, resulting in satisfaction and comfort in her female identity. She maintains social support from friends and colleagues but limited support from family. She identifies as a heterosexual female and reports occasional mild anxiety and depressive feelings but no recent suicidal ideation or self-harm.

Discussion: Ms. Rohmer’s history reflects late-onset gender dysphoria, with a consistent desire to live as female beginning around age 19. She previously experienced distress and functional impairment due to gender incongruence. Her transition, including legal and medical interventions, aligns with the posttransition specifier. She continues to experience residual anxiety and mild depressive symptoms but is generally well-adjusted in her affirmed gender role.
