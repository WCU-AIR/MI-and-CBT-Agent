--- DISORDER ---
Schizoaffective Disorder, Depressive Type

--- CASE STUDY ---

John Evans was a 25-year-old single, unemployed white male with a history of depression, anxiety, psychosis, and substance use, who had been seeing a psychiatrist for several years. Symptoms of dysphoria, anhedonia, low energy, social isolation, and panic attacks began at age 15, concurrent with daily alcohol and marijuana use. School performance declined in high school, and he did not attend college.

At age 20, Mr. Evans experienced a psychotic episode characterized by the conviction that he had murdered people as a child, accompanied by continuous auditory hallucinations confirming these delusions. He feared punishment and developed suicidal ideation. Hospitalization with combined antipsychotic and antidepressant treatment resolved the psychotic and depressive symptoms over 4 weeks. He had two additional similar episodes before age 22, despite adherence to medications.

During the 3 years prior to the evaluation, Mr. Evans was adherent to clozapine and antidepressants, remaining free of hallucinations and delusions, though residual dysphoria, irritability, and amotivation persisted. He had achieved sobriety from alcohol and marijuana at age 23 and remained abstinent for 2 years.

Discussion: Psychotic symptoms included persecutory delusions and paramnesias of homicide, often confirmed by auditory hallucinations, with negative social consequences. Depressive episodes with guilt, anhedonia, poor sleep, and suicidal ideation occurred concurrently or following psychotic episodes. Duration and pattern of symptoms meet criteria for schizoaffective disorder, depressive type. Substance-induced disorders are excluded due to persistent symptoms during periods of abstinence.
