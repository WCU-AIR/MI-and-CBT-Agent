--- DISORDER ---
Personality Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Grzegorz Buchalski was an 87-year-old white man brought to the psychiatric emergency room by paramedics after neighbors noticed an odd smell in his apartment. His 90-year-old sister had recently died, and he had delayed reporting her death. He had become increasingly disorganized as his sister’s health worsened, attempting to clean his apartment by simply moving items around. He was about to call for help when authorities arrived.

In the ER, Mr. Buchalski recognized that his actions were unusual. He alternated between tearfulness and calm, factual discussion regarding his sister’s death. He explained much of the mess in his apartment was due to his extensive collection of research materials on bioluminescence.

A licensed plumber, electrician, and locksmith, he worked until age 65. He had never had romantic or sexual relationships, nor many friends outside his family. He attributed his social isolation to poverty and his Polish background, while finding intellectual pursuits most gratifying. He denied depression, mania, psychosis, or prior psychiatric treatment.

Mental status examination: thin, elderly, neatly dressed; meticulous; preferred discussing science; polite and pleasant; appropriate eye contact; coherent, goal-directed speech; mood “fine”; affect appropriate though unusually cheerful under circumstances; cognitively intact; generally good insight and judgment, though historically impaired regarding delay in reporting sister’s death.

Discussion: Mr. Buchalski exhibits an aloof, taciturn, and asexual lifestyle consistent with schizoid personality disorder (SPD). Features include lifelong social isolation, inward preoccupation, flattened affect, eccentric intellectual interests, and weak ego skills in interpersonal situations. Cognitive intactness rules out a dementing etiology. Possible comorbid hoarding tendencies noted due to extensive accumulation of manuscripts and materials.
