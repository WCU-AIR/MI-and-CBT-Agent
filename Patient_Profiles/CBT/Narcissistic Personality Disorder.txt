--- DISORDER ---
Narcissistic Personality Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Larry Goranov, a 57-year-old man, requested an expert review of his psychiatric care spanning the previous seven years at a medical school–affiliated clinic. He reported feeling depressed for decades, despite weekly psychotherapy and multiple psychiatric medications, which he stated had little effect.

Mr. Goranov described chronic sadness, fatigue, and frustration, noting that he rarely looked forward to anything. He had lost his last job three years prior, ended a romantic relationship shortly thereafter, and expressed doubts about future work or dating. He was embarrassed to live with his elderly mother. He denied immediate suicidal intent but indicated that if he did not improve after his mother’s death, he would see no reason to live. Sleep, appetite, and concentration were reported as normal.

Clinic records showed adherence to adequate trials of fluoxetine, escitalopram, sertraline, duloxetine, venlafaxine, bupropion, and augmentation with quetiapine, aripiprazole, lithium, and levothyroxine. He experienced partial improvement on escitalopram but did not achieve remission. Early cognitive-behavioral therapy was largely ignored, with minimal engagement in homework or between-session application.

Mr. Goranov expressed frustration with his treatment and therapists, particularly rotating psychiatry trainees, whom he deemed less educated and sophisticated than himself. He preferred female therapists for their appearance. His work history included success as an insurance broker, followed by unemployment due to professional conflicts, and a brief, successful position at a car dealership that he quit after a disagreement. He has not pursued further employment or volunteer work, viewing such efforts as beneath him.

In relationships, he described distrust of women, responding to interest with suspicion, and maintaining his mother as his primary social connection. He valued fine dining, hotels, and physical fitness, spending most of his time at home watching television or reading.

On examination, Mr. Goranov was neatly groomed, wore youthful designer clothing, and had slicked-back hair. He was coherent, goal directed, and cooperative. Affect was constricted and dismissive. He expressed sadness and anger but denied suicidal intent. Cognitive function was intact.

Discussion: Mr. Goranov’s low mood appears secondary to chronic disappointment rather than a primary depressive disorder. His grooming, style, and concern with appearance reflect a self-perception of specialness and entitlement. He demonstrates classic features of narcissistic personality disorder (NPD), including grandiosity, belief in deserving special treatment, diminished empathy, and disdain for others. Depressive affect is reactive to unmet expectations rather than constituting a primary depressive disorder.

Diagnosis: Narcissistic Personality Disorder
