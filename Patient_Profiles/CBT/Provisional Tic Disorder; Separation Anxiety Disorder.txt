--- DISORDER ---
Provisional Tic Disorder; Separation Anxiety Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Ethan, a 9-year-old fourth grader at a private school for boys, was referred by his teacher due to declining attention and academic performance. Ethan had previously been among the best students but grades slipped in the spring semester. He became fidgety and distracted with more challenging academic work.

At home, Ethan appeared more emotional and clingy, following parents around and climbing into bed with them at night. He refused to attend sleepovers. Ethan had a few friends but avoided independent social activities. His mother noted frequent shrugging, grimacing, blinking, and throat-clearing movements, which worsened with frustration or fatigue and decreased during focused tasks such as homework or clarinet practice. Ethan also developed a superstition, touching doorjambs in a ritualistic way.

Ethan was an easy child, born after an uncomplicated pregnancy, with normal developmental milestones. No medical problems were reported, although he made frequent visits to the school nurse complaining of stomachaches.

On examination, Ethan was slightly built, fair-skinned, and blond. He was fidgety, tugging at his pants and shifting in his seat. Occasional blinks, eye rolls, and throat-clearing noises were observed. He expressed vague worries about harm coming to his parents, primarily fears of burglary.

Discussion: Ethan exhibits anxiety and ritualistic behaviors that impact attention and school performance. He meets DSM-5 criteria for separation anxiety disorder, as symptoms persist for more than 1 month in a child. He also demonstrates motor and vocal tics that have been present for about 6 months, consistent with provisional tic disorder. His attention difficulties are likely secondary to anxiety and tics rather than ADHD. OCD-like behaviors (doorway rituals) were noted but not sufficiently impairing for diagnosis.

--- BEHAVIOUR ---

Fidgety, shifts in seat, tugs at clothing

Shoulder shrugging, blinking, grimacing, throat clearing

Clingy with parents, reluctance to be alone or attend sleepovers

Performs ritualistic behaviors (doorway superstition)

--- DSM-5 SYMPTOMS ---
Provisional Tic Disorder

Motor tics: blinks, shoulder rolls, grimaces

Vocal tics: chirps, grunts, throat clearing

Duration < 1 year

Tics worsen with anxiety or fatigue, improve during focused activity

Separation Anxiety Disorder

Excessive distress when anticipating separation from parents

Clinginess and reluctance to be alone

Repeated complaints of physical symptoms when separation is expected

Persistent worry about harm coming to parents
