--- DISORDER ---
Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Elizabeth Olsen and Finn Nelson, both successful attorneys in their late 30s, presented for couples counseling due to increased arguments prior to their wedding in six months. They had known each other since high school, dated for 2 years, and both denied prior psychiatric treatment or diagnosis.

During joint sessions, both reported being in love but argued frequently. Ms. Olsen admired Mr. Nelson's intelligence and steadiness and was unsure why they fought. Mr. Nelson described Ms. Olsen’s lack of sexual interest and reliance on alcohol, which caused him distress. He reported difficulty arousing her or bringing her to orgasm and had begun initiating sex less frequently. Ms. Olsen’s scheduling of time together (one weekday night and one weekend night) was perceived by him as avoidance, though she stated it was to manage her time and social life.

In individual sessions, Mr. Nelson expressed concern about her lack of sexual desire and satisfaction. Ms. Olsen admitted she used alcohol to tolerate intercourse, had primarily sexual experiences while drunk, and rarely found sexual arousal tolerable. She had avoided disclosing her sexual history to Mr. Nelson to maintain a “near-virginal” image. She also noted she persisted in the relationship due to her desire for a child before age 40. She declined to explain the underlying reasons for her sexual difficulties.

Discussion: The couple sought therapy primarily for sexual issues, but broader relationship concerns are also present. Ms. Olsen’s diminished sexual interest, reduced enjoyment, inability to orgasm, and reliance on alcohol suggest female sexual interest/arousal disorder, with possible consideration for female orgasmic disorder or genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder if further assessment supports these. Her sexual issues may be lifelong and possibly related to past trauma. Alcohol use may contribute to relationship problems. A thorough assessment of psychological, relational, and health-related factors is indicated.
