--- DISORDER ---
Schizophrenia, childhood-onset, multiple episodes, currently in acute episode

--- CASE STUDY ---

Felicia Allen, a 32-year-old single woman, was brought to the ER by police after attempting to steal a city bus in Chicago. She threatened the driver with a kitchen knife, took control of the bus, and crashed it. A friend on the bus reported that the incident arose from frustration over the driver demanding exact change instead of dollar bills.

Ms. Allen was handcuffed, heavyset, and had a forehead bandage. She fidgeted, rocked back and forth, and mumbled repeatedly, saying “Sorry, sorry.” She was minimally responsive to questions.

She had a longstanding psychiatric history, with childhood-onset treatment-resistant paranoid schizophrenia. Auditory hallucinations began at age 5, and she had been hospitalized frequently since age 11. Previously, her thinking was concrete, but she was motivated and capable of some self-reflection. Her goals included independent living and socialization.

Ms. Allen had been taking clozapine for 1 year, which alleviated her hallucinations and improved functioning, despite a 35-pound weight gain. The bus incident represented a step toward independence, reflecting both cognitive limitations and limited real-world experience rather than a relapse of psychosis.

Discussion: Ms. Allen’s behavior illustrates the impact of chronic childhood-onset schizophrenia on cognition, social functioning, and judgment. The DSM-5 criteria for schizophrenia require at least two of five core symptoms (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, disorganized/abnormal behavior, negative symptoms), impairment in functioning, and continuous signs for ≥6 months. Ms. Allen meets these criteria. Her concrete thinking and limited life experience contributed to her unusual actions. Clozapine improved positive symptoms, but ongoing cognitive and social remediation are necessary.
