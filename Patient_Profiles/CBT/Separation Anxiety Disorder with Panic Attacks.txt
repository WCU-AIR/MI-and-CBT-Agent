--- DISORDER ---
Separation Anxiety Disorder with Panic Attacks

--- CASE STUDY ---

Logan, a 12-year-old boy, was referred for psychiatric evaluation due to longstanding anxiety about losing his parents and recent fears about developing severe illnesses. His acute concerns began 5 weeks prior, after watching a television program about rare, fatal diseases, resulting in recurrent panic attacks characterized by anxiety, dizziness, sweating, and shortness of breath. He also reported frequent headaches and stomachaches, which he attributed to his anxiety.

Logan’s history includes early childhood separation fears, intense kindergarten separation difficulties, and bullying in third grade, which contributed to the onset of panic attacks. He has a history of multiple anxiety presentations, including fears of the dark, sleeping alone, being alone, and social worries. His parents report that Logan’s anxiety is predominantly focused on the safety of his mother, leading to frequent phone calls and interruptions of her activities. Past psychotherapy at age 10 was helpful in reducing maternal overprotectiveness and improving anxiety symptoms.

Developmentally, Logan’s cognitive abilities are intact, and he has generally performed well academically. Socially, he is quiet but maintains friendships and collaborates with peers, although he is sensitive to perceived slights. Family history is notable for maternal anxiety disorders, including panic disorder, agoraphobia, and social anxiety, suggesting a potential genetic and environmental contribution to his anxiety.

On examination, Logan was friendly, articulate, cooperative, and goal-directed. Affect was generally positive, but he became tearful when discussing fears of illness or parental loss. Cognition, insight, and judgment were intact except as influenced by anxiety.

Discussion: Logan meets criteria for separation anxiety disorder, demonstrating long-standing, excessive fears of anticipated separation, harm to his parents, and being left alone, with associated somatic complaints. Panic attacks are present but appear secondary to separation fears rather than indicative of panic disorder. His fears about illness do not meet criteria for illness anxiety disorder, and generalized anxiety disorder is excluded because his concerns are specific to separation. Family history and parenting patterns likely contribute to symptom maintenance.
