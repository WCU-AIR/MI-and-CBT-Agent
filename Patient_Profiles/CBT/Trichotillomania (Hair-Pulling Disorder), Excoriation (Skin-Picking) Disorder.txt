--- DISORDER ---
Trichotillomania (Hair-Pulling Disorder)
Excoriation (Skin-Picking) Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Zoe Oliver, a 22-year-old single woman, presented after reading an article about hair-pulling disorder. She had long-standing hair-pulling behaviors beginning at age 12, primarily from the crown of her head, but also from eyebrows, eyelashes, and pubic regions. Hair pulling occurred as a ritual after returning home from work and was accompanied by biting off roots and sometimes swallowing the hair. Relief was felt when hairs were successfully pulled.

Ms. Oliver also engaged in skin-picking behaviors, especially at scabs, leading to scarring that caused her to avoid social situations. She had attempted to stop the behaviors for months at a time but relapsed, experiencing shame and self-directed anger. She had no prominent anxiety, depression, obsessions, compulsions, or other body-focused repetitive behaviors beyond hair pulling and skin picking.

During evaluation, she was aware and intentional in her hair-pulling and skin-picking behaviors. No medical or psychiatric comorbidities were identified, and she had no family history of obsessive-compulsive or related disorders. Her behaviors caused significant psychosocial impairment, including social withdrawal and avoidance of relationships.

Discussion: Ms. Oliver meets DSM-5 criteria for both trichotillomania and excoriation disorder. Both disorders share features such as repetitive behaviors, unsuccessful attempts to stop, and functional impairment. She experiences tension before the act and relief afterwards, although DSM-5 emphasizes attempts to reduce the behaviors rather than the tension–relief pattern. The behaviors are not attributable to other psychiatric or medical conditions.
