--- DISORDER ---
Bipolar and Related Disorder Due to HIV Infection, with Manic Features

--- CASE STUDY ---

Sebastian Lopez, a 27-year-old Hispanic freelance editor, was brought to his HIV clinic by his partner after claiming, “God has cured me! I can stop my antivirals!” The patient had recently engaged in an intense editing project with little sleep, elevated energy, pressured speech, irritability, and goal-directed activity. He used crystal methamphetamine once during this period, drank alcohol, and exhibited hypersexual behavior, weight loss, and refusal to take antiretroviral medication.

Mr. Lopez had a history of adolescent depression with a prior psychiatric hospitalization and suicide attempt. He had previously used methamphetamine frequently but achieved long-term abstinence after an HIV diagnosis at age 22. Family history included a maternal aunt treated with lithium.

On examination, he was sloppily dressed, exhibited pressured, disjointed speech, irritability, poor insight, poor judgment, and preoccupation with having discovered a cure for HIV. He denied hallucinations, suicidal or homicidal ideation. Cognitive testing was refused.

Discussion: Mr. Lopez displays classic manic symptoms: elevated and irritable mood, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, goal-directed activity, and impulsive hypersexuality. Although methamphetamine use and sleep deprivation may have contributed, his manic symptoms preceded stimulant use and persisted afterward, suggesting primary mania. HIV infection and potential neurocognitive decline complicate the presentation. Considering his HIV status, mania is best conceptualized as a bipolar and related disorder due to HIV infection, with careful monitoring for stimulant use and neurocognitive assessment recommended.
