--- DISORDER ---
Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder, tic-related, with absent insight/delusional beliefs

--- CASE STUDY ---

Trevor Lewis, a 32-year-old single man living with his parents, was brought to psychiatric consultation due to long-standing and worsening concerns about germs. Since adolescence, he has engaged in extensive hand-washing and showering rituals. Over the prior six months, his preoccupation with COVID-19 led to all-day cleaning of himself, clothing, and linens, strict control over his parents’ behaviors (mask-wearing, washing), and isolation from visitors. These behaviors caused conflicts with his family and prompted the current evaluation.

Mr. Lewis had previously received an SSRI and cognitive-behavioral therapy, which helped him complete high school. However, he has not worked outside the home or attended college due to OCD symptoms. Past history included obsessions about harming himself or others with associated checking behaviors and a childhood history of motor tics. He denied current substance use.

During a telehealth evaluation, he appeared disheveled and unkempt. He was completely convinced his home was contaminated by COVID-19 and insisted that cleaning rituals were necessary. He rejected contradictory information and could not suppress intrusive thoughts. No hallucinations or thought disorder were present. Cognition was intact, and he denied suicidal or homicidal ideation.

Discussion: Mr. Lewis meets DSM-5 criteria for OCD, with contamination fears and excessive washing rituals causing significant social and occupational impairment. Tic-related OCD is considered due to childhood motor tics. His beliefs are held with delusional intensity, reflecting absent insight. Differential diagnoses, including illness anxiety disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, and psychotic disorders, were excluded based on clinical presentation. Assessment of severity and insight could be supplemented with scales such as the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale and the Brown Assessment of Beliefs Scale.
