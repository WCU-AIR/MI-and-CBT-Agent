--- DISORDER ---
Cocaine Use Disorder, Moderate
Substance (Cocaine)-Induced Depressive Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Frank Young, a 40-year-old business executive, was brought for psychiatric evaluation by his wife due to marked changes in mood and behavior over the past 6 months. His wife reported he had become withdrawn, irritable, and increasingly secretive, often drinking excessively in social situations and coming home late or not at all.

Mr. Young admitted to significant work stress and personal financial losses over the past year. He reported persistent depressive symptoms including low mood, anhedonia, insomnia, low energy, and self-critical thoughts, accompanied by frequent passive suicidal ideation but no intent or plan. He also acknowledged heavy alcohol use and regular cocaine use several times per week for the past 9 months. His depressive symptoms began approximately 3 months after initiating regular cocaine use and persisted regardless of recent substance intake. He denied prior psychiatric history, including depressive episodes or substance use disorders outside of recent events.

Mental status examination revealed a cooperative, middle-aged man, oriented and coherent. Affect was depressed, and thought processes were linear. Insight and judgment were fair. No psychotic features were observed.

Discussion: The temporal relationship between Mr. Young’s cocaine use and the onset of depressive symptoms supports a diagnosis of substance-induced depressive disorder rather than independent major depressive disorder. Persistent depression outside periods of intoxication or withdrawal distinguishes the syndrome from acute substance effects. His cocaine use disorder is of at least moderate severity, and he may also meet criteria for an alcohol use disorder. Treatment planning should consider the interrelationship between substance use, depression, and psychosocial stressors. Abstinence from cocaine and monitoring depressive symptoms over time are recommended to clarify the long-term course of mood disturbance.
