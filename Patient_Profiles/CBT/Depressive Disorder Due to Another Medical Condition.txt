--- DISORDER ---
Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (Parkinson’s Disease), with Major Depressive–Like Episode
Rapid Eye Movement Sleep Behavior Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

George Anderson, a 73-year-old married man, was referred for psychiatric evaluation after screening positive for depression on the Geriatric Depression Scale during a neurology appointment. He was diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease 2 years prior and had previously coped well, remaining engaged in his usual activities. His wife noted subtle reductions in motivation and enthusiasm prior to his Parkinson’s diagnosis, but these changes were not functionally impairing.

Over the past 3 months, Mr. Anderson experienced social withdrawal due to loss of pleasure in previously enjoyed activities, fatigue, impaired concentration and memory, unintentional weight loss, and sleep-onset insomnia. He denied persistent sadness or worry and had no suicidal ideation. His wife reported chronic violent movements during sleep consistent with REM sleep behavior disorder, occasionally resulting in minor injuries. He remained independent in daily living activities and had no significant cognitive decline beyond mild, age-typical forgetfulness.

Past medical history included Parkinson’s disease, prostate cancer (in remission), glaucoma, and gout. Laboratory evaluations were unremarkable. There was no prior psychiatric history, and family psychiatric history was notable only for a granddaughter with ADHD. He reported minimal alcohol use and no other substances.

Mental status examination revealed a pleasant, cooperative man with mild-moderate parkinsonian features (resting tremor, bradykinesia, hypophonia, shuffling gait). Affect was difficult to gauge due to masked facies. Cognition was largely intact, though mild deficits were noted on executive and visuospatial tasks; standardized cognitive screening was within normal limits.

Discussion: Mr. Anderson meets criteria for depressive disorder due to another medical condition, likely linked to Parkinson’s disease, as evidenced by late onset, temporal association with neurodegenerative changes, and significant anhedonia. Although some symptoms overlap with typical Parkinson’s disease manifestations (fatigue, psychomotor slowing), the co-occurrence of new-onset anhedonia, subjective concentration difficulties, and insomnia supports a depressive episode. He also meets criteria for REM sleep behavior disorder, consistent with his Parkinson’s disease and sleep history.
