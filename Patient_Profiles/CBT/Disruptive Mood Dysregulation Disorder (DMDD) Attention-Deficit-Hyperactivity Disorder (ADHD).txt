--- DISORDER ---
Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD)
Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Combined Presentation

--- CASE STUDY ---

Wyatt Mustafa, a 12-year-old boy, was referred to an adolescent partial hospitalization program for severe, recurrent temper outbursts and persistent irritability. Wyatt’s parents reported that he has always been moody and easily frustrated, with frequent episodes of intense anger described as “a raging monster.” These outbursts have escalated over time, including incidents of property destruction and physical aggression at home and school. He has been suspended for punching another child and has ongoing conflicts at home regarding homework, video games, and bedtime.

Developmentally, Wyatt was extremely active and sensitive as a child, with low frustration tolerance. School reports noted fidgetiness, wandering attention, and impulsivity. At age 10, he was diagnosed with ADHD, combined type, and started on methylphenidate, which improved his symptoms. However, chronic irritability and moodiness persisted and worsened through fourth to sixth grade.

Wyatt’s mood between outbursts is consistently irritable and surly. He does not exhibit neurovegetative symptoms of depression, mania, psychosis, or substance abuse. Family history is notable for anxiety and depression in the father, alcoholism in paternal grandparents, and possible untreated ADHD in the mother.

Discussion: Wyatt’s presentation is consistent with DMDD, characterized by chronic, severe, developmentally inappropriate irritability and frequent temper outbursts. His ADHD and anxiety are important comorbidities that require monitoring. His temper outbursts are involuntary, distressing to him, and interfere with social and academic functioning. Longitudinal assessment and careful management are essential, as DMDD may increase the risk of future mood, anxiety, and substance use disorders.

Chronic, persistently irritable or angry mood between outbursts

Outbursts grossly out of proportion to situation

Irritability inconsistent with developmental level

Significant impairment in family, school, and social functioning

Comorbid ADHD, combined presentation
