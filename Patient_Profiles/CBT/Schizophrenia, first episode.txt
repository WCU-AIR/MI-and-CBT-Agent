--- DISORDER ---
Schizophrenia, first episode, currently in acute episode

--- CASE STUDY ---

Gregory Baker, a 20-year-old African American man, was brought to the ER by campus police after walking into a classroom shouting, “I am the Joker, and I am looking for Batman.” He refused to leave and was removed by security.

Historically, Mr. Baker was academically successful but, over the past year, became increasingly withdrawn and socially isolated. He spent most of his time lying in bed, stopped seeing friends, and had been suspended from college for poor attendance. Family members observed him mumbling to himself and performing unusual behaviors, such as standing on the roof and gesturing as if conducting music.

Mr. Baker had become preoccupied with a female friend, Anne, believing they were engaged despite minimal interaction. He wrote letters to her but never sent them. He had no history of substance use, and toxicology was negative.

On examination, Mr. Baker was well-groomed but uncooperative, constricted, guarded, and preoccupied. He became angry about hospital food, insisting it was poisoned. He exhibited paranoid, grandiose, and romantic delusions, was internally preoccupied, and denied hallucinations. He was oriented to person and place but less so to time, spoke articulately, refused formal cognitive testing, and had poor insight and judgment.

Family history revealed psychiatric illness: his grandmother lived for 30 years in a state psychiatric hospital, and his mother was described as mentally ill.

Discussion: Mr. Baker demonstrates acute-onset psychotic symptoms including delusions, preoccupation with internal stimuli, and negative symptoms such as social withdrawal and inactivity. There is no evidence of substance use or other medical causes. His presentation meets DSM-5 criteria for schizophrenia. The dimensional assessment of his symptoms can clarify severity, cognition, and prognosis.
