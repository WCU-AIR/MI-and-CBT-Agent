--- DISORDER ---
Medication-Induced Sexual Dysfunction

--- CASE STUDY ---

Gerhard Palmer, a 55-year-old married accountant, presented for a second psychiatric opinion regarding recurrent major depressive disorder (MDD). He had not responded to prior high-dose trials of fluoxetine and sertraline and had been off antidepressants for about a month.

At evaluation, he was severely depressed with profound psychomotor retardation, poor concentration, early insomnia, mildly diminished libido, and anhedonia. He denied substance use, drank minimally, and did not smoke. He had been taking propranolol for hypertension for 6 months. Physical examination and basic labs were normal, with blood pressure 135/85.

Treatment with clomipramine (up to 250 mg/day) and buspirone (30 mg/day) led to marked improvement after 5 weeks: sleep and appetite normalized, energy and enjoyment returned, and libido increased. However, upon resuming sexual activity, Mr. Palmer experienced inability to maintain an erection and inability to ejaculate, even during masturbation. These sexual difficulties persisted for a month. He recalled slightly delayed ejaculation on fluoxetine, but no sexual problems on prior bupropion therapy.

Discussion: Mr. Palmer’s erectile dysfunction and delayed ejaculation appear temporally linked to clomipramine initiation, suggesting medication-induced sexual dysfunction. His prior diminished libido due to MDD had improved, and there is no evidence of substance-related or other medical causes. While hypertension and propranolol can contribute to sexual dysfunction, symptoms began only after clomipramine initiation.
