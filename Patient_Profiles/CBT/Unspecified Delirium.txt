--- DISORDER ---
Unspecified Delirium

--- CASE STUDY ---

Victor Alvarez, a 76-year-old widower, was evaluated for depression and confusion the day after surgery to repair a broken hip. He had no known living family and no children. The fracture was reportedly caused by tripping in his messy apartment. His neighbor, a nurse, brought him to the hospital and noted that he had become more reclusive since his wife's death 6 months prior, with worsening self-care.

Routine labs revealed elevated blood urea nitrogen, low albumin, and high-normal mean corpuscular volume. Blood pressure was 160/110. He received haloperidol 2 mg for agitation.

On mental status examination, Mr. Alvarez lay at a 45-degree angle in bed, appearing thin with temporal wasting. His affect was sad, worried, and constricted. He appeared stiff and uncomfortable, did not immediately respond to questions, and generally kept his eyes closed. When prompted, he said only brief phrases such as "I'm fine" and "Get out," and was confused about his location. He declined further testing (e.g., clock drawing). Nursing notes indicated he was largely asleep or trying to get out of bed throughout the day.

Discussion: Mr. Alvarez exhibits acute disturbances in attention, awareness, and cognition, likely secondary to surgery and hospitalization. Fluctuating course, impaired orientation, and confusion suggest delirium. Contributing factors may include surgery, anesthesia, pain medications, dehydration, malnutrition, and possibly alcohol use (high-normal MCV). A chronic neurocognitive disorder (dementia) and depression are possible comorbidities but cannot be fully assessed at this time. Immediate diagnosis is unspecified delirium, with further evaluation warranted for underlying causes and comorbidities.
