--- DISORDER ---
Major Depressive Disorder
--- CASE STUDY ---

Barbara Reiss was a 51-year-old white woman who was brought to the emergency room by her husband with the chief complaint:
“I feel like killing myself.”
Ms. Reiss had begun to “lose interest in life” about 4 months earlier.
During that time, she reported depression every day for most of the day. Symptoms had been worsening.
She had lost 9 lb (current weight 105 lb) without dieting because she did not feel like eating.
She had trouble falling asleep almost every night and woke at 3:00 AM several mornings per week (she normally woke at 6:30).
She had diminished energy, concentration, and ability to perform her administrative job at a dog food processing plant.

She was convinced she had made a mistake at work that would lead to the deaths of thousands of dogs. This happened at the same time as the depression started.
She expected to be arrested and said she would rather kill herself than go to prison.
Her primary care physician recognized the depressive mood a week earlier, prescribed sertraline, and referred her for psychiatric evaluation.
She denied prior psychiatric history. One sister had depression.
No history of hypomania or mania. She usually drank one glass of wine with dinner, now increased to two at night for sleep.
She had been married for 20 years with three school-age children and employed at the same company for 13 years.
No illicit drug use. Physical exam and labs were normal.

Mental status:
cooperative, psychomotor agitation, brief answers, normal speech, denied hallucinations or unusual thoughts.
               Described work-related delusional guilt (not recognized as delusional). Memory intact.

Discussion: She meets all 9 DSM-5 criteria for MDD and has mood-congruent delusions. No signs of mania or substance-related mood disorder.
            Diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder, single episode, moderate, with psychotic features.
