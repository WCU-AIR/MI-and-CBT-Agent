--- DISORDER ---

Major neurocognitive disorder with Lewy bodies (NCDLB)

Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Carl Estel, a 74-year-old right-handed married man, presented with multi-year cognitive and motor decline, including stiffness, forgetfulness, apathy, and fluctuating attention. His wife reported that symptoms began shortly after his retirement at age 65, with progressive forgetfulness, misplacing items, neglecting bills, difficulty with appointments and calculations, and impaired household functioning. Over time, he ceased previously enjoyed activities and social interactions.

Mrs. Estel described violent enactment of dreams (punching, screaming, falling out of bed) that began before retirement and worsened over time. She also noted periodic visual misperceptions, such as swatting at invisible objects. He had no prior psychiatric history. A previous trial of an unknown "sleeping pill" caused severe rigidity and confusion.

Medical history included hypercholesterolemia, cardiovascular disease with stent placement, and possible transient ischemic attacks. Family history was positive for maternal dementia in the mid-70s.

On examination, Mr. Estel appeared stooped, stiff, and shuffling. He exhibited a tremor of the right hand, drooling, quiet speech, apathy, and intermittent inattention. Cognitive testing was limited due to his minimal participation.

Discussion: Mr. Estel demonstrates a progressive neurocognitive disorder with prominent cognitive fluctuations, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism, and REM sleep behavior disorder. These features satisfy the core and suggestive criteria for probable NCDLB. Memory improves with cues, differentiating it from Alzheimer’s disease. Sensitivity to antipsychotic medication (as evidenced by his reaction to the unknown sleeping pill) further supports NCDLB. While other etiologies (Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, cerebrovascular disease) were considered, the constellation of cognitive, motor, sleep, and behavioral symptoms points to NCDLB as the primary diagnosis.
