--- DISORDER ---
Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) with Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Brandon, a 12-year-old boy, was brought in by his mother for psychiatric evaluation due to temper tantrums, social difficulties, and declining school performance. His mother reported that things had always been difficult but became worse after he entered middle school. Around this time, he began complaining of stomachaches on school mornings and often resisted going to school.

Teachers described Brandon as academically capable but socially isolated. He mistrusted classmates who were genuinely kind and instead gravitated toward peers who mocked him. He brought toy cars and trucks to school, which other students ridiculed. Teachers reported he often cried, rarely spoke in class, and had recently begun screaming at classmates in hallways and classrooms. These outbursts embarrassed him, and he avoided swim practice to escape questions about them.

In interview, Brandon was shy and nonspontaneous, responding with mumbled answers when asked about school, friends, or family. However, when asked about toy cars, his demeanor brightened, and he spoke in detail, accurately naming different models (e.g., front-end loader, B-52, Jaguar). He carried toy cars and airplanes in his backpack. When asked about peers, he showed the examiner text messages he had saved: “dumbo!!!!” “mr stutter”; “LoSeR”; “freak!”; “EVERYBODY HATES YOU.” He reported classmates whispered insults and screamed in his ears. He added, “I hate loud noises” and admitted he sometimes wished he had never been born.

Developmentally, Brandon spoke his first word at 11 months and began using sentences by age 3. He had always been intensely focused on vehicles and struggled with humor and figurative language, often taking things literally. His mother described him as shy, without a best friend, and noted his father, a successful attorney, shared similar rigid routines and humorless demeanor. Brandon tried to appear “normal” around peers but found it exhausting and avoided socializing due to anxiety.

Examination revealed below-average eye contact, coherent and goal-directed speech, but with frequent stumbles, long pauses, and word repetitions consistent with stuttering. His affect was subdued, though he brightened when discussing his interests. He denied current suicidal intent or plans, homicidality, or psychosis. Cognition was intact.

Discussion:
Brandon meets DSM-5 criteria for Autism Spectrum Disorder, showing clear social communication deficits (difficulty forming friendships, misinterpreting social cues, literal thinking) and restricted, repetitive interests (trucks, cars, trains). His distress over changes in routine and sensory sensitivity to loud noises further support this diagnosis. He also meets criteria for childhood-onset fluency disorder, given his stuttering symptoms.

He shows signs of anxiety (school avoidance, stomachaches, social fear), common in ASD. Bullying exacerbates his distress and suicidal ideation. His awareness of his difficulties and desire for friends are positive prognostic factors, suggesting that appropriate interventions could significantly improve his functioning.

--- ASD & FLUENCY DISORDER SYMPTOMS from DSM-5 ---
Autism Spectrum Disorder:
- Deficits in social communication (difficulty making friends, mistrust, literal interpretation, poor eye contact)
- Restricted, repetitive interests (trucks, cars, trains obsession)
- Sensory sensitivities (distress with loud noises)
- Symptoms present since early childhood
- Significant impairment in school and social functioning

Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering):
- Repetitions of words/parts of words
- Long pauses in speech
- Speech disruptions causing social and academic dysfunction
- Onset in early development, persistent pattern
