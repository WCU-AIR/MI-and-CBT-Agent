--- DISORDER ---
Other Specified Dissociative Disorder (Acute Dissociative Reactions to Stressful Events)

--- CASE STUDY ---

Lourdes Zayas, a 33-year-old Puerto Rican woman born in the United States, was brought to the emergency room after attempting to swallow bleach in response to the sudden murder of her fiancé in Puerto Rico. She initially appeared unusually calm, then engaged in repetitive, unnecessary behaviors (folding and unfolding clothes) and later attempted self-harm. She experienced shaking, crying, screaming, and a brief period of apparent stupor, followed by partial unresponsiveness.

In the ER, she described feeling numb, disconnected from her body, emotions, and surroundings, and reported amnesia for the period surrounding the bleach incident. She continued to experience transient dissociative symptoms, depersonalization, derealization, nightmares, and intrusive memories over the following days. Physical, neurological, and laboratory evaluations were normal. She was oriented, without psychotic symptoms, and denied ongoing suicidal ideation.

Ms. Zayas was hospitalized for 10 days, during which her symptoms improved significantly. She required no medications and was discharged with outpatient follow-up. One month later, she had largely returned to baseline functioning. The acute dissociative reaction was brief, stress-related, and resolved with minimal sequelae, consistent with ataque de nervios, culturally recognized in Puerto Rican populations.

Discussion: Ms. Zayas’s acute dissociative episode included constriction of consciousness, depersonalization, derealization, transient stupor, micro-amnesia, and autonomic arousal. These acute dissociative reactions are typically self-limiting but may indicate vulnerability to future stress-induced psychiatric sequelae.
