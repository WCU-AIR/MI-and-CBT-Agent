--- DISORDER ---
Specific Learning Disorder; Generalized Anxiety Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Daphne, a 13-year-old ninth grader, was brought for evaluation due to academic and behavioral difficulties. She struggled to start and complete schoolwork, follow instructions, and received failing grades in math. When prompted to work, she became argumentative and irritable, and increasingly resisted attending school. These difficulties had been present since early childhood but worsened this year.

Psychological testing showed above-average intelligence and age-appropriate achievement in all subjects except math, with some visuospatial difficulties. School-provided tutoring was minimally attended and deemed unhelpful. Daphne had a previous ADHD diagnosis in childhood; a trial of stimulant medication was discontinued due to agitation.

At home, supervision of homework often led to arguments, crying, and screaming. She maintained two long-standing friends but had made no new close friends for several years. Daphne preferred younger peers and was generally quiet in groups but more interactive with family. She experienced chronic sleep onset difficulties requiring a nightlight and parental reassurance. No significant trauma or medical problems were reported.

On examination, Daphne was shy, tense, and had poor eye contact. She initially spoke only about her horse figurine collection but gradually revealed distress about school and peer relationships. She feared making mistakes, disappointing others, and felt socially inadequate. She denied persistent depressive feelings or suicidal thoughts. Daphne brightened when discussing her hobbies, friends, and family.

Discussion: Daphne exhibits persistent anxiety, inattention, academic difficulties, limited peer relationships, and low self-esteem. She has specific learning difficulties in mathematics and generalized anxiety. Her social and emotional development appears somewhat delayed, likely influenced by chronic anxiety, protective parenting, and inadequate school support. Other disorders (autism spectrum disorder, oppositional defiant disorder, ADHD) were considered but do not fully explain her presentation.
