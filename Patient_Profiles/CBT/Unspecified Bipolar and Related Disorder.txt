--- DISORDER ---
Unspecified Bipolar and Related Disorder

--- CASE STUDY ---

Victoria Owens, a 58-year-old divorced event planner, presented for psychiatric evaluation with depression and anxiety. Her symptoms began 2 years earlier during divorce proceedings from her fourth husband and included low mood, excessive worry, poor concentration, anhedonia, decreased energy, hypersomnia, passive suicidal ideation, and increased appetite with carbohydrate craving. These symptoms led her to stop working and spend most of her day in bed.

Initial treatment with alprazolam alleviated worry but not depressive symptoms. Sertraline 50 mg/day, titrated to 200 mg/day, improved sleep and reduced suicidal thoughts but precipitated anxiety, irritability, rapid thoughts, and increased energy.

Ms. Owens has a long-standing history of recurrent depressive episodes, and she described multiple periods of elevated mood and increased energy lasting several weeks. During these times, she was productive, goal-directed, and energetic but did not experience changes in sleep, grandiosity, or risky behaviors. These episodes were ego-syntonic and historically contributed to major life accomplishments, including her career as a party planner.

She reported prior heavy alcohol use in her 40s but has been largely abstinent. She denied prior psychiatric hospitalizations or suicide attempts. Family history is notable for maternal depression treated with sertraline and a brother treated with lithium for bipolar disorder.

Discussion: Ms. Owens exhibits recurrent depressive episodes with subthreshold hypomanic features. Antidepressant-induced mixed features (irritable, anxious mood with increased energy) suggest a bipolar spectrum disorder. Her clinical picture is further complicated by emotional lability, prior substance misuse, anxiety, and unstable relationships, raising the possibility of comorbid personality disorder. Careful longitudinal assessment is necessary to clarify her diagnosis and detect comorbidities.
