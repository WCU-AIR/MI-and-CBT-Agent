# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first utterance, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s presenting concern (anxiety about an exam) and imposes an unrelated agenda (quitting smoking). This shows a lack of respect for the patient’s autonomy and priorities. The repeated use of phrases like “you need to stop being so soft,” “stop making excuses,” and “no more delays or maybe” are harsh, critical, and invalidating. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the patient’s feelings or struggles in a supportive way, instead repeatedly blaming and pressuring Hana. This is the antithesis of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is judgmental and coercive, lacking any genuine acceptance or respect for the patient’s experience.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase Hana’s expressions of fear, anxiety, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with directives and challenges, ignoring the emotional content of the patient’s speech. For example, Hana describes physical symptoms of anxiety and her internal struggle, but the psychiatrist responds with “stop overthinking” or “stop being soft,” which invalidates her experience. The lack of reflective listening or emotional attunement results in a failure to convey empathy.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate empathy; the patient’s feelings are dismissed and ignored.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling Hana what she “must” do and demanding immediate compliance. There is no collaboration or partnership; the psychiatrist ignores Hana’s ambivalence and does not evoke her own motivations or values. Autonomy is undermined by coercive language and ultimatums. The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and confrontational, which is the opposite of MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive style with no collaboration or autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly pushes for commitment to action (setting a date, studying daily), this is done through pressure rather than skillful evocation. The patient does express some tentative change talk (e.g., “I want to try,” “I’m ready to make that happen”), but the psychiatrist does not strategically reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the focus is on demanding compliance rather than exploring the patient’s own reasons or confidence. The lack of reflective listening and affirmations means change talk is not cultivated effectively.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not skillfully evoked or reinforced; the approach is coercive rather than collaborative.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s expressions of fear, anxiety, hesitation) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels Hana’s concerns as “excuses,” “overthinking,” or “being soft,” which likely increases resistance rather than reducing sustain talk. There are no double-sided reflections or validations of ambivalence. The sustain talk is effectively reinforced by the psychiatrist’s harsh responses.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and not softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting Hana’s input or acknowledging her expertise in her own experience. The patient’s tentative statements are overridden by the psychiatrist’s demands. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist maintains an authoritarian stance with no partnership or collaboration.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Respect the patient’s experience and priorities. Avoid labeling or blaming language.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion. Validate the patient’s fears and ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support autonomy.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or readiness, reflect and amplify these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence rather than confronting or dismissing it.

6. **Use Open Questions to Explore Ambivalence:**  
   - Invite elaboration and exploration rather than yes/no or leading questions.

7. **Provide Affirmations:**  
   - Recognize patient strengths, efforts, and courage in facing difficult emotions.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Summarize motivation and negotiate concrete, manageable next steps with patient buy-in.

9. **Avoid Coercive or Directive Language:**  
   - Replace ultimatums with invitations and support.
