# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
□ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
☑ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and uses a critical, directive tone. There is no warmth or validation of the patient’s feelings, resulting in a judgmental and disapproving stance.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
□ High: active reflective listening  
☑ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
No reflective listening or empathy is demonstrated. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s fears and ambivalence, instead confronting and pressuring the patient without acknowledging their emotional experience.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
□ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
☑ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a controlling, expert-driven approach, repeatedly demanding immediate compliance and disregarding the patient’s autonomy and ambivalence. There is no collaboration or evocation of the patient’s motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
□ High: minimizes power differential  
☑ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s directives with no invitation for patient input or shared decision-making. The patient is positioned as passive and non-participatory.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
□ 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
☑ 2 (minimal cultivation)  
**Comments:**  
Although the patient expresses some change talk, the psychiatrist does not reinforce or explore these statements. Instead, the focus is on commands and pressure, missing opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
□ 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
☑ 1 (reinforces sustain talk)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is repeatedly labeled as “excuses” and met with confrontation. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or soften the patient’s ambivalence, likely increasing resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
□ Uses open questions  
□ Reflects more than asks  
□ Avoids roadblocks  
☑ Low engagement  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist uses mostly closed questions and issues commands rather than reflective listening. The confrontational style likely impairs rapport and patient engagement.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
□ Confirms agreed goal  
□ Redirects drifting conversation  
☑ Moderate focus  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on smoking cessation but in a rigid, directive manner without flexibility or patient input.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
□ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
□ Affirms autonomy  
☑ Minimal evocation  
**Comments:**  
There is no skillful evocation of the patient’s motivations or values. The psychiatrist pushes for change without exploring the patient’s own reasons or affirming autonomy.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
□ Strengthens commitment language  
□ Elicits concrete steps  
☑ Poor planning  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist demands immediate action without negotiation or support for readiness.
