# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s tone is overtly judgmental and confrontational, repeatedly accusing the patient of making excuses and failing to show respect or validation for the patient’s perspective. There is no warmth or acceptance, which leads to patient defensiveness.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
No reflective listening or empathy is demonstrated. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s expressed feelings and rationalizations, responding instead with accusations and directives, which breaks down rapport.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, prescriptive style. There is no collaboration or evocation of the patient’s own motivations, and autonomy is repeatedly denied through ultimatums and demands.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert role, issuing commands and ignoring the patient’s input. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient rather than an equal partner in the conversation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk from the patient is minimal and tentative. The psychiatrist fails to reinforce or explore these statements, missing opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and accusations, which likely increases resistance. There are no attempts to soften or normalize ambivalence.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [x] Avoids roadblocks (Not met)  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist uses mostly closed, leading questions and issues commands rather than engaging collaboratively. The patient’s resistance indicates poor engagement.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal (focus on drinking reduction)  
- [ ] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a rigid focus on reducing drinking but does so in a directive, inflexible manner that undermines rapport.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)

- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
No effective evocation of the patient’s motivations or values occurs. The psychiatrist ignores or dismisses tentative change talk.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [ ] Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning or goal setting is evident. The psychiatrist demands immediate commitment without negotiation or support.
