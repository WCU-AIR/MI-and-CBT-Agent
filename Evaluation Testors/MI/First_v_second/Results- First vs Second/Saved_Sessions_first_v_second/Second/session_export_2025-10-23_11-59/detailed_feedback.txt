# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. The tone is authoritarian and dismissive of Lukas’s ambivalence and concerns. Phrases such as “You have to stop making excuses,” “You’re just avoiding the real issue,” and “It’s that simple” communicate disapproval and lack of unconditional positive regard. There is no evidence of warmth, support, or validation of Lukas’s feelings or perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates Lukas’s social identity and fears, which likely increases resistance rather than acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect. The approach is confrontational and directive, which is counterproductive in motivational interviewing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or reflect Lukas’s perspective or emotional experience. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly challenges Lukas’s statements without reflective listening or validation. For example, when Lukas expresses fear of losing his social connections, the psychiatrist dismisses this as “making excuses” or “overcomplicating.” There are no complex or simple reflections to convey understanding or to explore Lukas’s ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey empathy, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the fundamental spirit of motivational interviewing: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling Lukas what he “has to” do and demanding a yes/no commitment. There is no evocation of Lukas’s own motivations or values, no support for autonomy, and no partnership. The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and coercive, which is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent; the session is dominated by persuasion and control rather than collaboration and evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

Although Lukas offers several statements indicating ambivalence and some desire to change (“I want to get there,” “I’m worried about what quitting means”), the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes for immediate quitting without exploring or amplifying Lukas’s own reasons or confidence. There are no strategic reflections or questions to deepen change talk or commitment language.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is largely ignored or overridden by the psychiatrist’s confrontational style.

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels Lukas’s concerns as “excuses” and “overthinking,” reinforcing sustain talk rather than softening it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate Lukas’s ambivalence while gently guiding toward change. The psychiatrist’s responses escalate resistance by invalidating Lukas’s fears and social context.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, increasing resistance rather than softening it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The session is dominated by commands and demands, with no invitation for Lukas to share his perspective or co-create a plan. The psychiatrist repeatedly asks for a yes/no commitment without exploring Lukas’s readiness or preferences. Power is held firmly by the psychiatrist, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for Lukas’s autonomy.

No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance.
