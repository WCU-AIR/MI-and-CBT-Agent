# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and directive rather than accepting. The repeated use of commands (“You really need to quit smoking,” “You have to stop,” “No excuses”) and admonishments (“You need to cut down immediately,” “You can’t keep putting this off”) communicates disapproval and pressure rather than unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings, struggles, or ambivalence. There is no evidence of warmth or supportive language; instead, the tone is confrontational and authoritative. This likely contributes to the patient’s resistance and minimal engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance. The stance is largely judgmental and directive, which undermines rapport and client openness.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
There is little to no evidence of empathic listening or understanding. The psychiatrist does not reflect or paraphrase the patient’s statements, nor do they attempt to explore the patient’s perspective or emotional experience. The patient expresses ambivalence and difficulty, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. The lack of reflective statements or validation of the patient’s struggle indicates minimal effort to understand or convey empathy.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to reflect or validate the patient’s experience, resulting in a cold and disconnected interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands and demands commitment without inviting the patient’s input or exploring their motivations. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of advice rather than an active partner. There is no evocation of the patient’s own reasons for change or support for autonomy; instead, the psychiatrist insists on immediate quitting and dismisses the patient’s ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent. The interaction is dominated by persuasion and directive statements, lacking partnership or evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient offers minimal change talk (“I guess so,” “maybe half sounds doable”), the psychiatrist ignores these openings and instead pushes for immediate quitting cold turkey. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s tentative statements about cutting down. The psychiatrist’s repeated demands and warnings likely suppress further change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not cultivated. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore or reinforce the patient’s small openings toward change.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and admonishment rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s reasons as “excuses” and insists on immediate quitting, which likely increases resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The patient’s sustain talk (“I don’t wanna quit,” “too much going on”) is met with pressure rather than understanding.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced rather than softened. The psychiatrist’s approach likely entrenches resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is expert-driven and directive, with no invitations for the patient to share their perspective or co-create goals. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment and compliance without negotiation or shared decision-making. The patient is positioned as noncompliant rather than as an equal partner.

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent. The psychiatrist dominates the interaction and fails to minimize power differentials.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Empathy and reflective listening are critical to build rapport and understand ambivalence.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must replace authoritarian directives.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced.  
- Sustain talk requires softening through strategic reflections and normalization.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making must be prioritized.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

- The psychiatrist’s style is overly directive, confrontational, and judgmental.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, reflective listening, and acceptance.  
- The spirit of MI is absent; collaboration and autonomy support must be prioritized.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid commands and admonishments. Use language that conveys respect and unconditional positive regard.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion. Validate the patient’s struggle and ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support autonomy.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses even tentative desire or ability, reflect and explore these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence to reduce resistance.

6. **Ask Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage the patient to elaborate on their thoughts and feelings.

7. **Avoid Unsolicited Advice:**  
   - Ask permission before offering information or suggestions.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Explore small, achievable steps and check for patient readiness and commitment.

9. **Summarize Key Points:**  
   - Use summaries to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To improve, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a collaborative, empathic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes change talk and softens sustain talk. Without these changes, the patient is unlikely to move toward meaningful behavior change.
