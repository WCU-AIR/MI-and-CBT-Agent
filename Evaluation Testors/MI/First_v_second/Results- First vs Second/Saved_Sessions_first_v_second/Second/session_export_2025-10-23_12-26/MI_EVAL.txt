# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s tone is repeatedly judgmental and directive, using phrases like “You really should just quit completely right now” and “You have to quit now.” There is no evidence of warmth, validation, or acceptance of the patient’s struggles or ambivalence.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal reflective listening or perspective-taking is demonstrated. The psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s fears and ambivalence with accusations such as “You’re making excuses” and “You’re overcomplicating this,” invalidating the patient’s experience.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by commands and pressure to quit immediately. There is no collaboration or evocation of the patient’s motivations, and autonomy is ignored in favor of an authoritarian approach.

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner

- [x] Low: expert-driven

# Summary

This session demonstrates a fundamental lack of adherence to motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s judgmental, confrontational, and directive style undermines rapport, empathy, and patient engagement. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change, softens sustain talk, and collaborates on planning.
