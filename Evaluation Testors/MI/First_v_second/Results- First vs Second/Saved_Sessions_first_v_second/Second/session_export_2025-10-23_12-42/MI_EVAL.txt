# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive, repeatedly challenging the patient’s ambivalence and reasons for not quitting alcohol. There is little to no evidence of acceptance or validation of the patient’s feelings or struggles.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal empathy is demonstrated. The psychiatrist does not reflect or acknowledge the patient’s emotional experience or physical limitations, instead pushing for compliance without understanding.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, prescriptive style with no collaboration or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands immediate behavior change without evoking the patient’s own motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
No partnership is established. The psychiatrist controls the agenda and dismisses the patient’s perspective, ignoring his expressed ambivalence and need for pacing.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [ ] 3  
- [x] 2  
- [ ] 1  
**Comments:**  
The patient offers some tentative change talk, but the psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce it. Instead, change talk is overshadowed by confrontational responses.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [ ] 3  
- [x] 1  
- [ ] 5  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and admonishment rather than exploration or softening. The psychiatrist labels patient statements as “excuses” and pressures immediate change.

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [x] Avoids roadblocks (not met)  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is poor. The psychiatrist uses closed, leading questions and issues commands, which likely increases patient resistance and withdrawal.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal (focus on alcohol use)  
- [ ] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on alcohol reduction but does so in a coercive manner without patient collaboration.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
There is no effective evocation of the patient’s motivations or values. The psychiatrist pushes directives rather than eliciting reasons for change.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [ ] Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist demands immediate quitting without negotiation or eliciting patient-driven steps.
