# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance from the outset. The opening statement, “you really need to quit smoking,” followed by “There’s no excuse for dragging this out any longer,” is confrontational and disapproving. The psychiatrist repeatedly uses language that implies blame and lack of understanding, such as “You have to start exercising and eating better right away” and “You have to stop making excuses.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s brief, minimal responses are met with impatience rather than acceptance. This approach likely inhibits open communication and trust.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and judgmental, lacking respect and support. This is a critical failure in MI practice.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no demonstration of empathy or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s perspective or feelings. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands and warnings without acknowledging the patient’s ambivalence or emotional state. The patient’s minimal and reserved responses are ignored or challenged rather than explored. For example, when the patient says, “It’s not that simple for me,” the psychiatrist dismisses this without inquiry or validation.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to convey understanding or empathy, missing all opportunities to connect with the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is highly directive and persuasive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly uses imperative language (“You have to…,” “Stop making excuses,” “Are you going to commit?”) and does not invite the patient’s input or perspective. The patient is positioned as passive and compliant rather than an active partner. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the psychiatrist pressures the patient to comply immediately.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is entirely absent. The session is authoritarian and coercive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not evoke or reinforce any change talk. When the patient expresses hesitation or ambivalence (“I’m not sure I’m ready,” “Maybe later”), the psychiatrist ignores these cues and presses harder. There are no reflections or affirmations of any statements favoring change. The psychiatrist misses all opportunities to explore the patient’s motivations or reasons for change.

**Summary:**  
No effort to elicit or strengthen change talk; the approach likely suppresses any emerging motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and pressure rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels patient responses as “excuses” and demands immediate compliance. There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The patient’s reluctance is met with impatience and challenge, which likely increases resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or managed skillfully.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritative role throughout, issuing commands and expecting obedience. There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The patient is not invited to express preferences or collaborate on goals. The psychiatrist’s language (“You have to…”) creates a power imbalance and undermines client-centeredness.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration; the interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, directive style.  
- Develop skills in acceptance, empathy, and reflective listening.  
- Foster collaboration and support patient autonomy.  
- Learn to evoke and reinforce change talk while softening sustain talk.  
- Build a genuine partnership with the patient.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health issues (blood pressure, medication adherence).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly directive, confrontational, and judgmental, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- No use of reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions.  
- No evocation or reinforcement of change talk; sustain talk is escalated.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid blaming or shaming language.  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient.

2. **Develop Empathic Listening Skills:**  
   - Use reflections to show understanding of the patient’s perspective and feelings.  
   - Validate ambivalence and struggles.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Invite the patient to elaborate on their experience and motivations.

4. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   - Invite the patient to set goals and make decisions.  
   - Ask permission before giving advice or information.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - Reflect and reinforce any statements favoring change.  
   - Explore reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Summarize key points and negotiate achievable next steps.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Pressure:**  
   - Replace commands with invitations and support.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Without immediate and substantial changes in approach, the psychiatrist risks alienating patients and reducing the effectiveness of counseling. Mastery of MI requires embracing empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and skillful evocation of motivation—none of which are present here.
