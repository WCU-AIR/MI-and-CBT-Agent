# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session with Sofia Delgado

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach throughout the session is judgmental, directive, and disapproving rather than accepting. The tone is confrontational and accusatory, exemplified by statements such as “You have to stop letting this drama mess up your school life,” “You really should quit avoiding school,” and “You have to toughen up and stop letting them push you around.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist repeatedly blames Sofia for the situation and invalidates her feelings by dismissing her explanations as “excuses.” This creates a hostile environment that likely increases Sofia’s defensiveness and resistance. There are no affirmations or supportive statements that acknowledge Sofia’s struggle or validate her experience.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or support, instead adopting a judgmental and directive stance that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect Sofia’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase Sofia’s feelings or experiences. Instead, the psychiatrist interrupts with commands and accusations, ignoring Sofia’s expressions of frustration, fear, and ambivalence. For example, when Sofia explains that teachers do not help or that standing up leads to more blame, the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns and insists on “toughening up.” This lack of empathy likely contributes to Sofia’s guarded and defensive responses. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge Sofia’s emotional pain or the complexity of her social situation.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate understanding or validation of Sofia’s perspective, resulting in a one-sided, confrontational interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the core elements of MI spirit: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling Sofia what she “has to” do and demanding commitment without exploring her motivations or values. There is no evocation of Sofia’s own reasons for change or support for her autonomy. Instead, the psychiatrist pressures Sofia to “commit fully” and “stop making excuses,” which is coercive and counterproductive. The interaction is directive and controlling, with no evidence of partnership or respect for Sofia’s agency.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and directive, lacking collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support fundamental to MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce change talk. While Sofia occasionally expresses tentative willingness to try attending school more, the psychiatrist does not amplify or explore these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment and compliance, which appears to increase Sofia’s resistance. There are no open-ended questions or reflections designed to elicit Sofia’s own motivations or hopes for change. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to explore Sofia’s expressed desire to maintain friendships or reduce drama.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not cultivated; the psychiatrist’s confrontational style suppresses rather than encourages client motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. Sofia’s expressions of fear, frustration, and skepticism about teachers and peers are labeled as “excuses” or “making up reasons.” The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or validate the difficulty of Sofia’s situation. Instead, sustain talk is met with pressure and ultimatums, which likely reinforce resistance and disengagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk, instead reinforcing it through confrontation and invalidation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and demands without inviting Sofia’s input or collaborating on goals. The language is directive and controlling (“You have to,” “No more excuses,” “Are you going to or not?”), leaving no room for Sofia’s voice or expertise about her own life. This power imbalance undermines trust and engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance with no collaboration or shared power.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  
The psychiatrist’s approach in this session is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The interaction is characterized by judgment, confrontation, lack of empathy, and authoritarian control. There is no evidence of acceptance, empathy, collaboration, or evocation of motivation. The psychiatrist repeatedly pressures Sofia to comply without exploring her perspective or supporting her autonomy. This style is likely to increase resistance, reduce engagement, and impede progress.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the presenting problem (school attendance and rumors).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly confrontational, judgmental, and directive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and reflective listening.  
- No effort is made to evoke or reinforce change talk; sustain talk is met with invalidation.  
- The psychiatrist fails to support autonomy or build partnership.  
- The use of closed questions and commands dominates the interaction.  
- No affirmations, summaries, or collaborative planning are evident.  
- Rapport and engagement are poor, as evidenced by Sofia’s defensiveness and guardedness.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid blaming or labeling language.  
   - Express genuine interest and respect for Sofia’s experience.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Use reflections to validate Sofia’s feelings and perspectives.  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to deepen understanding.

3. **Shift from Directive to Collaborative Approach:**  
   - Invite Sofia’s input and preferences.  
   - Use open-ended questions to explore her motivations and ambivalence.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When Sofia expresses willingness or hope, reflect and explore these statements.  
   - Avoid pressuring for commitment; instead, support gradual steps.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate sustain talk without reinforcing it.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize Sofia’s right to choose and control her change process.  
   - Avoid ultimatums or coercive language.

7. **Use Affirmations and Summaries:**  
   - Recognize Sofia’s strengths and efforts.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

8. **Develop Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Work with Sofia to identify realistic, client-centered goals and strategies.  
   - Check for her readiness and confidence.
