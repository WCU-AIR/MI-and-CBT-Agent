# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach throughout the session is judgmental, directive, and lacks warmth or positive regard. The tone is authoritarian and confrontational, exemplified by statements such as “you really need to quit smoking,” “you have to understand,” and “you have to stop making excuses.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or empathy for Oliver’s ambivalence or struggles. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses Oliver’s feelings and concerns, showing no acceptance of his perspective or emotional state. This is far from the respectful, supportive stance required in MI.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, critical tone that likely alienates the patient and undermines rapport.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not reflect or validate Oliver’s feelings, ambivalence, or challenges. Instead, the psychiatrist interrupts and overrides Oliver’s expressions with commands and accusations (“you’re just making this harder,” “you’re dragging your feet,” “you weren’t tough enough”). Oliver’s nuanced expressions of guilt, humor, and conflict are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or convey Oliver’s perspective, missing multiple opportunities for reflective listening.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey Oliver’s experience, resulting in a cold, unsupportive interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive style with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“you have to quit now,” “no more delays,” “just decide and do it”) and pressures Oliver to comply. There is no invitation for Oliver’s input or exploration of his motivations. Autonomy is explicitly undermined by commands and dismissals of Oliver’s readiness or planning process. The spirit of MI—partnership, evocation, and autonomy—is entirely absent.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a controlling, expert-driven stance that contradicts the fundamental principles of MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly asks about quitting and pressing for a quit date, these efforts are coercive rather than skillful evocation. The psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify Oliver’s change talk but instead challenges or dismisses his ambivalence and planning. For example, when Oliver suggests a quit date next week, the psychiatrist rejects it outright rather than exploring the reasons behind it or supporting gradual commitment. The psychiatrist’s style likely inhibits genuine change talk rather than cultivating it.

**Summary:**  
Attempts to evoke change talk are present but heavy-handed and counterproductive, lacking strategic reinforcement or amplification.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—Oliver’s expressions of ambivalence, guilt, and attachment to smoking—is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic reflection. The psychiatrist labels Oliver’s concerns as “excuses,” “procrastination,” and “making it complicated,” which likely increases resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. Sustain talk is actively reinforced by the psychiatrist’s harsh responses.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead escalates resistance through judgmental responses.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The interaction is one-sided, with the psychiatrist dominating the conversation and issuing commands. Oliver’s input is minimized or ignored, and there is no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for Oliver’s expertise in his own experience. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands answers and compliance, undermining any sense of equality or partnership.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks partnership, with a clear power imbalance and authoritarian approach.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with Oliver. Use inclusive language and invite his perspective.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect Oliver’s feelings and ambivalence. Validate his struggles and normalize ambivalence.

3. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   - Employ simple and complex reflections to show understanding and deepen the conversation.

4. **Softly Handle Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and affirm the legitimacy of ambivalence to reduce resistance.

5. **Skillfully Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - When Oliver expresses desire or commitment, reflect and amplify these statements to strengthen motivation.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize Oliver’s control over decisions and respect his readiness to change.

7. **Provide Affirmations:**  
   - Recognize Oliver’s strengths, efforts, and intentions to build confidence.

8. **Collaborate on Planning:**  
   - Help Oliver develop a realistic, personalized quit plan rather than demanding immediate action.

9. **Avoid Ultimatums and Pressure:**  
   - Replace confrontational language with supportive, open-ended questions and invitations.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is counterproductive, likely increasing resistance and reducing motivation. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a warm, empathetic, and collaborative approach that respects client autonomy and skillfully navigates ambivalence. Without these changes, the effectiveness of interventions will be severely limited, and client engagement compromised.
