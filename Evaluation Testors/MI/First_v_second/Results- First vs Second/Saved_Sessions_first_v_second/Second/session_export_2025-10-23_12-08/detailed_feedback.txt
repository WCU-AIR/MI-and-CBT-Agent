# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session with Maria Santiago

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance, frequently invalidating Maria’s emotional experience. Phrases such as “you really should quit thinking about suicide,” “stop feeling sorry for yourself,” and “you have to push yourself harder” communicate disapproval and lack of empathy. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the depth of Maria’s suffering or validate her pain, instead blaming her for not “trying hard enough.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The tone is authoritarian and dismissive, which likely exacerbates Maria’s distress and resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive, lacking respect and support. This is a critical failure in establishing a safe, accepting therapeutic environment.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect Maria’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase Maria’s feelings or struggles. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and questions that dismiss Maria’s emotional reality. For example, Maria expresses feeling “drowning” and “trapped,” but the psychiatrist responds with “stop feeling sorry for yourself” and “you have to take responsibility,” ignoring the emotional content. This lack of empathic listening likely increases Maria’s distress and disengagement.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is virtually absent. The psychiatrist fails to convey understanding or validate Maria’s experience, undermining rapport and therapeutic alliance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“you have to,” “just do it,” “can you promise me”) without inviting Maria’s input or exploring her motivations. There is no attempt to evoke Maria’s own reasons for change or to support her autonomy. The psychiatrist’s stance is controlling and prescriptive, which is antithetical to the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent. The psychiatrist adopts a coercive, expert-driven style that likely alienates the patient and diminishes motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When Maria tentatively expresses desire to try or mentions caring about loved ones, the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements, instead returning to commands and criticisms. There is no amplification or exploration of Maria’s fleeting change talk, nor any strategic reinforcement to deepen motivation. The psychiatrist’s focus on compliance and promises undermines genuine elicitation of change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not cultivated. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to reinforce Maria’s own motivations and reasons for living.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—Maria’s expressions of hopelessness, pain, and ambivalence—is met with confrontation and blame rather than softening or reflective responses. The psychiatrist repeatedly tells Maria to “stop feeling sorry for yourself” and accuses her of not trying hard enough, which likely intensifies sustain talk and resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence or validate the difficulty of change.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened, increasing resistance and disengagement.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by directives and demands, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for Maria’s expertise in her own experience. The psychiatrist repeatedly asks for promises and compliance without negotiating goals or exploring Maria’s perspective. This power imbalance undermines trust and client engagement.

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that marginalizes the patient’s voice and autonomy.
