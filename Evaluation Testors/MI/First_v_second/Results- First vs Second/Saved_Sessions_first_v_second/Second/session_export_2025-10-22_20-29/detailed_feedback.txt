# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist issues directives and ultimatums (“You really should stop smoking as soon as possible,” “You must take this seriously,” “No exceptions,” “Just do it”), which communicate disapproval rather than acceptance. The patient’s attempts to clarify and express ambivalence are dismissed or met with accusations of “making excuses.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s lived experience and emotional struggles, which is antithetical to acceptance in MI.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly confrontational and directive, lacking any genuine acceptance or respect for the patient’s perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate understanding or reflective listening. The patient expresses complex ambivalence about drinking, social anxiety, and loneliness, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflections, paraphrases, or attempts to understand the patient’s emotional state or motivations. The psychiatrist’s questions are accusatory rather than exploratory, and the tone is harsh and dismissive. This results in the patient becoming defensive and disengaged.

**Summary:**  
There is minimal to no effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, resulting in a breakdown of rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment and compliance, ignoring the patient’s autonomy and readiness. The patient is pressured to respond with a binary yes/no to quitting immediately, which is coercive and counterproductive. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or values, nor does she support the patient’s sense of control over change.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent; the session reflects a confrontational, expert-driven approach that undermines motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient offers statements indicating desire and reasons for change (“I want to cut back,” “I’m tired of hangovers”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these in favor of demands for immediate abstinence. There are no strategic reflections or affirmations to amplify the patient’s change talk. Instead, the psychiatrist challenges and invalidates the patient’s ambivalence, which likely suppresses further change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither cultivated nor reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits the patient’s motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk related to social anxiety, peer pressure, and fear of isolation is met with confrontation and accusations of “making excuses.” The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections, normalization, or gentle redirection to soften sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is met with dismissal and pressure, which can increase resistance and entrench the patient’s sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than skillfully softened or explored.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting collaboration or input. The patient is repeatedly pressured to comply with the psychiatrist’s agenda, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for the patient’s expertise in her own life. The psychiatrist does not negotiate goals or plans, nor does she acknowledge the patient’s autonomy.

**Summary:**  
There is no partnership; the session is dominated by the psychiatrist’s control and directive style.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the confrontational, directive approach.  
- Develop genuine acceptance and empathy for the patient’s experience.  
- Embrace the spirit of MI by fostering collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Learn to skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk while softening sustain talk.  
- Build a true partnership with the patient, respecting her expertise and readiness.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and judgmental, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Practice unconditional positive regard and avoid labeling or blaming language.

2. **Develop Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Use complex reflections to capture the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Foster collaboration, evoke the patient’s own motivations, and support autonomy.

4. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Invite elaboration and recognize patient strengths and efforts.

5. **Skillfully Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - Reflect and reinforce statements favoring change to build motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Realistic Plan:**  
   - Negotiate goals and next steps that fit the patient’s readiness and context.

8. **Avoid Ultimatums and Binary Choices:**  
   - Allow the patient space to explore ambivalence without pressure.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, stifles motivation, and alienates the patient. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation of the patient’s own motivations and autonomy. Without these changes, the session risks causing harm and disengagement rather than facilitating meaningful change.
