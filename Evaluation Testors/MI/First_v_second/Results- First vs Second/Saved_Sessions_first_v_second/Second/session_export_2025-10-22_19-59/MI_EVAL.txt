# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and confrontational, repeatedly accusing the patient of “making excuses” and lacking seriousness. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or validation of the patient’s experience, which likely increased resistance.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal to no reflective listening or acknowledgment of the patient’s feelings and struggles. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s stress and ambivalence, responding with challenges rather than empathy.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is highly directive and coercive, with repeated demands for immediate quitting and dismissal of ambivalence. Autonomy is not supported; the psychiatrist uses controlling language and fails to evoke the patient’s own motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert role, giving directives and demanding compliance without inviting patient input or collaboration. No shared decision-making is evident.

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)
