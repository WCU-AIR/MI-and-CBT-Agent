# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is judgmental, directive, and disapproving throughout the session. The tone is authoritarian and lacks any warmth or positive regard. The patient is repeatedly accused of “making excuses” and “being weak,” which is stigmatizing and likely to provoke resistance rather than engagement. There is no evidence of respect for the patient’s perspective or acknowledgment of her struggles. The psychiatrist fails to communicate unconditional positive regard or empathy, instead adopting a confrontational stance. This is far below the minimal standard of acceptance expected in motivational interviewing.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s stance is harsh, judgmental, and directive, undermining rapport and trust. There is no acceptance or positive regard demonstrated.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows no effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective or feelings. The patient expresses stress, ambivalence, and difficulty managing multiple life demands, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements, no validation of the patient’s experience, and no attempt to explore the emotional or contextual factors influencing smoking behavior. The lack of empathy is glaring and detrimental to the therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
No reflective listening or perspective-taking is evident. The psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s experience and emotions.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with the psychiatrist repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding immediate commitment. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist uses coercive language (“You have to,” “No excuses,” “No exceptions”) and does not invite the patient’s input or explore her motivations. This approach is antithetical to the spirit of motivational interviewing.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. It is rigid, controlling, and directive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Instead, the approach is confrontational and focused on compliance rather than motivation. While the patient does express some change talk spontaneously, the psychiatrist fails to reflect, amplify, or explore these statements. There are no strategic questions or reflections designed to deepen the patient’s own reasons or desire for change.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither elicited nor reinforced. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to build motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s expressions of difficulty, stress, and ambivalence) is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels these as “excuses” and “weakness,” which likely increases resistance. There are no double-sided reflections, no normalization of ambivalence, and no attempts to gently redirect or explore sustain talk. This approach exacerbates sustain talk rather than softening it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and reinforced rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, issuing commands and ultimatums. There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist does not invite collaboration or respect the patient’s expertise in her own life. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration is demonstrated; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Develop a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Practice reflective listening and empathy.  
- Embrace the spirit of MI: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Skillfully elicit and reinforce change talk.  
- Soften sustain talk with validation and double-sided reflections.  
- Build a genuine partnership with the patient.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Respectful Stance:**  
   Avoid labeling or blaming language. Recognize the patient’s struggles and strengths.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect the patient’s statements to demonstrate understanding and encourage elaboration.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   Invite the patient’s perspective and choices. Avoid ultimatums or demands.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   Ask open-ended questions about reasons for change, desires, and confidence. Reflect and reinforce change talk.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and gently guide toward change.

6. **Provide Advice with Permission:**  
   Ask before giving information or suggestions to respect patient autonomy.

7. **Develop Collaborative Plans:**  
   Work with the patient to set realistic, achievable goals and next steps.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is counterproductive and likely to alienate the patient. To improve, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a respectful, empathetic, and collaborative approach that supports the patient’s autonomy and motivation for change. Without these changes, the effectiveness of interventions will be severely compromised.
