# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the very first utterance, the psychiatrist issues commands (“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do,” “You have to stop smoking immediately,” “Just throw away what you have”) without any sign of warmth, respect, or positive regard. The patient’s attempts to express ambivalence and fear are met with dismissal and pressure rather than understanding. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or acceptance of the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist’s tone is authoritarian and confrontational, which likely increases patient defensiveness and disengagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a harsh, directive approach that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic listening or reflective statements. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings, fears, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and accusations (“You have to take this seriously,” “Stop making excuses,” “You have to decide—there’s no room for hesitation”). The patient’s expressions of fear, overwhelm, and ambivalence are ignored or invalidated. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s perspective or emotional state, nor do they reflect or paraphrase the patient’s statements to demonstrate understanding.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows a lack of empathy, failing to connect with or validate the patient’s experience, which likely increases resistance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely devoid of the MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing ultimatums and commands without inviting the patient’s input or exploring their motivations. There is no evocation of the patient’s own reasons for change, no support for autonomy, and no partnership. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate cessation with no room for negotiation or patient agency. This approach is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and controlling, lacking collaboration, evocation, and respect for autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient offers some tentative change talk (e.g., acknowledging health risks, expressing fear), the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead focuses on pushing for immediate cessation. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s motivation or reasons for change. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to explore the patient’s ambivalence or to elicit commitment language in a supportive manner.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and pressure rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s statements as “excuses” and demands immediate cessation, which likely entrenches sustain talk and resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses escalate pressure rather than gently guiding the patient toward change.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected, increasing resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands and ultimatums, with no invitation for patient input or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands answers and compliance, ignoring the patient’s expressed concerns and readiness. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and experiences.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support autonomy.

4. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Invite the patient to elaborate on their thoughts and feelings.

5. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize any positive steps or intentions the patient expresses.

6. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - Reflect and amplify statements favoring change.

7. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify realistic, achievable next steps.

9. **Summarize Key Points:**  
   - Periodically summarize to consolidate motivation and plan.
