# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance from the outset. The opening statement, “you really need to quit smoking,” is authoritative and prescriptive rather than accepting. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s initial correction that she does not smoke and persists with the smoking cessation agenda, showing a lack of respect for the patient’s perspective. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth; instead, the tone is confrontational and dismissive of the patient’s actual concerns. The patient’s attempts to redirect the conversation to alcohol use are not acknowledged or validated. This approach is inconsistent with acceptance and MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a judgmental, directive, and dismissive approach that undermines rapport and patient engagement.

**Explanation:**

The patient’s autonomy is ignored; the psychiatrist does not support choice or self-direction but instead imposes a rigid agenda. There is no evocation of the patient’s own reasons for change or exploration of ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is antithetical to the MI spirit, characterized by control, persuasion, and disregard for patient autonomy.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must develop foundational MI skills: acceptance, empathy, reflective listening, and collaboration.  
- Avoid confrontation and commands; instead, invite patient perspectives and support autonomy.  
- Correctly identify the target behavior before proceeding.  
- Use open-ended questions and reflections to evoke change talk.  
- Respond to sustain talk with understanding and normalization.  
- Collaboratively set goals and plan next steps with the patient.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   Begin sessions by listening carefully and validating the patient’s experience rather than imposing assumptions or directives.

2. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Reflective Listening:**  
   Encourage the patient to elaborate on their thoughts and feelings. Reflect back to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   Invite the patient to set the agenda and make decisions about change, emphasizing their control over the process.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses any motivation or desire to change, reflect and explore these statements to strengthen commitment.

5. **Skillfully Address Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and ambivalence.

6. **Avoid Confrontation and Commands:**  
   Replace “must” and “have to” language with collaborative, invitational language.

7. **Clarify and Confirm Target Behavior Early:**  
   Ensure the focus of the intervention matches the patient’s actual concerns and behaviors.
