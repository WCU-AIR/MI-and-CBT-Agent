# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance from the outset, repeatedly insisting on immediate change and dismissing the patient’s expressed barriers. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s frustration and realistic concerns about time constraints are met with impatience and admonishment rather than understanding. For example, the psychiatrist states, “You have to make time for exercise and better eating. No more excuses about being busy,” which invalidates the patient’s lived experience. This approach is contrary to MI principles of acceptance and respect.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and dismissive, lacking any genuine acceptance or validation of the patient’s perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist fails to reflect or acknowledge the patient’s feelings, frustrations, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly asks closed yes/no questions and issues commands. The patient’s statements about workload and time pressures are ignored or minimized. For example, when the patient says, “I’m not making excuses; I’m just telling you how it is,” the psychiatrist responds with “No more excuses,” which is confrontational rather than empathic. There are no reflective statements or attempts to understand the patient’s perspective.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey empathy, missing critical opportunities to connect with the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive style with no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands yes/no answers and insists on immediate commitment without exploring the patient’s motivations or barriers. The patient is positioned as passive and noncompliant rather than as an active partner. The psychiatrist’s repeated commands (“You have to make time,” “No more excuses,” “This is non-negotiable”) directly contradict the MI spirit of partnership and autonomy support.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to embody the MI spirit, instead adopting a controlling and prescriptive approach.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses some change talk (“The weight is something I would like to get hold of”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements and does not amplify or explore them. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance and commitment, pushing for immediate action without eliciting the patient’s own reasons or desires for change. There is no strategic use of reflections or open questions to deepen motivation.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but neglected; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to cultivate and strengthen it.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than skillful softening or redirection. The patient’s sustain talk about time constraints and workload is repeatedly labeled as “excuses” and met with commands to “stop making it so complicated.” There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that validate the patient’s ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s approach likely increases resistance rather than reducing sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issues ultimatums, and demands yes/no answers. The patient’s expertise about his own life is disregarded. The psychiatrist does not invite input or negotiate goals or plans. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, lacking any meaningful partnership.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Empathy and reflective listening are essential to understand and validate the patient’s perspective.  
- The MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support must replace the current authoritarian style.  
- Change talk should be actively elicited and reinforced.  
- Sustain talk requires skillful softening, not confrontation.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making must be prioritized.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is incompatible with motivational interviewing principles.  
- The session is dominated by closed questions, commands, and confrontation.  
- There is no empathy, acceptance, or partnership.  
- Change talk is neglected; sustain talk is escalated.  
- The patient’s autonomy and expertise are ignored.  
- The psychiatrist must learn to listen reflectively, ask open questions, affirm strengths, and collaborate on realistic goals.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses.”  
   - Validate the patient’s experience and barriers.

2. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage elaboration and exploration of ambivalence.

3. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize any positive intentions or past attempts.

5. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Invite patient input and respect their right to choose.

6. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - Explore reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

7. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence.

8. **Collaborate on Planning:**  
   - Develop realistic, patient-centered goals together.

9. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace directives with invitations and partnership language.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques is essential to prevent harm and improve patient outcomes.
