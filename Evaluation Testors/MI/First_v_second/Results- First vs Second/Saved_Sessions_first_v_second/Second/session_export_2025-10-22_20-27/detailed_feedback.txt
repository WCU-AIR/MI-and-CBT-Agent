# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist issues directives and ultimatums (“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do,” “This is non-negotiable,” “You have to commit”), which clearly undermine unconditional positive regard. The patient’s attempts to redirect the conversation to his actual concern (lateness) are dismissed or overridden. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or support for the patient’s perspective or feelings. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s ambivalence and frustration by labeling his responses as “excuses” and “overthinking.” This approach is likely to alienate the patient and reduce engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, lacking respect and acceptance. The patient’s autonomy and perspective are disregarded, resulting in a hostile and unsupportive environment.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s feelings or perspective beyond surface-level compliance. The patient expresses frustration about external pressures and ambivalence about change, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. The psychiatrist’s responses are confrontational and dismissive rather than validating or exploring the patient’s experience. For example, when the patient says, “It’s not just about being tough,” the psychiatrist responds with “No, you’re just making excuses again,” which is invalidating and lacks empathy.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate empathy or understanding, missing critical opportunities to connect with the patient’s emotional state and ambivalence.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without eliciting the patient’s own motivations or ideas. There is no evocation of the patient’s reasons for change beyond compliance, no support for autonomy, and no partnership. The psychiatrist issues ultimatums and insists on immediate compliance, which is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a directive, controlling approach with no evidence of MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient occasionally expresses desire and commitment (“For me, I want to get to school on time,” “I’m ready to commit”), the psychiatrist does not amplify or explore these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance and discipline, missing opportunities to deepen the patient’s intrinsic motivation or explore ambivalence. The patient’s change talk is minimal and largely reactive to the psychiatrist’s pressure rather than spontaneously generated or supported.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s sustain talk as “excuses” and “overcomplicating,” which reinforces resistance rather than softening it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate the patient’s ambivalence. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s expressions of frustration and external pressures, instead pushing a rigid agenda. This approach likely increases sustain talk and resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, lacking any skillful softening or redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting the patient’s input or co-creating a plan. The patient’s attempts to express his perspective are overridden or ignored. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and expert-driven, with no partnership or shared decision-making.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  
**Overall Score: 1/5**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is characterized by judgment, lack of empathy, authoritarianism, and failure to engage the patient’s intrinsic motivation. This style is likely to increase resistance, reduce engagement, and undermine the therapeutic alliance.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist attempts to maintain focus on the target behavior (lateness).  
- The patient expresses some change talk despite the challenging environment.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- No affirmations, reflections, or summaries are used.  
- The psychiatrist does not support autonomy or collaborate on planning.  
- The use of closed and leading questions limits exploration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directive commands to a partnership approach that respects the patient’s autonomy and perspective.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence. Avoid labeling statements as “excuses.”

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Encourage the patient to elaborate on their motivations, barriers, and feelings.

4. **Affirm Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and reinforce any positive statements or attempts at change.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   Explore the patient’s own reasons for change and reinforce commitment language.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

7. **Collaborate on Planning:**  
   Co-create a realistic and patient-centered plan rather than issuing ultimatums.

8. **Avoid Persuasion Without Permission:**  
   Ask permission before offering advice or information.

9. **Summarize Regularly:**  
   Use summaries to consolidate motivation and clarify next steps.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. To improve, the psychiatrist must fundamentally change their approach to embody the spirit and techniques of MI, focusing on empathy, collaboration, and evocation rather than control and persuasion.
