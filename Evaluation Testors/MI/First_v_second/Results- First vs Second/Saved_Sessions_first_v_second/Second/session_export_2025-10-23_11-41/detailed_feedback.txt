# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s presenting concerns and imposes an agenda focused on smoking cessation, which is irrelevant to the patient’s actual issues. The tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or respect for the patient’s autonomy or perspective. Statements such as “You need to stop making excuses,” “You have to commit,” and “No more wishy-washy attempts” are harsh, critical, and undermine acceptance. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or validation of the patient’s feelings or experiences. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s ambivalence and struggles, which is antithetical to acceptance in MI.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is judgmental and directive, lacking respect and support. This severely undermines rapport and patient engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to explore the patient’s feelings, values, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist interrupts and challenges the patient’s expressions of struggle and hesitation with blunt commands and accusations of “making excuses.” The patient’s nuanced concerns about mental health, sustainability, and past failures are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the emotional complexity underlying the patient’s ambivalence, resulting in a lack of empathy.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is virtually absent; the psychiatrist does not listen or reflect the patient’s experience, instead pushing a rigid agenda.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven approach with no collaboration or evocation. The psychiatrist repeatedly tells the patient what to do, demands commitment, and dismisses the patient’s autonomy and ambivalence. There is no evidence of partnership, evocation of the patient’s own motivations, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist’s style is coercive rather than collaborative, which is the antithesis of MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is entirely absent; the psychiatrist dominates the conversation and disregards the patient’s voice and choice.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient spontaneously offers some change talk (e.g., desire for sustainable health, enjoyment of exercise), the psychiatrist ignores or challenges these statements rather than amplifying them. The psychiatrist’s confrontational style likely suppresses further change talk. There are no strategic reflections or questions designed to deepen the patient’s motivation or explore ambivalence constructively.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither evoked nor cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently reinforces sustain talk and resistance by labeling the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and “hesitation.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist confronts sustain talk with demands and ultimatums, which likely increases resistance. The patient’s legitimate concerns about mental health and sustainability are dismissed rather than explored or softened.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and reinforced, increasing patient resistance rather than softening it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to establish any sense of partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and directives, with no invitation for the patient to share expertise or co-create goals. The patient’s attempts to express her perspective are overridden or ignored. The psychiatrist does not minimize power differentials or foster a collaborative environment.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that alienates the patient.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that honors the patient’s autonomy and perspective.  
- Reflective listening and empathy are critical to understanding and validating the patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must replace the current directive, confrontational style.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced, while sustain talk should be skillfully softened.  
- Partnership must be established by inviting the patient’s input and co-creating goals.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must fundamentally change approach to align with MI principles.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening, empathy, and acceptance.  
- Use open-ended questions to explore patient’s values and ambivalence.  
- Avoid confrontational language and commands; instead, support autonomy and collaboration.  
- Actively evoke and reinforce change talk while softening sustain talk.  
- Build partnership by inviting patient input and co-creating plans.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Respectful Stance:**  
   Replace directive commands with supportive, accepting language that validates the patient’s experience.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and encourage deeper exploration.

3. **Employ Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Invite the patient to elaborate on her goals, values, and ambivalence.

4. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Explicitly affirm the patient’s right to choose and control her change process.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   Use strategic questions and reflections to draw out the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

7. **Build Partnership:**  
   Collaborate with the patient to set goals and develop plans that fit her values and lifestyle.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   Replace “You have to” and “No more excuses” with collaborative language.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style alienates the patient, undermines rapport, and likely decreases motivation for change. To provide effective MI, the psychiatrist must radically shift to a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that honors the patient’s ambivalence and fosters intrinsic motivation. Without these changes, the risk of patient disengagement and treatment failure is high.
