# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and directive, repeatedly labeling the patient’s reasons as “excuses” and pressuring him to quit. There is little to no validation or acceptance of the patient’s struggles or ambivalence.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal empathy is demonstrated. The psychiatrist uses accusatory questions and dismisses the patient’s explanations without reflective listening or validation, which likely increases defensiveness.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with repeated commands and pressure to “just decide” and “commit.” There is no collaboration or support for autonomy.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist dominates the interaction, positioning the patient as someone who must comply rather than collaborate. The patient’s perspective is not invited beyond demands for answers.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk occurs. The psychiatrist dismisses patient’s expressions of desire and ambivalence rather than exploring or amplifying them.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist repeatedly challenges the patient’s reasons for not quitting without validating or softening resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [x] Avoids roadblocks (No)  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist uses mostly closed, confrontational questions and fails to reflect or affirm, resulting in a confrontational and alienating interaction.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal (smoking cessation focus)  
- [ ] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on smoking cessation but does so through pressure rather than collaborative goal-setting.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
There is no effective evocation of the patient’s motivations or reasons for change; the psychiatrist demands change without exploration.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [ ] Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist demands a quit date without assessing readiness or supporting the patient in developing a plan.
