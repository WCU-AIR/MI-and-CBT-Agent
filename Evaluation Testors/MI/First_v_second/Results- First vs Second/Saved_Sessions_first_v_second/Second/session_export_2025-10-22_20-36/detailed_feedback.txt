# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s concerns and perspective, telling her “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do” and repeatedly insisting she “stop hesitating” and “just do it.” The patient’s ambivalence and fears are ignored or invalidated rather than accepted. The psychiatrist uses language that implies blame and fault (“Why haven’t you made the call yet? Are you just procrastinating or what?”), which is likely to increase defensiveness and reduce rapport. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect for the patient’s perspective. The approach is authoritarian and dismissive, which is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal to no effort to understand or reflect the patient’s feelings or concerns. The patient expresses fear, confusion, and a desire for reassurance, but the psychiatrist ignores these emotional cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s worries. The psychiatrist’s responses are blunt commands and accusations rather than empathic reflections. For example, when the patient says she is scared and wants to be sure, the psychiatrist responds with “enough with the hesitation” and “stop overthinking,” which dismisses the patient’s emotional experience.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate perspective-taking or reflective listening, which undermines trust and engagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely lacking. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “must” do and questioning her motives (“Are you just procrastinating or what?”). There is no collaboration or invitation to explore the patient’s own motivations or values. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the patient is pressured and blamed. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s reasons for change but rather imposes a directive agenda.  

**Summary:**  
The session is antithetical to MI spirit, characterized by control, persuasion, and disregard for patient autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses tentative willingness and concern for her child’s health, the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead focuses on demanding action. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s expressed desire to protect her child or her attempts to understand the vaccine issue. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to elicit and strengthen the patient’s own motivations for vaccination.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither cultivated nor reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses it.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s fears, doubts, and ambivalence—is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s concerns as “hesitation,” “procrastination,” or “making excuses,” which likely entrenches sustain talk rather than resolving ambivalence. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that acknowledge the legitimacy of the patient’s concerns while gently guiding toward change.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for the patient’s expertise in her own life. The patient is repeatedly pressured and questioned in a way that implies blame and lack of trust. The psychiatrist does not invite the patient to participate in planning or express her own goals.  

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent; the session is authoritarian and directive.

- The session must embody MI spirit: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid blaming or accusing language.  
   - Respect the patient’s perspective and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s fears and concerns to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the difficulty of the decision.

3. **Embody the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than directing.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own reasons and motivations for vaccination.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or concern for her child’s health, reflect and explore these statements.  
   - Reinforce any statements indicating willingness or readiness.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Normalize hesitation as a common and understandable experience.

6. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Invite the patient to identify next steps she feels comfortable with.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Pressure:**  
   - Replace commands and accusations with open questions and affirmations.  
   - Build rapport and trust to facilitate change.
