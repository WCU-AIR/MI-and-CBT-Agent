# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist repeatedly uses language that blames or shames the patient (e.g., “You really should stop smoking now,” “You have to take this seriously,” “No more delays,” “This isn’t optional,” “You’re just digging your own grave”). The patient’s frustration and vulnerability are ignored or dismissed rather than accepted. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings or perspective, instead pushing a rigid agenda. This is the opposite of acceptance and is likely to increase resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and confrontational, lacking any genuine acceptance or respect for the patient’s experience.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no demonstration of empathy or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or convey the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses frustration, feelings of being unfairly targeted, and a strong desire to connect with family, but the psychiatrist ignores these emotional cues and focuses solely on delivering directives and warnings. The psychiatrist does not reflect or paraphrase the patient’s concerns, nor do they acknowledge the patient’s emotional state. This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s defensiveness and sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate any empathic understanding or validation of the patient’s feelings or situation.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely lacking in the spirit of motivational interviewing. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. Instead, the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding immediate commitment. The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist uses coercive language (“This isn’t optional,” “You have to take this seriously”). There is no attempt to evoke the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change, nor to explore ambivalence. The interaction is directive and confrontational.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s style is antithetical to MI spirit, undermining partnership, evocation, and autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce any change talk. The only patient change talk present is minimal and spontaneous (“I know smoking’s bad,” “Maybe I’ll think about it later”), but the psychiatrist does not respond to or amplify these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with warnings and demands, which likely suppresses any emerging motivation. There is no strategic use of open questions or reflections to deepen change talk or explore the patient’s reasons, desires, or confidence.

**Summary:**  
No skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully respond to sustain talk. The patient expresses sustain talk related to frustration with rules, feelings of unfair treatment, and prioritizing family connection over quitting smoking. The psychiatrist ignores these sustain talk elements and instead confronts the patient with ultimatums and blame. There are no double-sided reflections, no normalization of ambivalence, and no attempts to gently redirect or explore sustain talk. This approach likely increases resistance and sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than skillful softening or exploration.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issues commands, and ignores the patient’s expressed concerns and priorities. The patient is not invited to share their perspective or participate in decision-making. The psychiatrist’s language is controlling and directive, reinforcing a power imbalance. The patient’s autonomy and expertise in their own life are disregarded.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, lacking any meaningful partnership or shared decision-making.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that accepts the patient’s feelings and perspective.  
- Empathy and reflective listening are essential to understand and validate the patient’s experience.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must replace the current authoritarian approach.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced through open questions and reflections.  
- Sustain talk requires skillful softening and exploration, not confrontation.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making must be prioritized to engage the patient effectively.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid blaming or shaming language.  
   - Validate the patient’s feelings and experiences.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion.  
   - Show understanding of the patient’s frustration and priorities.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Use Open Questions and Complex Reflections:**  
   - Invite elaboration and exploration of ambivalence.  
   - Reflect sustain talk with double-sided reflections.

5. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize past compliance and desire to connect with family.

6. **Avoid Persuasion Without Permission:**  
   - Ask permission before giving advice or information.

7. **Develop Collaborative Plans:**  
   - Summarize change talk and negotiate next steps with the patient.

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Intensive training and supervision in MI techniques are urgently recommended.
