# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental and confrontational from the outset. Statements such as “You really should be able to control this,” “No excuses,” and “You need to quit or at least cut way back immediately” communicate disapproval and a lack of unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge Brandon’s perspective or feelings, instead imposing a rigid expectation. There is no warmth, support, or validation of Brandon’s experience, which is critical in establishing acceptance. This approach risks alienating the patient and shutting down open communication.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance, lacking respect and support. This severely undermines rapport and client engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand Brandon’s ambivalence or perspective. Instead, the psychiatrist immediately challenges and dismisses Brandon’s explanations and feelings. The tone is accusatory rather than curious or compassionate. Empathy requires at least some effort to see the situation from the client’s point of view, which is entirely absent here.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate any empathy, missing critical opportunities to connect with Brandon’s experience and ambivalence.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with no collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands immediate commitment and compliance, ignoring Brandon’s readiness or perspective. There is no invitation for Brandon to explore his own motivations or values. The spirit of MI—partnership, acceptance, evocation, and autonomy—is completely absent.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a coercive, expert-driven stance, contrary to the fundamental principles of motivational interviewing.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not evoke or reinforce any change talk. Instead, the approach is confrontational, which typically elicits resistance rather than motivation. There are no open-ended questions designed to explore Brandon’s reasons for change or to elicit his own motivations. The psychiatrist’s questions are closed, leading, and accusatory, which stifles any spontaneous change talk.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the approach likely suppresses client motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate sustain talk or ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk is met with direct confrontation and dismissal (“You really should be able to control this,” “No excuses”). This approach intensifies resistance and defensiveness rather than softening sustain talk or helping Brandon resolve ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than skillfully softened or explored.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, issuing commands and ultimatums. Brandon is not invited to participate in decision-making or to express his views meaningfully. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda with no client-centered collaboration.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior (drinking).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s confrontational, judgmental style is counterproductive and inconsistent with MI principles.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The session lacks open questions, reflections, affirmations, and autonomy support.  
- The approach risks increasing client resistance and disengagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   Begin sessions by expressing respect and understanding for the client’s experience, avoiding blame or criticism.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   Reflect both content and feelings to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   Collaborate with the client, evoke their own motivations, and support their autonomy.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   Use open-ended questions and reflections to draw out client reasons for change.

5. **Softly Explore Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validation to reduce resistance and ambivalence.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   Encourage elaboration and recognize client strengths and efforts.

7. **Avoid Directives and Ultimatums:**  
   Instead, invite the client to consider change at their own pace and readiness.

8. **Develop Collaborative Plans:**  
   Work with the client to set realistic, achievable goals and next steps.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and judgmental approach is likely to increase resistance and reduce client engagement. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive style that actively evokes and reinforces the client’s own motivations for change. Without these changes, the effectiveness of interventions will be severely compromised.
