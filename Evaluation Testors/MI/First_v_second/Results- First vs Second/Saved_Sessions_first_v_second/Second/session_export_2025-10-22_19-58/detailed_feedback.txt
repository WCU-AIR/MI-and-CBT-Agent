## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling or moralizing language.  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient’s experience and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s statements and feelings to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate ambivalence and normalize mixed feelings about change.

3. **Shift from Directive to Collaborative Spirit:**  
   - Invite the patient to share their perspective and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy by emphasizing choice and control.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions that explore the patient’s motivations, values, and goals.  
   - Reflect and reinforce any statements favoring change.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Avoid confrontation that increases resistance.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Encourage elaboration and exploration.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and intentions.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Summarize key points and elicit commitment when the patient is ready.  
   - Negotiate achievable next steps together.

This diagnostic session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and judgmental style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Without these changes, the session risks increasing resistance and failing to promote meaningful behavior change.
