# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental, directive, and authoritarian from the outset. The repeated insistence that the patient “needs to quit immediately” and “must take this seriously now” conveys a lack of unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s perspective, feelings, or autonomy. There is no warmth, support, or respect evident in the tone or content. The patient’s repeated attempts to express their values and readiness are ignored or overridden by the psychiatrist’s rigid stance. This is a clear violation of acceptance principles and risks alienating the patient.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, coercive style with no evidence of acceptance or respect for the patient’s autonomy or perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of empathic listening or understanding. The psychiatrist does not reflect or paraphrase the patient’s statements, nor do they attempt to understand the patient’s ambivalence or values. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with the same directive message, showing no effort to grasp the patient’s readiness or emotional state. The patient’s calm, values-based responses are met with repeated commands rather than reflective or validating statements. This lack of empathy undermines rapport and trust.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate any empathic engagement or reflective listening, ignoring the patient’s expressed feelings and values.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what they “need” to do without invitation or collaboration. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change, and autonomy is explicitly denied by commands and pressure. The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and coercive, which is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian and directive, lacking collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not attempt to elicit or reinforce change talk. Instead, they focus on delivering warnings and commands. The patient offers multiple statements indicating readiness, values, and desire for change, but these are ignored or overridden. There is no strategic reflection or amplification of change talk, nor any exploration of the patient’s own reasons or confidence. The psychiatrist misses all opportunities to cultivate motivation.

**Summary:**  
No effort is made to evoke or reinforce change talk; patient’s motivational statements are disregarded.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or soften sustain talk or ambivalence. Instead, they confront the patient with repeated ultimatums and warnings, which likely reinforce resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The patient’s expressions of needing to find their own pace and approach are ignored, which likely increases sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is ignored and likely reinforced by the psychiatrist’s confrontational style.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation with repeated commands and questions designed to interrogate rather than collaborate. The patient’s input is not integrated into the conversation meaningfully. The psychiatrist does not share power or invite the patient to co-create goals or plans. This creates a clear power imbalance and undermines the therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven, authoritarian stance with no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The patient demonstrates strong change talk and readiness.  
- The patient maintains engagement despite the psychiatrist’s style.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly problematic, characterized by judgment, coercion, and lack of empathy.  
- There is a complete absence of MI-consistent skills such as reflective listening, affirmations, evocation, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist repeatedly ignores or overrides the patient’s expressed values and motivations.  
- The session risks increasing resistance and disengagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directive commands to partnership and shared decision-making.  
   - Invite the patient’s perspective and respect their autonomy.

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Use reflections to deepen exploration of ambivalence and values.

3. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Values:**  
   - Recognize and reinforce the patient’s efforts, values, and readiness.  
   - Use affirmations to build confidence and rapport.

4. **Evoke Change Talk Actively:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions that invite the patient to articulate reasons, desires, and plans for change.  
   - Reflect and amplify change talk when it arises.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate the patient’s experience.

6. **Avoid Unsolicited Advice and Commands:**  
   - Ask permission before giving information or suggestions.  
   - Support the patient’s control over the change process.

7. **Build Engagement and Trust:**  
   - Use warmth, empathy, and acceptance to foster a safe environment.  
   - Avoid judgmental or confrontational language.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation. Despite the patient’s clear readiness and positive change talk, the psychiatrist fails to engage effectively, risking harm to the therapeutic relationship and patient motivation. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI skills and spirit is essential to prevent harm and improve clinical effectiveness.
