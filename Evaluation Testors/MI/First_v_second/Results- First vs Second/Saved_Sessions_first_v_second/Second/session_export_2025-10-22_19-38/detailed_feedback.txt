## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

Statements such as “You really should stop smoking immediately,” “You have to take this seriously,” and “Stop making excuses” communicate disapproval and lack of respect for the patient’s autonomy. The tone is authoritarian rather than supportive, and the psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s ambivalence and concerns. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. **Summary:**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directive commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s perspective and preferences.

2. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   Ask open-ended questions to explore ambivalence and motivations. Reflect the patient’s statements to demonstrate understanding and deepen dialogue.

3. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles. Avoid judgmental language and harsh commands.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or willingness, reflect and reinforce these statements to build motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control the pace and method of quitting.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   Work with the patient to identify feasible steps and supports, rather than imposing a rigid quitting method.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Judgment:**  
   Replace “stop making excuses” and similar phrases with supportive, nonjudgmental language.
