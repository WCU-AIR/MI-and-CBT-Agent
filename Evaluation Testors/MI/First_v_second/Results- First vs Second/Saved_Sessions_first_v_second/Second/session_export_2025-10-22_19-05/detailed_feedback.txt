# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and authoritarian stance throughout the session. The opening statement, “you really need to quit smoking,” and repeated commands such as “You have to stop now,” “No ifs, no buts,” and “You don’t get to negotiate on this” communicate a lack of unconditional positive regard. The patient’s attempts to clarify and express ambivalence are dismissed or overridden. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or support; instead, the tone is confrontational and directive. This approach risks alienating the patient and shutting down open communication.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, directive style that undermines rapport and patient engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not reflect or validate the patient’s feelings, concerns, or ambivalence. For example, when the patient expresses discomfort about telling friends or fear of social rejection, the psychiatrist responds with ultimatums rather than understanding or exploration. The patient’s nuanced concerns about peer relationships and readiness are ignored. The psychiatrist’s responses are one-sided, focusing on compliance rather than understanding the patient’s perspective.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, missing critical opportunities for reflective listening and emotional attunement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the core MI elements of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing commands and ultimatums (“You don’t get to ease into this,” “No ifs, no buts,” “This isn’t optional”). There is no evidence of partnership or invitation for the patient to explore their own motivations or values. Autonomy is denied rather than supported, with the patient pressured to commit immediately without space for ambivalence or self-direction.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent; the session is dominated by directive, coercive communication that undermines collaboration and autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment and compliance, there is little skillful evocation of change talk. The patient does express some change talk spontaneously (e.g., concern about soccer and scholarships, willingness to try), but the psychiatrist does not strategically reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the focus is on commands and ultimatums, which may provoke resistance rather than deepen motivation. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore the patient’s own reasons for change or to elicit confidence and commitment language.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist’s approach.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (e.g., patient’s minimization of drinking, fear of social rejection) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or validate the patient’s ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk is met with pressure and ultimatums, likely increasing resistance and disengagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk, instead reinforcing resistance through confrontational responses.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The communication is one-directional, with the psychiatrist dictating the agenda and plan. The patient’s input is ignored or overridden. There is no evidence of shared decision-making or negotiation. The power differential is emphasized rather than minimized.

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction without fostering equality or collaboration.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist consistently attempts to focus on the patient’s health risks and future goals.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, lacking acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values.  
- Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than softening.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and social concerns are ignored.  
- The session lacks the spirit and techniques of motivational interviewing.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and confrontational, lacking acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- There is no use of reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and social concerns are ignored or dismissed.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directive commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s perspective and co-create the agenda.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence. Avoid judgmental language.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage the patient to elaborate on their thoughts, feelings, and motivations.

4. **Evoke Change Talk Skillfully:**  
   - When the patient expresses reasons or desire for change, reflect and amplify these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control the pace of change.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Plans:**  
   - Work with the patient to create realistic, achievable goals and strategies.

8. **Avoid Ultimatums and Pressure:**  
   - Replace confrontational language with supportive, motivational dialogue.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation of change talk. The patient’s ambivalence and social concerns are ignored, and sustain talk is met with pressure rather than softening. To improve, the psychiatrist must fundamentally shift to a collaborative, autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully uses MI techniques to engage and motivate the patient.
