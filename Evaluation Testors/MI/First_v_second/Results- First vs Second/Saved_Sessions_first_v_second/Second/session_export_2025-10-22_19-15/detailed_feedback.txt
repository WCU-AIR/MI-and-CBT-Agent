## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the smoking cessation goal.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly confrontational, directive, and judgmental, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and reflective listening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- No affirmations, summaries, or collaborative planning are evident.  
- The psychiatrist does not support autonomy or partnership, instead imposing demands and ultimatums.  
- The questioning style is closed and leading, limiting patient engagement and exploration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “overcomplicating.”  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient’s experience and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s fears and social concerns.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   - Invite the patient to share her views and co-create goals and plans.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and demands; instead, explore readiness and willingness.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Softly Manage Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and the importance of social connection.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore the patient’s perspective.  
   - Affirm strengths, efforts, and intentions to build confidence.

7. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Summarize key points and change talk to reinforce motivation.  
   - Develop a concrete, patient-driven plan with achievable steps.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and judgmental approach is likely to increase resistance and disengagement. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and skillful evocation of the patient’s own motivations and ambivalence. Without these changes, the session risks alienating patients and undermining the goal of supporting meaningful behavior change.
