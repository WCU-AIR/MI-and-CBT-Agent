# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental, directive, and lacking in warmth or positive regard. From the very first utterance, the psychiatrist uses commanding language (“you really need to quit smoking,” “You have to take this seriously”) that communicates disapproval rather than acceptance. There is no evidence of respect for the patient’s autonomy or acknowledgment of the patient’s perspective or feelings. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s hesitations and reflections as “making excuses” or “overcomplicating,” which is highly likely to alienate the patient. This is the opposite of unconditional positive regard and creates a confrontational atmosphere.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic listening or understanding. The psychiatrist does not reflect or validate the patient’s feelings, concerns, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts or overrides the patient’s attempts to explain their perspective with admonitions and demands. For example, when the patient expresses ambivalence about quitting and the social pressures involved, the psychiatrist responds with “stop making excuses” and “you don’t get to decide when,” showing a lack of perspective-taking. The psychiatrist does not use reflective listening or paraphrasing to demonstrate understanding.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, resulting in a dismissive and confrontational interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, directive stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without invitation or collaboration. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values; instead, the psychiatrist insists on immediate compliance (“You have to quit now,” “Are you going to get the patches today or not?”). Autonomy is explicitly denied (“You don’t get to decide when”), which is antithetical to MI principles. The psychiatrist’s approach is coercive rather than collaborative.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a controlling, directive style that negates the core MI principles of partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient tentatively expresses ambivalence or subtle change talk (“I want to quit when I’m ready,” “I’m trying to figure out how to balance things”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly pushes for immediate commitment without exploring or eliciting the patient’s internal motivations.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither evoked nor cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses rather than encourages patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften sustain talk but rather confronts and challenges it aggressively. The patient’s sustain talk (e.g., “I’m not smoking daily,” “I’m not ready to quit cold turkey”) is met with accusations of “making excuses” and “avoiding responsibility.” There is no use of double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses are likely to increase resistance rather than reduce sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly overrides the patient’s voice and insists on compliance. The patient is not invited to participate in setting goals or exploring options. The psychiatrist’s language is authoritarian and coercive, reinforcing a power differential rather than minimizing it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to establish a collaborative partnership, instead adopting an expert-driven, directive stance.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Empathic listening and reflective responses are essential.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must be central.  
- Change talk should be actively elicited and reinforced.  
- Sustain talk requires skillful softening, not confrontation.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making must replace authoritarian directives.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   Practice unconditional positive regard and avoid labeling or blaming language.

2. **Develop Empathic Listening Skills:**  
   Use reflective listening to demonstrate understanding of the patient’s perspective and feelings.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   Foster collaboration, evoke the patient’s own motivations, and support autonomy.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.

5. **Softly Address Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   Encourage elaboration and recognize patient strengths and efforts.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Plans:**  
   Engage the patient in setting achievable goals and next steps.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Directives:**  
   Replace commands with invitations and permission-seeking.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is likely to increase patient resistance and reduce motivation for change. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques, including acceptance, empathy, evocation, and partnership, is essential to prevent harm and improve patient outcomes.
