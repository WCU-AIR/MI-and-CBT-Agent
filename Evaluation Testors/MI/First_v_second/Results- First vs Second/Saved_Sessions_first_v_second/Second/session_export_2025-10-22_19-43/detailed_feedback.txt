## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and confrontational stance throughout the session. The opening line, “you need to quit smoking,” followed by repeated commands and ultimatums (“You have to be strict with yourself,” “This isn’t optional”) clearly communicates disapproval and lack of unconditional positive regard. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or support for the patient’s ambivalence or perspective. **Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, directive, and judgmental tone that likely alienates the patient.

**Summary:**

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and judgmental, which is inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The session lacks collaboration, autonomy support, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist’s style likely increases patient resistance and disengagement.  

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as excuses or overthinking.  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient’s experience and ambivalence.  

2. **Develop Empathy and Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   - Use reflections to demonstrate understanding of patient feelings and perspectives.  
   - Reflect both content and emotional subtext to deepen connection.  

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support patient autonomy and choice.  

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Explore confidence and commitment language.  

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate sustain talk without reinforcing it.  

6. **Build Partnership and Shared Decision-Making:**  
   - Invite the patient to co-create goals and plans.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercion.  

7. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore patient thoughts and feelings.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and values.  

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Feasible Plan:**  
   - Explore small, manageable steps rather than demanding immediate drastic change.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment.

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Without these changes, the psychiatrist risks alienating patients and impeding meaningful behavior change.
