## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, confrontational, and directive stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“you need to quit smoking”—the tone is authoritative and dismissive of the patient’s actual concerns. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates Mei Lin’s ambivalence and struggles by labeling her willpower as “weak” and accusing her of “making excuses.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s feelings and perspectives are frequently dismissed or challenged harshly, which likely contributed to her defensiveness and reluctance to engage openly. This is a clear violation of MI principles of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and confrontational, lacking respect and support. This severely undermines rapport and client engagement.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate empathy or reflective listening. There are no attempts to understand or validate Mei Lin’s feelings, fears, or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly pushes a rigid agenda without acknowledging the patient’s perspective or emotional experience. For example, when Mei Lin expresses fear of losing friends or difficulty with willpower, the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns as “excuses” and demands compliance. The lack of reflective statements or paraphrasing means the patient’s perspective is neither understood nor conveyed back, which is critical for empathy.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not listen or respond to the patient’s emotional state or ambivalence.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely missing. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, directive stance, repeatedly ordering the patient to “just commit” and “stop making excuses.” There is no collaboration or partnership; the patient is not invited to explore her own motivations or solutions. Autonomy is explicitly undermined by demands for yes/no answers and threats of failure if the patient does not comply. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own reasons for change beyond superficial repetition of “you need to cut out those friends.” This approach is antithetical to MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by control and persuasion, with no evidence of MI’s collaborative, autonomy-supportive stance.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly asks for a commitment to change, this is done in a coercive manner rather than through skillful evocation. The patient does express some change talk (e.g., “I want to do better,” “I don’t want to go down the path my family did”), but the psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the focus is on demanding compliance rather than exploring or strengthening intrinsic motivation. The repeated insistence on a yes/no commitment without exploring the patient’s ambivalence or confidence limits the depth and quality of change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively cultivated or reinforced; the approach is more coercive than evocative.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s reasons for maintaining current behavior or ambivalence—is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic reflection. The psychiatrist labels patient concerns as “excuses” and “weakness,” escalating resistance rather than reducing sustain talk. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This harsh approach likely increases sustain talk and resistance, undermining motivation.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and not softened; this is counterproductive and increases resistance.

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling or blaming language.  
   - Express unconditional positive regard and validate the patient’s feelings and struggles.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Use complex reflections to capture ambivalence and emotional nuance.  
   - Reflect the patient’s fears, hopes, and challenges to build rapport.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than directing or commanding.  
   - Support autonomy by inviting the patient to explore options and make choices.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.  
   - Explore confidence and commitment in a supportive manner.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate the patient’s experience.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore motivations and barriers.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and intentions sincerely.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Realistic Plans:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify achievable steps.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment without pressure.

8. **Avoid Coercive Language and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace demands with invitations and collaborative goal-setting.  
   - Respect the patient’s pace and readiness for change.
