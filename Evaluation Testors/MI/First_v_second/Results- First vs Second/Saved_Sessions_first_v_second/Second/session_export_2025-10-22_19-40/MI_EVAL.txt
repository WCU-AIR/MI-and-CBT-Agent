# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a largely judgmental and directive stance, repeatedly telling the patient to “get a grip” and “stop making excuses.” There is little to no warmth or validation of the patient’s experience, resulting in a lack of acceptance.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal reflective listening is present. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the patient’s fear and ambivalence, instead responding with commands and questions that imply fault and impatience.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive style with frequent persuasion and little collaboration or evocation of the patient’s own motivations. Autonomy is not supported.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is expert-driven, with commands and directives minimizing patient input and autonomy.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is weak and tentative from the patient and is neither reflected nor amplified by the psychiatrist. Opportunities to evoke and reinforce motivation are missed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced by confrontational and pressuring responses. The psychiatrist invalidates the patient’s struggle, increasing resistance rather than softening sustain talk.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [ ] Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is minimal; the psychiatrist maintains control but does not build rapport or trust. The patient appears hesitant and uncomfortable.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal  
- [x] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on the patient’s avoidance and anxiety but does so rigidly and without collaboration.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
There is no effective evocation of the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change; the psychiatrist relies on commands rather than eliciting patient-driven reasons.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [ ] Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist demands immediate action without co-creating a plan or consolidating commitment.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Draw out narrative, values  
- [x] Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Questions are mostly closed or leading, serving more to interrogate than to explore the patient’s perspective.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)

- [x] Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Virtually no reflective statements are present; the psychiatrist does not paraphrase or reflect the patient’s feelings or ambivalence.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Pulls together change talk  
- [x] Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
No summaries are provided to consolidate understanding or reinforce motivation.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
- [x] Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
No affirmations are offered; the psychiatrist focuses on criticism and blame rather than recognizing patient efforts or strengths.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Provides advice/info only after asking

**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands and advice without seeking permission or consent.
