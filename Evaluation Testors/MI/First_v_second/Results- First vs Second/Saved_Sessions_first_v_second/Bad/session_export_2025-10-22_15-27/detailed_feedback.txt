## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is commanding rather than supportive. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses Elena’s feelings and ambivalence, labeling her concerns as “excuses” and “making excuses,” which is explicitly judgmental and invalidating. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s anxiety and hesitation are met with impatience and pressure rather than empathy or acceptance. This approach likely contributed to Elena’s withdrawal and resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect for Elena’s perspective. The interaction is dominated by judgment and directive commands, which is antithetical to MI principles.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and challenges Elena’s emotional experience (“You have to stop being scared and overwhelmed”). The psychiatrist does not demonstrate perspective-taking or emotional attunement.

**Summary:**

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive from start to finish. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“You have to call the clinic right after this,” “This isn’t negotiable”) and uses confrontational language (“stop making excuses,” “you don’t get to wait”). The psychiatrist ignores Elena’s attempts to express ambivalence and need for pacing, instead insisting on immediate compliance. This approach is the opposite of the MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, coercive stance that undermines collaboration and client autonomy.

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk

The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to explore Elena’s fears or to elicit her own reasons for change in a supportive way. Instead, the focus is on compliance rather than motivation.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling client concerns as “excuses.”  
   - Validate ambivalence and emotional difficulty.

2. **Increase Empathic Listening and Reflective Statements:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Use complex reflections to deepen engagement.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the client rather than directing.  
   - Evoke client’s own motivations and values.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Invite exploration of feelings, fears, and motivations.

5. **Affirm Client Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize any positive steps or intentions.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalization.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Explore options and readiness before pushing for immediate action.  
   - Summarize motivation and next steps.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace “you have to” with “what do you think about…” or “how might you approach…”
