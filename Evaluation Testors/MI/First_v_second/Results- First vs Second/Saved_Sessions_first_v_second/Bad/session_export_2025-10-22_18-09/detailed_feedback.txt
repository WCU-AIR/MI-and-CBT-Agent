# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s concerns and perspective, repeatedly invalidating Marco’s ambivalence and struggles. Phrases such as “You don’t get to pick and choose here,” “No exceptions,” and “No, you don’t get to decide” communicate a lack of unconditional positive regard and respect. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or normalize the patient’s difficulties or ambivalence, instead blaming and pressuring him. This approach is contrary to MI principles of acceptance and respect.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is rigid, directive, and dismissive, lacking any warmth or acceptance. This severely undermines rapport and client engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or validate the patient’s feelings of overwhelm, ambivalence, or the contextual challenges (family temptations, habitual behavior). Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and accusations (“You’re just making excuses,” “Why didn’t you just say no?”). The patient’s expressions of difficulty and need for gradual change are ignored or invalidated. The psychiatrist fails to capture or reflect the patient’s emotional state or perspective, resulting in a cold, confrontational tone.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate perspective-taking or emotional attunement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely missing. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without invitation or collaboration. The patient’s autonomy is denied outright, with repeated statements that he “does not get to decide” or “pick and choose.” There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values; instead, the psychiatrist issues ultimatums and threats. This approach is antithetical to MI spirit and likely to provoke resistance.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by control and directive commands, with no partnership or autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient offers tentative change talk (e.g., willingness to try cutting back gradually, desire to improve health), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements. There is no amplification or exploration of the patient’s own reasons or confidence. Instead, the psychiatrist pushes a rigid plan and demands immediate compliance, which likely suppresses further change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present in the patient but not cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s expressions of difficulty, ambivalence, and barriers) is met with confrontation and invalidation rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s statements as “excuses” and refuses to acknowledge the legitimacy of his struggles. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This approach likely entrenches sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or explored.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands, ultimatums, and a refusal to negotiate or listen. The patient is repeatedly told he “does not get to decide,” which creates a clear power imbalance. The psychiatrist fails to invite the patient’s input or co-create a plan, instead imposing a rigid agenda.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership or shared decision-making; the stance is authoritarian.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must radically shift from a directive, authoritarian style to one of acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening and emotional attunement.  
- Learn to evoke and reinforce change talk rather than suppress it.  
- Practice softening sustain talk with double-sided reflections and normalization.  
- Support patient autonomy and co-create plans rather than impose ultimatums.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

- The patient remains engaged enough to express ambivalence and some change talk despite the confrontational style.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly confrontational, directive, and lacking in MI spirit.  
- There is no acceptance, empathy, or partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions.  
- Change talk is not cultivated; sustain talk is harshly confronted.  
- The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly denied, which is counterproductive.  
- Planning is imposed rather than co-created.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt an Accepting, Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   - Practice unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient’s perspective and ambivalence.

2. **Develop Empathic Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   - Invite the patient’s input and co-create goals and plans. Avoid ultimatums.

4. **Use Open Questions to Evoke Motivation:**  
   - Ask questions that encourage elaboration on reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

5. **Amplify Change Talk and Softly Redirect Sustain Talk:**  
   - Reflect and reinforce statements favoring change; use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.

6. **Avoid Confrontation and Direct Commands:**  
   - Replace “You don’t get to decide” with collaborative language that respects the patient’s choice.

7. **Build a Concrete, Realistic Plan Together:**  
   - Develop achievable steps that the patient feels ready to commit to, with flexibility.

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, suppresses motivation, and denies patient autonomy. Without urgent and substantial changes in approach, this style risks alienating patients and impeding meaningful behavior change. The psychiatrist must undergo comprehensive MI training focused on acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation to provide effective, patient-centered care.
