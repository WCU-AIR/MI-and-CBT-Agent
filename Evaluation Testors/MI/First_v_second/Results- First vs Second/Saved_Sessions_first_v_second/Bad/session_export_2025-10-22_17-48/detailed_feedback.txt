# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a judgmental, confrontational, and authoritarian tone throughout the session. There is a persistent pattern of dismissing the patient’s perspective, labeling his responses as “excuses,” and invalidating his lived experience. For example, statements like “stop making excuses,” “no more ‘own pace’ nonsense,” and “no more ‘my way’ or ‘your way’ nonsense” communicate a lack of unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the patient’s ambivalence or resistance as understandable, instead repeatedly pushing for immediate compliance. This approach is antithetical to acceptance and MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a harsh, directive style that undermines acceptance and rapport. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or validation of the patient’s autonomy or experience.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase the patient’s feelings or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and accusations (“You’re just making excuses,” “You’re fooling yourself”), which likely alienate the patient. The patient’s expressions of weariness, guardedness, and faint openness are ignored or met with impatience rather than empathy. The lack of reflective listening or emotional attunement is stark.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is virtually absent. The psychiatrist fails to convey understanding or validate the patient’s experience, which damages rapport and engagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive stance with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands immediate action, dismisses the patient’s autonomy, and uses coercive language (“Are you going to do it or not?” “No more delays”). There is no invitation for the patient to explore his own motivations or values, nor any support for self-efficacy. The spirit of MI—partnership, acceptance, evocation—is entirely absent.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is the antithesis of MI spirit, relying on confrontation and pressure rather than partnership and evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not evoke or reinforce any change talk. Instead, the clinician repeatedly challenges and confronts sustain talk without attempting to explore or soften it. The patient’s faint change talk (“I’m open to listen,” “I’m listening and thinking about it”) is ignored or dismissed. There are no strategic reflections or questions designed to elicit the patient’s own reasons for change or confidence. The psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivates it.

**Summary:**  
No skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk is present; the psychiatrist’s style likely inhibits motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently confronts and challenges sustain talk with accusatory and dismissive language (“You’re just making excuses,” “Are you fooling yourself?”). There are no double-sided reflections, no normalization of ambivalence, and no attempts to gently redirect or explore sustain talk. This confrontational approach likely increases resistance and sustain talk rather than softening it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead exacerbates resistance through confrontation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative relationship. The interaction is dominated by commands, ultimatums, and dismissive remarks. The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly overridden with “you have to” statements and demands for immediate compliance. There is no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for the patient’s expertise in his own life. The psychiatrist’s stance is authoritarian and expert-driven.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist exerts control and power rather than working as an equal with the patient.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must radically shift from a confrontational, directive style to one that embodies acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- Reflective listening and validation of the patient’s perspective are essential.  
- The clinician should evoke and reinforce change talk rather than suppress it.  
- Sustain talk must be handled skillfully with softening strategies rather than confrontation.  
- Autonomy support and collaborative planning are critical to effective MI.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the confrontational, authoritarian style.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses.”  
   - Validate the patient’s experience and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Paraphrase patient statements to build rapport.

3. **Ask Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Invite the patient to elaborate on feelings, values, and motivations.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize the patient’s attempts to quit and openness to listen.

5. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Respect the patient’s pace and readiness to change.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercive language.

6. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - Explore reasons for change and confidence.  
   - Reinforce any statements favoring change.

7. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalization.  
   - Avoid direct confrontation.

8. **Collaboratively Plan Next Steps:**  
   - Develop achievable goals with patient input.  
   - Summarize progress and commitments.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session exemplifies a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and dismissive approach undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Without immediate and substantial changes to style and technique, the psychiatrist risks alienating patients and reducing the likelihood of successful behavior change. Mastery of MI requires embracing empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and skillful evocation of motivation—none of which are demonstrated here.
