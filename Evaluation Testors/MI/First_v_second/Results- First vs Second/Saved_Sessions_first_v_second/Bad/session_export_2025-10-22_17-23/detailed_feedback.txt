# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly directive and judgmental rather than accepting. From the outset, the psychiatrist states, “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do,” which immediately establishes an authoritarian tone. This stance undermines unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient’s autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressed concerns and rationale as “excuses” or “overcomplicating,” which is judgmental and invalidating. There is no evidence of warmth, empathy, or genuine support for the patient’s perspective. The patient’s attempts to explain her realistic approach to behavior change are met with criticism rather than acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate unconditional positive regard, instead adopting a judgmental and directive stance that likely alienates the patient.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient clearly articulates her motivations, barriers, and confidence, yet the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these in favor of pushing a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or paraphrases that demonstrate understanding of the patient’s ambivalence or lived experience. The psychiatrist’s questions and statements are closed, leading, and confrontational rather than exploratory or empathetic. The patient’s frustration and rationale are not acknowledged or validated.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is limited; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate accurate or consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the core elements of MI spirit: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “needs to do” and dismissing her reasons and plans. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values beyond superficial questioning. Autonomy is undermined by pressuring the patient to “just do it” and labeling her concerns as excuses. The psychiatrist does not invite partnership or co-create goals; instead, the interaction is one-sided and directive.

**Summary:**  
The session is fundamentally non-MI in spirit, characterized by persuasion, directive commands, and lack of collaboration.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
While the patient spontaneously offers strong change talk (desire, ability, reasons, commitment), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce it. Instead of amplifying the patient’s confidence and realistic goals, the psychiatrist challenges and minimizes them. There is no strategic reflection or reinforcement of change talk; rather, the psychiatrist interrupts or redirects toward a rigid prescription. The patient’s statements about confidence and planned steps are met with skepticism and pressure rather than encouragement.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not cultivated; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to deepen and reinforce motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften sustain talk or ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk (patient’s concerns about distractions, gym contracts, and realistic pacing) is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels these as “excuses” and “overthinking,” which likely increases resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that acknowledge the legitimacy of the patient’s concerns while gently guiding toward change.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and met with confrontation rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and demanding compliance. The patient’s input is repeatedly overridden or dismissed. The psychiatrist does not invite the patient to co-create goals or plans, nor does she acknowledge the patient’s expertise in her own life. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and expert-driven, lacking any meaningful partnership or collaboration.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from an authoritarian to a partnership approach. Invite the patient’s input and respect her expertise in her own life.

2. **Increase Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Use reflective listening to demonstrate understanding and validate the patient’s feelings and rationale. Avoid labeling concerns as “excuses.”

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Ask questions that invite elaboration and exploration of motivations, values, and barriers.

4. **Affirm Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and affirm the patient’s confidence, realistic planning, and commitment to change.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   Reflect and reinforce statements that indicate desire, ability, reasons, and commitment to change.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Explicitly acknowledge the patient’s right to choose and control the pace and nature of change.

8. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Work with the patient to co-create achievable goals and summarize progress to reinforce motivation.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s directive, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation effectively, and risks increasing resistance. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully cultivates change talk and softens sustain talk. Without these changes, the session is unlikely to facilitate meaningful behavior change and may harm the therapeutic relationship.
