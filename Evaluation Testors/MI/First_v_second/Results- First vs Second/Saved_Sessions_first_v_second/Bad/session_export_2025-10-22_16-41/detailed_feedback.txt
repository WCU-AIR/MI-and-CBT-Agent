# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective, using commands and ultimatums rather than respectful inquiry or support. Phrases such as “you have to quit,” “no ifs, ands, or buts,” and “this is not optional” communicate a lack of unconditional positive regard. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence and resistance are met with criticism and dismissal rather than acceptance. There is no evidence of warmth, empathy, or validation of the patient’s feelings or struggles. This approach is likely to increase patient resistance rather than foster collaboration.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, directive, and judgmental tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient’s statements about the role of smoking in stress relief, social routines, and ambivalence are ignored or invalidated. The psychiatrist does not use reflective listening or attempt to explore the patient’s feelings or motivations. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and challenges, showing no genuine curiosity or understanding. The patient’s emotional cues (frustration, defensiveness, ambivalence) are not acknowledged or validated. This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s increased resistance.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is virtually absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or validation of the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach that contradicts the core MI principles of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate action, dismisses the patient’s ambivalence, and refuses to engage in dialogue about the patient’s readiness or concerns. There is no evidence of evoking the patient’s own motivations or supporting autonomy; instead, the psychiatrist attempts to impose change through pressure and ultimatums. This approach is antithetical to the MI spirit and is likely to provoke resistance rather than facilitate change.

**Summary:**  
The MI spirit is entirely absent; the psychiatrist adopts a controlling, confrontational stance that undermines collaboration and autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient occasionally expresses tentative change talk (“Sure. I can quit,” “Maybe someday”), the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead focuses on demanding immediate commitment. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s own reasons or desires for change. The psychiatrist’s repeated commands and challenges likely suppress further change talk and increase sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to build motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften or strategically respond to sustain talk. The patient’s sustain talk about liking smoking, using it for stress relief, and not feeling ready is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections, normalization, or gentle redirection. Instead, sustain talk is met with accusations of making excuses and ultimatums, which likely intensify resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected, increasing patient defensiveness.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s directives and demands, with no invitation for patient input or shared decision-making. The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly overridden, and the psychiatrist refuses to negotiate or explore the patient’s perspective. This power imbalance is explicit and detrimental to engagement.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that accepts the patient’s ambivalence.  
- Reflective listening and empathy are critical to understanding the patient’s perspective.  
- The MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support must replace the current directive approach.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced, while sustain talk should be softened.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making need to be prioritized to engage the patient effectively.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and dismissive of patient autonomy and ambivalence.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, reflective listening, and acceptance.  
- The MI spirit is absent; collaboration and evocation are replaced by commands and ultimatums.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid commands and ultimatums. Respect the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Use affirmations to recognize patient strengths and efforts.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s experience and struggles.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than directing.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or ability to change, reflect and explore these statements.  
   - Avoid dismissing tentative change talk.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate sustain talk without reinforcing it.

6. **Use Open Questions and Summaries:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore the patient’s perspective.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Negotiate goals and next steps with the patient.  
   - Avoid imposing immediate action; instead, support readiness and pacing.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. To improve, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that respects the patient’s ambivalence and fosters intrinsic motivation for change. Without these changes, the risk of patient disengagement and treatment failure is high.
