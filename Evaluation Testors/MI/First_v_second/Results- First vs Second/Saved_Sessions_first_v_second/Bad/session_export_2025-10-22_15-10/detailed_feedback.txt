# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session with Sofia Delgado

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and directive from the outset. The opening statement, “We need to talk about these conflicts… You really should stop skipping classes and avoid all this drama,” immediately sets a critical tone rather than one of unconditional positive regard. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly blames Sofia (“you have to stop making excuses,” “you really need to get a grip”), which undermines acceptance. There is minimal evidence of warmth or genuine support; instead, the psychiatrist adopts a confrontational stance that likely increases Sofia’s defensiveness. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge Sofia’s feelings or validate her experience, which is critical given Sofia’s frustration and vulnerability.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s stance is largely judgmental and directive, lacking the respectful, supportive tone necessary to build trust and rapport with a vulnerable adolescent. This significantly impairs the therapeutic alliance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows limited effort to understand or reflect Sofia’s perspective. Sofia expresses frustration, hurt, and a sense of being unfairly targeted, but the psychiatrist does not reflect or validate these emotions. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on reprimanding and instructing Sofia on what she “must” do. For example, when Sofia says she tries ignoring the rumors but finds it hard, the psychiatrist dismisses this with “you really have to stop making excuses.” There are no reflective statements that capture Sofia’s emotional experience or ambivalence. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to demonstrate empathy by acknowledging Sofia’s distress and the difficulty of her situation.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is minimal and largely replaced by criticism and directive statements, which likely increases Sofia’s resistance and disengagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the core elements of MI spirit: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling Sofia what she “has to” do and demanding commitment without exploring her motivations or values. There is no evocation of Sofia’s own reasons for change or exploration of her ambivalence. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the psychiatrist pressures Sofia to “promise” and “commit” immediately, disregarding her hesitancy. The interaction is confrontational rather than collaborative, which is counterproductive for MI.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of MI spirit. This approach is unlikely to engage Sofia or foster intrinsic motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
While Sofia occasionally expresses tentative change talk (e.g., “maybe talking to that counselor could help,” “I’ll try”), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist demands immediate commitment and dismisses Sofia’s ambivalence (“No, ‘thinking about it’ isn’t good enough”). There is no strategic amplification of change talk or exploration of confidence and ability. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Sofia’s hopes or reasons for change and instead focuses on compliance.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but weakly supported and often overridden by pressure and demands, limiting its development.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is frequent, with Sofia expressing frustration, defensiveness, and doubts about change. The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully redirect sustain talk; rather, sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal (“you really have to stop making excuses,” “No more running away”). There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses likely reinforce sustain talk and resistance rather than reduce it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, exacerbating resistance rather than softening it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands, demands, and a lack of shared decision-making. Sofia’s input is minimized, and her autonomy is overridden by the psychiatrist’s insistence on immediate commitment. The psychiatrist does not invite Sofia to co-create goals or plans, nor does she acknowledge Sofia’s expertise in her own experience. This power imbalance undermines engagement and trust.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, lacking partnership and collaboration.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the presenting problem (school conflicts and attendance).

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is judgmental, confrontational, and directive, lacking acceptance and empathy.  
- There is no evidence of MI spirit; collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support are absent.  
- Change talk is not skillfully cultivated; sustain talk is met with confrontation.  
- Partnership is minimal; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and pressures Sofia for commitment.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1.5/5** (rounded down to 1 for strictness)

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Supportive Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions with open, empathetic statements that validate Sofia’s feelings and experiences.  
   - Avoid blaming or labeling language.

2. **Increase Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Use reflections to capture both content and emotion, demonstrating understanding of Sofia’s frustration and ambivalence.  
   - Validate the difficulty of her situation.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Foster collaboration by inviting Sofia’s perspective and ideas.  
   - Support autonomy by emphasizing choice and control over change.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When Sofia expresses tentative willingness, reflect and explore it further.  
   - Ask open-ended questions about her hopes, values, and reasons for change.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize ambivalence as a natural part of the process.

6. **Avoid Pressuring for Immediate Commitment:**  
   - Allow Sofia space to consider options and express ambivalence without coercion.  
   - Collaboratively develop a plan that feels manageable and realistic.

7. **Use Affirmations and Summaries:**  
   - Recognize Sofia’s strengths and efforts, even small ones.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive style undermines acceptance, empathy, and collaboration, leading to increased client defensiveness and weak change talk. To improve, the psychiatrist must fundamentally shift to a more supportive, reflective, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes motivation and softens sustain talk. Without these changes, the likelihood of engaging Sofia and facilitating meaningful change is minimal.
