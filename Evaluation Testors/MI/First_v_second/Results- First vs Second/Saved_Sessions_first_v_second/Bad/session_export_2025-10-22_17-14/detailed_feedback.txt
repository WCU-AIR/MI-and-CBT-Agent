# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, authoritarian, and directive stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s perspective and autonomy, using phrases like “you have to,” “no excuses,” and “I’m not interested in negotiating.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or respect for the patient’s experience or concerns. The patient’s attempts to express ambivalence and seek collaboration are repeatedly ignored or shut down. This approach is contrary to MI principles and likely to alienate the patient.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a controlling and confrontational style.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses concerns about feasibility, motivation, and the need for a realistic plan, but the psychiatrist disregards these cues and does not attempt reflective listening or validation. The tone is harsh and dismissive, lacking warmth or understanding. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge the patient’s feelings or struggles, which undermines rapport and trust.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or reflective listening.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely directive and authoritarian, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on compliance with a rigid plan, refuses to negotiate or explore the patient’s motivations, and uses coercive language (“you don’t get to decide,” “no exceptions”). This approach contradicts the core MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership and respect for client autonomy.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely lacking; the psychiatrist adopts a controlling, expert-driven stance.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient expresses willingness to consider liver tests or to discuss a manageable exercise plan, these are ignored or dismissed. Instead, the psychiatrist demands immediate, full commitment without exploring the patient’s own reasons or confidence. There is no strategic amplification of change talk or elicitation of commitment language beyond coercion.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to build motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s ambivalence about exercise and drinking) is met with confrontation and insistence rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or validate the patient’s concerns. Instead, sustain talk is met with dismissal and pressure, likely increasing resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist repeatedly denies the patient any role in decision-making, explicitly stating “we’re not working together” and “you don’t get to decide.” The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of orders rather than an active participant. This power imbalance undermines engagement and motivation.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist maintains an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**

- Reflective listening and empathy need to be integrated to understand and validate patient concerns.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally incompatible with motivational interviewing principles.  
- The session is dominated by authoritarian commands, lack of empathy, and refusal to collaborate.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s input and respect their autonomy.

2. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect patient statements to demonstrate understanding and validate feelings.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Encourage exploration of patient motivations, barriers, and values.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and reinforce any positive intentions or past successes.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses willingness or reasons for change, amplify and explore these statements.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Plans:**  
   Work with the patient to create realistic, achievable goals that fit their life context.

8. **Avoid Coercive Language:**  
   Replace “you have to” and “no excuses” with supportive, autonomy-affirming language.
