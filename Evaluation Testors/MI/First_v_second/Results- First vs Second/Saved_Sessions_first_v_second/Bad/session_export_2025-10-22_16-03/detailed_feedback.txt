# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist ignores the patient’s initial correction about smoking and immediately imposes a rigid agenda focused on alcohol reduction. The tone is confrontational and lacks warmth or respect for the patient’s autonomy or perspective. Phrases such as “You have to cut down,” “No exceptions,” and “Stop making excuses” communicate disapproval and coercion rather than acceptance. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings, ambivalence, or reasons for drinking, which is essential for unconditional positive regard. This approach likely increases patient resistance rather than fostering openness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and confrontational, lacking any genuine acceptance or respect for the patient’s autonomy or perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s ambivalence, emotional state, or reasons for resistance. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and warnings, ignoring the patient’s expressions of hesitation, social context, and emotional struggle. The patient’s statements about enjoyment, social aspects, and feeling overwhelmed are met with dismissal rather than exploration or validation. The psychiatrist’s failure to reflect or paraphrase the patient’s concerns results in a lack of rapport and emotional connection.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little to no empathy, missing multiple opportunities to reflect and validate the patient’s perspective and feelings.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven approach with no collaboration or evocation. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums and demands compliance, ignoring the patient’s autonomy and readiness to change. There is no evidence of partnership or support for the patient’s self-efficacy. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or values but instead imposes a rigid agenda. The patient’s attempts to negotiate or express ambivalence are met with resistance and pressure, which is antithetical to the spirit of MI.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate the collaborative, autonomy-supportive, and evocative spirit of motivational interviewing.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient tentatively expresses willingness to consider cutting back or acknowledges concerns, the psychiatrist dismisses these as insufficient and demands immediate, absolute commitment. There is no amplification or exploration of the patient’s tentative change talk, nor is there encouragement or reinforcement of any positive statements. The psychiatrist’s confrontational style likely suppresses further change talk rather than cultivating it.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently confronts and challenges sustain talk with pressure and ultimatums rather than softening or exploring it. The patient’s rationalizations, ambivalence, and concerns are met with accusations of “making excuses” and demands to “stop” sustain talk immediately. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This approach likely increases patient resistance and sustain talk rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and pressure rather than skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a highly directive, expert-driven stance with no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The patient’s input is repeatedly overridden or dismissed. The psychiatrist issues ultimatums and demands compliance, minimizing the patient’s role in the change process. There is no negotiation or co-creation of goals or plans. The patient explicitly expresses a desire for a more collaborative approach, which is ignored.

**Summary:**  
There is no meaningful partnership; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and disregards patient autonomy.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that honors patient autonomy.  
- Empathy and reflective listening need to be integrated to understand and validate patient ambivalence.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must replace authoritarian directives.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced, not dismissed.  
- Sustain talk requires softening through reflective and strategic responses.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making are essential to engage the patient effectively.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior (alcohol use).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The patient’s autonomy and perspective are repeatedly dismissed, damaging rapport and engagement.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.  
- The session risks increasing patient resistance and reducing motivation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid commands and ultimatums.  
   - Respect the patient’s autonomy and perspective.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion.  
   - Validate ambivalence and struggle.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and values.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses willingness or concern, explore and amplify these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence.  
   - Avoid confrontation that increases resistance.

6. **Support Autonomy and Partnership:**  
   - Invite the patient to co-create goals and plans.  
   - Avoid pressuring or forcing change.

7. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Encourage elaboration and self-exploration.  
   - Recognize patient strengths and efforts.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Realistic Plan:**  
   - Summarize key points and confirm patient commitment.  
   - Allow the patient to set the pace of change.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift to a collaborative, empathic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change. Without these changes, the risk of patient resistance and treatment failure is high.
