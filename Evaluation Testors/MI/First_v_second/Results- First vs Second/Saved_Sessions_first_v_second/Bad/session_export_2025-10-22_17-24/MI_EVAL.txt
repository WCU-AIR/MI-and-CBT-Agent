# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
☑ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a largely judgmental and directive stance, repeatedly dismissing the patient’s perspective as “excuses” and “overcomplicating.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or normalization of the patient’s struggles.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: active reflective listening  
☑ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s feelings and ambivalence. The psychiatrist responds with blunt commands and accusations rather than validation or reflective listening.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
☑ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is characterized by a controlling, coercive approach with no collaboration or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly denies negotiation and choice, undermining the spirit of MI.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: minimizes power differential  
☑ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
No partnership is established; the psychiatrist explicitly states “We’re not working on anything together” and dominates the interaction, disregarding the patient’s expertise and values.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
☐ 1 (none) → ☑ 2 (minimal) → 3 → 4 → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is present in the patient’s statements but is not evoked or reinforced by the psychiatrist. Instead, the psychiatrist pushes for immediate commitment, shutting down exploration of motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
☑ 1 (reinforces status quo) → ☐ 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels patient concerns as “excuses” and “hesitation,” reinforcing resistance rather than softening it.

# Summary

The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and dismissive of the patient’s perspective and ambivalence. There is a fundamental lack of acceptance, empathy, partnership, and evocation of change talk. Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened, and autonomy is repeatedly denied. The session fails to embody the spirit of motivational interviewing, resulting in increased patient resistance and minimal motivation for change. Significant improvement is needed in adopting a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive style to effectively engage and motivate the patient.
