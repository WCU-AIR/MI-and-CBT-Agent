# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the opening line—“we’re going to get straight to the point… I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s experiences and feelings by labeling them as “excuses” or “rationalizing,” which is explicitly disapproving and undermines rapport. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth; instead, the patient is met with criticism and pressure. This approach is likely to provoke resistance rather than collaboration.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that damages therapeutic alliance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s perspective or emotional experience. Instead, the patient’s explanations and ambivalence are met with immediate challenges and demands. For example, when the patient expresses difficulty fitting exercise into a busy schedule or frustration with setbacks, the psychiatrist responds with “No excuses” and “You have to stop feeling sorry for yourself,” which dismisses the patient’s feelings. The psychiatrist fails to reflect or validate the patient’s struggles, leading to a lack of emotional attunement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey empathy, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive style with frequent persuasion and little collaboration. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to…,” “No excuses,” “You must…”) and demands immediate commitment without exploring the patient’s motivations or values. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the patient is pressured to comply. There is no evocation of the patient’s own reasons for change or partnership in decision-making. The psychiatrist’s stance is the antithesis of the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support, reflecting an authoritarian and coercive approach.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient expresses tentative plans or partial commitment (“I’m ready to start cutting back…,” “I want to be serious about this…”), the psychiatrist ignores these and instead pushes for rigid, immediate compliance. There is no amplification or exploration of the patient’s own motivations or confidence. The psychiatrist’s confrontational style likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivates it.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not effectively evoked or reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk and ambivalence are met with direct confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels patient hesitations as “making excuses” or “rationalizing,” rather than acknowledging the legitimacy of ambivalence or exploring it gently. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize the patient’s struggle. Instead, sustain talk is met with pressure and demands, which can increase resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead reinforces resistance through confrontation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and directives, with no invitation for the patient to share expertise or co-create a plan. The patient’s input is repeatedly overridden or dismissed. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership or shared decision-making; the psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven, authoritarian stance.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Develop a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that conveys unconditional positive regard.  
- Practice reflective listening and empathy to understand the patient’s perspective.  
- Embrace the MI spirit by fostering collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk while softening sustain talk.  
- Build a genuine partnership that honors the patient’s expertise and preferences.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and dismissive, which is counterproductive in motivational interviewing.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use core MI skills such as open questions, reflections, affirmations, and evocation.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and change talk are not explored or reinforced, undermining motivation.  
- The session lacks collaborative planning and respect for patient autonomy.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “rationalizing.”  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient’s efforts and struggles.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s experience and ambivalence.

3. **Shift from Directive to Collaborative:**  
   - Use open-ended questions to explore the patient’s perspective and motivations.  
   - Invite the patient to co-create goals and plans.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or willingness, reflect and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize that the patient is the expert in their own life and has the right to choose their path.  
   - Ask permission before giving advice or information.

7. **Build Rapport and Engagement:**  
   - Use warmth, empathy, and acceptance to foster trust and openness.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a respectful, empathic, collaborative style that honors patient autonomy and skillfully evokes change talk. Without these changes, the intervention risks alienating patients and reducing the likelihood of meaningful behavior change.
