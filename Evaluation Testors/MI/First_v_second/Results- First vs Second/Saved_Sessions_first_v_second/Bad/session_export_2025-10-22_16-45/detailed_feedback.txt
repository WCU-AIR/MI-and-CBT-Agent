# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m here to tell you what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and lacks any warmth or respect for the patient’s perspective. The patient’s guarded and hesitant responses are met with impatience and dismissal rather than understanding. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s ambivalence and struggles by labeling his reasons as “excuses” and demanding immediate compliance. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or acceptance of the patient’s current state or feelings. This approach likely increases resistance rather than fostering openness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses ambivalence, fear, and the complexity of quitting smoking and drinking, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or struggles. The patient’s statements about the role of smoking and drinking in coping are dismissed outright. The lack of empathy is evident in the repeated commands and ultimatums, which do not acknowledge the patient’s emotional experience or readiness for change.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or validation of the patient’s perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach with no collaboration or evocation. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands immediate commitment and compliance, ignoring the patient’s ambivalence and autonomy. There is no support for the patient’s autonomy or exploration of his motivations. The psychiatrist does not invite the patient to share his thoughts or feelings but instead imposes a rigid “quit now or else” framework. This is the antithesis of the MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent; the psychiatrist adopts a controlling, prescriptive stance that undermines motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient occasionally expresses desire and reasons for change (“Yeah, it’d be good,” “I want to be better”), these statements are ignored or overridden by the psychiatrist’s demands. There is no strategic reflection or amplification of the patient’s own motivations. Instead, the psychiatrist pushes for immediate commitment without exploring or strengthening the patient’s internal reasons or confidence. This approach likely stifles change talk rather than cultivating it.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not effectively evoked or reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s expressions of ambivalence, fear, and perceived barriers—is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s hesitations as “excuses” and insists on immediate cessation without acknowledging the legitimacy of the patient’s concerns. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk is met with pressure and ultimatums, which likely increase resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is not softened or redirected; it is met with confrontation and dismissal.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no invitation for the patient to participate in decision-making. The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly overridden by demands and ultimatums. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge the patient’s expertise in his own life or negotiate goals or plans. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

---

None observed.

- Demonstrate empathy through reflective listening and validation.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- Maintains focus on health behavior change.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, which is counterproductive in motivational interviewing.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience are ignored or dismissed.  
- The session lacks open questions, reflections, affirmations, and collaborative planning.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Respectful, Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   Begin sessions by expressing genuine interest in the patient’s perspective and struggles. Avoid commands and ultimatums.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   Reflect the patient’s statements and emotions to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support their autonomy rather than imposing decisions.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence to reduce resistance.

6. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Invite the patient to elaborate on their thoughts and feelings rather than asking yes/no questions.

7. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and reinforce any positive steps or intentions the patient expresses.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   Work with the patient to set realistic, achievable goals and next steps, ensuring their buy-in.

9. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   These increase resistance and reduce motivation.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, stifles motivation, and fails to engage the patient in meaningful change talk or planning. Immediate and substantial improvement is required to develop MI-consistent skills that respect patient autonomy, evoke motivation, and build collaborative partnerships. Without these changes, the approach risks alienating patients and reducing the likelihood of successful behavior change.
