# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s tone is harsh and critical, repeatedly blaming the patient for avoidance and laziness. There is no warmth or support, only pressure and disapproval, which undermines acceptance.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
No reflective listening or validation of the patient’s fears and ambivalence. The psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s feelings as excuses and overcomplication, showing minimal empathy.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by commands and pressure, ignoring the patient’s autonomy and readiness. There is no collaboration or evocation of the patient’s own motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist imposes an agenda and demands compliance, showing an expert-driven, authoritarian approach without shared decision-making.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Minimal patient change talk is present but ignored or overridden by pressure. No efforts to evoke or reinforce the patient’s tentative motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, labeling the patient’s concerns as excuses rather than exploring or validating ambivalence.
