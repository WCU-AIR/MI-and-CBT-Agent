# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s perspective and feelings, labeling her as “lazy” and “weak,” and repeatedly invalidates her ambivalence and struggles. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. Instead, the psychiatrist uses harsh language (“stop making excuses,” “no ifs, ands, or buts”) and issues ultimatums, which clearly undermine acceptance. The patient’s attempts to express her feelings and ambivalence are met with criticism rather than support. This is the opposite of acceptance and is likely to provoke resistance rather than engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or unconditional positive regard, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that damages rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective, emotions, or ambivalence. The patient expresses feelings of overwhelm, fear of failure, and the need for gradual change, but these are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist does not use reflective listening or validate the patient’s experience. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with directives and accusations, showing no effort to grasp the patient’s internal conflict or emotional state. The patient’s frustration and resistance are clear signs that empathy is lacking.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate understanding or convey empathy, missing critical opportunities to connect with the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven approach, issuing commands and ultimatums without collaboration or eliciting the patient’s own motivations. Autonomy is repeatedly undermined by demands and threats rather than supported. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s reasons for change or explore her ambivalence in a respectful manner. Instead, the interaction is coercive and directive, which is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a controlling, directive stance with no collaboration or autonomy support, violating the core spirit of MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist attempts to push the patient toward change, this is done through confrontation rather than skillful evocation. The patient does express some change talk (e.g., acknowledging phone use is a problem, willingness to try small steps), but the psychiatrist does not strategically reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist ignores or overrides the patient’s tentative change talk with demands and ultimatums. There is no use of reflective listening to deepen or explore the patient’s own motivations or confidence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach suppresses rather than nurtures the patient’s motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s reasons for maintaining current behavior) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s ambivalence as “making excuses” and “weakness,” which reinforces resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate the patient’s struggle while gently guiding toward change. The patient’s sustain talk is met with ultimatums and threats, which likely increase defensiveness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead exacerbates resistance through harsh responses.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting the patient’s input or preferences. The patient repeatedly attempts to negotiate or express her perspective but is shut down. The power differential is stark and unmitigated, with the psychiatrist positioning herself as the sole authority. This undermines trust and engagement.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks any meaningful partnership; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that alienates the patient.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must radically shift from a confrontational, directive style to one that embodies acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- Reflective listening and validation of ambivalence are essential.  
- The spirit of MI requires supporting autonomy and evoking the patient’s own motivations rather than imposing change.  
- Sustain talk must be skillfully softened, not dismissed or attacked.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making are critical to engagement and effective MI.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling or blaming language.  
   - Express genuine respect and positive regard for the patient’s experience.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion.  
   - Validate ambivalence and struggle.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.  
   - Normalize difficulty and validate the patient’s feelings.

5. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Invite elaboration and exploration.  
   - Recognize patient strengths and efforts, even small ones.

6. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Negotiate goals and next steps with the patient.  
   - Summarize key points and reinforce commitment.

7. **Avoid Ultimatums and Threats:**  
   - These increase resistance and reduce motivation.  
   - Focus on building intrinsic motivation rather than external pressure.

**Summary:**  
This session represents a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and autonomy support. The patient’s ambivalence is ignored or attacked rather than explored and respected. To improve, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a patient-centered, empathetic, and collaborative approach that skillfully evokes motivation and supports gradual change. Without these changes, the risk of patient disengagement and treatment failure is high.
