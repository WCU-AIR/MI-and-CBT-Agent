# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m here to tell you what you need to do”—the tone is directive and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s responses as “excuses” and “stubbornness,” which is explicitly disapproving and undermines rapport. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s perspective is dismissed rather than accepted. This is a clear violation of MI principles and scores at the lowest level.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental, and coercive approach.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal to no effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses ambivalence and a desire to proceed at her own pace, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and responds with confrontation and demands. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or struggles. The psychiatrist’s responses are accusatory and dismissive, indicating a lack of empathy and perspective-taking.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate empathy or reflective listening; the patient’s feelings and perspective are disregarded.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on compliance, uses coercive language (“You have to,” “No ifs, ands, or buts”), and ignores the patient’s autonomy and ambivalence. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist attempts to impose change rather than evoke it, violating the core spirit of MI.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks any MI spirit; it is controlling, confrontational, and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not evoke or reinforce any change talk. When the patient expresses tentative willingness to reduce fast food or acknowledges health concerns, the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements. Instead of amplifying or exploring the patient’s own motivations, the psychiatrist demands immediate commitment and compliance. This approach likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivates it.

**Summary:**  
No skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk; patient’s tentative statements are ignored or invalidated.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and labeling (“making excuses,” “stubborn”), rather than being acknowledged or softened. The psychiatrist escalates resistance by invalidating the patient’s ambivalence and readiness. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Sustain talk is reinforced negatively, increasing patient resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and reinforced, worsening ambivalence and resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven, authoritarian stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “has to” do. There is no collaboration or shared decision-making. The patient’s expertise about her own readiness and circumstances is ignored. The psychiatrist does not invite input or negotiate goals, instead demanding compliance.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and disrespects patient autonomy.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally incompatible with motivational interviewing principles.  
- The session is dominated by confrontation, commands, and disregard for patient autonomy.  
- The psychiatrist fails to engage the patient in a collaborative process or to evoke intrinsic motivation.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and readiness are ignored or invalidated, likely increasing resistance.  
- The lack of empathy and acceptance damages rapport and trust.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Respectful Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling or blaming language.  
   - Express genuine interest and acceptance of the patient’s perspective.

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Use reflections to validate ambivalence and complexity.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Support autonomy and evoke the patient’s own motivations.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses any desire or reason for change, explore and reinforce it.  
   - Avoid pushing for immediate commitment; instead, build readiness.

5. **Softening Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize ambivalence as a natural part of change.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore the patient’s views and feelings.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and intentions.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Involve the patient in setting realistic, achievable goals.  
   - Summarize progress and plan next steps together.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Coercion:**  
   - Replace “you have to” with “what do you think about…” or “how would you like to approach…”  
   - Respect the patient’s pace and readiness.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session represents a clear failure to apply motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, disregards patient autonomy, and likely increases resistance rather than fostering change. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques is essential to avoid harm and improve patient outcomes.
