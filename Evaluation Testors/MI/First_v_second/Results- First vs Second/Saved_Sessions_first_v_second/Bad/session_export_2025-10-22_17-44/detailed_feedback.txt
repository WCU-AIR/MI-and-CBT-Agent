# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first utterance—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and dismissive of the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s feelings and fears, labeling her concerns as “excuses” and “overcomplicating,” which is antithetical to unconditional positive regard. There is no evidence of warmth, support, or respect for the patient’s ambivalence or autonomy. The patient’s attempts to express vulnerability and hesitation are met with pressure and criticism rather than acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or positive regard, instead adopting a harsh, directive approach that likely alienates the patient.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase the patient’s feelings or fears. Instead, the psychiatrist interrupts and challenges the patient’s expressions of anxiety and ambivalence without validation. For example, when the patient shares fear of medication or therapy, the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns rather than exploring or acknowledging them. The lack of empathic listening is glaring and undermines rapport.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s experience is heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely missing. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “must” do and demanding immediate compliance. There is no collaboration or invitation for the patient to explore her own motivations or values. Autonomy is denied outright, with statements like “This is not optional” and “You don’t get to pick and choose.” The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own reasons for change but instead imposes a rigid agenda.

**Summary:**  
The session is antithetical to MI spirit, characterized by control, persuasion, and disregard for patient autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist attempts to push the patient toward change by demanding action (calling a doctor, starting medication or therapy). However, this is done through confrontation rather than evocation. The patient does express some change talk (e.g., “I want to get better,” “I scheduled the exam”), but the psychiatrist does not skillfully elicit or reinforce these statements. Instead, the patient’s change talk is often ignored or overridden by pressure. There is no strategic amplification or exploration of the patient’s own motivations.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach risks suppressing rather than encouraging patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s fears, ambivalence, and resistance—is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and “overcomplicating,” and repeatedly insists on immediate action without acknowledging the legitimacy of the patient’s fears. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This approach likely intensifies sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened, increasing patient defensiveness.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no invitation for the patient to participate in decision-making. The patient’s expertise in her own experience is ignored, and power is wielded in a coercive manner. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands compliance and dismisses patient input, undermining any sense of equality or shared responsibility.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the session is authoritarian and directive.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Minimal; the psychiatrist attempts to direct the patient toward change.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Complete lack of acceptance and empathy.  
- Absence of MI spirit: no collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support.  
- Failure to cultivate or reinforce change talk.  
- Sustain talk is confronted aggressively rather than softened.  
- No partnership or shared decision-making.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the patient’s anxiety and the need for treatment.  
- The patient demonstrates some change talk and willingness to engage.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, confrontational, and dismissive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions.  
- Change talk is not cultivated or reinforced; sustain talk is harshly confronted.  
- The patient’s autonomy and readiness are ignored, increasing resistance and disengagement.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Communicate unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient’s feelings and ambivalence. Avoid labeling or dismissing concerns.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy Through Reflective Listening:**  
   - Use reflections to validate and explore the patient’s fears and motivations. Reflect both content and emotion.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Foster collaboration, evoke the patient’s own reasons for change, and support autonomy. Avoid coercion or directives.

4. **Use Open-Ended Questions to Explore Ambivalence:**  
   - Invite the patient to elaborate on her experiences, fears, and hopes to deepen motivation.

5. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize and reinforce the patient’s courage, past successes, and steps taken toward change.

6. **Softly Address Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and gently guide toward change.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify manageable next steps that respect her readiness and preferences.

8. **Summarize to Reinforce Motivation:**  
   - Periodically summarize key points, change talk, and agreed-upon plans to consolidate progress.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To provide effective MI, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation of the patient’s own motivations and autonomy. Without these changes, the session risks alienating the patient and impeding progress.
