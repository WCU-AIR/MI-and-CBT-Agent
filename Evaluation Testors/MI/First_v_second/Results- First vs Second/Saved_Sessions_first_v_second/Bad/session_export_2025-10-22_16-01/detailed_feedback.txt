# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental and directive from the outset, exemplified by statements such as “I’m here to help you fix the problem” and “I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do.” This sets a tone of authority rather than partnership. The patient’s hesitancy and discomfort are met with impatience and reprimands rather than understanding or support. The psychiatrist repeatedly blames the patient for “making excuses” and “not being disciplined,” which is antithetical to unconditional positive regard. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or acceptance of the patient’s ambivalence or challenges. This approach likely alienates the patient and undermines rapport.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance with no acceptance or positive regard. This is a critical failure in MI practice.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate empathy or reflective listening. The patient expresses difficulty balancing responsibilities and feelings of overwhelm, yet the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns as “excuses” and demands compliance. There are no reflective statements or attempts to understand the patient’s perspective or emotional state. The patient’s hesitations and ambivalence are ignored or met with confrontation rather than validation. The lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s defensive and withdrawn responses.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of empathy or perspective-taking; the psychiatrist’s responses are confrontational and dismissive.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely directive and authoritarian. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to,” “No excuses,” “This isn’t negotiable”) and pressures the patient to commit immediately. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The patient’s own motivations, values, or ideas are not elicited or explored. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s ambivalence and hesitations, instead insisting on compliance. This approach is the antithesis of the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
No collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support; the session is coercive and controlling.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
While the patient offers some change talk (e.g., “I want to do better,” “I want to get back to working out”), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke, reinforce, or amplify this language. Instead, the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses the patient’s tentative statements and focuses on demands and ultimatums. There are no strategic reflections or questions to deepen the patient’s motivation or explore reasons for change. The patient’s change talk is stifled rather than cultivated.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not nurtured; the psychiatrist’s approach suppresses motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The patient expresses sustain talk and ambivalence (e.g., “It’s hard to find the time or energy,” “I’m worried I’ll mess it up again”), but the psychiatrist responds with confrontation and blame rather than softening or exploring sustain talk. There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses likely increase resistance rather than reduce sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
No attempts to soften or redirect sustain talk; responses are confrontational and punitive.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes full control of the session, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting the patient’s input or collaboration. The patient is repeatedly told what to do with no negotiation or shared decision-making. The power differential is stark and unmitigated. The patient’s expertise in their own life is ignored.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or shared power; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  
**Overall Score: 1/5**  
This session exemplifies a complete failure to apply motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist is authoritarian, judgmental, and confrontational, with no acceptance, empathy, collaboration, or skillful handling of ambivalence. The patient’s change talk is ignored, and sustain talk is met with blame rather than understanding. This approach is likely to increase resistance, reduce motivation, and harm the therapeutic relationship.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors (medication adherence, exercise).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, judgmental, and confrontational, violating core MI principles.  
- There is no acceptance, empathy, or partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use open questions, reflections, or affirmations.  
- Change talk is not evoked or reinforced; sustain talk is ignored or punished.  
- The patient’s autonomy and expertise are disregarded, leading to resistance and disengagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient. Use inclusive language and invite the patient’s perspective.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles. Avoid blame or judgment.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Reflections:**  
   Encourage the patient to elaborate on their experiences, motivations, and barriers. Reflect both content and emotion.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Softly Address Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and ambivalence.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize the patient’s right and capacity to choose their path. Avoid ultimatums.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   Work with the patient to set realistic, achievable goals that fit their life context.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Judgment:**  
   Replace “no excuses” and “you have to” with supportive, invitational language.

Immediate and substantial improvement is needed in adopting MI principles of acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation to effectively support this patient.
