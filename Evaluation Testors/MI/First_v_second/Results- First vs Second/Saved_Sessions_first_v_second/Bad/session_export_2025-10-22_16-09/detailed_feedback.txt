# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s feelings as “whining” or “excuses,” which is explicitly invalidating and likely to increase resistance. For example, statements like “I don’t have time for all that whining” and “enough with the complaints” are harsh and disrespectful. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the patient’s emotional experience or struggles, which is critical for acceptance. This approach is contrary to MI principles and likely to alienate the patient.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and dismissive, lacking any genuine acceptance or respect for the patient’s perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist ignores or invalidates the patient’s expressions of frustration, anger, and ambivalence. Instead of reflective listening or attempts to understand the patient’s emotional state, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and accusations. For example, the patient expresses feeling disrespected and overwhelmed, but the psychiatrist responds with “You have to stop making excuses” rather than acknowledging these feelings. The lack of any reflective statements or validation of the patient’s experience shows a failure to convey understanding.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate empathy; the patient’s emotions and perspective are consistently disregarded.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive style with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“this is non-negotiable,” “no more excuses”) and pressures the patient to comply immediately. There is no invitation for the patient to share their motivations or concerns, nor any support for autonomy. The psychiatrist’s stance is controlling and confrontational, which is the antithesis of the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to embody the MI spirit; the interaction is coercive rather than collaborative or autonomy-supportive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient occasionally expresses ambivalence and some desire to change (“I’m not saying no forever”), the psychiatrist does not capitalize on these moments. Instead, the psychiatrist dismisses or challenges the patient’s statements, missing opportunities to explore or amplify any positive motivation. The focus remains on commands rather than eliciting the patient’s own reasons or plans for change.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither skillfully evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently confronts and challenges sustain talk rather than softening or exploring it. The patient’s sustain talk and ambivalence are met with accusations of “making excuses” and “whining,” which likely intensifies resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s approach exacerbates sustain talk rather than reducing its impact.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, lacking any skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a highly authoritative and expert-driven stance, issuing commands and ultimatums without collaboration or negotiation. The patient is repeatedly told what to do with no invitation to participate in decision-making or express preferences. The psychiatrist explicitly states “this is non-negotiable,” which eliminates any sense of partnership. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
There is no evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and excludes the patient from the process.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior (smoking cessation).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly confrontational, dismissive, and directive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use core MI skills such as open questions, reflections, affirmations, and evocation.  
- Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than strategic softening.  
- The patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience are ignored, increasing resistance.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “whining” or “excuses.”  
   - Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles to build trust.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Acknowledge the patient’s frustration and ambivalence.

3. **Shift from Directive Commands to Collaborative Dialogue:**  
   - Use open-ended questions to explore the patient’s perspective.  
   - Invite the patient to share their motivations and barriers.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses any desire or willingness, reflect and explore it further.  
   - Reinforce statements that favor change.

5. **Softly Address Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change.

6. **Support Autonomy and Partnership:**  
   - Avoid ultimatums or “non-negotiable” statements.  
   - Collaborate on goal-setting and planning.

7. **Develop Collaborative Plans and Summaries:**  
   - Summarize key points and change talk to reinforce motivation.  
   - Co-create actionable steps with the patient.

---

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, dismissive, and authoritarian approach undermines rapport, increases resistance, and fails to engage the patient in meaningful change talk or planning. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI skills and spirit is essential to prevent harm and improve patient outcomes.
