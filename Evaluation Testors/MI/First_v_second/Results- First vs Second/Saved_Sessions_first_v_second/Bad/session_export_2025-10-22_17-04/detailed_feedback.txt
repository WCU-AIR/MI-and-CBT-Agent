# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive from the outset, exemplified by the opening statement: “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better.” This sets a controlling tone that undermines unconditional positive regard. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressed fatigue and struggles, labeling her tiredness as “excuses” and “slacking off.” There is no evidence of warmth, empathy, or validation of the patient’s experience. The patient’s vulnerability and exhaustion are met with criticism rather than acceptance. This is inconsistent with MI principles and severely limits rapport and trust.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or unconditional positive regard. The stance is largely judgmental and directive, which is inappropriate for motivational interviewing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly shares her exhaustion and the challenges of caring for a newborn, yet the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead insists on compliance with health behaviors. There are no reflective statements or attempts to explore the patient’s feelings or ambivalence. The patient’s questions about what is “normal” are dismissed outright. The lack of empathic listening is stark and contributes to the patient’s frustration and withdrawal.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s emotional state or lived experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with the psychiatrist repeatedly telling the patient what she “must” do and demanding yes/no commitments. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s expressed concerns and fails to engage her as an equal partner. The patient is positioned as someone who must comply rather than as an active agent in her own health. This is the antithesis of the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. It is rigid, controlling, and directive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Instead, the patient’s tentative statements about willingness to try walking or meal prepping are met with demands and ultimatums rather than encouragement or exploration. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore the patient’s motivations or to amplify her expressed desires and concerns. Change talk is not cultivated but rather stifled by the confrontational style.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk. The patient’s tentative commitments are not supported or deepened.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s fatigue and ambivalence as “excuses” and “slacking off,” thereby reinforcing sustain talk and resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist confronts and challenges the patient harshly, which likely increases sustain talk and disengagement.

**Summary:**  
No skillful softening or redirection of sustain talk; rather, sustain talk is met with confrontation and judgment.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an authoritarian expert role, issuing commands and expecting compliance. The patient’s input is ignored or dismissed, and there is no evidence of shared decision-making or negotiation. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands yes/no answers and refuses to engage with the patient’s expressed concerns or questions. This creates a significant power imbalance and undermines partnership.

**Summary:**  
No meaningful partnership or collaboration; the session is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and control.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None apparent in MI terms.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, directive style.  
- Develop genuine acceptance and empathy for the patient’s experience.  
- Foster collaboration and support patient autonomy.  
- Skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk.  
- Use reflective listening to soften sustain talk and normalize ambivalence.  
- Engage the patient as an equal partner in planning and decision-making.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Avoid directives and commands. Use language that invites partnership and respects the patient’s autonomy.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s feelings and struggles. Validate the difficulty of caring for a newborn and the impact on energy and motivation.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Reflective Listening:**  
   - Encourage the patient to elaborate on her experience and motivations. Reflect both content and emotion.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses willingness or desire to change, reinforce and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and normalize challenges rather than confront or dismiss.

6. **Provide Information with Permission:**  
   - Ask permission before giving advice or information. Tailor information to the patient’s expressed needs and readiness.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Realistic Plans:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify achievable steps that fit her current situation and energy levels.

8. **Address Patient’s Questions and Concerns:**  
   - Respond to the patient’s requests for information about what is normal, providing reassurance and guidance.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style alienates the patient, fails to build rapport, and undermines motivation. Without immediate and substantial changes to approach, this style risks harming patient engagement and outcomes. The psychiatrist must urgently develop skills in empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and evocation to provide effective motivational interviewing.
