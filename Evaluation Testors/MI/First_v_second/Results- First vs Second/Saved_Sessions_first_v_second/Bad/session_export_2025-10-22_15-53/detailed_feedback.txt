# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m here to get straight to the point today” and “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or respect for the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressed concerns and ambivalence, labeling her reasons as “excuses” and questioning her seriousness about health. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or validation of the patient’s experience. The patient’s attempts to express her feelings and realistic concerns are met with rebuke and pressure rather than acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a confrontational and coercive approach that undermines rapport and trust.

### 2. Empathy

The patient clearly articulates her struggles with stress, fatigue, and past failures with strict diets and exercise regimens, yet the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead insists on rigid compliance. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase or validate the patient’s feelings. The psychiatrist’s responses are dismissive and directive, showing little to no empathy for the patient’s lived experience or emotional state.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s perspective is heard or valued.

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven style with no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands compliance, refuses to negotiate or explore the patient’s motivations, and invalidates the patient’s expressed ambivalence. The patient’s autonomy is ignored; the psychiatrist does not invite the patient to share her ideas or preferences but instead insists on a rigid plan with “no exceptions.” This approach is antithetical to the spirit of MI, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent; the psychiatrist adopts a coercive, directive stance that undermines motivation and engagement.

There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s motivation. The psychiatrist repeatedly challenges the patient’s ambivalence rather than exploring it, which likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivating it.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the psychiatrist’s approach likely diminishes motivation.

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk

The psychiatrist labels the patient’s explanations as “excuses” and “hesitation,” and repeatedly insists on “no excuses” and “no exceptions.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses escalate resistance rather than softening sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than skillfully softened or redirected.

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands and demands, with no invitation for the patient to participate in decision-making or express her preferences. The psychiatrist repeatedly refuses to negotiate or explore alternative approaches, instead insisting on strict adherence to a predetermined plan. This creates a clear power imbalance and undermines the patient’s sense of agency.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

### Overall Global Ratings Summary  
**Overall Score: 1/5**  

The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is marked by judgment, lack of empathy, coercion, and disregard for the patient’s autonomy and perspective. This style is likely to increase patient resistance, reduce motivation, and damage the therapeutic relationship.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient. Use inclusive language and invite the patient’s input.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   Validate the patient’s feelings and experiences. Reflect back what the patient says to show understanding.

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   Encourage the patient to explore her motivations, barriers, and ideas by asking open-ended questions and reflecting her statements.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements to build motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize that the patient has control over her choices and pace of change.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   Work with the patient to develop realistic, achievable steps rather than demanding immediate full compliance.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Judgment:**  
   Replace “no excuses” and “no exceptions” language with supportive, nonjudgmental dialogue.
