# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a judgmental, directive, and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is prescriptive and lacks any warmth or positive regard. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressions of ambivalence and attempts to explain his circumstances, labeling them as “excuses” and “hesitation.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or respect for the patient’s autonomy or lived experience. The psychiatrist’s repeated insistence on “no excuses” and “no middle ground” further undermines acceptance. This approach is likely to alienate the patient rather than engage him.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a confrontational and judgmental approach that is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s perspective or feelings. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts or overrides the patient’s attempts to explain his challenges and preferences. For example, when the patient expresses concerns about feasibility and pacing, the psychiatrist responds with “No, it doesn’t really make sense” and “No, you’re not ready to commit if you’re making excuses.” These statements dismiss the patient’s experience and shut down dialogue. The psychiatrist fails to use reflections or paraphrases that convey understanding or validate the patient’s ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows a lack of empathy, failing to acknowledge or explore the patient’s perspective, which damages rapport and engagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing ultimatums and commands (“You have to…,” “No exceptions,” “This is not optional”). There is no collaboration or partnership; the psychiatrist refuses to negotiate or explore the patient’s readiness or preferences. Autonomy is not supported but rather undermined by coercive language and pressure tactics. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or values but imposes a rigid agenda.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a directive, controlling approach that contradicts the collaborative, autonomy-supportive spirit of MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the patient does express some change talk (e.g., pride in quitting smoking, willingness to improve health), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce this language. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance with a strict plan rather than exploring the patient’s own reasons or desires for change. There are no strategic reflections or amplifications of change talk. The psychiatrist’s repeated demands may provoke resistance rather than genuine change talk. The patient’s change talk is largely self-generated and not nurtured by the counselor.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively cultivated or deepened by the psychiatrist.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully respond to sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk and ambivalence are met with confrontation and dismissal (“stop making excuses,” “no middle ground,” “no negotiations”). The psychiatrist reinforces sustain talk by framing it as resistance or failure rather than normalizing ambivalence or exploring underlying concerns. This approach likely increases sustain talk and resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, lacking any softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly imposes a rigid plan and demands compliance. The patient’s input is ignored or invalidated. The psychiatrist refuses to negotiate or co-create a plan, instead insisting on “my way or the highway.” This creates a clear power imbalance and undermines the therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to establish a collaborative partnership, instead adopting an authoritarian stance.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership and shared decision-making.  
   - Invite the patient’s input and preferences in setting goals.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “hesitation.”

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore the patient’s motivations and barriers.  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to deepen understanding.

4. **Cultivate Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, amplify and reinforce these statements.  
   - Explore confidence and commitment language gently.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate the patient’s experience.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control the pace of change.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercive language.

7. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, manageable steps.  
   - Summarize key points and reinforce motivation regularly.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Pressure:**  
   - Replace “just do it” commands with supportive, evocative dialogue.  
   - Recognize that pressure often increases resistance and disengagement.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation. The patient’s ambivalence and change talk are dismissed rather than nurtured, leading to resistance and disengagement. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a respectful, empathic, and collaborative approach that supports the patient’s autonomy and elicits intrinsic motivation for change.
