# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“Today we’re going to get straight to the point... I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is directive and dismissive of the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s expressions of struggle and ambivalence, labeling them as “excuses” and “not good enough.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s attempts to explain barriers and express realistic concerns are met with impatience and reprimand rather than empathy or support. This is a clear violation of MI principles of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or support, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that undermines rapport and trust.

### 2. Empathy

There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist’s responses are blunt commands and challenges, which likely increase patient defensiveness. The lack of reflective listening or perspective-taking is a critical failure in empathy.

**Summary:**  
No genuine empathy is demonstrated; the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s feelings and struggles, missing opportunities to build understanding.

The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist attempts to coerce commitment rather than evoke it. This approach is antithetical to the MI spirit.

**Summary:**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified. The psychiatrist’s approach is inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, confrontational style.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening, empathy, and acceptance.  
- Use open-ended questions to explore patient perspectives and ambivalence.  
- Affirm patient strengths and efforts to build motivation.  
- Evoke and reinforce change talk rather than demanding compliance.  
- Soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and normalization.  
- Collaborate with the patient to co-create realistic, manageable goals.  
- Respect patient autonomy and readiness for change.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient. Invite the patient’s input and respect their expertise about their own life.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   Practice reflecting both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and validate the patient’s experience.

3. **Ask Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Replace yes/no questions with open questions that encourage elaboration and exploration of motivation and barriers.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths:**  
   Recognize and reinforce the patient’s efforts and progress, no matter how small.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and amplify these statements to build motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and explore ambivalence.

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control the pace and nature of change.

8. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   Work with the patient to develop realistic, achievable goals and plans, rather than demanding immediate, absolute commitments.

9. **Avoid Confrontation and Judgment:**  
   Replace confrontational language with supportive, nonjudgmental communication to build trust and engagement.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To provide effective MI, the psychiatrist must radically change approach to one grounded in empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and skillful evocation of change talk. Without these changes, the patient’s health outcomes and engagement will be compromised.
