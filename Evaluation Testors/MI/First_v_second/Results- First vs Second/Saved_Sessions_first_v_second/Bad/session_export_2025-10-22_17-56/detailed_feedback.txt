# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first utterance, the psychiatrist states, “I’ll be telling you exactly what steps you must take,” which immediately sets a controlling tone and undermines client autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s feelings and hesitations with “No, you don’t get to…” statements, invalidating the patient’s experience and reinforcing shame and resistance. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or unconditional positive regard. The patient’s ambivalence and vulnerability are met with impatience and reprimand rather than empathy or acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is harsh, directive, and lacking in acceptance, which is likely to alienate the patient and reduce engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate understanding or reflective listening. The patient expresses hesitation, ambivalence, and difficulty with change, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflections or attempts to understand the patient’s perspective. For example, when the patient says, “I don’t know if it’s that simple,” or “It feels harder than just saying no,” the psychiatrist responds with commands and accusations rather than empathy or validation. This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s discomfort and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is virtually absent; the psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings or perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely missing. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding immediate compliance. There is no collaboration or invitation for the patient to explore their own motivations or ambivalence. Autonomy is denied repeatedly with phrases like “You don’t get to say…” and “This isn’t optional.” This approach is the antithesis of MI spirit and is likely to provoke resistance rather than motivation.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by control and directive commands, with no evidence of MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist does elicit some change talk by asking direct questions about drinking behavior and commitment, this is done in a coercive manner rather than through evocation. The patient offers tentative change talk (“I think I’ll try no drinks after work today”), but the psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the focus is on demanding compliance rather than exploring the patient’s own reasons or confidence. The change talk is superficial and not deepened or strategically supported.

**Summary:**  
Some change talk is elicited, but it is forced and not skillfully cultivated or reinforced.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The patient’s expressions of difficulty, ambivalence, and fear are labeled as “making excuses” or “avoiding responsibility.” The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or validate the patient’s struggle. This approach likely increases sustain talk and resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no sense of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist repeatedly asserts authority and demands compliance, ignoring the patient’s input or concerns. The patient is positioned as someone who must obey orders rather than as an equal partner in change. The psychiatrist does not invite the patient to share their perspective or co-create a plan.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, lacking any genuine partnership.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist consistently maintains focus on the target behavior (drinking after work).  
- Attempts to elicit commitment, albeit coercively.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, confrontational style.  
- Develop genuine empathy and acceptance for the patient’s ambivalence and struggle.  
- Embrace the MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Use reflective listening and affirmations to build rapport and trust.  
- Skillfully cultivate and reinforce change talk.  
- Soften sustain talk rather than confront it.  
- Build a true partnership with the patient.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is counterproductive.  
- Lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening undermines rapport and motivation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Replace commands with invitations and open-ended questions.  
   - Respect the patient’s autonomy and pace of change.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Avoid judgmental or confrontational language.

3. **Cultivate Change Talk Skillfully:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or ability to change, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Explore the patient’s own reasons and motivations.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change.

5. **Use Affirmations and Summaries:**  
   - Affirm the patient’s strengths, efforts, and willingness to consider change.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

6. **Avoid Yes/No Questions Demanding Immediate Commitment:**  
   - Instead, explore ambivalence and readiness with open questions.  
   - Allow the patient to express concerns and develop their own plan.

7. **Build Rapport and Trust:**  
   - Engage the patient with warmth and genuine interest.  
   - Recognize the patient’s expertise in their own life.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach is likely to increase resistance and reduce motivation. To be effective, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive style that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change. Without these changes, the patient is unlikely to engage meaningfully or sustain behavior change.
