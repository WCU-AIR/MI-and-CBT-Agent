# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and directive, lacking warmth or genuine acceptance. The opening statement, “We need to talk about what you’re doing that’s harming you and how you’re going to fix it,” immediately sets a confrontational tone. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly uses language that implies blame and failure (“You have to stop making excuses,” “You’re just not prioritizing it enough”), which is likely to evoke defensiveness rather than openness. There is minimal evidence of unconditional positive regard or validation of the patient’s struggles. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge the patient’s feelings or efforts in a supportive way, which is critical for acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s stance is largely authoritarian and critical, undermining acceptance. This approach risks alienating the patient and reducing engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows limited effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. While the patient expresses vulnerability and ambivalence (“It’s hard,” “I want to do better, but it’s not easy”), the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns and pushes for compliance without exploring underlying emotions or barriers. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s experience. The patient’s expressions of fear, overwhelm, and social challenges are met with commands rather than empathy. This lack of empathetic listening likely contributes to the patient’s defensiveness and guarded responses.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is minimal; the psychiatrist fails to convey understanding or emotional attunement, which is essential for building trust and facilitating change.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is highly directive and controlling, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“You must check your blood sugar every day, no exceptions,” “No, we can’t work with that”) and demands yes/no commitments without exploring the patient’s motivations or values. The patient’s autonomy is disregarded, and the psychiatrist does not invite the patient to share her thoughts or preferences. There is no partnership or shared decision-making; instead, the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that contradicts the spirit of MI.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks the fundamental MI elements of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support, severely limiting its effectiveness.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist attempts to elicit commitment by asking direct yes/no questions about daily blood sugar checks and medication adherence. However, these efforts are coercive rather than evocative. The patient offers tentative change talk (“I want to try,” “I’ll commit to doing it every day”), but the psychiatrist does not reinforce, amplify, or explore these statements to deepen motivation. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance and consequences, missing opportunities to explore the patient’s own reasons for change or to build confidence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is minimally evoked and not skillfully cultivated; the approach is more about demanding compliance than fostering intrinsic motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s expressions of difficulty, forgetfulness, social challenges) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels these as “excuses” and insists on strict adherence without acknowledging the patient’s ambivalence or barriers. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize the patient’s struggles. This approach likely reinforces sustain talk and resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk, instead escalating resistance through confrontational responses.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative relationship. The interaction is dominated by commands and ultimatums, with no invitation for the patient to participate in decision-making or express preferences. The patient’s attempts to negotiate or express concerns are overridden. The psychiatrist’s language (“I’m here to tell you what you need to do,” “Forget about feeling supported”) reinforces a power imbalance and expert-driven stance.

**Summary:**  
There is no meaningful partnership; the psychiatrist assumes full control, undermining client-centered care.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist consistently addresses the importance of diabetes management and attempts to secure commitment.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The approach is authoritarian, lacking acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke and reinforce change talk effectively.  
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than softened.  
- The patient’s autonomy and perspective are disregarded, risking disengagement.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1.5/5** (rounded down to 1 for strictness)

### Areas for Improvement

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from commanding to partnering with the patient. Use inclusive language and invite the patient’s input.

2. **Increase Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s feelings and struggles. Validate ambivalence and normalize challenges.

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   - Encourage elaboration and deeper exploration of motivations and barriers.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize any positive behaviors or intentions to build confidence.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or commitment, reflect and explore these statements to strengthen motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and avoid labeling patient concerns as “excuses.”

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control her behavior, reducing resistance.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify realistic, achievable steps and problem-solve barriers.

9. **Summarize Key Points:**  
   - Use summaries to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

The failure to evoke and reinforce change talk, soften sustain talk, and support autonomy severely limits the potential for patient engagement and behavior change. Immediate and substantial improvement in MI skills is necessary to avoid harm and enhance clinical effectiveness.
