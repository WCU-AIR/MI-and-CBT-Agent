# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**

This approach likely alienates the patient and undermines rapport. There are zero instances of supportive or nonjudgmental language.

**Summary:**

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely lacking. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “must” do and demanding immediate commitment. There is no collaboration or partnership; the patient’s autonomy is ignored and undermined by ultimatums and coercion. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change but instead imposes a rigid agenda.

**Summary:**  
The session is antithetical to the MI spirit, characterized by control, persuasion, and disregard for patient autonomy.

The psychiatrist labels patient statements as “excuses” and insists on immediate change, which likely increases resistance. **Summary:**

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   Practice unconditional positive regard and avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “not thinking straight.”

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   Use reflections to demonstrate understanding and validate patient feelings and ambivalence.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   Invite the patient to share her perspective and co-create goals and plans.

4. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Encourage elaboration and exploration rather than yes/no answers or immediate commitments.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses any desire or willingness, reflect and explore these statements to build motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and gently guide toward change.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   Replace commands with invitations and support to reduce resistance.

8. **Build Rapport and Engagement:**  
   Attend to the patient’s emotional state and barriers to change with empathy and patience.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and skillful evocation of change talk. Without these changes, the patient’s health outcomes and engagement will be compromised.
