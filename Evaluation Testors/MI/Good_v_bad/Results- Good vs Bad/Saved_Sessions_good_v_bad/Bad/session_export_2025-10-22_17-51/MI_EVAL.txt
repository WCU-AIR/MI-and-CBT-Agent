# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
□ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
☑ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a predominantly judgmental and directive stance, repeatedly labeling the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and demanding compliance. There is little evidence of warmth or unconditional positive regard, which undermines rapport and patient engagement.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
□ High: active reflective listening  
☑ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal reflective listening is present. The psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s expressed ambivalence and life challenges rather than validating or exploring them, missing opportunities to build empathy and trust.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
□ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
☑ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven style with frequent ultimatums and commands. There is no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy, which is contrary to MI principles.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
□ High: minimizes power differential  
☑ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist dominates the interaction, repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding yes/no answers. The patient’s input is largely ignored or dismissed, indicating minimal partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
□ 1 (none) → ☑ 2 (limited, inconsistent) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Some patient change talk is present (willingness to walk, acknowledging health risks), but the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce it. Instead, change talk is often met with skepticism or demands for immediate total change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
□ 1 (reinforces status quo) → ☑ 1 (harsh confrontation) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels patient ambivalence as “excuses” and issues ultimatums rather than using reflective or gentle strategies to soften resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
□ Uses open questions  
□ Reflects more than asks  
□ Avoids roadblocks  
☑ Score: 2/5  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist asks mostly closed or leading questions and rarely reflects. Despite the confrontational style, the patient remains engaged, likely due to personal motivation rather than counselor skill.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
□ Confirms agreed goal  
□ Redirects drifting conversation  
☑ Score: 3/5  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on alcohol cessation and exercise but does so rigidly and without collaboration.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
□ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
□ Affirms autonomy  
☑ Score: 1/5  
**Comments:**  
Little to no effort is made to evoke the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change. The psychiatrist relies on commands rather than eliciting patient-driven change talk.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
□ Strengthens commitment language  
□ Elicits concrete steps  
☑ Score: 2/5  
**Comments:**  
A plan is imposed rather than collaboratively developed. The patient is told what to do without negotiation or consolidation of commitment.
