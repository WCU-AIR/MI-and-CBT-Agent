# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Luis Ramirez Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a predominantly judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the outset, the tone is authoritarian and confrontational (“I’m going to tell you exactly what you have to do,” “No more excuses, no more delays,” “I’m not asking, I’m telling you”). There is little to no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses Luis’s expressions of overwhelm and frustration, labeling them as “excuses” and “wasting time.” This approach likely exacerbates Luis’s defensiveness and resistance. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or validate Luis’s feelings or perspective, which is critical given Luis’s expressed frustration and ambivalence.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive, lacking the respect and support necessary to foster a collaborative therapeutic relationship. This undermines acceptance and likely contributes to patient resistance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not reflect or attempt to understand Luis’s perspective beyond superficial acknowledgment. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates Luis’s feelings of overwhelm and frustration by labeling them as excuses or resistance. For example, when Luis says, “I don’t think you get my situation,” the psychiatrist responds with commands rather than exploring or reflecting this statement. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to reflect Luis’s emotional state or to validate the complexity of his lived experience. The lack of empathic listening likely fuels Luis’s sarcasm and defensiveness.  

**Summary:**  
The session is characterized by a lack of empathic engagement, with the psychiatrist failing to accurately understand or convey Luis’s perspective, which is critical in motivational interviewing.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is almost entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing ultimatums and commands (“You have to,” “No exceptions,” “This is not optional”). There is no evidence of evoking Luis’s own motivations or reasons for change, nor is there support for his autonomy. Instead, the psychiatrist pressures Luis to commit immediately, disregarding his ambivalence and resistance. The interaction is confrontational rather than collaborative, which is counterproductive in MI.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and controlling, lacking the fundamental MI spirit of partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce change talk. When Luis offers tentative or minimal change talk (e.g., “I’m trying here,” “Maybe I’ll try to say something eventually”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements, instead focusing on commands and ultimatums. There is no strategic reflection or amplification of Luis’s own reasons or efforts toward change. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Luis’s ambivalence or to elicit commitment language in a supportive manner.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is largely unacknowledged and unreinforced, limiting the potential for motivating Luis toward change.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than skillful softening or redirection. Luis’s expressions of overwhelm, frustration, and environmental challenges are labeled as “excuses” or “wasting time.” The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or validate the legitimacy of sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is met with pressure and ultimatums, which likely increases resistance.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk, instead reinforcing resistance through confrontational responses.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly asserts authority and control, issuing commands without inviting Luis’s input or perspective. The language used (“I’m telling you,” “This is not optional,” “You have to commit”) reflects a hierarchical, expert-driven approach. Luis’s attempts to express his viewpoint are dismissed or ignored. The psychiatrist does not negotiate goals or collaborate on a plan, which is essential for effective MI.  

**Summary:**  
The session lacks partnership, with the psychiatrist dominating the interaction and minimizing Luis’s role in the process.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on asthma management and the importance of adherence.  

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian and confrontational, lacking acceptance, empathy, and MI spirit.  
- There is no effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk.  
- Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than softening.  
- Partnership and autonomy support are absent.  
- The session likely increases patient resistance rather than fostering motivation.  

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership and shared decision-making.  
   - Use inclusive language and invite Luis’s input.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect and validate Luis’s feelings of overwhelm and frustration.  
   - Avoid labeling expressions of ambivalence as “excuses.”

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore Luis’s perspective and ambivalence.  
   - Use reflective listening to demonstrate understanding and deepen the conversation.

4. **Affirm Efforts and Strengths:**  
   - Recognize and affirm any attempts Luis makes to manage his asthma.  
   - Build on these strengths to foster motivation.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - When Luis expresses any desire or reasons for change, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Explore his motivations and confidence in making changes.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize ambivalence and the difficulty of change.

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize Luis’s control over decisions and pace of change.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercive language.

8. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Co-create a realistic and manageable plan with Luis.  
   - Summarize key points and reinforce commitment.
