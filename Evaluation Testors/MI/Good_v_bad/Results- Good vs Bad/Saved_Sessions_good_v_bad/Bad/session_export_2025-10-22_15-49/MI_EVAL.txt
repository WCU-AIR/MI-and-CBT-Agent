# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s tone is blunt and directive, often judgmental (e.g., “You really need to be more careful”). There is no warmth or validation of the patient’s concerns or time constraints, resulting in a lack of acceptance.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal to no empathy demonstrated. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s signals about being pressed for time and does not reflect or acknowledge her feelings or perspective.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, expert-driven approach. The psychiatrist repeatedly commands and questions without inviting collaboration or supporting autonomy.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
No evidence of partnership. The psychiatrist controls the conversation with yes/no questions and directives, leaving the patient passive and excluded from decision-making.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is minimal and implicit; the psychiatrist does not evoke or reinforce it. Opportunities to explore the patient’s motivations or plans are missed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and admonishment rather than softening or validation, increasing defensiveness.

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [ ] Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is poor; the psychiatrist uses mostly closed questions and does not reflect or validate the patient’s concerns, resulting in a harsh, confrontational tone.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal  
- [ ] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on medication safety but in a rigid, inflexible manner that ignores patient concerns and ambivalence.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
No attempts to evoke the patient’s motivations or reasons for change; the psychiatrist issues commands without exploring patient perspective.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps

- [ ] Strengthens commitment language

**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs; the psychiatrist directs immediate cessation without co-creating a plan or assessing readiness.
