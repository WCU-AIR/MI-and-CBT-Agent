# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly authoritarian and directive, lacking warmth or genuine acceptance. The tone is blunt and at times judgmental, exemplified by statements such as “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do” and “You really need to be more careful with these drugs.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or supportive language that acknowledges the patient’s situation or feelings. The psychiatrist does not normalize the patient’s concerns or validate her efforts to seek information despite time constraints. The patient’s polite but rushed responses are met with impatience rather than understanding. This results in a clinical stance that feels more punitive than accepting.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect and support adequately, instead adopting a judgmental and directive tone that undermines acceptance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy. The psychiatrist does not reflect or acknowledge the patient’s perspective, feelings, or time constraints. The patient repeatedly signals being pressed for time and seeking brief, clear information, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and persists with a rigid, lecture-style approach. The lack of reflective listening or attempts to understand the patient’s experience is stark. For example, the psychiatrist’s repeated questioning about why the patient did not check with her doctor or stop medication earlier comes across as accusatory rather than empathetic. The patient’s polite but hurried responses are not met with validation or understanding.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks any evidence of the MI spirit—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support are absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what she “must” do and questioning her decisions in a confrontational manner. There is no invitation for the patient to share her thoughts or feelings, no evocation of her motivations or concerns, and no support for her autonomy. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of instructions rather than an active participant in her care. The psychiatrist’s repeated direct commands and “yes/no” questions further diminish collaboration.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a directive, controlling approach that contradicts the fundamental principles of motivational interviewing.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce change talk. The patient expresses some implicit change talk by acknowledging the risks and indicating willingness to follow up with her doctor, but the psychiatrist fails to amplify or explore these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on admonishing the patient for not checking earlier and insists on immediate cessation without exploring the patient’s readiness or confidence. There are no open-ended questions or reflections designed to elicit the patient’s own motivations or plans for change.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither actively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to deepen the patient’s motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk or resistance is met with confrontation rather than softening. The psychiatrist repeatedly questions the patient’s judgment (“Why didn’t you check?” “You really need to be more careful”) and insists on immediate action without acknowledging the patient’s ambivalence or barriers. The patient’s time constraints and limited engagement are ignored rather than accommodated. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that validate the patient’s concerns while gently guiding toward change.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced or ignored rather than softened or redirected, increasing patient defensiveness.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is one-sided, with the psychiatrist dominating the conversation and issuing commands. The patient is not invited to share her perspective beyond brief yes/no answers, nor is she involved in decision-making. The psychiatrist’s repeated “yes/no” questions and directive statements reinforce a power imbalance. There is no evidence of shared agenda-setting or negotiation.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and expert-driven, lacking genuine partnership or client-centeredness.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist provides clear, direct information about medication risks.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, lacking acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- No reflective listening or evocation of patient motivation.  
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than softened.  
- Partnership and autonomy support are absent.  
- The patient’s time constraints and concerns are ignored.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directive commands to a partnership approach that respects the patient’s expertise and autonomy.

2. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Encourage the patient to share her thoughts, feelings, and concerns to deepen engagement and understanding.

3. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect patient statements to demonstrate empathy and validate her experience.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths:**  
   Recognize and affirm the patient’s proactive efforts to seek information despite time pressures.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   Explore the patient’s motivations, concerns, and readiness to change, reinforcing any statements favoring change.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence rather than confronting or blaming.

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Explicitly acknowledge the patient’s right to make decisions and control the pace of change.

8. **Collaboratively Plan Next Steps:**  
   Work with the patient to develop a feasible plan that fits her schedule and priorities.

9. **Summarize Key Points:**  
   Use summaries to consolidate understanding and reinforce motivation.

10. **Respect Time Constraints:**  
    Acknowledge the patient’s limited time and tailor the conversation accordingly, offering follow-up options.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, judgmental, and directive style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. To provide effective care, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that respects the patient’s perspective and fosters her intrinsic motivation for safe medication use.
