## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

From the very first statement, the psychiatrist uses language that is controlling and accusatory (“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do,” “You really should cut down immediately,” “You have to stop relying on it so much”). There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or respect for Nathaniel’s autonomy or perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses Nathaniel’s ambivalence and attempts to express his views, labeling them as “excuses” and “rationalizing.” This approach is likely to alienate the patient and reduce engagement rather than foster collaboration.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect. The approach is authoritarian and dismissive, which is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

**Explanation:**

The psychiatrist explicitly rejects any negotiation or partnership (“I’m not here to negotiate or explore options,” “This is not negotiable”). Autonomy is not supported; rather, the patient is pressured and coerced. This approach is the opposite of MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
No collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support; the psychiatrist is directive and controlling throughout.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Respectful, Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling, blaming, or commanding.  
   - Express genuine interest and acceptance of the client’s perspective.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate ambivalence and concerns.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the client rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the client’s own motivations and support autonomy.

4. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - Use open questions and reflections to draw out reasons for change.  
   - Reinforce and explore any statements favoring change.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and affirm the legitimacy of sustain talk.  
   - Gently guide toward exploring ambivalence without confrontation.

6. **Build Partnership and Shared Decision-Making:**  
   - Invite the client to participate in setting goals and plans.  
   - Respect the client’s readiness and pace for change.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Coercion:**  
   - Replace commands with invitations and suggestions.  
   - Recognize that pushing too hard can increase resistance.
