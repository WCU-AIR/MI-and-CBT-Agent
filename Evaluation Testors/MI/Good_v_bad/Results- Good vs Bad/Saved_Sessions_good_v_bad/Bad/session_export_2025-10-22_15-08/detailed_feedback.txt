## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better”—the tone is authoritarian and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy and perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates Margaret’s experience and feelings, labeling her reasons as “excuses” and refusing to acknowledge her ambivalence or partial willingness to change. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth; instead, the psychiatrist uses coercive language (“You have to quit completely starting now”) and repeatedly demands a yes/no commitment without exploring the patient’s readiness or concerns. This approach is antithetical to acceptance in motivational interviewing.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental, and directive style that likely alienates the patient and undermines rapport.

The psychiatrist never reflects or validates Margaret’s concerns about social drinking, independence, or readiness to change. **Score: 1/5**

The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling Margaret what she “has to do” and demanding immediate compliance. There is no collaboration or partnership; the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s input and refuses to explore her motivations or values. **Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a directive, coercive approach that contradicts the fundamental spirit of MI.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and coercive, violating core MI principles.  
- No acceptance, empathy, or partnership demonstrated.  
- No use of reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions.  
- Change talk is ignored rather than cultivated.  
- Sustain talk is confronted aggressively rather than softened.  
- Autonomy is denied repeatedly, undermining motivation and engagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions by inviting the patient’s perspective and expressing genuine curiosity.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercive language.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Affirm patient strengths and efforts, even small ones.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage elaboration and exploration of motivations, values, and concerns.

4. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses willingness or reasons for change, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Explore the patient’s own goals and values related to health and lifestyle.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize that the patient is in control of decisions and pace of change.  
   - Avoid pressuring for immediate commitments.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, achievable goals.  
   - Summarize progress and plans to reinforce motivation.
