# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or respect for the patient’s autonomy or ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s concerns and feelings, labeling her hesitation as “resistance” or “excuses.” The tone is authoritarian and coercive, with frequent ultimatums (“Are you going to do it right now or not?”) and no warmth or support. This approach is likely to alienate the patient rather than engage her. There are zero instances of affirming or validating the patient’s experience.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental, and directive style that undermines rapport and trust.

### 2. Empathy

Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and accusations, showing a lack of attunement to the patient’s feelings or readiness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey empathy, missing critical opportunities to connect with the patient’s ambivalence and emotional state.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior (smoking cessation).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly confrontational, directive, and coercive, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use core MI skills such as open questions, reflections, affirmations, and evocation.  
- Sustain talk is escalated rather than softened, likely increasing patient resistance.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient responses as “excuses” or “resistance.”  
   - Show genuine respect for the patient’s experience and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the difficulty of quitting and the patient’s fears.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy and choice.

4. **Cultivate Change Talk and Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses willingness or reasons to change, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.

5. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Ask questions that invite elaboration and exploration.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and intentions.

6. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to identify manageable steps and build confidence.  
   - Summarize key points and reinforce commitment.

7. **Avoid Ultimatums and Coercion:**  
   - Replace “do it now or else” language with supportive, autonomy-respecting dialogue.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is likely to increase patient resistance and reduce motivation. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift to a collaborative, empathic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change.
