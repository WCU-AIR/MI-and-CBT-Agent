# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a judgmental, authoritarian, and directive stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or respect for the patient’s perspective. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s concerns and feelings, labeling his reasons as “excuses” and invalidating his lived experience (e.g., “No, you’re seriously underestimating the risks here,” “You really need to stop making excuses”). The tone is confrontational rather than supportive, which likely contributes to patient resistance. There is no normalization or validation of ambivalence or struggle.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a critical and coercive approach that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or convey the patient’s perspective beyond superficial acknowledgment. Patient statements about being busy, feeling overwhelmed, or needing to proceed at his own pace are met with dismissal or challenge rather than empathy (e.g., “No more thinking—just get out there and walk,” “You’re just making excuses about being busy”). The psychiatrist does not reflect or validate the patient’s feelings or concerns, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and explore ambivalence.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist fails to listen or respond to the patient’s emotional state or perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely directive and authoritarian, with the psychiatrist repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding compliance (“You must start exercising,” “No more ‘trying’ or ‘maybe later’”). There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s expressed ambivalence and resistance, instead escalating pressure and ultimatums. The patient is not invited to share his own motivations or goals, nor is his autonomy respected. This approach is the antithesis of the MI spirit.  

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent; the session is coercive and controlling rather than collaborative and autonomy-supportive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient expresses any tentative openness (“I’m doing what I can, when I can,” “I’m willing to take care of my health”), the psychiatrist ignores or overrides these statements with commands and ultimatums. There is no strategic amplification or exploration of the patient’s own reasons or desires for change. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses solely on pushing a predetermined plan, which likely suppresses any emerging motivation.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s reasons for not changing, ambivalence, and resistance) is met with confrontation and challenge rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels patient concerns as “excuses” and repeatedly demands immediate compliance, which likely entrenches sustain talk rather than softening it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate ambivalence while gently guiding toward change.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes full control of the session, setting the agenda unilaterally and issuing commands without negotiation. The patient is repeatedly told what to do with no invitation to collaborate or share decision-making. The psychiatrist explicitly states, “I’m setting this plan for you, and you need to follow it—no questions asked,” which exemplifies a complete lack of partnership. The patient’s expertise and autonomy are disregarded.  

**Summary:**  
There is no partnership; the interaction is authoritarian and paternalistic.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Empathic listening and reflective responses are essential.  
- The session should be collaborative, evoking the patient’s own motivations and supporting autonomy.  
- Change talk should be actively cultivated and sustain talk skillfully softened.  
- Partnership must replace authoritarian control.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors (smoking, exercise).  

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and coercive, which is counterproductive in motivational interviewing.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and instead escalates sustain talk.  
- No reflective listening, affirmations, or autonomy support are present.  
- The imposed plan is unlikely to be effective given the patient’s resistance and lack of collaboration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses.”  
   - Show respect for the patient’s perspective and ambivalence.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles.

3. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   - Invite the patient to share his own goals and ideas.  
   - Negotiate agenda and plans jointly rather than imposing directives.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore reasons for change.  
   - Reflect and reinforce any statements favoring change.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Avoid confrontation that entrenches resistance.

6. **Seek Permission Before Offering Advice:**  
   - Ask if the patient is open to suggestions before giving information or recommendations.

7. **Develop Collaborative, Realistic Plans:**  
   - Co-create achievable goals with the patient’s input.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session exemplifies a poor application of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivations, and likely increases resistance. To improve, the psychiatrist must fundamentally shift toward a respectful, empathic, collaborative approach that supports autonomy and skillfully navigates ambivalence. Without these changes, effective behavior change is unlikely.
