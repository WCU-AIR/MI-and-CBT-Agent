# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the outset, the tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or positive regard. Phrases such as “I’m here to tell you exactly what you need to do,” “You have to stop,” and “No ifs or buts” communicate disapproval and a lack of respect for the patient’s autonomy or perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressed difficulties as “excuses” and “hesitation,” which is invalidating and likely to increase resistance. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or normalization of the patient’s ambivalence.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is harsh, directive, and dismissive, undermining acceptance and rapport. This is a critical failure in MI practice.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses complex feelings about peer pressure, belonging, and difficulty quitting, but the psychiatrist ignores these emotional nuances and instead responds with blunt commands and accusations of “making excuses.” There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist’s responses do not convey understanding or empathy but rather impatience and frustration.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or explore the patient’s feelings and struggles, which damages therapeutic alliance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums and demands immediate compliance, ignoring the patient’s expressed ambivalence and readiness. There is no invitation for the patient to share their motivations or ideas, nor any support for autonomy. The psychiatrist’s style is coercive rather than motivational.  

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely missing. The approach is controlling and confrontational, which is contraindicated in MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the patient offers some change talk (e.g., “I want to do better,” “I’m ready to start giving it a shot”), the psychiatrist does not reinforce, reflect, or amplify these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on demanding immediate change and dismissing the patient’s ambivalence. There are no strategic efforts to evoke or deepen change talk, nor to explore the patient’s own reasons or confidence for change.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is ignored and not cultivated, missing a core MI opportunity.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s sustain talk as “excuses” and “hesitation,” directly confronting and invalidating it rather than softening or exploring it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The patient’s sustain talk about peer pressure and belonging is met with ultimatums and threats, which likely reinforce resistance.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than skillfully softened, undermining engagement.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, telling the patient what to do and demanding compliance. There is no evidence of partnership, shared decision-making, or respect for the patient’s expertise in their own life. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of orders rather than an active collaborator.  

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent; the interaction is hierarchical and directive.

---

### Overall Global Ratings Summary  
The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is characterized by judgment, lack of empathy, authoritarianism, failure to evoke change talk, harsh confrontation of sustain talk, and absence of partnership. This style is likely to increase patient resistance, reduce engagement, and undermine motivation for change.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and dismissive, which is antithetical to MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- No use of reflective listening or affirmations.  
- No evocation or reinforcement of change talk.  
- Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing.  
- Open questions are absent; the session is dominated by closed, directive questions.  
- The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly denied.  

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to a partnership approach that respects the patient’s autonomy and expertise.

2. **Increase Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and struggles, avoiding judgmental language.

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   Invite the patient to explore their own motivations, ambivalence, and barriers through open-ended questions and complex reflections.

4. **Affirm Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and affirm the patient’s positive intentions and small steps toward change.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or intention to change, reflect and explore these statements to deepen motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   Work with the patient to create a realistic, personalized plan that respects their readiness and context.

8. **Avoid Ultimatums and Threats:**  
   Replace coercive language with supportive, autonomy-enhancing communication.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s directive, confrontational style is likely to increase resistance and reduce motivation. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change.
