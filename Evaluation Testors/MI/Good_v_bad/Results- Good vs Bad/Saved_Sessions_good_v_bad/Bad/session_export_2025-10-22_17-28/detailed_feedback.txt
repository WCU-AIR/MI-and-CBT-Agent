# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and authoritarian stance throughout the session. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence, concerns about sustainability, and mental well-being are repeatedly dismissed or invalidated. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s hesitations as “making excuses” and “overcomplicating,” which is explicitly disapproving and undermines rapport. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The tone is directive and confrontational rather than supportive. This is the opposite of acceptance and risks alienating the patient.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or unconditional positive regard. The approach is judgmental and dismissive, severely damaging the therapeutic alliance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly shares nuanced concerns about burnout, sustainability, and mental health, but the psychiatrist ignores these and responds with blunt commands and accusations. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or struggles. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate perspective-taking or emotional attunement, instead focusing on compliance and adherence.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or convey understanding of the patient’s experience, leading to a breakdown in communication.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely lacking. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do and demanding yes/no answers. There is no collaboration or partnership; the patient’s autonomy is ignored and actively suppressed. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change but instead insists on compliance with a rigid plan. This is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The session is directive and coercive, with no evidence of MI spirit. The psychiatrist controls the agenda and dismisses the patient’s input.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses desire and reasons for change (e.g., wanting sustainable exercise, caring about mental health), the psychiatrist ignores these statements and instead focuses on compliance. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s motivation. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment without exploring or eliciting the patient’s own motivations or confidence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present in the patient’s speech but is not cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s ambivalence and hesitation as “excuses” and “hesitation,” reinforcing sustain talk rather than softening it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist confronts sustain talk with pressure and ultimatums, which likely increases resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, exacerbating resistance rather than softening it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no partnership evident in the session. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issues commands, and refuses to negotiate or collaborate. The patient’s expertise in her own experience is ignored. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on a yes/no answer to a rigid plan, refusing to acknowledge the patient’s perspective or co-create a plan.

**Summary:**  
The session is authoritarian and expert-driven, with no genuine collaboration or shared decision-making.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, confrontational style.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening and empathy to understand the patient’s perspective.  
- Use open-ended questions to explore ambivalence and motivations.  
- Affirm patient strengths and efforts to build rapport and motivation.  
- Emphasize patient autonomy and collaborate on goal-setting and planning.  
- Use softening strategies to address sustain talk and reduce resistance.  
- Evoke and reinforce change talk to strengthen motivation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   Avoid labeling patient statements as excuses or overcomplication. Show respect for the patient’s experience.

2. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Invite the patient to elaborate on motivations, barriers, and values.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths:**  
   Recognize the patient’s efforts and willingness to engage.

5. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Explicitly acknowledge the patient’s right to choose and control the pace of change.

6. **Collaborate on Planning:**  
   Work with the patient to develop realistic, personalized goals.

7. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

8. **Evocation of Change Talk:**  
   Strategically elicit and reinforce the patient’s own reasons and confidence for change.
