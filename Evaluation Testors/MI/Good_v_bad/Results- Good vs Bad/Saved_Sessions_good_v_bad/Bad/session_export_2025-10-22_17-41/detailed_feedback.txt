# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Asthma Management Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a judgmental, directive, and authoritarian tone throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s emotional experience and ambivalence, labeling his concerns as “excuses” and “hesitation.” The patient’s attachment to his dog and stressors are invalidated rather than acknowledged or normalized. This approach likely increases patient resistance and disengagement. For example, statements like “You have to stop making this about feelings” and “No more excuses” demonstrate a lack of acceptance and respect for the patient’s perspective.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a confrontational and directive stance that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or convey the patient’s emotional state, stress, or ambivalence. The patient expresses feeling overwhelmed, emotionally attached to his dog, and stressed by life circumstances, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead insists on compliance. The lack of paraphrasing, validation, or emotional reflection is stark. For example, when the patient says, “I’m stressed, overwhelmed, and yes, attached to my dog,” the psychiatrist responds with “You have to stop making this about feelings,” which dismisses the patient’s experience rather than empathizing with it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows no genuine effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective, resulting in a cold, unsupportive interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, expert-driven approach with no collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to…,” “No exceptions,” “This is not optional”) and ignores the patient’s expressed ambivalence and values. There is no attempt to evoke the patient’s own motivations or explore his readiness for change. Autonomy is explicitly denied, and the patient is pressured to comply immediately. This is the antithesis of the MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and autonomy.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is directive and controlling, lacking any collaborative or autonomy-supportive elements essential to MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient expresses some desire and willingness to improve (“I want to get better,” “I’m willing to think about ways I could change”), the psychiatrist ignores these statements and instead focuses on demands and ultimatums. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s motivation. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to explore the patient’s reasons for change or to build confidence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but neglected; the psychiatrist fails to cultivate or reinforce it, missing critical opportunities to build motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (patient’s resistance, ambivalence, and emotional barriers) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and “hesitation,” and repeatedly demands immediate compliance. There are no double-sided reflections or empathetic statements that acknowledge the patient’s struggle while gently guiding toward change. This approach likely entrenches sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened, undermining the patient’s willingness to engage in change.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands and ultimatums, with no invitation for the patient to share his perspective or co-create a plan. The patient repeatedly expresses his feelings and barriers, but these are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist does not share power or negotiate goals, instead imposing a rigid agenda.

**Summary:**  
There is no evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that alienates the patient.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must adopt a respectful, empathetic stance that validates the patient’s experience.  
- Reflective listening and emotional attunement are essential to build rapport.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—must replace the current directive approach.  
- Change talk should be actively evoked and reinforced.  
- Sustain talk requires softening through empathetic reflections and double-sided statements.  
- Partnership and shared decision-making must be prioritized.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles without labeling them as excuses.  
   - Use affirmations to recognize the patient’s efforts and strengths.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Avoid dismissive or confrontational language.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Evoke the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy by acknowledging the patient’s right to choose.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or willingness, reflect and explore these statements.  
   - Ask open-ended questions to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate emotional barriers.

6. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Invite the patient to co-create manageable steps.  
   - Avoid ultimatums; instead, negotiate realistic goals.

7. **Enhance Engagement:**  
   - Build rapport through warmth, empathy, and genuine interest.  
   - Avoid confrontational or authoritarian language.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely increases resistance. To improve, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach that respects the patient’s emotional experience and ambivalence while skillfully evoking and reinforcing change talk. Without these changes, the effectiveness of the intervention will be severely compromised, potentially harming patient outcomes.
