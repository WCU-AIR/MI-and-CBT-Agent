# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
☑ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s tone is largely judgmental and directive, frequently dismissing the patient’s struggles as “excuses.” There is little to no demonstration of unconditional positive regard or validation of the patient’s experience.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: active reflective listening  
☑ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal reflective listening is present. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings about stress and social triggers, responding instead with admonishments and factual statements.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
☑ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an expert-driven, directive style with frequent commands and little collaboration or evocation of the patient’s own motivations. Autonomy is not supported.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
☐ High: minimizes power differential  
☑ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains control throughout, issuing instructions without inviting patient input or shared decision-making. The patient’s perspective is overridden by the clinician’s agenda.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
☐ 1 (none) → ☑ 2 (minimal) → 3 → 4 → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Although the patient expresses change talk (desire and commitment to quit), the psychiatrist does not actively evoke, reinforce, or explore these statements, missing opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
☑ 1 (reinforces status quo) → ☐ 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk related to stress and social triggers is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than gentle reflection or normalization, which risks increasing resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
☐ Uses open questions  
☐ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks (minimal)  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is minimal; the patient remains polite but the psychiatrist’s confrontational style risks alienation. Few open questions and almost no reflections are used.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation but does so rigidly and without flexibility or patient collaboration.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
☐ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☐ Affirms autonomy  
☑ Minimal or no evocation  
**Comments:**  
Little to no effort is made to draw out the patient’s own reasons for change or affirm autonomy; the approach is directive rather than evocative.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
☐ Strengthens commitment language  
☐ Elicits concrete steps  
☑ No collaborative planning  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs; the psychiatrist issues commands about medication and avoidance without co-creating a realistic plan with the patient.

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☐ Draw out narrative, values  
☑ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Most questions are closed or leading, focusing on facts rather than eliciting patient thoughts or feelings.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☐ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
☑ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are minimal and mostly factual paraphrasing; emotional or complex reflections are absent.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☐ Pulls together change talk  
☑ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
No summaries are provided to consolidate motivation or reinforce change talk.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☐ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
☑ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
No affirmations are offered; the psychiatrist’s tone is critical and dismissive rather than supportive.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☐ Provides advice/info only after asking

Advice and directives are given without seeking permission, often in a coercive manner (“You have to,” “No ifs or buts”).

- Autonomy is not affirmed or supported.
