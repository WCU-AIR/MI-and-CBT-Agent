# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach throughout the session is predominantly confrontational and directive rather than accepting. Statements such as “You have to get serious about quitting,” “You’re just making excuses,” and “You’re wasting time” communicate judgment and pressure rather than unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses Eric’s perspective and feelings, failing to acknowledge his ambivalence or validate his experience. There is little to no warmth or supportive language; instead, the tone is authoritative and somewhat harsh. This approach risks alienating the patient and undermining rapport.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates inconsistent respect and frequent judgment, which is counterproductive in MI. A more accepting, nonjudgmental stance is essential.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows limited effort to understand Eric’s perspective. While the patient expresses ambivalence, rationalizations, and concerns about readiness, the psychiatrist largely ignores these cues and pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase Eric’s feelings or thoughts. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge Eric’s expressed stress, his belief in the protective effect of physical labor, or his need to be ready on his own terms. This lack of empathy likely contributes to Eric’s defensiveness and resistance.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is minimal, with a focus on persuasion rather than understanding. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect and validate the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks the core MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, directive stance, repeatedly telling Eric what he “has to” do and demanding commitment. There is no genuine partnership or invitation for Eric to explore his own motivations. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the psychiatrist pressures Eric to commit immediately, disregarding his expressed ambivalence and readiness. This approach is antithetical to MI principles and likely to increase resistance.  

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent. The psychiatrist is directive, controlling, and dismissive of client autonomy.

The patient offers some mild change talk (e.g., acknowledging doctor’s advice, considering quitting “someday”), but the psychiatrist does not skillfully reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the focus is on pushing immediate commitment, which shuts down exploration. There is no strategic use of reflections or open questions to deepen change talk.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt an Accepting, Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   - Avoid confrontational language and commands.  
   - Use supportive, respectful language that validates the patient’s experience.

2. **Increase Empathy and Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate ambivalence and explore the patient’s perspective.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than directing.  
   - Support autonomy and evoke the patient’s own motivations for change.

4. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.  
   - Avoid arguing or confronting sustain talk directly.

5. **Cultivate and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it.  
   - Use open questions to deepen exploration of reasons and desires for change.

6. **Use Affirmations and Summaries:**  
   - Recognize patient strengths and efforts.  
   - Summarize key points to consolidate motivation and plan next steps.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Explore readiness and preferences before suggesting aids or quitting strategies.  
   - Negotiate feasible, patient-centered next steps.

8. **Avoid Pressuring for Immediate Commitment:**  
   - Respect the patient’s stage of change and readiness.  
   - Encourage gradual movement toward change rather than demanding instant action.
