# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s stance throughout the session is judgmental, confrontational, and directive rather than accepting. From the very first statement—“we’re going to get straight to the point… I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do”—the psychiatrist sets an authoritarian tone that dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence and struggle are met with accusations of “making excuses” and “not listening,” which clearly violates unconditional positive regard. There is no warmth, empathy, or validation of the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s feelings and readiness, which likely increases resistance rather than engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental approach that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand the patient’s perspective or emotional state. Instead, the psychiatrist interrupts and challenges the patient’s statements with blunt commands and accusations. The patient’s explanations about stress, heroin use, and readiness are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist does not paraphrase, reflect, or validate the patient’s feelings or ambivalence. This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s guarded and resistant responses.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates little effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate quitting, issues ultimatums, and refuses to acknowledge the patient’s readiness or priorities. The patient is not invited to share their motivations or goals; instead, the psychiatrist imposes an agenda and demands compliance. This approach is antithetical to the MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership and client autonomy.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is controlling and coercive, lacking the fundamental MI elements of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient expresses some readiness and willingness to engage in safer heroin use and acknowledges the harms of smoking, the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s change talk. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses solely on demanding immediate cessation of smoking, which the patient is not ready for, leading to resistance rather than motivation.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither elicited nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to build on the patient’s expressed motivations.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly confronts and challenges sustain talk with accusations and ultimatums rather than softening or exploring it. The patient’s explanations about smoking as a coping mechanism and the difficulty of quitting are labeled as “excuses.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This approach likely entrenches sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than skillful softening or exploration, exacerbating resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  
**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no negotiation or shared decision-making. The patient’s preferences and readiness are ignored, and the psychiatrist explicitly states that the patient “doesn’t get to pick and choose” what to focus on. This power imbalance is stark and counterproductive.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership or shared power; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  
**Overall Score: 1/5**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is characterized by judgment, confrontation, lack of empathy, and authoritarianism. There is no evidence of acceptance, collaboration, or evocation of change talk. This approach is likely to increase patient resistance and disengagement, undermining any potential for behavior change.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains a consistent focus on smoking cessation.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, confrontational, and lacks empathy.  
- There is no acceptance or validation of the patient’s experience.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use MI techniques such as open questions, reflections, affirmations, or evocation.  
- The patient’s autonomy and readiness are ignored, leading to resistance.  
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than softened or explored.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s perspective and preferences.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles. Use reflective listening to show understanding.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Reflections:**  
   Encourage the patient to explore their motivations and ambivalence.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses readiness or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence rather than confronting or dismissing sustain talk.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize that change is the patient’s choice and pace.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   Work with the patient to identify achievable steps that fit their readiness and context.

8. **Avoid Ultimatums and Pressure:**  
   Recognize that coercion undermines motivation and engagement.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and non-empathetic approach is likely to alienate the patient and impede any progress toward behavior change. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques and spirit is essential to improve clinical effectiveness and patient outcomes.
