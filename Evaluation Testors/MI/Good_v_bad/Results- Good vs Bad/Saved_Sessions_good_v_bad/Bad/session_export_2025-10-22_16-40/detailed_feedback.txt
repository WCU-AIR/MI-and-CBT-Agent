# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session with Marek Novak

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do”—the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective. The patient is repeatedly interrupted, pressured, and denied space to express ambivalence or concerns. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s behavior as “excuses” and “making excuses,” which is explicitly judgmental and likely to provoke resistance. This approach is antithetical to acceptance in MI.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect. The stance is directive, confrontational, and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy and perspective.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly challenges and invalidates the patient’s statements (e.g., “No, you’re not okay,” “You don’t get to decide when you’re ready”). The psychiatrist’s tone is harsh and lacks attunement to the patient’s emotional state.

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**

The psychiatrist repeatedly demands yes/no answers, insists on immediate commitment, and refuses to acknowledge the patient’s right to self-determination. The patient is not invited to explore motivations or values; instead, the psychiatrist imposes a rigid agenda and threatens negative consequences. This is the opposite of the MI spirit, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and autonomy.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is coercive and controlling, lacking any MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support.

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**

There is no amplification, reflection, or exploration of the patient’s emerging motivation. **Summary:**

The psychiatrist’s approach escalates conflict rather than resolving ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected, undermining the MI process.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style is counterproductive and undermines MI principles.  
- Lack of empathy, reflective listening, and acceptance damages rapport and engagement.  
- Failure to evoke or reinforce change talk misses critical opportunities to build motivation.  
- Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened, increasing resistance.  
- No partnership or autonomy support; patient is repeatedly pressured and dismissed.  
- Use of closed questions and demands stifles dialogue and exploration.  
- No affirmations or summaries to support the patient’s process.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions by inviting the patient’s perspective and expressing genuine interest in their experience.  
   - Avoid commanding language; instead, use inclusive language (“Let’s explore this together,” “What are your thoughts?”).

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate ambivalence and acknowledge the difficulty of change.

3. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Explicitly affirm the patient’s right to make their own choices.  
   - Avoid pressuring for immediate commitment; instead, evoke readiness and confidence.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses any motivation, reflect and explore it further.  
   - Use open-ended questions to deepen exploration of reasons and desires for change.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize ambivalence as part of the change process.

6. **Use Open Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Replace yes/no questions with open-ended ones to invite elaboration.  
   - Affirm patient strengths, efforts, and values to build confidence.

7. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, incremental goals.  
   - Summarize key points and change talk to reinforce motivation.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session exemplifies a poor application of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach alienates the patient, suppresses motivation, and damages rapport. To improve, the psychiatrist must fundamentally shift to a collaborative, empathetic, autonomy-supportive style that skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk while softening sustain talk. Without these changes, the psychiatrist risks increasing patient resistance and undermining the potential for meaningful behavior change.
