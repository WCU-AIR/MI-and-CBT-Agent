# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, confrontational, and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“Today we’re going to focus on getting you to stop smoking. I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence and struggle are met with accusations of making excuses and being irresponsible rather than empathy or understanding. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s feelings and rationalizations, which likely increases resistance rather than fostering collaboration.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect, instead adopting a harsh, directive, and critical tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly shares her conflicted feelings about smoking as a coping mechanism and her concerns for her son, but the psychiatrist ignores these emotional cues and instead focuses on lecturing and demanding compliance. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist’s responses are dismissive and confrontational, which likely alienates the patient and shuts down open communication.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s feelings and struggles are heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is completely lacking. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without invitation or partnership. The patient’s autonomy is ignored or overridden, and the psychiatrist explicitly states that the patient does not get to decide how she feels or when she is ready. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values; instead, the psychiatrist attempts to impose change through coercion and threats.

**Summary:**  
The session is antithetical to MI spirit, characterized by control, persuasion, and disregard for patient autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the patient does express some change talk (e.g., concern for her son, desire to quit), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke, reinforce, or amplify this language. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts or overrides the patient’s ambivalence with demands and accusations. There is no strategic use of reflections or open questions to deepen the patient’s own reasons for change. The psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivating it.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the psychiatrist’s style likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully respond to sustain talk. The patient’s rationalizations and ambivalence are met with confrontation and labeling as “excuses” or “resistance.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk is met with direct challenges and demands, which likely increase patient defensiveness and sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s directives and ultimatums. The patient’s input is ignored or dismissed, and the psychiatrist explicitly states that the patient does not get to decide or negotiate. There is no shared decision-making or co-creation of goals or plans.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of partnership; the psychiatrist exerts full control and disregards patient autonomy.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must radically shift from an authoritarian, confrontational style to one that embodies acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- Reflective listening and validation of patient experience are essential.  
- The psychiatrist should evoke and reinforce change talk rather than suppress it.  
- Sustain talk must be met with skillful softening rather than confrontation.  
- Partnership and autonomy support must replace coercion and ultimatums.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is counterproductive and likely to increase resistance and reduce motivation.  
- The session lacks all core MI elements: acceptance, empathy, collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- The psychiatrist must learn to listen reflectively, affirm patient strengths, and evoke change talk.  
- Sustain talk should be met with understanding and strategic softening, not confrontation.  
- Planning must be collaborative and patient-driven.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s perspective and respect her autonomy.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and validate the patient’s experience.

3. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   Use open-ended questions and reflections to draw out the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and ambivalence.

5. **Affirm Patient Strengths:**  
   Recognize and reinforce the patient’s efforts, concerns, and courage in facing a difficult challenge.

6. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   Work with the patient to develop a quit plan that feels achievable and respects her readiness.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   Replace threats and demands with supportive, autonomy-enhancing language.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive, and dismissive approach undermines rapport, increases resistance, and fails to support the patient’s motivation or autonomy. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques is essential to prevent harm and improve patient outcomes.
