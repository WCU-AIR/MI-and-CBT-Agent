# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian tone that dismisses the patient’s perspective and autonomy. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence and hesitation are met with impatience and accusations of “making excuses” or “procrastinating.” This approach likely alienates the patient rather than fosters a safe, supportive environment. There are no affirmations or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or struggles.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance or respect. The stance is judgmental and directive, which is contraindicated in motivational interviewing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly expresses ambivalence, concerns about readiness, and the emotional complexity of quitting smoking, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or attempts to paraphrase or validate the patient’s feelings. The psychiatrist’s responses are accusatory and dismissive, which likely inhibits open communication and trust.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s perspective is heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach that contradicts the core MI principles of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate action, disregards the patient’s readiness, and uses confrontational language (“You have to quit now,” “No ifs, ands, or buts”). There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values, no support for autonomy, and no partnership. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation with commands and accusations, which is antithetical to MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent. The approach is coercive and controlling rather than collaborative and autonomy-supportive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient spontaneously expresses change talk (concerns about children, health worries), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements and instead focuses on pushing behavior change through commands. There are no strategic reflections or amplifications of change talk, no invitations to explore reasons or confidence, and no reinforcement of the patient’s expressed desires or concerns.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither cultivated nor reinforced. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s ambivalence as “making excuses,” “procrastinating,” and “putting health at risk,” which likely increases resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The patient’s legitimate concerns about readiness and coping are invalidated rather than explored.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or managed skillfully.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes full control of the conversation, issuing commands and demanding compliance. There is no evidence of partnership, shared decision-making, or respect for the patient’s expertise in her own life. The patient’s autonomy is repeatedly overridden with ultimatums and pressure. The psychiatrist does not invite collaboration or negotiate goals.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the interaction is authoritarian and directive.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally incompatible with motivational interviewing principles.  
- The session is dominated by confrontation, commands, and judgment, which likely increases patient resistance and reduces engagement.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use core MI skills such as reflective listening, affirmations, and evocation of change talk.  
- Planning and summarizing are absent.  
- The psychiatrist does not respect the patient’s autonomy or readiness, repeatedly overriding her expressed feelings and concerns.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient.  
   - Use inclusive language and invite the patient’s input.

2. **Develop Empathy and Reflective Listening:**  
   - Practice reflecting both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s ambivalence and struggles.

3. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Explicitly acknowledge the patient’s right to choose and control the pace of change.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and coercive language.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses concerns or desires for change, reflect and explore these statements.  
   - Ask open-ended questions to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change.

6. **Avoid Confrontation and Judgment:**  
   - Replace accusatory language with curiosity and acceptance.  
   - Recognize that resistance is a signal to change approach, not escalate pressure.

7. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop achievable goals when ready.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, empathy, and patient engagement. There is no evidence of MI spirit, acceptance, or skillful handling of ambivalence. To improve, the psychiatrist must undergo substantial training in MI techniques, focusing on collaboration, empathy, and evocation rather than persuasion and control. Without these changes, the approach risks increasing patient resistance and reducing the likelihood of meaningful behavior change.
