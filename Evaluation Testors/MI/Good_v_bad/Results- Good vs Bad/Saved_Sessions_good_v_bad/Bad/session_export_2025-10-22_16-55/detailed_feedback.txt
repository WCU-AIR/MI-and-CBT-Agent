# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach throughout the session is judgmental, directive, and dismissive of the patient’s perspective. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or respect. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s honest expressions of difficulty as “making excuses” or “feeling sorry for yourself,” which is stigmatizing and undermines acceptance. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or validation of the patient’s struggles. The patient’s emotional expressions are met with criticism rather than empathy or support. This is a clear violation of MI principles and results in a hostile environment that likely discourages openness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental stance that alienates the patient and undermines rapport.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s emotional cues and concerns, such as feeling overwhelmed, discouraged, or needing a manageable plan. Instead of reflecting or validating these feelings, the psychiatrist dismisses them as excuses or weakness. The patient’s expressions of frustration and fear are met with commands and ultimatums rather than attempts to understand or explore underlying emotions. The lack of empathy is stark and detrimental to the therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate empathy; patient emotions and perspectives are disregarded or invalidated.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on compliance with rigid instructions, refuses to negotiate or explore the patient’s readiness, and uses coercive language (“no ifs, ands, or buts,” “no room for negotiation”). The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist’s stance is controlling rather than partnership-oriented. This approach is antithetical to the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates an authoritarian, coercive style with no collaboration or support for patient autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist asks some direct questions about exercise and diet, these are framed as commands rather than open invitations to explore motivation. The patient does express change talk (desire to exercise, frustration with current state), but the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce this language. Instead, the psychiatrist’s responses tend to shut down elaboration by focusing on compliance and discipline. There is little effort to deepen or amplify the patient’s own reasons for change.

**Summary:**  
Some change talk is present from the patient, but the psychiatrist fails to effectively evoke or cultivate it.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly labels the patient’s sustain talk as “excuses” and “feeling sorry for yourself,” which reinforces sustain talk rather than softening it. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate ambivalence. Instead, the psychiatrist confronts and challenges the patient harshly, likely increasing resistance. This approach is counterproductive and contrary to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and judgment, with no effort to soften or redirect it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative relationship. The patient is repeatedly told what to do with no input or negotiation. The psychiatrist refuses to consider the patient’s perspective or preferences, dismissing any attempts at dialogue or compromise. The power differential is stark and unmitigated, with the psychiatrist adopting an expert, authoritarian role.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration is evident; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors (exercise, diet).  
- The patient remains engaged and expresses motivation despite the challenging interaction.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and dismissive, which is antithetical to motivational interviewing.  
- There is a complete lack of acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use reflective listening, affirmations, or open questions effectively.  
- Change talk is not cultivated or amplified; sustain talk is confronted harshly.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.  
- The approach likely increases patient resistance and decreases motivation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient. Use inclusive language and invite the patient’s input.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   - Validate the patient’s feelings and struggles. Avoid judgmental language and labels like “excuses.”

3. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to show understanding and encourage deeper exploration.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and ask open questions to deepen motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence rather than confronting or dismissing it.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s control over choices and pace of change.

7. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to set realistic, achievable goals that fit their life context.

8. **Avoid Ultimatums and Coercion:**  
   - Replace rigid demands with supportive guidance and negotiation.

---

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, empathy, and patient motivation. Without acceptance, empathy, and collaboration, the patient is unlikely to engage meaningfully or sustain behavior change. Immediate and substantial improvement in MI skills is essential to avoid harm and improve patient outcomes.
