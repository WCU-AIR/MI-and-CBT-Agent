## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a harsh, judgmental tone throughout, repeatedly blaming and shaming the patient. There is no evidence of respect or acceptance; the patient’s feelings and perspective are dismissed.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal to no empathy is demonstrated. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s emotional cues and does not reflect or validate the patient’s frustration and vulnerability.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive approach with no collaboration or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist imposes demands rather than evoking the patient’s own motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
No partnership is established. The psychiatrist controls the agenda and dismisses the patient’s priorities, offering no shared decision-making or negotiation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is minimal and tentative; the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce it, instead demanding compliance and ignoring ambivalence.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)

- [ ] Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist fails to build rapport or engage empathetically. Questions are mostly closed and leading; no reflective listening is used, resulting in patient defensiveness.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)

- [x] Confirms agreed goal  
- [ ] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**

### Evoking (5-point Likert)

- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
There is no effective evocation of the patient’s motivations or values. The psychiatrist issues commands rather than eliciting reasons for change.

### Planning (5-point Likert)

**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist imposes a plan without negotiation or patient involvement.
