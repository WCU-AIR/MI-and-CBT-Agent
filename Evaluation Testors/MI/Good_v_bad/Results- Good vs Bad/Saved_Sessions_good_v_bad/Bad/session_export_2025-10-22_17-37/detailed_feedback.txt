# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better”—the tone is directive and controlling, lacking any warmth or unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s perspective and uses language that implies blame and failure (“You really need to get those habits in check,” “You have to be motivated—this isn’t optional,” “No excuses”). There is no evidence of respect for the patient’s autonomy or acknowledgment of the patient’s struggles. The patient’s humor and guardedness are met with impatience and commands rather than acceptance. This is the opposite of unconditional positive regard and is likely to increase resistance rather than reduce it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental and directive, failing to communicate acceptance or respect. This severely undermines the therapeutic alliance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient’s guarded, humorous, and resistant communication style is met with blunt commands and rhetorical questions that imply fault (“Why haven’t you started exercising regularly yet?” “Why haven’t you just done that already?”). There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or ambivalence. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s subtle cues of frustration and resistance, instead escalating pressure. The patient’s statements about life being “a bit of a mess” or difficulty changing habits are dismissed rather than explored empathetically.

**Summary:**  
There is a near-complete absence of reflective listening or perspective-taking. The psychiatrist fails to convey understanding or empathy.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive style with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“No excuses,” “This isn’t optional,” “You don’t get to pick and choose here”) and demands immediate compliance. The patient’s autonomy is ignored or overridden. There is no attempt to evoke the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change; instead, the psychiatrist imposes a rigid agenda. The patient’s resistance is met with increased pressure rather than partnership or exploration.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is entirely absent. The session is coercive and controlling, undermining motivation and engagement.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce any change talk. When the patient offers tentative or ambiguous statements about trying or considering change (“Maybe I should cut the junk food,” “I’ve tried a couple of times”), the psychiatrist ignores these openings and instead doubles down on commands and demands. There is no strategic reflection or amplification of any language favoring change. The psychiatrist fails to explore or deepen the patient’s ambivalence or reasons for change.

**Summary:**  
No skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk is present. Opportunities to build motivation are missed.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and pressure rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly challenges the patient’s resistance with ultimatums and blame (“You have to be motivated—this isn’t optional,” “No excuses,” “You don’t get to negotiate with your health”). There are no double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. The patient’s sustain talk is effectively reinforced by the psychiatrist’s confrontational style, likely increasing resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead escalates conflict.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a purely expert-driven, authoritarian stance. There is no evidence of collaboration or shared decision-making. The patient is repeatedly told what to do with no invitation to share their perspective or co-create a plan. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s cues and does not acknowledge the patient’s expertise or autonomy. The power differential is maximized rather than minimized.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration is evident; the session is dominated by the psychiatrist’s directives.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, directive approach.  
- Develop skills in acceptance, empathy, and reflective listening.  
- Embrace the spirit of MI: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Learn to skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk.  
- Practice softening sustain talk with double-sided reflections and normalization.  
- Build genuine partnership and shared decision-making.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is counterproductive, characterized by confrontation, commands, and lack of empathy.  
- The session lacks all core elements of motivational interviewing.  
- The patient’s resistance is likely increased by the authoritarian style.  
- The psychiatrist must develop fundamental MI skills: acceptance, empathy, reflective listening, evocation, and partnership.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Replace commands with open-ended questions and reflective listening.  
   - Invite the patient’s perspective and acknowledge their autonomy.

2. **Develop Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s feelings and ambivalence without judgment.  
   - Normalize struggles and validate the patient’s experience.

3. **Evoking Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient hints at willingness or attempts, reflect and amplify these statements.  
   - Explore the patient’s own reasons and motivations for change.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.  
   - Avoid confrontation and ultimatums.

5. **Avoid Directives Without Permission:**  
   - Ask permission before offering advice or information.  
   - Collaboratively develop goals and plans.

6. **Practice Summarizing:**  
   - Summarize patient statements to reinforce motivation and clarify ambivalence.

7. **Training and Supervision:**  
   - Engage in formal MI training and supervision to develop skills.  
   - Use role-plays and feedback to improve MI adherence.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, increases resistance, and fails to engage the patient in meaningful change talk or planning. Immediate and substantial improvement is required to meet basic MI competency and to avoid harm in clinical practice.
