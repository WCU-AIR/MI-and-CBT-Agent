# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, authoritarian, and directive stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is commanding and lacks any warmth or respect for the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s expressions of overwhelm and ambivalence, invalidating his feelings by saying “No, you don’t get to say it’s ‘too much’” and “No, you don’t get to feel stuck or worried.” This approach is disempowering and does not communicate unconditional positive regard. There is no evidence of acceptance or normalization of the patient’s struggle, only repeated demands and ultimatums.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is harsh, directive, and lacking in acceptance. This creates a hostile environment that likely inhibits open communication and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly shares feelings of overwhelm, fear of failure, and need for manageable steps, but the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these cues. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s emotional experience. Instead, the psychiatrist responds with blunt commands and rhetorical questions that imply blame (“Why haven’t you made this a priority?”). The lack of empathy is stark and undermines rapport.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate empathy or reflective listening, missing critical opportunities to connect with the patient’s emotional state.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on strict adherence to the plan without negotiation or exploration of the patient’s motivations or barriers. The patient’s autonomy is explicitly denied (“No, you don’t get to say you’re not ready,” “No, we’re not working on any plan you can ‘stick with’”). This approach is antithetical to the spirit of MI, which emphasizes partnership, evocation, and respect for client choice.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s style is coercive and controlling, completely lacking the collaborative and autonomy-supportive spirit essential to MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitment to change, there is no skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk. The patient does express some change talk (e.g., willingness to try testing twice a day, desire to avoid complications), but the psychiatrist does not amplify or explore these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance and adherence, missing opportunities to deepen the patient’s intrinsic motivation or explore ambivalence constructively.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not cultivated or reinforced; the psychiatrist’s approach is more about compliance than motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently ignores or invalidates sustain talk and ambivalence. The patient’s expressions of difficulty, fear, and need for manageable steps are met with blunt refusals and demands. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. Instead, sustain talk is met with accusations of “making excuses” and threats about consequences, which likely increase resistance rather than soften sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften or redirect sustain talk, instead reinforcing resistance through confrontation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a clear expert-driven, authoritarian stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The patient’s input is repeatedly overridden or dismissed. The psychiatrist uses language that excludes the patient from the process (“You have to,” “No exceptions,” “This isn’t negotiable”). This power imbalance is detrimental to engagement and motivation.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is established; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and excludes the patient from collaborative planning.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the seriousness of diabetes management, which is clinically important.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and lacks acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- There is no evocation or reinforcement of the patient’s own motivations or strengths.  
- Sustain talk and ambivalence are ignored or punished rather than explored.  
- The patient’s autonomy and perspective are repeatedly denied, undermining engagement.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s perspective and co-create goals and plans.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Reflect the patient’s feelings and struggles. Normalize ambivalence and validate the difficulty of change.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Reflections:**  
   Encourage elaboration and deeper exploration of motivations and barriers.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses willingness or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and affirm the legitimacy of the patient’s concerns while gently guiding toward change.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize the patient’s control over decisions and pace of change.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   Develop realistic, manageable steps together, ensuring the patient feels capable and supported.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   Replace threats and demands with supportive, motivational dialogue.

This session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards.
