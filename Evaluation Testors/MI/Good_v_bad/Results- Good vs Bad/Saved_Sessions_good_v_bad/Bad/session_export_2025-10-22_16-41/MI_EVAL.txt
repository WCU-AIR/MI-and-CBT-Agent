# Summary

This evaluation reveals a fundamental lack of motivational interviewing skills. The psychiatrist’s confrontational, directive approach fails to build rapport, demonstrate empathy, or support patient autonomy. Change talk is neither evoked nor reinforced, and sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than strategic softening. The psychiatrist relies on commands and ultimatums, which increase patient resistance and reduce the likelihood of behavior change. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a collaborative, empathetic stance that respects ambivalence, uses reflective listening, and supports autonomy to effectively engage and motivate the patient.
