# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and authoritarian stance from the outset. The opening statement, “I’m here to tell you exactly what you need to do,” immediately sets a confrontational tone that dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s views, labels the patient’s responses as “excuses,” and issues ultimatums rather than expressing respect or understanding. There is no evidence of warmth, positive regard, or acceptance of the patient’s experience. The patient’s defensiveness and resistance are clear reactions to this lack of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate unconditional positive regard. The approach is directive, judgmental, and dismissive, which undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. Instead of reflective listening, the psychiatrist issues commands and challenges the patient’s statements without exploring underlying feelings or motivations. For example, when the patient expresses that quitting cold turkey is unrealistic, the psychiatrist ignores this and insists on immediate compliance. The patient’s frustration and ambivalence are met with confrontation rather than empathy. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s experience.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent. The psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s perspective is heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach with no collaboration or evocation. The psychiatrist repeatedly tells the patient what to do, demands commitment, and dismisses the patient’s autonomy. There is no evidence of supporting the patient’s choice or inviting their input. The psychiatrist’s language (“You don’t get to decide,” “You have to commit”) is coercive and controlling, directly opposing the MI spirit of partnership, evocation, and autonomy support.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is entirely absent. The session is dominated by control and persuasion rather than collaboration and evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Instead, the patient’s tentative ambivalence and hints of concern are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist fails to explore or amplify any statements that might indicate readiness or desire for change. Instead, the focus is on demanding immediate commitment, which leads the patient to resist and shut down. There are no open-ended questions or reflections designed to elicit change talk.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk occurs. The psychiatrist’s approach suppresses rather than cultivates motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s reasons and explanations as “excuses” and repeatedly insists on compliance without acknowledging the legitimacy of the patient’s ambivalence or concerns. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is met with increased pressure, which exacerbates resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk and instead reinforces it through confrontation and coercion.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative relationship. The patient is repeatedly told what to do, with no invitation to share their views or participate in decision-making. The psychiatrist’s language is controlling and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy. There is no evidence of shared power or mutual respect.

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance that alienates the patient.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, directive approach.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening and empathy.  
- Embrace the MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Learn to skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk.  
- Practice softening sustain talk rather than confronting it.  
- Build genuine partnership with patients.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified. The session is dominated by confrontation and directive commands.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must fundamentally change approach to align with MI principles.  
- Develop skills in empathy, reflective listening, and acceptance.  
- Use open-ended questions to explore patient perspectives.  
- Avoid confrontation and coercion; instead, evoke patient motivations.  
- Respect and support patient autonomy.  
- Collaboratively develop plans and goals.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership. Invite the patient’s views and preferences.

2. **Use Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and build rapport.

3. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   Ask open-ended questions that explore the patient’s reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

5. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Explicitly affirm the patient’s right to choose and control their change process.

6. **Avoid Directives and Ultimatums:**  
   Replace “You have to” with “What do you think about…” or “How would you like to approach…”

7. **Build Engagement:**  
   Attend to the patient’s emotional state and defensiveness; respond with empathy rather than confrontation.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style alienates the patient, suppresses motivation, and fails to build rapport or evoke change talk. Without substantial improvement in empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and evocation skills, the psychiatrist risks causing harm and missing opportunities to support meaningful behavior change. Immediate retraining in MI fundamentals is strongly recommended.
