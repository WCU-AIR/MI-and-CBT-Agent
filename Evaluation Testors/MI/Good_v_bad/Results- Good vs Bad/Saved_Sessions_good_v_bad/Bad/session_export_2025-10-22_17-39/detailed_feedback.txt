# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive from the outset. The opening statement, “I’m here to get straight to the point about your health,” followed by “We need to talk about what you’re doing that’s harming you and how you’re going to fix it,” sets a confrontational tone rather than one of unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist repeatedly challenges the patient’s statements with skepticism and accusations of “making excuses,” “overthinking,” and “not taking this seriously.” This undermines acceptance and creates a defensive atmosphere. There is no evidence of warmth, support, or normalization of the patient’s ambivalence or current stage of change. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge the patient’s perspective or validate her feelings, which is critical for acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a predominantly judgmental and confrontational stance, lacking the respectful, supportive tone necessary for effective MI. This severely limits rapport and client engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. Instead of reflective listening, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s explanations and concerns as “excuses” or “overthinking.” There are no complex or simple reflections that capture the patient’s ambivalence, motivations, or emotional state. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge the patient’s expressed desire for sustainable change or her realistic approach to starting small. The patient’s statements about feeling overwhelmed and wanting to build consistency are ignored or invalidated. This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s defensive posture.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is largely absent, with the psychiatrist failing to demonstrate understanding or validation of the patient’s experience and perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive throughout. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate, rigid commitments (“You must go to the gym three times this week,” “No ifs, no buts”) and dismisses the patient’s autonomy and readiness. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. The psychiatrist does not invite the patient’s ideas or preferences, nor does he explore her motivations or values. Instead, the interaction is characterized by commands, accusations, and pressure. This is the antithesis of the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, confrontational stance with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist attempts to elicit commitment language but does so through pressure and demands rather than skillful evocation. The patient offers change talk about wanting to increase activity and build strength, but the psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify these statements. Instead, the psychiatrist challenges and invalidates the patient’s readiness and approach, which likely suppresses further change talk. There is no strategic use of reflections or open questions to deepen motivation or explore ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is minimally cultivated and often undermined by the psychiatrist’s confrontational style.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently confronts and challenges sustain talk with accusations and demands rather than softening or redirecting it. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence and realistic pacing are met with “stop making excuses,” “overthinking,” and “laziness” accusations. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that acknowledge the legitimacy of the patient’s concerns while gently guiding toward change. This approach likely entrenches sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands and ultimatums, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for the patient’s expertise in her own life. The psychiatrist repeatedly overrides the patient’s expressed preferences and readiness, refusing to negotiate or explore alternatives. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of orders rather than an active partner.

**Summary:**  
There is a clear power imbalance with no meaningful partnership or client-centered collaboration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Supportive Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions with warmth and acceptance. Avoid confrontational language and judgments.  
   - Normalize ambivalence and validate the patient’s feelings and perspective.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Use complex reflections to deepen engagement and explore ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient, evoking their own motivations and reasons for change.  
   - Support autonomy by respecting the patient’s readiness and choices.

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - Use open questions and reflections to draw out change talk.  
   - Amplify and reinforce statements favoring change.

5. **Softly Redirect Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and affirmations to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.

6. **Collaboratively Develop Plans:**  
   - Negotiate feasible, realistic steps with the patient.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment.

7. **Avoid Authoritarian Language and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace commands with invitations and collaborative goal-setting.  
   - Seek permission before offering advice or information.
