# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s perspective and feelings, using language that is directive and confrontational (“I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do,” “You don’t get to decide,” “No excuses”). There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s attempts to express ambivalence and emotional complexity are met with dismissal or reprimand rather than acceptance. This approach likely undermines rapport and trust.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or acceptance, instead adopting a controlling and punitive tone that is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses ambivalence, emotional struggle, and a desire for support, but the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead pushes a rigid agenda. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings. The psychiatrist’s responses are accusatory and dismissive, which likely alienates the patient and inhibits open communication.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s experience is heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive approach with no collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on immediate cessation of drinking, refusing to acknowledge the patient’s readiness or capacity for change. The patient’s autonomy is explicitly denied (“You don’t get to decide,” “This isn’t optional”). This coercive style contradicts the core MI spirit of partnership and respect for client choice.  

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is completely absent; the psychiatrist adopts a controlling, prescriptive stance that undermines motivation and engagement.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the psychiatrist repeatedly asks the patient to commit to quitting drinking immediately, these attempts are coercive rather than evocative. The psychiatrist does not skillfully elicit or reinforce the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change. Instead, the patient’s change talk is largely self-generated and met with resistance rather than amplification. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore the patient’s values (e.g., love for her son) as motivators for change.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist’s approach.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully respond to sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal (“You’re just making excuses,” “No ifs or buts”). There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This likely increases resistance and disengagement.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected, which is counterproductive.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish or maintain a collaborative partnership. The patient is repeatedly told what to do without negotiation or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist explicitly denies the patient’s autonomy and expertise in her own life. This power imbalance is stark and detrimental to effective MI.  

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and excludes the patient from the process.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist consistently maintains focus on the target behavior (drinking).  

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must radically shift from a directive, authoritarian style to one that embodies acceptance, empathy, and collaboration.  
- Reflections, affirmations, and open-ended questions are absent and needed to build rapport and evoke motivation.  
- The psychiatrist should learn to recognize and soften sustain talk rather than confront it.  
- Supporting autonomy and eliciting the patient’s own reasons for change are critical.  

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior (alcohol use).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and lacks core MI elements such as acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- There is a complete absence of reflective listening, affirmations, and open-ended questions.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The patient’s autonomy and readiness are ignored, which likely increases resistance and reduces motivation.  
- Collaborative planning and summarizing are missing.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from authoritarian commands to partnership and shared decision-making.  
   - Respect the patient’s autonomy and expertise in her own life.

2. **Increase Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Avoid judgmental or confrontational language.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions and Affirmations:**  
   - Invite the patient to explore her motivations and barriers.  
   - Affirm her strengths, efforts, and values (e.g., love for her son).

4. **Skillfully Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   - Explore the patient’s reasons for change and build on them.  
   - Avoid pushing for immediate commitment; instead, support readiness.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and normalize ambivalence.  
   - Avoid confronting or dismissing sustain talk.

6. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, achievable steps.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and plan.

7. **Training and Supervision:**  
   - The psychiatrist would benefit from intensive MI training and supervision to internalize MI principles and techniques.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines the therapeutic alliance and likely decreases the patient’s motivation and engagement. To provide effective MI, the psychiatrist must embrace acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation, and abandon coercive tactics. Without significant improvement, this approach risks harming the patient’s willingness to change and trust in healthcare providers.
