# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance from the outset. The opening statement, “I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do,” sets an authoritarian tone that undermines acceptance. The repeated use of “You have to,” “No excuses,” and “Stop making excuses” conveys disapproval and blame rather than unconditional positive regard. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or validate the patient’s feelings or perspective, nor does he express warmth or support. The patient’s frustration and resistance are met with confrontation rather than empathy. There is no evidence of normalization or acceptance of ambivalence.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive, lacking respect and support. This severely limits rapport and client engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate understanding or reflective listening. The patient expresses tiredness, stress, and prioritization of school attendance, but the psychiatrist dismisses these concerns as “excuses” and repeatedly challenges the patient’s motives. There are no reflections or paraphrases that convey understanding of the patient’s perspective or emotional state. The psychiatrist does not explore the patient’s ambivalence or acknowledge the difficulty of balancing competing priorities. The patient’s frustration and resistance are met with pressure rather than empathy.  

**Summary:**  
There is minimal to no effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, resulting in a lack of empathy.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is highly authoritarian and directive. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to,” “Stop making excuses,” “Just do it”) and demands yes/no commitments without collaboration or evocation. The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist does not invite the patient’s ideas or motivations. There is no partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist’s style is coercive rather than supportive, which is antithetical to the spirit of MI.  

**Summary:**  
The session lacks collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support, reflecting an authoritarian and confrontational style.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses some change talk related to school attendance (“I have to start getting to school on time,” “For me”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements and instead focuses exclusively on exercise. The psychiatrist repeatedly demands commitments to exercise without exploring the patient’s own reasons or motivations. There is no amplification or reinforcement of the patient’s change talk, and no strategic elicitation of commitment or confidence language.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor reinforced; the psychiatrist misses opportunities to build on the patient’s expressed motivations.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften sustain talk but rather confronts and challenges it aggressively. The patient’s sustain talk about tiredness, stress, and prioritizing school is met with accusations of “making excuses” and demands to “stop putting it off.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate the patient’s ambivalence. The confrontational approach likely increases resistance rather than reducing sustain talk.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than skillful softening or redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The session is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no invitation for the patient to share ideas or participate in decision-making. The patient is repeatedly pressured to commit to behaviors on the psychiatrist’s terms. The power differential is emphasized rather than minimized.  

**Summary:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared power; the interaction is authoritarian and directive.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  
**Overall Score: 1/5**  

The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is characterized by judgment, confrontation, lack of empathy, and authoritarian directives. There is no collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy. Change talk is ignored, and sustain talk is confronted aggressively. This style is likely to increase resistance and reduce motivation, undermining therapeutic effectiveness.

- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, which is counterproductive.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and reflective listening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.

- No affirmations or positive reinforcement are offered.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   Shift from directing and commanding to partnering with the patient. Invite the patient’s perspective and ideas.

2. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Encourage elaboration and exploration by asking open questions rather than yes/no queries.

3. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   Reflect the patient’s feelings and perspectives to build rapport and trust. Avoid judgmental language.

4. **Evoke and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   Listen for and amplify the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change, including those related to school attendance.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence rather than confronting or dismissing sustain talk.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   Emphasize the patient’s right and ability to choose and control their behavior change process.

7. **Affirm Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and affirm the patient’s efforts, values, and strengths to build confidence.

8. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Work with the patient to develop achievable goals and summarize progress to reinforce motivation.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach is likely to increase resistance and reduce motivation. To improve, the psychiatrist must adopt a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive style that skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own motivations for change.
