# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’ll be telling you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and lacks any warmth or respect for the patient’s autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s perspective, labels his responses as “excuses,” and issues ultimatums (“You must quit immediately,” “No ifs or buts”). There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or acceptance of the patient’s ambivalence or resistance. The patient’s guarded and defensive responses are met with pressure rather than empathy or understanding.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate acceptance, instead adopting a harsh, directive approach that likely increases patient resistance.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or validate the patient’s feelings, perspective, or ambivalence. The patient’s statements about peer influence, feeling forced to attend, and skepticism about addiction are ignored or dismissed. The psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with lectures and commands rather than listening or reflecting. For example, when the patient says, “I’m not making excuses,” the psychiatrist immediately counters with “You’re just making excuses,” showing a lack of perspective-taking.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, with no evidence of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly tells the patient what to do, uses ultimatums, and ignores the patient’s expressed resistance and ambivalence. There is no invitation for the patient to share his own motivations or concerns, nor any support for his autonomy. The psychiatrist’s approach is the antithesis of MI spirit, which requires partnership and evocation rather than confrontation and persuasion.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to embody the MI spirit, instead adopting a controlling and confrontational style that undermines motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke or reinforce any change talk. The patient’s minimal expressions of ambivalence or acknowledgment of smoking are met with dismissal or commands rather than exploration. The psychiatrist does not ask open-ended questions that invite the patient to articulate reasons for change, nor does he reflect or amplify any tentative change talk. Instead, the focus is on telling the patient what to do, which likely suppresses any emerging motivation.

**Summary:**  
No skillful evocation or reinforcement of change talk is present; the psychiatrist’s approach likely stifles motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently confronts and challenges sustain talk rather than softening or exploring it. The patient’s statements about peer influence, lack of readiness, and skepticism are met with accusations of “making excuses” and demands to “quit immediately.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s approach likely intensifies sustain talk and resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften sustain talk, instead escalating resistance through confrontation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting the patient’s input or perspective. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of orders rather than an active participant in his own change process. The psychiatrist does not negotiate agenda, share power, or acknowledge the patient’s expertise about his own life.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks any meaningful partnership; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the confrontational, directive style and adopt a respectful, collaborative stance.  
- Empathy and reflective listening are critical to build rapport and understand the patient’s perspective.  
- The spirit of MI—partnership, evocation, and autonomy support—must be central to the approach.  
- The psychiatrist should skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk while softening sustain talk.  
- The patient must be engaged as an equal partner in the change process.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behaviors (smoking and drinking).

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly confrontational, directive, and lacks empathy.  
- There is no evidence of MI spirit or techniques such as reflective listening, affirmations, or evoking change talk.  
- The patient’s resistance and sustain talk are met with escalation rather than softening.  
- The psychiatrist fails to engage the patient as a partner or support autonomy.  
- The session is unlikely to foster motivation or behavior change in its current form.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from telling and directing to partnering and inviting the patient’s perspective.  
   - Use inclusive language and avoid ultimatums.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Reflect the patient’s statements and feelings to show understanding.  
   - Validate ambivalence and resistance as normal parts of change.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage the patient to explore and articulate their own reasons for change.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses any desire, ability, or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and avoid confronting or challenging sustain talk directly.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s right to choose and control their own change process.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, patient-driven goals and next steps.

8. **Avoid Confrontation:**  
   - Replace judgmental or accusatory language with curiosity and respect.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session falls far short of motivational interviewing standards. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient, and likely increases resistance. To be effective, the psychiatrist must fundamentally change approach to embody the spirit and techniques of MI, fostering collaboration, empathy, and evocation of the patient’s own motivations for change.
