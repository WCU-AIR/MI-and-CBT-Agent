# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Asthma Management Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a judgmental, authoritarian, and directive stance throughout the session. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s fears and concerns as “excuses” or “stalling,” which is likely to evoke resistance rather than collaboration. The patient’s expressions of anxiety and ambivalence are met with reprimands and commands rather than empathy or validation. This approach is contrary to the acceptance criterion, which requires respect and support regardless of the patient’s ambivalence or fears.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate acceptance, instead adopting a confrontational and dismissive tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not acknowledge or explore the patient’s fears about side effects or the emotional difficulty of managing asthma. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with directives and challenges the patient’s feelings as invalid or irrelevant. The patient’s nuanced ambivalence and anxiety are ignored rather than understood. Empathy requires accurate perspective-taking and conveying understanding, which is absent here.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective, missing critical opportunities to build rapport and trust.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely lacking. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly ordering the patient what to do and refusing to engage in dialogue or negotiation. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values, no support for autonomy, and no partnership. The psychiatrist explicitly states “I’m not here to debate or negotiate,” which directly contradicts MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by a directive, controlling approach that negates the core MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient expresses some willingness and desire to manage asthma better, the psychiatrist ignores these cues and instead focuses on commands and reprimands. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s expressed motivations or tentative commitments. The psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses change talk rather than cultivates it.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the psychiatrist’s style likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s fears, doubts, and ambivalence—is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s concerns as “excuses” and “stalling,” which reinforces resistance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate the patient’s experience while gently guiding toward change. The psychiatrist’s approach escalates sustain talk rather than softens it.

**Summary:**  
No evidence of softening sustain talk; instead, sustain talk is met with confrontation and invalidation.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a hierarchical, expert-driven stance throughout, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without invitation or collaboration. The patient is not treated as an equal partner in decision-making. The psychiatrist explicitly states refusal to negotiate or debate, which further entrenches the power imbalance. Partnership is a fundamental MI principle that is entirely absent here.

**Summary:**  
No partnership or collaboration; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and excludes the patient from shared decision-making.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None identified. The psychiatrist’s approach is inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must develop skills in acceptance, empathy, and reflective listening.  
- Replace confrontational commands with open questions and reflections that explore patient ambivalence.  
- Support patient autonomy by inviting collaboration and respecting the patient’s pace and concerns.  
- Actively evoke and reinforce change talk rather than suppress it.  
- Use affirmations to recognize patient strengths and efforts.  
- Employ strategic softening of sustain talk to reduce resistance.  
- Collaboratively develop plans with patient input and agreement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Supportive Stance:**  
   Avoid labeling patient concerns as “excuses” or “stalling.” Instead, validate fears and acknowledge the difficulty of managing chronic illness.

2. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and encourage deeper exploration of ambivalence.

3. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   Invite the patient to share their thoughts and feelings rather than asking yes/no or leading questions.

4. **Support Autonomy and Collaboration:**  
   Invite the patient to participate in decision-making and respect their readiness to change.

5. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses desire or willingness, reflect and reinforce these statements to build motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and affirm the legitimacy of patient concerns while gently guiding toward change.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Commands:**  
   Replace directives with invitations and suggestions, and seek permission before offering advice.

8. **Develop Collaborative Plans:**  
   Work with the patient to create achievable, agreed-upon steps rather than imposing a plan.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivations, and likely increases resistance. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically change approach to embody acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and evocation. Without these changes, patient outcomes and engagement will be compromised.
