# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist repeatedly adopts a judgmental, authoritarian, and confrontational stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is directive and dismissive of the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist invalidates the patient’s ambivalence and repeatedly labels the patient’s concerns as “excuses” or “negotiating,” which is highly disempowering and disrespectful. There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s attempts to express their feelings and limits are met with blunt commands and pressure rather than acceptance. This is the opposite of the acceptance criterion and risks alienating the patient.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or positive regard, instead adopting a harsh, judgmental tone that undermines rapport and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal to no effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses ambivalence, concerns about energy, and a desire for gradual change, but the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these cues. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or struggles. Instead, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with commands and challenges the patient’s statements as “excuses.” This lack of empathy likely contributes to the patient’s guarded and polite but resistant responses.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist does not demonstrate reflective listening or perspective-taking, missing critical opportunities to build understanding and connection.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of motivational interviewing—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, directive stance, repeatedly telling the patient what to do without invitation or collaboration. The patient’s autonomy is ignored; the psychiatrist demands immediate, full commitment and rejects any negotiation or gradual approach. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values, only insistence on compliance. This approach is antithetical to MI principles.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is controlling and coercive, lacking collaboration, evocation, or respect for autonomy.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient offers several statements indicating willingness to consider change and ambivalence about how to proceed, the psychiatrist ignores these and instead focuses on demanding compliance. There are no reflections or affirmations of the patient’s change talk, no strategic amplification, and no exploration of the patient’s reasons or confidence. The psychiatrist’s repeated commands likely suppress further change talk.

**Summary:**  
No effective evocation or reinforcement of change talk; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits patient motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften or skillfully respond to sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is labeled as “excuses” and met with confrontation and pressure. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to validate the patient’s ambivalence. The psychiatrist’s responses escalate resistance rather than reducing sustain talk’s impact.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal, exacerbating resistance rather than softening it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly insists on unilateral commands and rejects any patient input or negotiation. The patient’s expertise about their own readiness and limits is ignored. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to establish a collaborative partnership, instead adopting an authoritarian stance.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Must adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental tone.  
- Develop reflective listening and empathy skills.  
- Embrace MI spirit: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Learn to skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk.  
- Respond to sustain talk with validation and softening strategies.  
- Build genuine partnership and shared decision-making.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The patient remains polite and engaged despite the psychiatrist’s approach.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is highly directive, confrontational, and dismissive, which is counterproductive in motivational interviewing.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, acceptance, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- The use of closed questions and commands undermines patient autonomy and engagement.  
- The session lacks collaborative planning or summarizing.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Respectful, Nonjudgmental Stance:**  
   Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “negotiating.” Show unconditional positive regard.

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   Use reflections to demonstrate understanding and validate patient feelings and ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support autonomy rather than coercion.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses willingness or reasons for change, reflect and reinforce these statements.

5. **Softly Respond to Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and validation to reduce resistance and explore ambivalence.

6. **Use Open Questions:**  
   Ask questions that invite elaboration and exploration rather than yes/no answers.

7. **Collaborate on Planning:**  
   Work with the patient to develop realistic, achievable goals and plans.

8. **Avoid Confrontation and Commands:**  
   Replace directives with invitations and collaborative problem-solving.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational approach undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. To become an effective motivational interviewer, the psychiatrist must radically shift toward empathy, acceptance, collaboration, and evocation of the patient’s own motivations and goals. Without these changes, the approach risks alienating patients and reducing the likelihood of meaningful behavior change.
