# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do”—the tone is authoritarian and dismissive of the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s expressed barriers and concerns, labeling them as “excuses” and “overthinking.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s attempts to explain her situation are met with criticism rather than empathy or acceptance. This is a clear violation of MI principles and severely undermines rapport.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or acceptance, instead adopting a confrontational and judgmental approach that likely alienates the patient.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient repeatedly shares her real-life challenges—childcare demands, limited time, financial constraints—but the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these. There are no reflective statements or attempts to validate the patient’s feelings or situation. Instead, the psychiatrist pushes a rigid agenda without acknowledging the patient’s ambivalence or emotional state. The lack of reflective listening and perspective-taking is stark.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is essentially absent; the psychiatrist does not demonstrate understanding or convey that the patient’s experience is heard or valued.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, directive style with no collaboration or evocation. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to…,” “No excuses,” “This is not optional”) and pressures the patient to comply. Autonomy is not supported; instead, the patient is confronted and challenged aggressively. There is no partnership or invitation to explore the patient’s motivations or values. The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is antithetical to MI spirit, relying on control and persuasion rather than partnership and evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. While the patient expresses some willingness to try walking, the psychiatrist dismisses this as insufficient and demands immediate, total commitment to multiple behavior changes. The psychiatrist fails to explore or amplify the patient’s expressed confidence in walking or her realistic approach to change. Instead, the patient’s tentative change talk is met with pressure and ultimatums, which likely suppress further change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is neither effectively evoked nor cultivated; the psychiatrist’s confrontational style likely inhibits the patient’s motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk—patient’s explanations about childcare, time constraints, and financial limitations—is met with direct confrontation and labeling as “excuses.” The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections, affirmations, or any strategies to soften sustain talk. Instead, sustain talk is challenged aggressively, which likely increases patient resistance and defensiveness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to soften or strategically respond to sustain talk, instead escalating resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting patient input or collaboration. The patient’s concerns and preferences are ignored or overridden. The power differential is stark and unmitigated.

**Summary:**  
The session lacks any meaningful partnership; the psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance.

---

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Immediate need to adopt a respectful, accepting stance.  
- Develop reflective listening and empathy skills.  
- Embrace MI spirit by collaborating and supporting autonomy.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

### Strengths

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s style is authoritarian, confrontational, and dismissive.  
- There is a complete lack of MI-consistent behaviors such as empathy, acceptance, reflective listening, and partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and instead escalates sustain talk resistance.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Accepting Stance:**  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses.”  
   - Express genuine respect for the patient’s experience and challenges.

2. **Develop Reflective Listening Skills:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Use complex reflections to deepen engagement.

3. **Embrace MI Spirit:**  
   - Collaborate with the patient rather than dictate.  
   - Support patient autonomy and choice.  
   - Evoke patient’s own motivations and values.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When patient expresses willingness, explore and reinforce it.  
   - Avoid dismissing partial change talk as insufficient.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and affirmations to reduce resistance.  
   - Normalize ambivalence and validate barriers.

6. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, manageable goals.  
   - Summarize progress and reinforce commitment.

7. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace “You have to…” with invitations and open questions.  
   - Recognize that motivation is built, not imposed.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely decreases the likelihood of behavior change. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI techniques is essential to prevent harm and improve patient outcomes.
