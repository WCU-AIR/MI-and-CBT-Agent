# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is overtly judgmental and directive from the outset. The opening statement, “I’ll be telling you exactly what steps you must take,” immediately establishes an authoritarian tone that dismisses the patient’s autonomy and perspective. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s expressed feelings and concerns by labeling them as “excuses” and “weak willpower.” This creates a hostile environment that likely inhibits honest disclosure and engagement. There is no evidence of warmth, respect, or unconditional positive regard. The patient’s attempts to express ambivalence and realistic concerns are met with dismissal and pressure rather than acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate respect or unconditional positive regard. The stance is judgmental and confrontational, which is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates minimal to no empathy. The patient’s expressions of ambivalence, fear, and desire for manageable change are ignored or dismissed. Instead of reflective listening or validating the patient’s experience, the psychiatrist repeatedly interrupts with accusations and demands. For example, when the patient says, “I’m not trying to make excuses,” the psychiatrist responds with “No, you’re just making excuses,” showing a lack of understanding or attempt to see the patient’s perspective. The psychiatrist does not paraphrase, reflect feelings, or acknowledge the patient’s emotional state, which results in a breakdown of rapport.

**Summary:**  
There is a clear absence of empathic listening or understanding. The psychiatrist’s responses are confrontational rather than reflective or validating.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, directive stance, repeatedly telling the patient what they “must” do and refusing to entertain any patient input or negotiation. The patient’s autonomy is explicitly denied (“No, you don’t get to decide how this works”). There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values; instead, the psychiatrist imposes a rigid agenda. This approach is likely to provoke resistance and disengagement rather than motivation.

**Summary:**  
The session is dominated by control and directive commands, with no collaboration or support for autonomy. The MI spirit is completely missing.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses desire and reasons for change (“I want to be healthier,” “I’m worried about my drinking”), the psychiatrist ignores or dismisses these statements. There are no reflections or affirmations that amplify the patient’s motivation. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on demanding immediate cessation and detox, which the patient is not ready for, thereby shutting down potential change talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present in the patient’s speech but is not cultivated or reinforced by the psychiatrist.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not soften sustain talk; rather, sustain talk is met with confrontation and invalidation. The patient’s ambivalence and concerns about quitting cold turkey are labeled as “excuses” and “weak willpower.” There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. This approach likely increases sustain talk and resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist repeatedly asserts control and denies the patient any role in decision-making. The patient is told what to do with no negotiation or shared agenda setting. This power imbalance is explicit and undermines the therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian stance with no partnership or shared decision-making.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- None observed.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Immediate need to adopt a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Develop empathic listening skills to understand and validate patient ambivalence.  
- Embrace the MI spirit of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.  
- Learn to skillfully evoke and reinforce change talk.  
- Use strategies to soften sustain talk rather than confront it.  
- Build partnership by sharing power and co-creating plans.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1/5**

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Nonjudgmental, Respectful Stance:**  
   Begin sessions with warmth and unconditional positive regard. Avoid labeling or blaming language.

2. **Develop Empathic Listening Skills:**  
   Use reflective listening to understand and validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.

3. **Embrace the Spirit of MI:**  
   Collaborate with the patient, evoke their own motivations, and support their autonomy.

4. **Use Open Questions and Complex Reflections:**  
   Encourage elaboration and deeper exploration of ambivalence and motivation.

5. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   Recognize and reinforce any positive steps or intentions expressed by the patient.

6. **Softly Redirect Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance.

7. **Collaboratively Develop Plans:**  
   Work with the patient to create achievable, personalized goals rather than imposing rigid demands.

8. **Seek Permission Before Offering Advice:**  
   Always ask before giving information or suggestions to respect patient autonomy.

This diagnostic session demonstrates a profound misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, fails to engage the patient’s motivation, and likely decreases the chance of meaningful behavior change. Immediate and substantial retraining in MI fundamentals is essential to prevent harm and improve clinical effectiveness.
