# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the opening statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better”—the tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or respect for the patient’s autonomy. The psychiatrist repeatedly uses language that implies blame or failure on the patient’s part (“Hard is no excuse,” “That’s exactly the kind of thinking that’s holding you back,” “You have to just do it”). There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or normalization of struggle. The patient’s hesitations and ambivalence are met with criticism rather than acceptance. This is a clear violation of MI principles and warrants the lowest score.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate empathy or reflective listening. The patient’s expressions of difficulty and ambivalence are ignored or dismissed. For example, when the patient says, “I just can’t seem to get back in the gym,” the psychiatrist responds with a reprimand rather than exploring the underlying barriers or feelings. The patient’s hesitations and discomfort are not acknowledged or validated. There are no reflective statements or attempts to understand the patient’s perspective. The interaction is one-sided, focused on telling rather than listening.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is entirely authoritarian and directive, lacking collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands (“You have to be consistent,” “You must do that,” “No excuses,” “You have to stop all screen time”) and uses coercive language (“If you don’t take this seriously, your health will keep getting worse”). There is no invitation for the patient’s input or exploration of their motivations. The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist assumes an expert-driven, paternalistic role. This is the antithesis of MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient offers tentative change talk (“I guess I could try,” “I can try, yeah”), the psychiatrist dismisses the patient’s ambivalence and pushes for immediate commitment without exploring or amplifying these statements. There are no open-ended questions designed to elicit the patient’s own reasons, desires, or confidence for change. Instead, the psychiatrist demands compliance, which likely suppresses authentic change talk.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and admonishment rather than softening or strategic redirection. The patient’s expressions of difficulty (“It’s hard to stick with it,” “I’m not sure it’ll make much difference”) are met with “Hard is no excuse” and “That’s exactly the kind of thinking that’s holding you back.” There are no double-sided reflections or validations of ambivalence. The psychiatrist reinforces sustain talk by invalidating the patient’s experience and increasing resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is no evidence of partnership or collaboration. The psychiatrist dominates the conversation, issuing commands and directives without inviting the patient’s perspective or input. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of instructions rather than an active participant in their care. The power differential is stark and unmitigated. The psychiatrist does not share decision-making or negotiate goals.

---

### **Summary of Global Ratings:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, lacking acceptance, empathy, collaboration, or skillful handling of ambivalence. This severely undermines the potential for patient engagement and behavior change.

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is authoritarian, confrontational, and directive, violating core MI principles.  
- There is no acceptance, empathy, or partnership.  
- The psychiatrist fails to use reflective listening or affirmations.  
- Change talk is neither skillfully evoked nor reinforced.  
- Sustain talk is met with confrontation rather than softening.  
- The patient is not engaged or empowered; autonomy is undermined.  
- No collaborative planning or summarizing occurs.  
- The session likely increases patient resistance and disengagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions with open-ended questions and invitations to share the patient’s perspective.  
   - Avoid directives and commands; instead, explore the patient’s own motivations and barriers.

2. **Demonstrate Acceptance and Empathy:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and ambivalence.  
   - Normalize struggles and avoid judgmental language.

3. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - Ask open questions about the patient’s desires, reasons, and confidence for change.  
   - Reflect and amplify any statements favoring change.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence without confrontation.  
   - Avoid invalidating or dismissing patient concerns.

5. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Explicitly affirm the patient’s right to choose and control their behavior change process.  
   - Ask permission before offering advice or information.

6. **Use Affirmations and Summaries:**  
   - Recognize patient strengths and efforts, even small ones.  
   - Summarize key points to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

7. **Collaborative Planning:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop achievable, personalized goals.  
   - Check for readiness and confidence before moving forward.

---

Immediate and substantial improvement in MI skills is essential to avoid harm and improve patient outcomes.
