# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a predominantly judgmental and directive stance throughout the session. From the outset, the tone is authoritarian and lacks warmth or unconditional positive regard. For example, statements like “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do” and “No excuses” communicate disapproval and a lack of empathy. The patient’s expressions of difficulty and ambivalence are met with reprimands rather than understanding. There is no normalization of struggle or validation of the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist’s repeated insistence on “no negotiation” and “no excuses” further undermines acceptance.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely judgmental and directive, failing to communicate respect or unconditional positive regard. This risks alienating the patient and reducing engagement.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist shows minimal effort to understand or reflect the patient’s perspective. The patient expresses ambivalence, stress, and difficulty managing lifestyle changes, but the psychiatrist responds with commands and admonishments rather than reflective listening or validation. For instance, when the patient says, “It’s hard to keep track all the time,” the psychiatrist replies with “You have to just force yourself,” ignoring the emotional content. There is no attempt to explore the patient’s feelings or barriers empathetically. The patient’s fears and concerns are dismissed rather than acknowledged.  

**Summary:**  
Empathy is limited; the psychiatrist fails to accurately understand or convey the patient’s perspective, missing opportunities for reflective listening and emotional validation.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is authoritarian and directive, lacking collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues ultimatums (“You have to do this,” “No excuses,” “No negotiation”) and does not invite the patient’s input or explore their motivations. The patient’s autonomy is ignored, and the psychiatrist does not support self-efficacy or choice. There is no evocation of the patient’s own reasons for change beyond superficial acknowledgment. The spirit of MI—partnership, acceptance, evocation, and autonomy—is absent.  

**Summary:**  
The session is antithetical to MI spirit, dominated by control and directive commands rather than collaboration and evocation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
While the patient offers some change talk (e.g., “I want to stay healthy,” “I’m willing to try”), the psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce this language. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on commands and warnings, missing opportunities to amplify the patient’s motivation or explore their reasons for change. The psychiatrist does not ask open-ended questions to deepen change talk or elicit commitment language beyond forced yes/no responses. The patient’s tentative offers to try are met with demands rather than encouragement.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but poorly cultivated; the psychiatrist misses chances to reinforce or deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk (e.g., “It’s hard to keep up,” “I haven’t cut out sugary foods completely,” “I feel overwhelmed”) is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s experience and insists on immediate, total compliance. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that acknowledge ambivalence while gently guiding toward change. This approach likely increases resistance rather than reducing sustain talk.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and not softened; the psychiatrist’s responses likely exacerbate resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist assumes an expert, authoritarian role, issuing commands and ultimatums without inviting collaboration or input. The patient is positioned as a passive recipient of orders rather than an active partner. There is no negotiation of agenda or shared decision-making. The psychiatrist repeatedly dismisses the patient’s concerns and attempts at compromise, undermining partnership.  

**Summary:**  
Partnership is absent; the psychiatrist dominates the interaction and excludes the patient from meaningful participation.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- The psychiatrist is clear and consistent in messaging about health risks and necessary behaviors.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is overly directive, judgmental, and lacks empathy.  
- There is no evidence of MI spirit—no collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support.  
- Change talk is not effectively cultivated or reinforced.  
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than softened, increasing resistance.  
- Partnership is nonexistent; the patient is not engaged as an equal participant.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **1.5/5** (rounded down to 1 for strictness)

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Shift from directing to partnering with the patient. Invite their perspective and preferences.

2. **Use Open-Ended Questions:**  
   - Encourage the patient to elaborate on their experiences, feelings, and motivations.

3. **Practice Reflective Listening:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding and validate the patient’s experience.

4. **Affirm Patient Strengths and Efforts:**  
   - Recognize and reinforce any positive intentions or attempts the patient makes.

5. **Evoke Change Talk:**  
   - Explore the patient’s own reasons for change and reinforce their motivation.

6. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

7. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize the patient’s right and ability to make choices about their health.

8. **Collaboratively Develop a Plan:**  
   - Work with the patient to set realistic, achievable goals and strategies.

9. **Summarize Key Points:**  
   - Periodically summarize the patient’s statements and motivation to reinforce commitment.

10. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
    - Replace commands with invitations and support to foster engagement.
