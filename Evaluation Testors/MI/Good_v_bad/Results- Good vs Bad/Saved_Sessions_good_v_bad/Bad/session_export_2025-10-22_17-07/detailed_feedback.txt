## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance

**Explanation:**

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is largely disrespectful and coercive, lacking acceptance and positive regard. This is a critical failure in MI practice.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no evidence of empathic understanding or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand Marisol’s perspective beyond labeling it as problematic. **Summary:**  
The session lacks empathy and perspective-taking, which undermines the therapeutic alliance and MI spirit.

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session is characterized by an authoritarian, directive style that contradicts the core MI principles of collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist repeatedly issues commands and demands compliance (“No more brushing like before,” “This isn’t optional,” “Are you going to commit, yes or no?”). There is no genuine partnership or invitation for Marisol to explore her own motivations or concerns. Autonomy is explicitly denied multiple times, and evocation of change talk is replaced by coercion. The psychiatrist’s approach is more akin to confrontation than motivational interviewing.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is absent; the session is dominated by control and persuasion rather than collaboration and evocation.

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Instead, the patient’s minimal expressions of willingness (“I’ll try using the mouthwash”) are met with demands for commitment rather than encouragement or amplification. The psychiatrist fails to explore or deepen any tentative change talk and instead pushes for immediate compliance. There is no strategic use of reflections or open questions to elicit the patient’s own reasons or desires for change. The patient’s change talk is weak and fragile, and the psychiatrist’s style likely suppresses further elaboration.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach likely inhibits the patient’s motivation rather than fosters it.

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk
