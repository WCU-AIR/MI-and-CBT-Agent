# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is predominantly judgmental and directive, frequently dismissing the patient’s perspective and labeling his responses as “excuses.” There is little to no evidence of acceptance or respect for the patient’s feelings or views.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Minimal reflective listening is demonstrated. The psychiatrist fails to acknowledge or validate the patient’s concerns about social belonging or ambivalence, instead confronting and challenging the patient repeatedly.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is highly authoritarian and directive, with commands and ultimatums dominating. There is no evocation of the patient’s own motivations or support for autonomy.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains an expert-driven stance, issuing orders without inviting collaboration or shared decision-making. The patient’s expertise and autonomy are disregarded.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [x] 1 (none) → 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is minimal and not cultivated. The psychiatrist does not build on any patient statements that hint at awareness or concern, instead focusing on confronting sustain talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo) → 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is harshly confronted and labeled as “excuses.” There is no use of reflective or double-sided statements to soften resistance or explore ambivalence.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Uses open questions  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [ ] Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is poor due to confrontational and judgmental style. The psychiatrist asks mostly closed, leading questions and does not reflect or affirm, resulting in defensiveness.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal  
- [x] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently maintains focus on alcohol use and health risks, though in a directive manner rather than collaboratively.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
There is no effective evocation of the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change. The psychiatrist issues commands rather than eliciting patient-driven change talk.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [ ] Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
No collaborative planning occurs. The psychiatrist demands immediate compliance without negotiating or supporting patient-driven goal setting.
