# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Diagnostic Session Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a judgmental, authoritarian stance from the outset. The opening statement, “I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better,” is directive and dismissive of the patient’s autonomy. Throughout the session, the psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s expressed barriers and frustrations, labeling them as “excuses” and “rationalizations.” There is no evidence of unconditional positive regard or warmth. The patient’s defensive and irritated responses reflect a lack of acceptance and respect from the psychiatrist. This is a clear example of a judgmental and disapproving approach, consistent with a score of 1.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fails to demonstrate any genuine understanding or reflective listening. The patient’s repeated expressions of time pressure, frustration, and ambivalence are ignored or dismissed rather than acknowledged or explored. The psychiatrist does not attempt to paraphrase or validate the patient’s perspective, instead responding with commands and accusations. For example, when the patient explains the difficulty of fitting exercise or meal prep into a busy schedule, the psychiatrist responds with “You’re just rationalizing” or “No more excuses,” showing no empathy for the patient’s lived experience. This lack of perspective-taking and absence of reflective listening justifies a score of 1.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support—is entirely absent. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing ultimatums and demands (“You have to,” “No exceptions,” “This isn’t optional”). There is no collaboration or partnership; the patient is positioned as someone who must comply or fail. The psychiatrist does not evoke the patient’s own motivations or values, nor does he support autonomy or choice. Instead, the interaction is coercive and directive, which is the antithesis of MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. Although the patient expresses some change talk (e.g., “The weight is something I’d like to get hold of,” “I want to take control”), the psychiatrist ignores these statements or responds with criticism rather than amplification or exploration. There are no strategic reflections or questions designed to deepen the patient’s motivation or confidence. Instead, the psychiatrist focuses on compliance and adherence to a rigid plan, missing opportunities to build on the patient’s expressed desire for change.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist labels the patient’s explanations as “excuses” and “rationalizations,” and repeatedly insists on immediate, non-negotiable behavior change. There are no double-sided reflections or affirmations that acknowledge the patient’s ambivalence or legitimate barriers. This approach likely increases resistance rather than reducing sustain talk.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by commands and ultimatums, with no invitation for the patient to share expertise or co-create a plan. The patient’s input is dismissed or overridden. The psychiatrist’s language (“You have to,” “No exceptions,” “Are you going to do it or not?”) reinforces a power imbalance and an authoritarian dynamic, inconsistent with MI principles.

---

### **Summary of Global Ratings:**  
The psychiatrist’s approach is fundamentally inconsistent with motivational interviewing principles. The session is characterized by judgment, lack of empathy, authoritarianism, failure to evoke change talk, confrontation of sustain talk, and absence of partnership. This results in a very low-quality MI interaction that is unlikely to engage or motivate the patient.

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 1/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains focus on relevant health behaviors.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist’s approach is highly directive, confrontational, and judgmental, which is counterproductive in MI.  
- There is a complete lack of empathy, reflective listening, and acceptance.  
- The psychiatrist fails to evoke or reinforce change talk and does not soften sustain talk.  
- No partnership or collaboration is established; patient autonomy is ignored.  
- The session lacks open questions, reflections, affirmations, and summaries.  
- The rigid, ultimatum-driven style is likely to increase patient resistance and disengagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions by expressing genuine interest in the patient’s perspective and acknowledge their expertise about their own life.  
   - Avoid ultimatums and commands; instead, invite dialogue and shared decision-making.

2. **Demonstrate Empathy and Acceptance:**  
   - Use reflective listening to validate the patient’s feelings and barriers.  
   - Avoid labeling patient statements as “excuses” or “rationalizations.”

3. **Use Open Questions and Reflections:**  
   - Ask open-ended questions to explore ambivalence and motivation.  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to deepen understanding.

4. **Evoke and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses desire or reasons for change, reflect and explore these statements to build motivation.

5. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and gently guide toward change.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate the patient’s experience.

6. **Support Autonomy:**  
   - Emphasize that change is the patient’s choice and control.  
   - Avoid coercive language; instead, support the patient’s right to decide.

7. **Collaborative Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Work with the patient to develop realistic, manageable goals.  
   - Summarize key points and change talk to reinforce motivation.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session reflects a fundamental misunderstanding and misapplication of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, engagement, and motivation. Without significant changes to adopt a collaborative, empathetic, and autonomy-supportive approach, the psychiatrist risks alienating patients and reducing the effectiveness of interventions. Immediate training and supervision in MI techniques are strongly recommended.
