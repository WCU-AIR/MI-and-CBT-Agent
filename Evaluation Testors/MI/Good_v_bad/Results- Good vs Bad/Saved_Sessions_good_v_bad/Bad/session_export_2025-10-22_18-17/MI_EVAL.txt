# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
- [ ] High: consistently respectful and supportive  
- [x] Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist adopts a judgmental and directive tone from the start, repeatedly challenging the patient’s efforts and labeling explanations as “excuses.” There is minimal warmth or validation of the patient’s struggle.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
- [ ] High: active reflective listening  
- [x] Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Empathy is limited; the psychiatrist rarely reflects or validates the patient’s feelings or ambivalence, instead responding with blunt commands and dismissive remarks.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
- [ ] High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
- [x] Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The session is dominated by an authoritarian, prescriptive style with frequent commands and ultimatums, lacking collaboration, evocation, or support for autonomy.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
- [ ] High: minimizes power differential  
- [x] Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist controls the interaction, issuing directives without inviting patient input or collaboration, minimizing the patient’s role as an active partner.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
- [ ] 1 (none)  
- [x] 2 (minimal, inconsistent)  
- [ ] 3  
- [ ] 4  
- [ ] 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is present but not reinforced or amplified. The psychiatrist often challenges or dismisses patient statements of progress or desire to change, limiting deeper exploration.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
- [x] 1 (reinforces status quo)  
- [ ] 2  
- [ ] 3  
- [ ] 4  
- [ ] 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk and ambivalence are met with confrontation and dismissal, labeled as “excuses” or “procrastination,” which likely increases patient resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
- [x] Uses open questions (limited)  
- [ ] Reflects more than asks  
- [ ] Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Basic engagement is maintained through questions and responses, but the confrontational style undermines rapport and acceptance.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
- [x] Confirms agreed goal (quit date set)  
- [x] Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains focus on smoking cessation and quit date setting, though rigidly and without collaboration.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Explores values, hopes, past successes  
- [ ] Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
Minimal evocation of patient motivations; the psychiatrist mostly issues commands rather than eliciting the patient’s perspective or reasons for change.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
- [ ] Strengthens commitment language  
- [x] Elicits concrete steps (quit date, nicotine replacement)  
**Comments:**  
A quit date and plan to use nicotine replacement are established, but only after persistent pressure and without collaborative planning.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Draw out narrative, values  
- [x] Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Questions are mostly closed or leading, aimed at compliance rather than exploration.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
- [x] Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Virtually no reflective listening; missed opportunities to demonstrate understanding or deepen conversation.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Pulls together change talk  
- [x] Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
No summaries are provided to consolidate motivation or reinforce change talk.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
- [x] Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
No affirmations or positive reinforcement; patient efforts are dismissed or challenged.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
- [ ] Provides advice/info only after asking  
- [x] Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
Advice and directives are given without permission, often as ultimatums or commands.

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflection to Question ratio is low; more questions than reflections.  
- Sustain talk is confronted rather than softened.  
- Autonomy is not affirmed; patient is pressured to comply.  
- Commitment language is weakly strengthened, mostly through pressure.

### Empathy: (2/5)

---

### Partnership: (1/5)

---

### Cultivating Change Talk: (2/5)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is present but not effectively nurtured or amplified.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk: (1/5)

---

### Overall MI Adherence: (1/5)

The session falls short of MI standards due to confrontational style, lack of empathy, and failure to support autonomy or evoke motivation.
