# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently judgmental, directive, and authoritarian stance throughout the session. From the very first statement—“I’m going to tell you exactly what you need to do to get better”—the tone is commanding rather than collaborative or respectful. The patient’s resistance and ambivalence are met with blunt commands and dismissive language (“No excuses,” “You have to cut out fast food completely”), which clearly undermines acceptance. There is no evidence of warmth, positive regard, or validation of the patient’s perspective or struggles. The psychiatrist repeatedly invalidates the patient’s concerns and life context, which likely exacerbates resistance rather than reducing it.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist fails to communicate unconditional positive regard or respect. The approach is authoritarian and confrontational, which is antithetical to MI principles.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal to no demonstration of empathy or reflective listening. The psychiatrist does not attempt to understand or convey the patient’s perspective, feelings, or ambivalence. Instead, the patient’s statements are met with immediate challenges or commands. For example, when the patient expresses being busy or overwhelmed, the psychiatrist responds with “No excuses” or “You have to make time,” ignoring the patient’s lived experience. The psychiatrist does not use reflections, paraphrases, or affirmations that would demonstrate understanding or emotional attunement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows little effort to understand or validate the patient’s perspective, resulting in a cold, confrontational interaction.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session lacks any sense of collaboration, evocation, or autonomy support. The psychiatrist adopts an authoritarian, expert-driven stance, repeatedly issuing commands and ultimatums (“You have to,” “No more excuses,” “Are you ready to commit?”). There is no invitation for the patient to explore their own motivations or values, nor is there support for autonomy or choice. The psychiatrist ignores the patient’s ambivalence and resistance, instead pushing a rigid agenda without negotiation or partnership.

**Summary:**  
The spirit of MI is entirely absent; the approach is directive, controlling, and coercive.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not skillfully evoke or reinforce change talk. When the patient offers tentative change talk (“I guess so,” “I’ll try”), the psychiatrist ignores these openings and immediately returns to commands and demands. There is no amplification or exploration of the patient’s own reasons or willingness to change. The psychiatrist misses multiple opportunities to elicit and reinforce the patient’s small steps toward change or openness.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is not cultivated; the psychiatrist’s approach likely suppresses rather than encourages motivational statements.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is met with confrontation and dismissal rather than softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist repeatedly labels patient statements as “excuses” and demands immediate compliance. There are no double-sided reflections or attempts to normalize ambivalence. The patient’s resistance is met with ultimatums rather than empathy or gentle guidance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is reinforced and escalated rather than softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 1/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist does not establish a collaborative partnership. The interaction is dominated by the psychiatrist’s agenda and commands, with no evidence of shared decision-making or respect for the patient’s expertise in their own life. The patient is repeatedly pressured to comply without negotiation or invitation to co-create goals or plans.

**Summary:**  
No partnership is evident; the psychiatrist maintains an authoritarian, expert-driven stance.

---

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- The psychiatrist must abandon the authoritarian, directive style and adopt a respectful, collaborative stance.  
- Empathy and reflective listening are critically lacking and must be developed.  
- The spirit of MI—collaboration, evocation, autonomy support—must be embraced.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- None apparent. The psychiatrist’s approach consistently undermines MI principles.

### Areas for Improvement  
- The psychiatrist must fundamentally change approach to embody MI spirit.  
- Develop skills in reflective listening, empathy, and acceptance.  
- Use open-ended questions to explore ambivalence and evoke change talk.  
- Avoid commands, ultimatums, and confrontational language.  
- Support patient autonomy and collaborate on goal-setting and planning.  
- Use affirmations and summaries to reinforce motivation and progress.  
- Learn to soften sustain talk rather than escalate resistance.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a Collaborative Stance:**  
   - Begin sessions by inviting the patient’s perspective and expressing genuine interest in their experience.  
   - Avoid telling the patient what to do; instead, explore their own goals and values.

2. **Use Reflective Listening and Empathy:**  
   - Reflect both content and emotion to demonstrate understanding.  
   - Validate the patient’s struggles and ambivalence.

3. **Elicit and Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When the patient expresses even tentative willingness, explore and reinforce it.  
   - Ask open-ended questions about reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

4. **Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge resistance while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize ambivalence as a natural part of change.

5. **Support Autonomy and Partnership:**  
   - Emphasize that change is the patient’s choice.  
   - Collaborate on setting realistic, patient-driven goals.

6. **Avoid Confrontation and Ultimatums:**  
   - Replace “You have to” with “What do you think about…” or “How might you approach…”  
   - Recognize that pushing too hard often increases resistance.

7. **Develop Planning and Summarizing Skills:**  
   - Summarize key points and change talk to reinforce motivation.  
   - Collaboratively develop concrete, achievable next steps.

---

## Overall Score: **1/5**

**Summary:**  
This session exemplifies a poor application of motivational interviewing principles. The psychiatrist’s authoritarian, confrontational style undermines rapport, increases resistance, and fails to evoke or support change talk. Without a fundamental shift toward empathy, collaboration, and autonomy support, the psychiatrist risks alienating patients and reducing the likelihood of meaningful behavior change. Immediate and substantial training in MI techniques is essential.
