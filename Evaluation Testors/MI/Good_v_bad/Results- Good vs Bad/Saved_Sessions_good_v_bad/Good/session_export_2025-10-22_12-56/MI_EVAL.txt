# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and supportive tone, validating the patient’s frustration and time constraints. However, acceptance is somewhat surface-level and could be deepened by more explicit normalization of ambivalence and barriers.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident, including effective metaphors that capture the patient’s struggle. Empathy is somewhat formulaic and could be enhanced by exploring deeper emotional nuances beyond content.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates collaboration and supports autonomy, inviting patient input and focusing on manageable goals. The style is somewhat formulaic and could be more evocative and co-creative to fully embody MI spirit.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites patient perspectives. However, the partnership is somewhat one-sided with the psychiatrist guiding the agenda rather than explicitly sharing power or co-creating plans.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is effectively elicited and reinforced throughout the session, including desire, reasons, and need. Commitment and confidence language are present but not deeply explored or amplified.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
☑ 3 (acknowledges sustain talk)  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo) → ⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but responses are mostly neutral reflections rather than strategic softening or reframing. More skillful handling could better reduce resistance and ambivalence.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist establishes rapport despite patient frustration, using open questions and reflective listening. Engagement is solid but could be deepened with more personalized emotional attunement.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
Focus on weight and health is maintained throughout, with exploration of barriers and motivations. Agenda negotiation could be more explicit to sharpen focus.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively draws out reasons for change and supports autonomy. However, evocation of commitment and confidence language is limited and could be strengthened.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
A realistic, manageable plan is developed collaboratively, respecting patient constraints. The plan could be more explicitly consolidated with a summary and commitment check.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Several open-ended questions invite elaboration and reflection, though some are somewhat formulaic.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are frequent and accurate but mostly simple paraphrases. More complex reflections capturing emotional subtext would enhance engagement.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☑ Pulls together change talk  
⬜ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Implicit summaries occur but no explicit, structured summaries to consolidate motivation or clarify next steps.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Some affirmations of strengths and skills are present but tend to be generic. More specific, personalized affirmations would strengthen rapport.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist generally avoids unsolicited advice but does not explicitly ask permission before offering suggestions, which would better align with MI principles.

---

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflections outnumber questions, but complexity could increase.  
- Sustain talk acknowledged but not dwelled on excessively.  
- Autonomy affirmed consistently.  
- Commitment language elicited but could be strengthened near session close.

---

### Empathy: (4/5)

---

### Partnership: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Collaborative and client-centered, though partnership could be deepened by explicit sharing of power and co-creation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk: (4/5)

Consistent elicitation and reinforcement of change talk. More active elicitation of commitment and confidence language recommended.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk: (3/5)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected. More strategic handling needed to pivot toward change.

---
