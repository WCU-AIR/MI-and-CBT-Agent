# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and acceptance throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist explicitly states a nonjudgmental stance (“I’m here to listen without judgment”) and validates Maria’s intense emotional pain repeatedly (“I want to honor how real and intense this is for you,” “It takes a lot of courage to let someone in on that”). The psychiatrist avoids any form of labeling or minimizing Maria’s suffering and instead normalizes her feelings of fear and ambivalence. The tone is warm, supportive, and patient, which is essential given Maria’s vulnerability and emotional rawness. This acceptance is sustained and genuine, creating a safe space for Maria to express her deepest distress.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary unconditional positive regard and validation, creating a safe, nonjudgmental environment for disclosure.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of Maria’s perspective. Complex reflections capture both the content and emotional depth of Maria’s experience (e.g., “That image of screaming in silence really captures how isolated and desperate this experience is for you,” “That mix of longing for relief and fear of the unfamiliar makes a lot of sense”). The psychiatrist skillfully reflects ambivalence and emotional nuance, validating Maria’s hopelessness alongside her faint hope and fear. The reflections are not merely surface-level paraphrases but convey deep attunement to Maria’s internal world, which is critical in working with suicidal ideation and emotional dysregulation.

**Summary:**  
Consistently deep, nuanced reflections that validate and convey understanding of complex emotional states.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, autonomy-supportive stance throughout, inviting Maria’s perspective and emphasizing her control over the process (“this space is yours,” “what feels most pressing for you”). The psychiatrist evokes Maria’s own motivations and ambivalence without imposing solutions or directives. There is a clear respect for Maria’s pace and readiness, and the psychiatrist avoids persuasion or pressure. However, while the collaboration is strong, the session could further enhance evocation by more explicitly inviting Maria to explore her own values or reasons for change beyond the immediate relief from pain. The partnership is good but could be deepened by more explicit co-creation of goals or agenda-setting.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could more actively evoke Maria’s intrinsic motivations and co-create the change agenda.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, especially around small hopes and tentative steps toward connection (“that’s really important to notice,” “you’re already taking important steps”). The use of scaling (“On a scale from zero to ten…”) is a good strategy to quantify readiness and evoke change talk. The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies Maria’s small expressions of curiosity and willingness (“a small flicker of curiosity and a tired openness”). However, there is room to more consistently and explicitly reinforce commitment language and explore confidence or ability in greater depth. The change talk is present and nurtured but could be more strategically amplified to build momentum.

**Summary:**  
Effective evocation and reinforcement of change talk, with some missed opportunities to deepen commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges and validates sustain talk without confrontation or judgment (“I don’t know if it would change anything,” “the pain itself feels relentless”). The reflections often incorporate sustain talk in a way that normalizes ambivalence and reduces resistance (“That tension between wanting connection and feeling swallowed by hopelessness must be exhausting”). While the psychiatrist does not use explicit double-sided reflections frequently, the overall approach gently softens sustain talk by validating its legitimacy and focusing on small, manageable steps forward. There is a consistent effort to avoid reinforcing sustain talk and instead redirect toward hope and possibility.

**Summary:**  
Consistent validation and gentle redirection of sustain talk, though more explicit double-sided reflections could enhance this skill.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, frequently inviting Maria’s input and respecting her autonomy. The language used is inclusive and empowering (“this space is yours,” “what do you imagine could be a small step”). However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit negotiation of the agenda and shared decision-making about the session’s direction. While the psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven approach, there is limited explicit acknowledgment of Maria’s expertise in her own life or explicit invitations to co-create the plan beyond the final steps. The partnership is strong but not fully equal or co-constructed.

**Summary:**  
Good collaboration and respect for autonomy, with room to deepen shared power and co-creation of goals.
