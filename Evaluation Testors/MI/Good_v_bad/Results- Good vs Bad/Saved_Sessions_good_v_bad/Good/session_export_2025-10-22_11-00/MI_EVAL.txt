# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, warmly affirming the patient’s progress and struggles without judgment. The tone is supportive and respectful throughout, creating a safe environment for open sharing.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Reflections accurately capture the patient’s ambivalence and motivations, validating emotional experiences. However, some reflections are somewhat formulaic and could explore deeper emotional nuances, especially regarding discouragement and self-criticism.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting patient input and emphasizing autonomy. The session supports evocation of the patient’s own motivations and values, though at times the approach feels structured and could be more spontaneously evocative.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist invites the patient’s perspective and decisions, asking permission before advice and encouraging goal-setting. Partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of agenda and plans.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent, deep evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (exceptional)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is skillfully elicited and reinforced, with multiple statements of desire, reasons, and ability. Use of scaling questions effectively draws out confidence. There is room to more actively amplify commitment and taking steps language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo)  
☑ 3 (acknowledges sustain talk neutrally)  
⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment but mostly met with neutral reflections rather than strategic softening or reframing. More active techniques to reduce sustain talk’s impact would strengthen the session.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Rapport is strong with warm, respectful communication. Open questions invite elaboration and reflection. The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and maintains engagement effectively.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The session maintains clear focus on gym attendance and diet goals. The psychiatrist gently guides conversation toward manageable steps but could more explicitly summarize and consolidate focus.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively draws out the patient’s motivations related to health, family, and pain relief. Autonomy is emphasized. Evocation could be deepened by more actively eliciting commitment language.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
A clear, manageable plan is developed with the patient (two gym days, one diet swap). Supports and reminders are discussed. The plan could be more explicitly consolidated with stronger reinforcement of commitment.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Open questions are used effectively to encourage patient reflection and elaboration, though some questions are somewhat formulaic.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are frequent and accurate but tend to be simple and repetitive in phrasing. More complex, emotionally nuanced reflections would enhance depth and impact.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☐ Pulls together change talk  
☑ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Summaries are implicit rather than explicit. The psychiatrist could use more structured summaries to consolidate change talk and reinforce motivation and commitment.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Affirmations are genuine, specific, and supportive, recognizing resilience, progress, and thoughtful approaches.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently asks permission before offering advice or information, respecting patient autonomy.

---

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflections outnumber questions, but could be more complex and varied.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not dwelled on excessively; however, more active softening is needed.  
- Autonomy is affirmed consistently throughout the session.  
- Commitment language is present but could be strengthened toward session close.

---

## 5. Overall Evaluation

### MI Spirit (Collaboration, Evocation, Autonomy): (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support are evident. The session avoids persuasion and emphasizes client choice. Evocation is effective but could be more spontaneous and less formulaic.

### Empathy: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Empathy is consistently demonstrated through reflective listening and validation. Deeper emotional nuance could be explored to enhance connection.

### Partnership: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist works well as an equal partner, inviting input and decisions. More explicit co-creation of agenda and plans would further minimize power differentials.

### Cultivating Change Talk: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is well elicited and reinforced. Amplification of commitment and taking steps language could be improved to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk: (3/5)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged neutrally but not skillfully softened or redirected. More strategic handling would help pivot toward change.

### Overall MI Adherence: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
The session demonstrates solid MI adherence with strengths in acceptance, empathy, and collaboration. Areas for growth include deeper reflections, strategic softening of sustain talk, explicit summarizing, and stronger amplification of commitment language.
