## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on or talk about?”) to affirming the patient’s struggles and perspectives (“Thank you for sharing that with me,” “I really hear how much you’re carrying”), the tone is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental. The psychiatrist avoids any hint of criticism or labeling, instead validating the patient’s experience and ambivalence. This acceptance is sustained and genuine, creating a safe space for the patient to explore difficult feelings.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary acceptance, with consistent warmth, respect, and validation that supports patient openness.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and nuanced understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both the content and emotional subtext of the patient’s statements, such as the dual role of smoking as stress relief and a source of worry (“smoking really serves as your go-to way to unwind... At the same time, you’re noticing some of the worries it brings”). The psychiatrist skillfully mirrors the patient’s ambivalence and emotional conflict, showing deep perspective-taking. The reflections are complex and move beyond surface-level paraphrasing, validating the patient’s feelings and struggles.

**Summary:**  
Consistently accurate, emotionally attuned, and insightful reflections that deepen understanding and rapport.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist embodies the MI spirit fully, maintaining a collaborative stance and supporting autonomy throughout. The patient is invited to set the agenda, and the psychiatrist avoids directive or persuasive language, instead evoking the patient’s own motivations and ideas (“What do you imagine life might look like...?”, “What do you think it would take for you to feel ready...?”). The psychiatrist honors the patient’s pace and readiness, reinforcing choice and control (“On a scale from 0 to 10... how ready do you feel?”). The session is evocative, patient-centered, and autonomy-supportive.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support evident throughout the session.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk by inviting the patient to explore hopes, concerns, and strengths. For example, the patient’s statements about worry for her son and desire to try new coping strategies are reflected and amplified. The psychiatrist also skillfully evokes commitment language by asking about readiness and manageable first steps. However, while change talk is present and encouraged, there could be more strategic amplification or summarizing of change talk to consolidate motivation. The psychiatrist misses some opportunities to deepen the patient’s own verbalizations of desire, ability, and reasons for change.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could more actively amplify and consolidate it.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without judgment and gently redirects focus toward change. For example, the patient’s rationalizations (“some people have a glass of wine...”) and minimizations (“he doesn’t have asthma”) are met with reflective listening that validates the patient’s perspective while highlighting ambivalence. The psychiatrist uses double-sided reflections (“You’re weighing the immediate relief smoking gives you against the hope that maybe someday...”) to soften sustain talk. However, some sustain talk is left unchallenged or not fully explored, and the redirection could be more consistently strategic.

**Summary:**  
Generally effective softening of sustain talk with room for more consistent and strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a fully collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input and respecting her expertise in her own life. The session begins with an open invitation to set the agenda, and the psychiatrist consistently uses inclusive language (“What do you think...?”, “How do you feel about...?”). The patient is positioned as the expert on her own experience and the driver of change. There is no evidence of an expert-driven or authoritative approach.

**Summary:**  
Excellent partnership with clear minimization of power differential and full client-centeredness.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist demonstrates excellent acceptance, empathy, and partnership.  
- Uses open questions and complex reflections skillfully.  
- Maintains a strong MI spirit with collaboration and autonomy support.  
- Effectively evokes change talk and supports realistic planning.  
- Engages the patient warmly and maintains focus.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Amplify and consolidate change talk more actively to strengthen motivation.  
- Use more explicit summaries to reinforce motivation and plan.  
- More consistently and strategically soften sustain talk, exploring ambivalence in greater depth.  
- Increase frequency and specificity of affirmations to highlight patient strengths earlier.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When change talk arises, reflect it back with emphasis and invite elaboration to deepen motivation.  
   - Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language more frequently.

2. **Use Explicit Summaries:**  
   - Summarize key change talk and plans clearly to consolidate motivation and next steps.  
   - Use summaries to reinforce patient autonomy and readiness.

3. **Strategically Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Explore sustain talk more fully to understand underlying concerns.  
   - Use double-sided reflections and gentle reframing consistently to reduce sustain talk’s impact.

4. **Increase Affirmations:**  
   - Offer specific, genuine affirmations early and throughout to build confidence and reinforce strengths.

5. **Consolidate Planning:**  
   - Collaboratively develop and confirm a concrete, actionable plan with the patient’s buy-in.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a high level of motivational interviewing skill, with excellent acceptance, empathy, and collaboration. The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk and supports autonomy, helping the patient identify manageable first steps. To reach excellence, the psychiatrist should more actively amplify and consolidate change talk, use explicit summaries, and strategically soften sustain talk. Increasing affirmations and consolidating planning will further strengthen the session’s impact and patient motivation.
