## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on…?”) to affirming the patient’s concerns and ambivalence, the tone remains warm, supportive, and nonjudgmental. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the patient’s feelings and struggles without any hint of blame or criticism (e.g., “It sounds like you’re carrying a lot of concern…”). The patient’s anxiety and ambivalence are met with validation and normalization, which fosters a safe environment for open disclosure. This level of acceptance is sustained consistently across the session.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a respectful, supportive atmosphere that encourages patient openness and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and nuanced understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both the content and emotional undertones of the patient’s statements, such as the tension between social pressures and personal goals (“There’s this tension between wanting to be responsible and the fear of feeling isolated…”). The psychiatrist skillfully paraphrases the patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience, showing deep perspective-taking. The reflections go beyond surface-level content to acknowledge the complexity of the patient’s situation and feelings, which is evident in statements like “Your self-compassion around ‘messing up but still trying’ is a powerful strength.” This level of empathy is consistent and enhances rapport.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is a clear strength, with reflections that accurately and sensitively convey the patient’s internal experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist fully embodies the spirit of motivational interviewing by maintaining a collaborative, autonomy-supportive, and evocative stance throughout. The session begins with an open invitation for the patient to set the agenda, emphasizing partnership. The psychiatrist consistently draws out the patient’s own motivations and ideas (“What do you imagine might be some small, manageable steps…?”), avoids directive or prescriptive language, and supports the patient’s autonomy (“It’s completely natural to feel nervous…”). The conversation is clearly patient-centered, with the psychiatrist acting as a guide rather than an expert, and the patient’s expertise and preferences are honored. The psychiatrist also evokes change talk skillfully and reinforces the patient’s strengths and self-awareness.

**Summary:**  
The session exemplifies the MI spirit, with strong collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist actively and skillfully cultivates change talk throughout the session. Change talk is evoked by open-ended questions exploring the patient’s reasons, desires, and strategies for change (“What have you noticed about times when you’ve been able to say no…?” “What do you think would help you feel more confident…?”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies change talk effectively, reinforcing the patient’s insight and commitment (“Those sound like thoughtful and realistic steps…”). The patient’s statements about wanting to avoid family alcoholism and improve birth control reliability are consistently highlighted and linked to actionable steps. The psychiatrist also elicits confidence ratings and explores ambivalence without reinforcing sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is consistently and skillfully evoked, reflected, and reinforced, supporting motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without judgment and gently redirects focus toward change. For example, the patient’s concerns about social pressure and fear of slipping up are validated and reframed as understandable challenges rather than barriers. However, while sustain talk is acknowledged and not reinforced, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly used double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and normalize sustain talk (“On one hand you want to cut back, on the other hand you worry about social isolation…”). The redirection is subtle and effective but could be more strategic in softening sustain talk’s impact.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is handled respectfully and redirected, though more explicit double-sided reflections could enhance this skill.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a fully collaborative stance, minimizing power differentials and working as an equal partner. The patient is invited to lead the conversation and set goals, and the psychiatrist consistently seeks the patient’s input and preferences (“What do you think would help you feel ready…?”). The psychiatrist avoids expert-driven advice and instead facilitates the patient’s own problem-solving and planning. The use of inclusive language and respect for the patient’s autonomy is evident throughout. The partnership is genuine and sustained.

**Summary:**  
The session demonstrates a strong, client-centered partnership with shared power and mutual respect.
