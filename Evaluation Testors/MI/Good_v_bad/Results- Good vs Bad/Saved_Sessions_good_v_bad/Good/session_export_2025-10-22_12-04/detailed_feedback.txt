# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient’s agenda (“what would you most like to focus on?”) to affirming the patient’s feelings and experiences, the psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the patient’s struggles and strengths without any hint of blame or criticism (e.g., “You’re carrying a lot,” “You’ve shown resilience”). The language is supportive and validating, which fosters a safe environment for the patient to openly share ambivalence and barriers.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a supportive and respectful atmosphere that encourages openness and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both the content and emotional undertones of the patient’s experience (e.g., “juggling your responsibilities feels like a heavy weight,” “the emotional load of wanting to protect your kids while stepping forward for yourself”). The psychiatrist skillfully mirrors the patient’s ambivalence and emotional complexity, validating feelings of guilt, isolation, and hope. The reflections are nuanced and deepen the patient’s self-awareness, showing excellent perspective-taking.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is a clear strength, with reflections that are both accurate and emotionally attuned, enhancing rapport and patient engagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input and emphasizing autonomy (“what do you think would need to change?”). The conversation is evocative, drawing out the patient’s motivations and barriers without imposing solutions. The psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritative language and supports the patient’s agency. However, while collaboration is strong, the session could further enhance evocation by more explicitly reinforcing the patient’s own strengths and motivations as drivers of change, rather than primarily reflecting back the patient’s statements. There is some room to deepen the partnership by explicitly inviting the patient to co-create the agenda or plan.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more actively evocative and explicitly co-creative to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient articulates desire, reasons, need, and commitment language (e.g., “I want to move forward,” “It sits about at a 10,” “I think I’m pretty confident about it—maybe around an 8”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, helping the patient explore practical steps and emotional readiness. However, there are missed opportunities to more explicitly highlight and reinforce change talk by summarizing or selectively emphasizing it to strengthen motivation. For example, the psychiatrist could have more actively amplified the patient’s confidence and resilience to deepen commitment.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could be more strategic and explicit in amplifying and consolidating motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk (e.g., “It feels like a mountain,” “It’s hard to ask because I don’t want to be a burden”) without judgment or confrontation. The responses validate the patient’s concerns and ambivalence, normalizing the difficulty of change. The psychiatrist uses reflective listening to gently shift focus toward possibilities and strengths without dismissing the sustain talk. However, the session could benefit from more explicit use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to further soften sustain talk and guide the patient toward change.

**Summary:**  
Effective acknowledgment and gentle redirection of sustain talk, though more strategic softening techniques could enhance this skill.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered stance throughout, inviting the patient’s perspective and avoiding expert-driven directives. The language is inclusive and respectful, emphasizing the patient’s autonomy and choice. However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly sharing power, such as inviting the patient to set the agenda or co-create the plan in a more overt way. The session is somewhat therapist-led in guiding the conversation, which, while supportive, limits full equality in the partnership.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for autonomy, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and power-sharing.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Consistently warm, respectful, and accepting stance.  
- Highly accurate and emotionally attuned empathy.  
- Effective elicitation of change talk and validation of sustain talk.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive approach.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Increase explicit evocation and amplification of change talk.  
- Use more strategic softening of sustain talk (e.g., double-sided reflections).  
- Deepen partnership by explicitly inviting co-creation of agenda and plan.  
- Move beyond reflection to more active reinforcement and summarizing of motivation.

   Confirm and negotiate session goals with the patient to enhance focus and engagement.
