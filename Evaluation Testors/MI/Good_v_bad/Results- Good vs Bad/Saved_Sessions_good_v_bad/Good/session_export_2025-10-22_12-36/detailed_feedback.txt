# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation – Jamal Rivera Session

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Jamal’s frustration and impatience without dismissing or challenging his perspective, e.g., “Thanks for sharing that with me,” “I hear you—wanting to keep things straightforward.” The psychiatrist avoids labeling or moralizing about marijuana use or possession, which is appropriate given Jamal’s defensive stance. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and lacks warmth or explicit expressions of unconditional positive regard that might help build deeper rapport with a resistant adolescent. There is no explicit normalization of Jamal’s feelings or situation, which could have enhanced acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive but somewhat formulaic and lacking deeper warmth or normalization.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate but somewhat surface-level understanding of Jamal’s perspective. They reflect content and some feelings (“It sounds like efficiency and fairness are really important to you,” “You want to keep control and not have this feel like a bigger deal”), but these reflections are mostly paraphrasing rather than deep empathic reflections that capture underlying emotions such as frustration, defensiveness, or fear of loss of autonomy. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Jamal’s emotional experience more fully or validate his guardedness and impatience. The reflections do not consistently convey a nuanced understanding of Jamal’s internal world.

**Summary:**  
Basic perspective-taking is present but lacks emotional depth and nuance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Jamal’s input and avoiding overt persuasion or confrontation. They support Jamal’s autonomy by acknowledging his control and preferences (“It sounds like maintaining your autonomy and avoiding extra complications are key priorities”). However, the session is largely focused on compliance and procedural navigation rather than evoking Jamal’s own motivations or values for change. The psychiatrist does not actively evoke Jamal’s reasons for change beyond compliance or explore ambivalence meaningfully. The spirit is moderate partnership but lacks strong evocation and deeper collaboration.

**Summary:**  
Collaborative and autonomy-supportive but limited evocation and exploration of intrinsic motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
Change talk from Jamal is minimal and mostly limited to compliance (“I just wanna get whatever I need to get done, done,” “I’m good at shutting out the noise and just doing what’s needed”). The psychiatrist makes some attempts to elicit confidence and past successes (“Thinking back, have there been times…?”), but these are met with sustain talk or minimal elaboration. The psychiatrist does not strategically amplify or reinforce change talk, nor do they explore desire, reasons, or need for change beyond procedural compliance. There is no elicitation of commitment language or exploration of Jamal’s values or goals beyond “not messing up.”

**Summary:**  
Minimal evocation and reinforcement of change talk; missed opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is frequent and strong (“It’s not a lot though,” “I’m not trying to change or anything,” “I don’t need anyone telling me I gotta change”). The psychiatrist acknowledges this sustain talk but primarily reflects it neutrally without actively softening or redirecting it. There are no double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to gently shift Jamal’s perspective. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to normalize ambivalence or validate Jamal’s resistance while guiding toward change. Sustain talk remains largely unchallenged and unsoftened.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Jamal’s input and preferences, using inclusive language and avoiding an expert-driven stance (“What do you think would help you get through this?” “What support or resources do you think would help you?”). Jamal is treated as an active participant in the process. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial, focusing on procedural compliance rather than co-creating a meaningful agenda or plan that reflects Jamal’s values or goals. The power differential remains implicit, with the psychiatrist largely guiding the session toward compliance rather than shared decision-making.

**Summary:**  
Good collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit power-sharing and co-creation.

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflective Listening:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture Jamal’s emotions, ambivalence, and underlying values. Reflect not just content but feelings and meaning.

2. **Amplify and Reinforce Change Talk:**  
   When Jamal expresses any change talk, reflect and amplify it. Strategically evoke desire, reasons, need, ability, and commitment language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.

4. **Enhance Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations that recognize Jamal’s strengths, past successes, and autonomy.

5. **Strengthen Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize change talk and collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan. Reinforce commitment and check for buy-in.

6. **Increase Emotional Engagement:**  
   Validate Jamal’s frustration and resistance more explicitly. Use empathy to build rapport and reduce defensiveness.

7. **Explore Values and Ambivalence:**  
   Move beyond procedural compliance to explore Jamal’s values, goals, and ambivalence about marijuana use and the justice process.

8. **Offer Information with Permission:**  
   When appropriate, ask permission before offering advice or information to maintain collaboration and respect autonomy.
