## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Rowan’s resistance and frustration without criticism, for example, “I appreciate you being open to exploring this a bit more,” and “I really appreciate your honesty.” The psychiatrist validates the patient’s feelings about the hassle and competing priorities, which supports acceptance. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard that might help soften Rowan’s defensiveness further. There is no explicit normalization of resistance or ambivalence, which could have enhanced acceptance.  

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive throughout, but could deepen warmth and unconditional positive regard to better engage a resistant patient.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**

There is a missed opportunity to explore Rowan’s irritability or resistance more deeply to build rapport and trust.  

**Summary:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, but reflections could be more nuanced and emotionally attuned.

### 3. Spirit of MI

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and personalized to deepen motivation.

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk

**Explanation:**

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but lacks consistent reinforcement and amplification to build momentum.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk (e.g., “I don’t wanna charge it,” “It’s a pain,” “I’m not promising anything fancy”) without judgment or confrontation. However, the responses are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly validate the legitimacy of Rowan’s resistance while gently guiding toward change. There is little effort to normalize ambivalence or reduce the impact of sustain talk beyond simple acknowledgment.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed.

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Rowan’s perspective and preferences, such as asking what to focus on and how to provide support. The language is inclusive and collaborative (“What do you think would help you…?” “How would you like me to support you?”). This reduces power differential and respects Rowan’s autonomy. **Summary:**  
Good collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit power sharing.

### Areas for Improvement

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feelings and meaning to deepen engagement.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it to build momentum. Use strategic questions to evoke commitment and confidence language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Validate resistance as understandable but explore ways to reduce its impact.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing Rowan’s strengths, past successes, and efforts. Avoid generic praise.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize change talk and collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan. Reinforce commitment and check for buy-in.

6. **Increase Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out Rowan’s own motivations, values, and ideas for change. Share power by explicitly inviting Rowan to co-create the agenda and plan.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive or scripted phrasing. Be present and responsive to the unique flow of the conversation.

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing, with consistent respect, empathy, and collaboration. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, lack of depth in reflections, insufficient amplification of change talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To reach a higher level of MI proficiency, the psychiatrist must deepen emotional attunement, skillfully handle ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce the patient’s own motivations and strengths.
