# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Session with Mateo Quiñones

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect and nonjudgmental acceptance toward Mateo. Early in the session, the psychiatrist validates Mateo’s pain and discouragement without minimizing or dismissing it (“It sounds like the pain, especially in your back, is really weighing on you…”). The tone remains supportive and warm throughout, affirming Mateo’s efforts and struggles. However, the acceptance, while solid, occasionally feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper expressions of unconditional positive regard or explicit normalization of Mateo’s ambivalence and frustration. For example, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly normalized the difficulty of rehab progress or the commonality of pain-related discouragement to deepen acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Generally respectful and supportive, but could enhance warmth and normalization to reach the highest level.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Mateo’s emotional state and ambivalence. Reflections such as “That mix of hope and frustration must be really heavy to carry” show an attempt to understand Mateo’s internal experience. The psychiatrist also acknowledges the tension between wanting change and feeling worn down. However, some reflections remain surface-level and do not consistently delve into the emotional nuance or deeper meaning behind Mateo’s statements. For instance, the psychiatrist could have explored Mateo’s feelings of discouragement or resignation more deeply, or reflected on the emotional impact of his pain beyond the factual level.

**Summary:**  
Good understanding and reflective listening, but reflections could be more emotionally nuanced and deeper.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Mateo’s perspective and supporting autonomy (“What do you think has helped you keep going so far?” “What do you imagine might be different for you?”). There is no evidence of directive or authoritarian behavior. The psychiatrist evokes Mateo’s own motivations and values, such as his desire for independence and responsibility toward his wife. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat formulaic and lacks a fully evocative style that draws out Mateo’s intrinsic motivations more dynamically. The psychiatrist could have more explicitly reinforced Mateo’s agency and co-created the plan with him, rather than primarily guiding the conversation.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and co-creative.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk by asking about Mateo’s hopes, strengths, and possible next steps. Mateo offers several statements of desire, ability, reasons, and need for change (“Maybe I could try doing some of the exercises at home more regularly,” “I guess I’m just stubborn”). The psychiatrist reflects and reinforces these statements, helping to deepen motivation. However, there are missed opportunities to amplify change talk more strategically or to explicitly elicit commitment language beyond the numeric confidence ratings. For example, the psychiatrist could have asked more about Mateo’s confidence or readiness to commit to the home exercise plan.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could more actively amplify and elicit commitment.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in Mateo’s expressions of pain, discouragement, and fear of worsening his condition (“It’s hard to stay confident when everything hurts,” “I don’t want to end up worse off”). The psychiatrist acknowledges these concerns empathetically but generally responds with neutral reflections rather than actively softening or redirecting sustain talk. There is little use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to gently shift Mateo’s focus toward change while validating his concerns. This limits the effectiveness of managing ambivalence and reducing the impact of sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Acknowledges sustain talk but lacks skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative and client-centered approach, frequently inviting Mateo’s input and avoiding an expert-driven stance. Language such as “What do you think would help you feel comfortable and confident trying this at home?” supports partnership. However, the collaboration is somewhat one-sided, with the psychiatrist leading the agenda and the plan development. There is limited explicit sharing of power or co-creation of the plan beyond eliciting Mateo’s ideas. Greater emphasis on joint decision-making and explicit negotiation of goals would strengthen partnership.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened through more explicit co-creation.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, empathetic, and supportive stance.  
- Good use of open questions and reflective listening to explore Mateo’s experience.  
- Effectively evokes change talk and supports autonomy.  
- Maintains collaboration and client engagement.  

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections often lack emotional depth and complexity; more complex reflections needed.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; use double-sided reflections and reframing.  
- Affirmations are generic; increase specificity and personalization.  
- Explicitly ask permission before offering advice or information.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak; consolidate and check commitment to next steps.  
- Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit co-creation and power sharing.  
- Avoid formulaic questions and responses; personalize and deepen evocation.  

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:** Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not just content but emotional experience.  
2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:** Employ double-sided reflections and gentle reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence.  
3. **Amplify Change Talk:** When change talk arises, reflect and reinforce it strategically. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.  
4. **Strengthen Affirmations:** Offer specific, genuine affirmations that recognize Mateo’s unique strengths, efforts, and values. Avoid generic praise.  
5. **Explicitly Ask Permission:** Before giving advice or information, ask permission to respect autonomy and collaboration.  
6. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:** Provide clear, structured summaries that consolidate motivation and change talk. Collaboratively develop and check commitment to a concrete plan.  
7. **Increase Partnership:** Share power more explicitly by co-creating goals and plans with Mateo. Invite him to lead agenda setting.  
8. **Personalize and Evoke More Deeply:** Avoid formulaic questions and responses. Tailor evocation to Mateo’s unique situation and emotional state.  

---

## Overall Score: **3.5/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing with consistent respect, empathy, and collaboration. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, limited emotional depth in reflections, insufficient handling of sustain talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully manage ambivalence, amplify change talk more strategically, and more explicitly co-create plans with the client. These improvements will enhance the motivational interviewing spirit and effectiveness in supporting Mateo’s rehab progress.
