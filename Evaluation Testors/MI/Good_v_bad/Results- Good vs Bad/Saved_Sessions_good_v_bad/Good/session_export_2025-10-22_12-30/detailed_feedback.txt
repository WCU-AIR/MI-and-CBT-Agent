# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and supportive tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Rafael’s difficulties without judgment, using affirming language such as “Thank you for sharing that with me,” and “That’s really important.” The psychiatrist avoids labeling or disapproval and consistently validates Rafael’s experience of tiredness and busyness as barriers. However, the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard. For example, the repeated phrase “I do want to… but I just can’t seem to…” from Rafael is acknowledged but not explicitly normalized or embraced as a natural part of ambivalence. The psychiatrist could improve by more explicitly normalizing struggle and reinforcing unconditional acceptance.  

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive, but somewhat mechanical and lacking deeper warmth or normalization of ambivalence.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. They accurately capture Rafael’s ambivalence and barriers, e.g., “your energy feels pretty drained,” “daily demands feel like a heavy fog,” and “caught between wanting to be active and the tough reality.” These reflections show understanding of both content and emotional undertones. However, the reflections tend to be somewhat repetitive and occasionally surface-level, often restating Rafael’s words without deeper exploration of emotional nuance or underlying values. For instance, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Rafael’s discouragement or frustration more deeply, or to reflect on the emotional impact of his repeated sustain talk.  

**Summary:**  
Good understanding and reflective listening, but reflections could be more nuanced and emotionally attuned.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Rafael’s input and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would need to change…?”, “What do you imagine might be a next small step?”). There is no evidence of directive or authoritarian behavior. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks a fully evocative style. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Rafael’s own motivations or values beyond surface-level statements. The repeated use of similar phrases (“I do want to… but I just can’t seem to…”) is acknowledged but not deeply explored or evoked. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explicitly reinforce Rafael’s agency or to co-create the agenda and plan in a more equal partnership.  

**Summary:**  
Collaborative and autonomy-supportive but lacks depth in evocation and partnership; somewhat formulaic.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by asking about past successes (“What helped you then?”), confidence (“On a scale from 0 to 10…”), and next steps (“What do you imagine might be a next small step?”). Rafael offers several statements of desire and ability. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify this change talk. For example, when Rafael says, “Maybe I could try going to the gym just once a week,” the psychiatrist acknowledges it but does not strategically reflect or amplify this commitment language. The psychiatrist also misses chances to explore Rafael’s strengths or reasons for change more deeply. The evocation is present but shallow and inconsistent.  

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but missed opportunities to deepen, amplify, and reinforce commitment language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges Rafael’s sustain talk (e.g., “I just can’t seem to get back in the gym,” “I feel too tired”), but responses are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly validate the legitimacy of sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. For example, the repeated sustain talk phrase is reflected but not reframed or softened. There is little evidence of helping Rafael resolve ambivalence or minimize the impact of sustain talk.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; mostly neutral reflections.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist invites Rafael’s perspective and ideas, maintaining a collaborative tone. However, the partnership feels somewhat superficial and formulaic. The psychiatrist does not explicitly share power or co-create the agenda or plan in a way that would demonstrate full equality. The session lacks explicit negotiation of goals or explicit acknowledgment of Rafael’s expertise in his own life. The repeated use of similar phrases without deeper personalization reduces the sense of genuine partnership.  

**Summary:**  
Basic collaboration present but lacks depth and explicit co-creation; partnership feels formulaic.
