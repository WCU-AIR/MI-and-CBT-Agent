# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation – Medication Resistance Session with Samir Haddad

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. Early affirmations such as “Thank you for sharing that honestly” and “I really appreciate how clearly you’re holding both those concerns in mind” demonstrate acceptance of the patient’s guarded and resistant stance. The psychiatrist avoids labeling or overt judgment and validates the patient’s ambivalence and humor. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or personalized. For example, the psychiatrist’s responses occasionally lean toward formulaic reflections rather than fully embracing the patient’s emotional experience. There is no explicit normalization of resistance or struggle with medication adherence, which would have strengthened the acceptance.  

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive stance is maintained, but the acceptance lacks the depth and warmth that would fully convey unconditional positive regard.  

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and attempts to understand the patient’s perspective, as seen in reflections like “It sounds like you’re feeling a bit uncertain…” and “That sounds really tough—feeling caught between wanting relief and fearing the potential downsides.” These reflections capture some of the patient’s ambivalence and emotional conflict. However, the empathy is somewhat surface-level and does not consistently delve into the emotional nuance or underlying fears. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore the patient’s guardedness and humor as protective mechanisms or to validate the emotional toll of medication resistance more deeply. The reflections often paraphrase content but do not consistently capture the emotional subtext or complexity of the patient’s experience.  

**Summary:**  
Adequate perspective-taking is present but lacks consistent depth and emotional attunement. More nuanced and complex reflections are needed to fully convey empathy.  

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input (“What do you think would need to happen…?” “What do you think might be a small, manageable step…?”). Autonomy is supported by acknowledging the patient’s right to choose (“I’m not making any promises beyond that”). However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks strong evocation of the patient’s own motivations or values beyond surface-level statements. The psychiatrist does not explicitly explore or reinforce the patient’s strengths or past successes in managing health, nor does the conversation fully embody the evocative, partnership-driven spirit of MI. The humor and guardedness of the patient are acknowledged but not fully leveraged to deepen engagement or motivation.  

**Summary:**  
Collaboration and autonomy support are present but only moderately evocative. The session could better embody the MI spirit by more actively drawing out the patient’s intrinsic motivations and reinforcing their agency.  

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as when asking about what would need to happen to feel differently or what small steps might be taken. The patient offers tentative statements about wanting to feel less tired or worrying less, and even agrees to try medication for a week. However, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to amplify or deepen this change talk. For example, the patient’s tentative commitment (“Maybe I could try… but I’m not making any promises”) is accepted without further exploration or reinforcement. The psychiatrist does not strategically reflect or summarize change talk to build momentum or explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not consistently cultivated or reinforced. More strategic evocation and amplification are needed to strengthen motivation.  

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk, such as the patient’s doubts about medication efficacy and concerns about side effects. There are attempts to validate these concerns (“That sounds really tough…”) and to reflect ambivalence. However, the responses tend to be neutral reflections rather than active softening or redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or other MI techniques to gently shift sustain talk toward change. The patient’s humor and resistance are accepted but not skillfully reframed or minimized in a way that would reduce sustain talk’s impact.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not effectively softened or redirected. More skillful handling of sustain talk is needed to reduce its influence on ambivalence.  

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites the patient’s perspective and avoids an expert-driven approach. Language such as “What do you think…” and “That fits with your way of handling things” supports a collaborative stance. The patient is positioned as the expert in their own experience, and the psychiatrist respects the patient’s autonomy. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic. There is limited explicit negotiation of agenda or co-creation of the treatment plan beyond the tentative agreement to try medication for a week. The power differential is minimized but not fully equalized.  

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for autonomy are evident, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and shared decision-making.

**Strengths:**  
- Consistent respect and nonjudgmental stance.  
- Adequate reflective listening and collaboration.  
- Some elicitation of change talk and support for autonomy.  

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Empathy lacks emotional depth and nuance.  
- Change talk is not sufficiently amplified or reinforced.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Partnership is present but could be more fully equal and co-created.  
- The session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks dynamic evocation of motivation.  

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.3/5** (rounded down to 3 for strictness)

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- Maintains respectful, nonjudgmental stance.  
- Uses open questions and reflections to engage patient.  
- Supports autonomy and collaboration.  
- Elicits some change talk and tentative commitment.  

### Areas for Improvement  
- Deepen empathy with more complex, affective reflections.  
- Amplify and reinforce change talk more consistently.  
- Use double-sided reflections and other techniques to soften sustain talk.  
- Provide more specific, personalized affirmations.  
- Explicitly ask permission before offering suggestions.  
- Develop and consolidate planning with summaries and exploration of supports/barriers.  
- Avoid formulaic responses; personalize and evoke motivation more dynamically.  

---

## Recommendations

1. **Enhance Empathy:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture emotional nuance and underlying fears or hopes. Reflect the patient’s guardedness and humor as meaningful expressions rather than just surface content.  

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it to build momentum. Ask evocative questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need for change. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.  

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence and validate the difficulty of medication adherence.  

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific affirmations that recognize the patient’s strengths, past successes, and values. Avoid generic praise.  

5. **Ask Permission Before Advice:**  
   Explicitly seek permission before offering suggestions or information to respect autonomy and enhance collaboration.  

6. **Improve Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Summarize key points, change talk, and ambivalence to consolidate motivation. Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with exploration of supports and barriers.  

7. **Personalize and Evoke Motivation:**  
   Avoid formulaic responses. Tailor questions and reflections to the patient’s unique expressions and humor. Actively evoke the patient’s own reasons and values for change.

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing with consistent respect, collaboration, and some elicitation of change talk. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, limited emotional depth in empathy, insufficient amplification of change talk, and weak handling of sustain talk. Planning and summarizing are underdeveloped. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully manage ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce the patient’s intrinsic motivations and strengths.
