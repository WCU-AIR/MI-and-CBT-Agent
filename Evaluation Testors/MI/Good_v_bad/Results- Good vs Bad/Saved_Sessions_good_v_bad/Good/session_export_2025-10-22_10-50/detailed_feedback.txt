# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient’s agenda (“what’s on your mind or what would you most like to focus on?”) to affirming the patient’s feelings and ambivalence, the psychiatrist maintains a warm, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance. Statements such as “Thank you for sharing that with me” and “It sounds like you’re aware…” validate the patient’s experience without criticism or pressure. There is no evidence of labeling or judgment, and the psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the complexity of the patient’s situation, which fosters a safe environment for open dialogue.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary acceptance and respect, creating a supportive atmosphere that encourages honest sharing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience. For example, reflections like “That tension between wanting to stay connected and wanting to be healthier feels like it’s pulling you in two directions” show understanding of the patient’s internal conflict. The psychiatrist also reflects the social importance of smoking as a “bridge” and “familiar language,” which aligns well with the patient’s descriptions. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and descriptive rather than deeply exploring the emotional nuances or underlying fears (e.g., the patient’s fear of social exclusion or vulnerability). There is room to deepen empathy by exploring the emotional impact of ambivalence more explicitly.

**Summary:**  
Good empathic understanding and reflections, but could be enhanced by deeper emotional attunement and exploration.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, consistently inviting the patient’s perspective and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would need to happen…?” “What do you imagine your next step might look like?”). The conversation avoids directive or authoritative language and respects the patient’s pace and readiness. The psychiatrist evokes the patient’s own motivations and strengths, such as recalling past successes with change and emphasizing the patient’s insight and honesty. However, the session is somewhat structured and formulaic, with repeated phrasing (“It sounds like…”) and limited spontaneous evocation or creative exploration. The psychiatrist could further enhance the spirit of MI by more actively evoking the patient’s values and desires beyond the immediate topic and by explicitly reinforcing the patient’s autonomy and choice throughout.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more evocative and less formulaic to fully embody MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient articulates desire, reasons, and ability related to change (e.g., “I want to feel healthier,” “Maybe I could try talking to a couple of friends,” “I’m pretty good at explaining how I feel”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, encouraging the patient to consider small, manageable steps and to draw on past strengths. The use of scaling (“On a scale from 0 to 10…”) is appropriate and helps quantify confidence. However, while change talk is consistently evoked, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to deepen commitment language or to explore confidence and readiness more explicitly. For example, after the patient rates confidence as a 5, the psychiatrist could have explored what might increase that confidence or addressed ambivalence more directly.

**Summary:**  
Effective evocation and reinforcement of change talk, though deeper exploration of commitment and confidence would strengthen this area.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation or judgment. The patient’s sustain talk about the social benefits of smoking and fear of exclusion is met with reflective statements that validate these concerns (“That sense of belonging feels deeply tied to those moments”). The psychiatrist uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence (“You’re weighing the comfort and ease… against the uncertainty…”). This approach softens sustain talk and helps the patient feel understood rather than pressured. However, the psychiatrist could have been more explicit in normalizing ambivalence and gently guiding the patient toward resolving it, which would further minimize the impact of sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Generally effective handling of sustain talk with reflective and validating responses; could improve by more explicitly normalizing ambivalence.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently fosters a strong partnership, minimizing power differentials and working as an equal collaborator. The patient is invited to set the agenda, express thoughts, and identify next steps. The psychiatrist uses inclusive language (“What do you think…?” “What feels most doable and right for you?”) and avoids expert-driven directives. The patient’s autonomy and expertise in their own life are respected throughout. This creates a genuine sense of shared ownership of the change process.

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership and client-centered collaboration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, fears, and values, not just content or ambivalence.

2. **Enhance Evocation of Commitment:**  
   When change talk arises, actively explore what would increase confidence and commitment, and reinforce these statements.

3. **Normalize Ambivalence Explicitly:**  
   Use statements that validate the difficulty of change and normalize mixed feelings to reduce resistance.

4. **Increase Spontaneity and Personalization:**  
   Avoid repetitive phrasing and formulaic responses; tailor reflections and questions more dynamically to the patient’s unique expressions.

5. **Strengthen Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize key change talk and collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with clear next steps.

6. **Continue Supporting Autonomy and Partnership:**  
   Maintain the excellent collaborative stance and respect for patient choice.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a strong foundation in motivational interviewing with excellent acceptance, empathy, and partnership. The psychiatrist skillfully explores ambivalence and evokes change talk while supporting autonomy. However, the session would benefit from deeper, more emotionally nuanced reflections, more active evocation of commitment, and more explicit planning and summarizing. Addressing these areas will elevate the quality of MI and better support the patient’s motivation and confidence for change.
