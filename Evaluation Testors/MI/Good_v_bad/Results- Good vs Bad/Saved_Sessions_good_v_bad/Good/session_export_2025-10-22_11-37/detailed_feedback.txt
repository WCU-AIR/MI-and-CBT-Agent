# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening, the psychiatrist invites the patient’s agenda (“What’s on your mind…?”), validating the patient’s concerns about blood pressure and smoking without judgment. The tone remains warm, respectful, and supportive, even when the patient expresses resistance or ambivalence (“I don’t really wanna quit. I mean, I like smoking.”). The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s feelings and experiences without labeling or disapproval, affirming the difficulty of change and the importance of the patient’s perspective. This is evident in statements like “Thanks for sharing that with me,” and “That’s a really important insight.” The psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence and respects the patient’s autonomy throughout.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space that encourages open sharing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both content and emotional nuance, such as describing smoking as a “personal pause button” and recognizing the ritualistic and stress-relieving aspects of smoking. The psychiatrist skillfully reflects ambivalence and underlying needs (“trust and simplicity are key for you to feel confident”), showing deep perspective-taking. The patient’s concerns about coughing, chemicals, and routine are acknowledged empathetically, and the psychiatrist’s responses validate these concerns without minimizing them. The psychiatrist’s reflections are complex and move beyond surface-level paraphrasing, showing genuine attunement.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is a clear strength, with nuanced reflections that validate and deepen understanding of the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input and supporting autonomy (“Would it be okay if I shared a bit about vaping…?”). The conversation is evocative, drawing out the patient’s own motivations, concerns, and ideas. The psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritarian language and respects the patient’s pace and readiness. However, while collaboration is strong, the session occasionally leans toward providing information and reassurance in a somewhat didactic manner (e.g., the detailed explanation of vaping risks and benefits). Although permission is asked before sharing information, the balance between evocation and informing could be more fluid and client-led. The psychiatrist could more explicitly co-create the agenda or next steps with the patient.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could better balance evocation with information-sharing and deepen co-creation of the plan.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient expresses desire, ability, reasons, and need for change, such as openness to alternatives and concerns about health. The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements (“A 3 or 4 shows you’re open to the idea…”), helping the patient articulate ambivalence and motivation. The patient’s tentative commitment to trying vaping is explored and supported. However, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to deepen commitment language or explore confidence in more detail beyond the single confidence ruler question. The evocation is consistent but could be more strategically focused on eliciting stronger commitment and taking steps language.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, with room to more explicitly elicit and amplify commitment and confidence.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation or judgment. The patient’s attachment to smoking’s ritual and stress relief is reflected and validated (“It sounds like smoking has become more than just a habit…”). The psychiatrist uses double-sided reflections that recognize both the value of smoking and the patient’s openness to change (“At the same time, there’s a part of you that’s open…”). Sustain talk is neither reinforced nor ignored but gently softened by emphasizing the patient’s autonomy and exploring alternatives. However, the session could include more explicit normalization of ambivalence and strategic reframing to further reduce sustain talk’s impact.

**Summary:**  
Effective handling of sustain talk with respectful acknowledgment and gentle redirection, though more explicit normalization could enhance this.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently fosters a collaborative relationship, inviting the patient’s thoughts and preferences (“What do you think would feel like a manageable first step?”). The patient is positioned as the expert in their own experience, and the psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance. Permission is sought before sharing information, supporting shared decision-making. However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit negotiation of the agenda and more frequent invitations for the patient to lead the direction of the conversation. The plan is discussed but not fully co-created or consolidated.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for patient expertise, with potential to deepen shared agenda-setting and co-creation of plans.
