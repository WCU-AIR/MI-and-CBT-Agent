## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect and nonjudgmental acceptance of Anya’s ambivalence and struggles. Early on, the psychiatrist validates Anya’s tension between fun and responsibility (“That tension between fun and responsibility can be really tough to hold”) and acknowledges the complexity of her social environment and values. The tone remains warm and supportive throughout, with no signs of labeling or judgment. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper expressions of unconditional positive regard or normalization of relapse and struggle that would elevate this to a 5. For example, the psychiatrist could have explicitly normalized the difficulty of change or affirmed Anya’s courage in facing these challenges.

**Summary:**  
Generally respectful and supportive, but could deepen unconditional positive regard and normalization of ambivalence.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. Reflections accurately capture Anya’s ambivalence and emotional state, such as describing her “delicate balancing act” and “powerful mix of hope and hesitation.” The psychiatrist also acknowledges the social and emotional complexity of Anya’s situation, including the role of friends and stress relief. However, reflections tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally restate content without fully exploring the emotional nuance or deeper meaning behind Anya’s statements. For example, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Anya’s nervous laughter or self-deprecating humor as expressions of anxiety or vulnerability.

**Summary:**  
Good understanding and reflective listening, but lacks consistent depth and emotional nuance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, frequently inviting Anya’s perspective and supporting her autonomy (“What do you think would be the most important reason…?” “What do you think might help you build on those successes?”). There is no evidence of persuasion or directive advice. The psychiatrist skillfully evokes Anya’s own motivations and values, linking her goals (travel, relationships, motherhood) to change. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat formulaic and could be more evocative and dynamic in drawing out Anya’s intrinsic motivations and reinforcing her sense of agency. The psychiatrist could also more explicitly share power by co-creating the agenda or plan.

**Summary:**  
Strong partnership and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and collaborative.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. Anya offers multiple statements of desire, reasons, and ability to change (“cutting up the credit cards,” “avoiding pubs,” “getting tougher with myself”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, linking them to Anya’s values and goals. The psychiatrist also explores confidence and readiness with scaling questions and elicits strategies to maintain change. However, there are some missed opportunities to deepen commitment language or explore confidence more explicitly (e.g., asking what would increase confidence from 4/5 to a higher level). Overall, change talk is well cultivated but could be more consistently amplified.

**Summary:**  
Frequent and effective evocation of change talk, with room to deepen commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in Anya’s ambivalence and doubts (“It’s just a bit of cash,” “I’m not sure I can keep it up”). The psychiatrist acknowledges this ambivalence and does not reinforce sustain talk. However, responses to sustain talk are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly normalize ambivalence to reduce the impact of sustain talk. There is limited evidence of gently guiding Anya away from sustain talk toward change talk. This is an area for improvement.

**Summary:**  
Acknowledges sustain talk but lacks skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered stance throughout, inviting Anya’s input and avoiding an expert-driven approach. Language such as “What do you think…” and “How confident do you feel…” supports partnership. The psychiatrist respects Anya’s autonomy and encourages her to co-create a plan. However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit sharing of power, such as negotiating the agenda or explicitly inviting Anya to lead the session’s direction. The collaboration is strong but somewhat implicit rather than fully equal.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could further minimize power differential.

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not just content but emotional subtext and meaning.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce the impact of sustain talk and gently guide toward change.

3. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, personalized affirmations that recognize Anya’s unique strengths, efforts, and values.

4. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Explicitly negotiate agenda and co-create the session’s direction and plan to minimize power differentials.

5. **Amplify Commitment and Confidence:**  
   When commitment language emerges, reflect and reinforce it explicitly. Explore ways to increase confidence beyond scaling.

6. **Use Explicit Summaries:**  
   Provide structured summaries that pull together change talk, reinforce motivation, and consolidate plans.

7. **Personalize Engagement:**  
   Attend to emotional cues such as nervous laughter or humor to deepen connection and attunement.
