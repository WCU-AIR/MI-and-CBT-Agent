# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and supportive tone throughout, acknowledging the patient’s ambivalence without judgment. Validates meaningful goals like retirement and staying active. However, acceptance is somewhat formulaic and could show deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing ambivalence and emotional state. Reflections validate patient experience (“That makes a lot of sense”). Yet, reflections remain surface-level and repetitive, missing deeper emotional nuances such as anxiety or fear about future health.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
Maintains collaborative stance and supports autonomy by inviting patient’s perspective and strategies. Avoids directive language. However, collaboration feels somewhat formulaic and less evocative, with limited explicit reinforcement of autonomy and agency.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
Collaborative and client-centered approach with frequent invitations for patient input. Partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic, lacking explicit negotiation of agenda or power sharing. Could better emphasize patient expertise and co-creation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk including desire, reasons, and ability. Patient articulates concrete steps and values. Some missed opportunities to deepen commitment language and explore confidence beyond a single ruler question.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo)  
☑ 3 (acknowledges sustain talk)  
⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but generally met with neutral reflections rather than strategic softening or reframing. Little use of double-sided reflections or normalization to gently shift patient toward change.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☐ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Good rapport and acceptance established. Uses open-ended questions to invite elaboration. However, reflections are fewer and simpler than ideal, limiting emotional depth.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
Maintains focus on cholesterol management and ambivalence about medication. Conversation stays on topic, though agenda-setting could be more explicit.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
Consistently draws out patient’s reasons for change and values (e.g., active retirement, wife’s involvement). Affirms autonomy but could more actively evoke commitment and confidence language.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
Facilitates discussion of next steps such as involving wife, scheduling appointments, preparing questions. However, lacks a concrete, detailed plan or explicit summary of commitment.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Uses several open-ended questions that encourage patient reflection and idea generation. Some questions are formulaic and could be more strategically placed.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☐ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
☑ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are present and generally accurate but tend to be simple and surface-level. Few complex reflections that capture underlying emotions or meaning.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☐ Pulls together change talk  
☑ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Implicit summaries occur but no explicit, structured summaries that consolidate change talk or reinforce motivation.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Some affirmations of insight and readiness are present but tend to be generic and lack specificity. Missed opportunities to affirm strengths and past successes in detail.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
⬜ Provides advice/info only after asking  
☑ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
Information and suggestions about medication and tracking tools are offered without explicit permission, missing an opportunity to fully respect autonomy.

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflections are fewer and simpler than questions; R:Q ratio could improve.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not dwelled on excessively.  
- Autonomy is affirmed and choice supported throughout.  
- Commitment language is elicited but could be strengthened toward session close.

---

## 5. Overall Evaluation

### MI Spirit (Collaboration, Evocation, Autonomy): (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support with consistent evocation of patient perspective. Could be less formulaic and more evocative to deepen engagement and motivation.

### Empathy: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and validation of patient experience. Reflections could be deeper and more emotionally nuanced.

### Partnership: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Collaborative and client-centered but partnership could be enhanced by explicit power sharing and co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk but could better amplify commitment and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk: (3/5)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling needed.

### Overall MI Adherence: (3.5/5)  
**Comments:**  
Solid foundation in MI with respect, empathy, and collaboration. Improvements needed in emotional depth of reflections, handling sustain talk, affirmations, permission seeking, and planning to reach higher adherence.
