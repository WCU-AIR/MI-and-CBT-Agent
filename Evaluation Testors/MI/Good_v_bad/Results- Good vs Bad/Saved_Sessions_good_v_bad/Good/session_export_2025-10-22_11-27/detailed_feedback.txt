# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient throughout the session. From the opening, the psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s distress without judgment (“Thank you for sharing that with me—it sounds like you’re carrying a heavy weight…”). The tone remains warm, validating, and supportive, especially when reflecting the patient’s hopelessness and exhaustion. The psychiatrist explicitly affirms the patient’s strength in sharing difficult feelings (“You’ve shown a lot of strength just by sharing this with me today…”). There is no evidence of labeling, criticism, or disapproval. The acceptance is genuine and consistent, meeting the highest standard.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, nonjudgmental environment that fosters openness and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Empathy is demonstrated at a high level with accurate and nuanced reflections of the patient’s emotional state and perspective. The psychiatrist captures both the content and the emotional subtext, such as the patient’s exhaustion, hopelessness, and ambivalence (“It sounds like the weight of hopelessness is really heavy for you right now…”; “That relief, even if temporary, seems really important to you.”). The reflections are complex and go beyond surface-level paraphrasing, showing deep understanding of the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist also validates the patient’s feelings of isolation and the difficulty of change, which is critical for this patient’s engagement.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate, consistent, and emotionally attuned empathy throughout the session.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s perspective and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would be a first small step…?”). The approach is evocative, gently drawing out the patient’s own motivations and hopes without pressure or persuasion. However, while the collaboration is strong, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, particularly in the repeated use of scaled questions and standard MI phrasing (“On a scale from 0 to 10…”). The psychiatrist could enhance the spirit by more explicitly reinforcing the patient’s autonomy and co-creating the agenda or next steps with more explicit invitations to lead the process.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more fluid and personalized to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, especially given the patient’s low readiness and ambivalence. Reflections highlight the patient’s small flickers of hope and past efforts (“That walk sounds like a small but meaningful pause…”). The psychiatrist asks evocative questions that invite the patient to consider alternatives and supports even minimal expressions of hope (“What do you imagine it would be like to have that kind of support…?”). However, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to amplify change talk more explicitly or to explore the patient’s confidence and commitment in greater depth. The change talk is present and nurtured but could be more actively deepened.

**Summary:**  
Effective evocation of change talk given the patient’s stage, but could be more consistently amplified and explored.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without judgment and gently redirects focus toward change. For example, the patient’s statements about cutting being the only relief are met with reflections that validate the difficulty while softly inviting consideration of alternatives (“That relief, even if temporary, seems really important to you.”). The psychiatrist does not confront or challenge sustain talk but rather normalizes ambivalence and validates the patient’s experience. However, the session could benefit from more explicit use of double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Good handling of sustain talk with validation and gentle redirection, but could use more strategic softening techniques.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered stance, frequently inviting the patient’s input and avoiding an expert-driven approach. The language is inclusive and respectful (“What do you think would be a first small step…?”). However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit negotiation of the agenda and more frequent invitations for the patient to lead or direct the conversation. The session occasionally feels guided by the psychiatrist’s structure rather than fully co-created.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for autonomy, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit sharing of control.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Personalize and Vary Questioning:**  
   Avoid repetitive or formulaic questions (e.g., scaling) and tailor inquiries to the patient’s unique language and experience.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect it back with emphasis and explore the patient’s confidence and commitment to change.

3. **Use Double-Sided Reflections:**  
   Highlight ambivalence by reflecting both sustain and change talk to gently soften sustain talk and promote movement.

4. **Ask Permission Before Advice:**  
   Explicitly seek permission before offering suggestions or information to respect autonomy fully.

5. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Invite the patient to set the agenda, lead the conversation, and co-create plans to deepen collaboration.

6. **Consolidate Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Use explicit summaries to pull together motivation and collaboratively develop concrete, manageable next steps.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates strong foundational skills in motivational interviewing, particularly in acceptance, empathy, and evocation of change talk with a highly ambivalent patient. The psychiatrist creates a supportive environment that encourages openness and gently nurtures hope. However, the session could be improved by reducing formulaic elements, deepening exploration of change talk, softening sustain talk more strategically, and enhancing partnership and planning. Addressing these areas will elevate the psychiatrist’s MI proficiency and better support patients struggling with complex, entrenched behaviors like self-harm.
