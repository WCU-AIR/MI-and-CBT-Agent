## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening question, the psychiatrist invites Tyler to set the agenda, signaling respect for his autonomy and priorities. The psychiatrist’s tone remains warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive, especially when acknowledging Tyler’s ambivalence and concerns (e.g., “That’s a lot to hold,” “I really appreciate your openness to trying”). There is no evidence of labeling, judgment, or disapproval. The psychiatrist normalizes Tyler’s struggle by reflecting the tension between fun and responsibility, which helps Tyler feel understood and accepted.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a safe and respectful environment that encourages open sharing.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. Reflections accurately capture Tyler’s ambivalence and emotional experience, such as the tension between social enjoyment and fear of consequences (“balancing your future and your friendships in a way that feels pretty heavy”). The psychiatrist also acknowledges Tyler’s feelings of isolation and pressure (“feeling like you might be the only one thinking about limits”). However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and descriptive rather than deeply exploring underlying emotions or values. For example, the psychiatrist could have probed more into Tyler’s fears or the emotional impact of feeling isolated.

**Summary:**  
Strong empathy is evident, but reflections could be deepened to explore emotional nuance and underlying motivations more fully.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, consistently inviting Tyler’s input and supporting his autonomy (“What would you like to focus on?” “What do you think might help?”). The session avoids directive or authoritative language and instead evokes Tyler’s own ideas and motivations. The psychiatrist skillfully balances evocation and support, reinforcing Tyler’s agency and willingness to try. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat formulaic, with repeated phrasing around “plans” and “limits” without fully exploring Tyler’s values or eliciting deeper personal meaning behind his goals. More explicit evocation of Tyler’s intrinsic motivations or values (beyond school and trouble avoidance) could enhance the MI spirit.

**Summary:**  
The session demonstrates strong collaboration and autonomy support but could be more evocative and personalized to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. Tyler offers multiple statements of desire, ability, reasons, and need for change (e.g., wanting to avoid trouble, setting limits, involving friends). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, encouraging Tyler to consider concrete steps and strategies. The use of scaling questions to assess confidence is appropriate and helps deepen commitment. However, while change talk is present and supported, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to further amplify commitment language or explore ambivalence more strategically (e.g., exploring the “scary” feelings around talking to friends in more depth).

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could more consistently deepen and amplify commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in Tyler’s ambivalence about drinking as part of social life and fear of being seen as “boring.” The psychiatrist acknowledges these concerns but generally responds with neutral reflections rather than actively softening or redirecting sustain talk. For example, when Tyler expresses worry about friends’ reactions, the psychiatrist could have used double-sided reflections or normalized these feelings to reduce resistance. The session lacks strategic efforts to minimize the impact of sustain talk or gently guide Tyler toward resolving ambivalence.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed to reduce resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently fosters a strong partnership, inviting Tyler to set the agenda and co-create the plan. Language is inclusive and collaborative (“What do you think might help?” “What would be a manageable next step?”). The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance and respects Tyler’s autonomy and expertise about his own life. The session demonstrates a clear working alliance with shared decision-making.

**Summary:**  
Excellent partnership with clear client-centered collaboration and shared power.
