# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and supportive tone throughout, affirming the patient’s efforts and struggles. Acceptance is consistent but occasionally feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or personalized.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking are demonstrated, capturing ambivalence and challenges. However, empathy is somewhat surface-level and could deepen emotional attunement by exploring feelings of frustration and isolation more fully.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist supports autonomy and collaboration, inviting patient input and avoiding directive language. The spirit of MI is present but somewhat formulaic, lacking deeper evocation of intrinsic motivations and shared agenda-setting.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
Collaboration is evident with frequent invitations for patient input and co-creation of next steps. Partnership is somewhat superficial and could be enhanced by more explicit sharing of power and patient leadership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent, deep evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (exceptional)  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reflects change talk, encouraging patient exploration of reasons, ability, and confidence. Some missed opportunities to amplify change talk more enthusiastically and explicitly elicit stronger commitment language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo)  
☑ 3 (acknowledges but limited softening)  
⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged through neutral reflections but not actively softened or reframed. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections or normalize ambivalence to reduce resistance.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
Criteria:  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Engagement is strong with good rapport and acceptance. Open questions effectively invite elaboration. Reflective listening is present but could be deeper.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
Criteria:  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The session remains clearly focused on asthma management and smoking cessation, maintaining direction toward exploring change and barriers.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
Criteria:  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist draws out patient’s reasons and confidence for change and supports autonomy. Evocation is consistent but could be more dynamic with explicit elicitation of commitment.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
Criteria:  
⬜ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
Some discussion of next steps occurs (talking to wife, setting small goals), but no strong consolidation of commitment or detailed plan is developed.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Consistent use of open-ended questions effectively engages the patient and encourages reflection.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are generally accurate but tend to be simple and surface-level. More complex reflections capturing emotional nuance would deepen the conversation.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☑ Pulls together change talk  
⬜ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Summaries are implicit rather than explicit. Clearer, structured summaries that reinforce motivation would improve the session.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Affirmations acknowledge patient progress and insight but are somewhat generic and could be more personalized.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist invites patient perspective before suggesting ideas, maintaining respect for autonomy, though explicit permission is not always clearly sought.

---

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflections are present but could be more frequent and complex relative to questions.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not dwelled on excessively; however, more active softening is needed.  
- Autonomy is consistently affirmed and supported.  
- Commitment language is tentative and not strongly strengthened toward session end.

### Empathy: (4/5)
