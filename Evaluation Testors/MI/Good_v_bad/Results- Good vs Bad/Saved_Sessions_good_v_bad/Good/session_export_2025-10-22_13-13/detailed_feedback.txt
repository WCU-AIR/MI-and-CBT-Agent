## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and warmth toward the patient. From the outset, the psychiatrist validates the patient’s exhaustion and emotional burden without judgment (“It sounds like you’re really worn down...,” “Thank you for trusting me with how hard this has been”). The tone is supportive and respectful throughout, affirming the patient’s feelings and efforts. There is no evidence of labeling, disapproval, or minimizing the patient’s experience. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the difficulty of the situation and the patient’s resilience, which reflects a high level of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, nonjudgmental space that encourages openness and trust.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both the content and emotional nuance of the patient’s experience (“You’re carrying a heavy weight with this exhaustion,” “That mix of willingness and worry really shows how much you care”). The psychiatrist skillfully mirrors the patient’s ambivalence and fatigue, validating the complexity of feelings without oversimplification. The empathic reflections are nuanced and deepen the patient’s expression, showing excellent perspective-taking.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is a clear strength, with reflections that convey deep understanding and emotional attunement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, frequently inviting the patient’s input and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would feel most helpful for you to focus on today?” “Would it be okay if I shared a few brief ideas?”). The session is evocative, drawing out the patient’s motivations and concerns. However, while autonomy is supported, the psychiatrist occasionally leads with information and suggestions that, although permission-based, could be more explicitly framed as options rather than recommendations. The partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of goals and plans.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, with room to deepen evocation and shared agenda-setting.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, exploring the patient’s hopes, motivations, and past successes (“What do you wish could be different about these nights?” “Can you tell me about a time... when you felt you were able to try something new?”). The patient articulates desire, ability, reasons, and need for change, and the psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements. However, explicit elicitation of commitment language is limited, and the psychiatrist could more actively reinforce confidence and readiness to change.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could more explicitly elicit and strengthen commitment language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s sustain talk and ambivalence, particularly the exhaustion and worry about whether strategies will work (“I’m also pretty worn out and worried that they might not work”). The responses validate these concerns without reinforcing them, gently redirecting toward hope and resilience. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly normalize ambivalence, which would further soften sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Effective acknowledgment and gentle redirection of sustain talk, with potential to use more strategic softening techniques.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s perspective and permission before sharing information. The language is inclusive and respectful, minimizing power differentials (“Would it be okay if I shared...?”). However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit negotiation of the agenda and co-creation of the plan, ensuring the patient feels fully equal in decision-making.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect, with opportunity to deepen shared decision-making and explicit partnership.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist demonstrates excellent acceptance and empathy, creating a safe and supportive environment.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive approach is consistently maintained.  
- Effective use of open questions and complex reflections deepens patient understanding and expression.  
- Permission is always sought before offering advice, respecting patient autonomy.  

### Areas for Improvement  
- Increase explicit elicitation and reinforcement of commitment language to strengthen motivation.  
- Use more double-sided reflections and normalization to soften sustain talk and ambivalence.  
- Enhance planning by collaboratively developing concrete, actionable steps with the patient.  
- Employ more explicit summarizing to consolidate change talk and focus the session.  
- Frame suggestions more clearly as options to avoid any subtle directive tone.  
- Deepen partnership by explicitly negotiating agenda and co-creating plans.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Elicit and Amplify Commitment:**  
   - Ask more direct questions about readiness and intention to change (“What would you be willing to try first?” “How confident do you feel about taking that step?”).  
   - Reflect and reinforce any commitment language expressed.

2. **Softening Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence (“On one hand you’re hopeful, and on the other hand you’re worried about whether it will work…”).  
   - Normalize ambivalence as a common and understandable experience.

3. **Strengthen Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Summarize key points explicitly, highlighting change talk and patient strengths.  
   - Collaboratively develop a clear, manageable plan with the patient’s input and agreement.

4. **Enhance Partnership and Collaboration:**  
   - Explicitly negotiate the agenda and invite the patient to co-create goals and next steps.  
   - Frame suggestions as options rather than implicit recommendations.

5. **Maintain Depth in Reflections and Affirmations:**  
   - Continue using complex reflections that capture emotional nuance.  
   - Increase frequency and specificity of affirmations to bolster confidence and motivation.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a strong foundation in motivational interviewing with excellent acceptance, empathy, and collaboration. The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk and supports autonomy while maintaining a warm, nonjudgmental stance. To reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should focus on explicitly eliciting and reinforcing commitment, softening sustain talk with advanced techniques, and strengthening planning and summarizing. Enhancing partnership through explicit agenda-setting and framing suggestions as options will further improve the MI spirit and effectiveness.
