## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect toward Elena throughout the session. From the opening question inviting Elena to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on…?”) to affirming her ambivalence and hesitancy without judgment (“I appreciate your honesty about not feeling ready…”), the psychiatrist maintains a warm, supportive tone. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges Elena’s feelings and choices without pressure or disapproval, validating her pace and limits repeatedly. There is no evidence of labeling, judgment, or dismissiveness.  

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space that encourages Elena’s openness and honesty.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Elena’s ambivalence and emotional state. For example, reflections such as “That mix of wanting to be careful but also feeling caught up in the moment must feel frustrating and maybe a bit exhausting” show an understanding of Elena’s internal conflict. The psychiatrist also validates Elena’s feelings of overwhelm and fear about change. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and formulaic, lacking deeper emotional nuance or exploration of underlying fears or values. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Elena’s anxiety or avoidance more deeply, which could have enhanced emotional attunement.  

**Summary:**  
Good empathic understanding is evident, but reflections could be more complex and emotionally nuanced to deepen connection.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Elena’s input and supporting autonomy (“What would you like to focus on?”, “What do you think would be most helpful?”). The session avoids directive or authoritative language, and the psychiatrist respects Elena’s pace and readiness. Evocation is present, with questions designed to draw out Elena’s thoughts and feelings about contraception and change. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and cautious, lacking more dynamic evocation or explicit reinforcement of Elena’s own motivations and values. The psychiatrist could more actively evoke Elena’s intrinsic reasons for change and reinforce her agency beyond surface-level statements.  

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the spirit could be more evocative and personalized to fully engage Elena’s motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as Elena’s relief at not being pregnant and her openness to learning more about contraception. The use of scaling questions about readiness and confidence (0–10 scales) is appropriate and helps quantify ambivalence. However, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to amplify and reinforce change talk more consistently. For example, when Elena expresses a 4 or 5 on readiness/confidence, the psychiatrist acknowledges but does not strategically deepen or explore what might increase those numbers. There is limited use of reflective amplification or summarizing change talk to build momentum. Commitment language is minimal and not explicitly elicited or reinforced.  

**Summary:**  
Change talk is present but not skillfully cultivated or amplified; missed opportunities to deepen motivation and commitment.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without judgment, which is appropriate. However, responses to sustain talk are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. For example, Elena’s repeated hesitations (“not ready,” “maybe another time”) are accepted but not gently challenged or reframed to reduce resistance. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or other MI techniques to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change. This results in sustain talk lingering without being effectively softened or resolved.  

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed to resolve ambivalence.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently works as an equal partner with Elena, inviting her to set the agenda and respecting her pace and choices. Language such as “What would you like to focus on?” and “What do you think would help you feel more in control?” demonstrates genuine collaboration. The psychiatrist avoids expert-driven or directive approaches and explicitly honors Elena’s autonomy throughout. The partnership is authentic and client-centered.  

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership, minimizing power differentials and fostering a collaborative environment.
