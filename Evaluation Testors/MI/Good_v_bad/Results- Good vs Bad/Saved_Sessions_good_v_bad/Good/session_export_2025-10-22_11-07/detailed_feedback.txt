# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation – Maria Alvarez Asthma Management Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for Maria’s concerns and feelings throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist acknowledges Maria’s time constraints and validates her worries about inhalers without judgment or pressure (“I hear you—you’re feeling pressed for time and a bit overwhelmed,” “It sounds like you’ve really taken the initiative to learn about your options”). The psychiatrist avoids any labeling or dismissive language and explicitly honors Maria’s autonomy and preferences. This respectful, warm, and supportive stance is maintained consistently, even when Maria expresses skepticism and resistance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space that respects the patient’s perspective and emotional state.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Maria’s ambivalence, worries, and frustrations (“You’re balancing a lot—trying to stay safe and healthy while also wanting to avoid treatments that feel risky or uncomfortable”). The reflections are generally well-tuned to Maria’s emotional state and concerns, showing understanding of her need for control and reassurance. However, some reflections could have been deeper or more nuanced, particularly in exploring the emotional impact of Maria’s frustration and guardedness. For example, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly acknowledged Maria’s defensiveness and impatience as understandable responses to feeling overwhelmed and pressured.

**Summary:**  
Good empathy overall, with accurate reflections and perspective-taking, but occasional missed opportunities to deepen emotional attunement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, autonomy-supportive stance throughout, inviting Maria’s input and honoring her control over decisions (“What do you think would be the most helpful way for us to use this time?” “Would it be okay if I briefly shared some key points…”). The session avoids directive or authoritarian approaches, and the psychiatrist skillfully evokes Maria’s own motivations and concerns. However, the session remains somewhat cautious and formulaic, with limited explicit evocation of Maria’s values or deeper motivations beyond safety and control. The psychiatrist could have more actively explored Maria’s personal reasons for managing asthma beyond fear of side effects, or invited her to articulate what a healthier future might look like.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and personalized in drawing out intrinsic motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, particularly around Maria’s openness to a short inhaler trial and her desire for support and clear information. However, these moments are limited and not consistently amplified or deepened. For example, when Maria expresses cautious openness (“Maybe around a 3 or 4”), the psychiatrist acknowledges this but does not strategically reflect or amplify this tentative commitment to increase motivation. Similarly, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Maria’s implicit desire to improve asthma control or reduce flare-ups beyond avoidance strategies. Change talk is present but shallow and underutilized.

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but lacks consistent reinforcement and strategic amplification to strengthen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges and validates sustain talk without confrontation or judgment (“I hear that clearly—you’re feeling uncertain and want some tangible reassurance,” “It makes sense that questions about long-term effects… would linger”). The use of reflective listening and normalization helps reduce resistance and keeps the conversation open. However, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly used double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and gently guide Maria toward considering change, rather than mostly neutral reflections. For example, a reflection like, “On one hand, you want to avoid side effects, and on the other, you’re frustrated by flare-ups that limit your life,” might have softened sustain talk more effectively.

**Summary:**  
Generally effective at softening sustain talk through validation and reflection, but could employ more strategic double-sided reflections.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a strong partnership stance, inviting Maria’s input, asking permission before sharing information, and emphasizing shared decision-making (“Would it be okay if I briefly shared…?”). The language is inclusive and collaborative, minimizing power differentials and respecting Maria’s expertise in her own experience. The psychiatrist avoids any directive or expert-driven tone, instead working alongside Maria to explore options and concerns.

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership, fostering equality and client-centered collaboration throughout.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist creates a respectful, accepting, and supportive environment.  
- Partnership and autonomy support are consistently strong.  
- Reflective listening and validation of sustain talk are effective.  
- Permission is sought before providing information, respecting client control.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and complexity; more nuanced reflections are needed.  
- Change talk is not sufficiently evoked, amplified, or reinforced.  
- Sustain talk could be softened more strategically with double-sided reflections.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak; the session lacks a clear, collaborative plan or summary of motivation.  
- The session could more actively evoke Maria’s intrinsic motivations and personal values to strengthen engagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feelings such as frustration, fear, and guardedness.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When tentative change talk arises, reflect and amplify it to strengthen motivation (e.g., “You’re open to trying a short trial even though you have worries—that shows real willingness to explore options”).

3. **Strategically Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and gently guide toward change (e.g., “You want to avoid side effects, and at the same time, you’re frustrated by flare-ups that limit your life”).

4. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize key points, pull together change talk, and collaboratively develop a concrete plan with clear next steps and client commitment.

5. **Increase Evocation of Intrinsic Motivation:**  
   Explore Maria’s personal values, goals, and hopes related to asthma management and health to deepen engagement and motivation.

6. **Personalize and Evoke More:**  
   Avoid formulaic questions and reflections; tailor responses to Maria’s unique concerns and emotional state to foster deeper connection.

---

## Overall Score: **3.5/5** (rounded down to 3 for strictness)

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing with excellent acceptance, partnership, and autonomy support. However, it falls short of excellence due to limited depth in reflections, insufficient evocation and amplification of change talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, strategically handle ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce the client’s own motivations and strengths, culminating in a clear, collaborative plan.
