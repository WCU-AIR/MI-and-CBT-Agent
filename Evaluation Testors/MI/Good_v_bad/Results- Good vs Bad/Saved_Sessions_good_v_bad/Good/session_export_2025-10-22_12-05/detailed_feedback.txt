# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and supportive tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Jamal’s perspective without judgment, for example, by validating his frustration with nagging and emphasizing his autonomy (“It sounds like having the space to take charge of your mornings in your own way is really important to you”). The psychiatrist avoids labeling or disapproval and consistently reflects Jamal’s statements in a neutral or positive light. However, the acceptance is somewhat surface-level and formulaic. There is little warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard beyond professional politeness. The psychiatrist could improve by more explicitly normalizing Jamal’s ambivalence and validating the difficulty of change, which would deepen the sense of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive, but lacks deeper warmth and explicit normalization of struggle.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. They accurately capture Jamal’s frustration with external pressure and his desire for autonomy (“It’s like you’re ready to take responsibility, but the way it’s brought up feels like added pressure rather than support”). The reflections often include inferred emotional content, such as frustration and the desire for control, which shows an effort to understand Jamal’s experience beyond surface content. However, the empathy is somewhat consistent but not fully nuanced. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Jamal’s emotional experience more deeply, such as the underlying feelings about his mother’s nagging or his internal conflict between compliance and autonomy.

**Summary:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, but could deepen emotional nuance and explore underlying feelings more fully.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance, frequently inviting Jamal’s input and supporting his autonomy (“What do you think would help you feel more in control…?” “What do you think could be a first step…”). There is no evidence of persuasion or directive advice; instead, the psychiatrist elicits Jamal’s own ideas and motivations. The session supports Jamal’s sense of ownership and choice, which aligns well with MI spirit. However, the interaction feels somewhat formulaic and lacks a fully evocative quality. The psychiatrist could do more to explicitly evoke Jamal’s deeper values or personal reasons for change beyond avoiding nagging, and to reinforce Jamal’s agency more strongly.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and personalized in drawing out intrinsic motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, particularly around Jamal’s desire to get to school on time and his willingness to set his own alarm. However, the evocation of change talk is limited in depth and frequency. The psychiatrist reflects and reinforces Jamal’s statements but does not consistently amplify or strategically respond to deepen motivation. For example, Jamal’s change talk is often linked to external pressures (“not dealing with the nagging”), and the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Jamal’s own intrinsic reasons or benefits of change. Commitment language is elicited but not consolidated or reinforced strongly.

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but lacks consistent amplification and deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in Jamal’s references to external pressure and annoyance with nagging. The psychiatrist acknowledges this sustain talk and reflects it neutrally (“It’s like you’re ready to take responsibility, but the way it’s brought up feels like added pressure”), which avoids confrontation. However, the psychiatrist does not actively soften or redirect sustain talk through double-sided reflections or reframing. There is little evidence of strategic efforts to minimize the impact of sustain talk or to help Jamal resolve ambivalence more skillfully.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; responses remain mostly neutral.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Jamal’s ideas and avoiding an expert-driven approach. Language such as “What do you think…” and “What would be a good first step…” supports partnership and client-centeredness. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic. There is no explicit negotiation of agenda or explicit acknowledgment of Jamal’s expertise beyond general invitations. The psychiatrist could enhance partnership by more explicitly sharing power and co-creating the plan with Jamal.

**Summary:**  
Good collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit power-sharing and co-creation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also emotional experience and meaning.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect, reinforce, and strategically evoke more. Explore intrinsic motivations beyond external pressures.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing Jamal’s strengths, past efforts, and values. Avoid generic praise.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize change talk and reinforce commitment to next steps. Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan and check for buy-in.

6. **Increase Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out Jamal’s own motivations, values, and ideas for change. Share power by explicitly inviting Jamal to co-create the agenda and plan.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive phrasing. Be present and responsive to the unique flow of the conversation.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing, with consistent respect, empathy, and collaboration. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, limited depth in reflections, insufficient amplification of change talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To reach a higher level of MI proficiency, the psychiatrist must deepen emotional attunement, skillfully handle ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce the client’s own motivations and strengths.
