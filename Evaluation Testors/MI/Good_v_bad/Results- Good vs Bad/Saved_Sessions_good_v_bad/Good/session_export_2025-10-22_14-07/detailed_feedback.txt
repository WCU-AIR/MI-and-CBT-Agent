## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**

**Summary:**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive, and collaborative stance.  
- Good use of open questions and reflective listening to explore ambivalence.  
- Patient is engaged and shares both fears and tentative motivations.  

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are often surface-level and lack emotional depth.  
- Change talk is not consistently amplified or strategically evoked.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Affirmations are limited in frequency and specificity.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak; no explicit consolidation of commitment or next steps.  
- The session occasionally feels formulaic rather than dynamically responsive.  
- Partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and power sharing.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, fears, and values. Reflect not just content but emotional experience and ambivalence.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it to deepen motivation. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and strategic reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing the patient’s strengths, courage, and insight. Avoid generic praise.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Provide explicit summaries that pull together change talk and reinforce motivation. Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan and check for commitment.

6. **Increase Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out the patient’s own motivations, values, and ideas for change. Share power by explicitly inviting co-creation of agenda and plan.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive or scripted phrasing. Be present and responsive to the unique flow of the conversation.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing with consistent respect, empathy, and collaboration. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, limited emotional depth in reflections, inconsistent amplification of change talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully handle ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce the patient’s own motivations and strengths.
