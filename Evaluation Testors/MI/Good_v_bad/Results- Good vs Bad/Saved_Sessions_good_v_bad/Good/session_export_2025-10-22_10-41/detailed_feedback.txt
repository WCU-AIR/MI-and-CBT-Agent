## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening question inviting Jordan to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on…?”) to affirmations of his struggles and strengths (“Thank you for sharing that with me,” “It sounds like you’ve shown a lot of strength…”), the tone is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the difficulty of Jordan’s situation without blame or criticism, validating his ambivalence and fears. There is no evidence of labeling or disapproval. This consistent acceptance creates a safe space for Jordan to openly express ambivalence and sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary acceptance and respect, creating a supportive environment for honest disclosure.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Jordan’s ambivalence and emotional conflict. Reflections such as “You’re balancing a lot—wanting peace of mind but also fearing what might be lost socially and materially” and “That sense of being caught between those two worlds feels heavy and confusing” show nuanced understanding. The psychiatrist also validates Jordan’s feelings of isolation and difficulty. However, some reflections, while accurate, remain somewhat surface-level and descriptive rather than deeply evocative of underlying emotions (e.g., guilt, shame, fear). There is room to deepen emotional attunement by exploring the emotional impact of peer pressure and internal conflict more explicitly.

**Summary:**  
Strong empathy with accurate reflections, but could deepen emotional nuance and explore feelings more fully.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Jordan’s input and supporting autonomy (“What would you like to focus on…?”, “What do you think would need to happen…?”). There is consistent evocation of Jordan’s own motivations and barriers, and the psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritative language. The session respects Jordan’s pace and readiness, exploring ambivalence without pressure. However, the interaction occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, with repeated phrasing (“It sounds like…”) that limits a more dynamic, evocative dialogue. The psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce Jordan’s autonomy and co-create the plan with him to enhance partnership.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more fluid and evocative in drawing out intrinsic motivation and shared planning.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. Questions about the meaning of stopping, confidence scaling, and identifying strengths (“What do you think might help you move from a 4 to a 5 or 6?”) effectively evoke desire, ability, and reasons for change. The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies Jordan’s statements about wanting relief from stress and the desire to stop shoplifting. However, while change talk is present and encouraged, there is some missed opportunity to deepen commitment language or explore specific plans more concretely. The psychiatrist could more explicitly elicit commitment and taking steps language to strengthen motivation.

**Summary:**  
Effective evocation of change talk, though commitment language could be more explicitly explored and reinforced.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without judgment, which is appropriate. However, responses to sustain talk are mostly neutral reflections rather than strategic softening or redirection. For example, peer influence and desire for stolen goods are recognized but not actively reframed or minimized to reduce their impact. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections or to gently challenge sustain talk by highlighting discrepancies or normalizing ambivalence. This limits the session’s ability to shift sustain talk toward change talk more effectively.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully but not skillfully softened or redirected to reduce its influence.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Jordan’s perspective and choices, maintaining a collaborative stance. The session begins by asking Jordan what he wants to focus on, and throughout, the psychiatrist checks in about what would feel helpful or doable. This reduces power imbalance and supports client-centeredness. However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit co-creation of the plan and shared decision-making language. The psychiatrist tends to lead the conversation with structured questions rather than explicitly inviting Jordan to set the agenda or propose solutions, which would enhance equality.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit shared planning and agenda-setting.

- Good use of open questions and reflective listening to explore ambivalence.  
- Effective evocation of change talk and recognition of client strengths.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive stance maintains client engagement.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections could be more emotionally evocative and varied to deepen understanding.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected to reduce its impact.  
- Commitment language and planning are tentative; more explicit elicitation and consolidation needed.  
- Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of agenda and plan.

- Some responses feel formulaic, limiting dynamic, personalized engagement.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Emotional Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying feelings such as guilt, shame, fear, and social isolation to deepen emotional attunement.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and gentle reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while minimizing its influence and guiding toward change.

3. **Explicitly Elicit Commitment and Taking Steps:**  
   Ask direct questions about commitment and confidence, and reinforce client statements that indicate readiness to act.

4. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Collaboratively develop a clear, concrete plan with specific steps and timelines, and use explicit summaries to consolidate motivation and next steps.

5. **Strengthen Partnership:**  
   Invite the client to co-create the agenda and plan more explicitly, sharing power and reinforcing autonomy.

6. **Avoid Formulaic Language:**  
   Personalize reflections and responses to the client’s unique experience to foster a more dynamic and engaging conversation.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates strong foundational motivational interviewing skills, including acceptance, empathy, collaboration, and effective evocation of change talk. However, to reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional reflections, skillfully soften sustain talk, explicitly elicit and reinforce commitment, and enhance planning and summarizing. Strengthening partnership and avoiding formulaic responses will further improve the quality and impact of the intervention.
