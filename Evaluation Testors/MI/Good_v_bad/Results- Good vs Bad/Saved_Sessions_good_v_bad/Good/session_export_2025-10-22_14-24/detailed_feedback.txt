## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist validates the patient’s feelings of frustration and exhaustion without judgment (“Thank you for sharing that with me—it sounds like this cycle… has been really discouraging and exhausting for you”). The tone remains warm, supportive, and respectful, affirming the patient’s insight and determination multiple times. There is no evidence of labeling, criticism, or minimizing the patient’s struggles. The psychiatrist normalizes the difficulty of change and the patient’s ambivalence, which fosters a safe and accepting environment.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary acceptance is maintained throughout, creating a strong foundation for motivational interviewing.

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective. Reflections capture both content and emotional nuance, such as the “push and pull” between health goals and life demands, and the draining nature of juggling responsibilities. The psychiatrist skillfully reflects the patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience (“That push and pull… must be really draining”). The reflections are complex and deepen the conversation, showing genuine perspective-taking. The psychiatrist also picks up on subtle motivational cues and emotional shifts, reinforcing the patient’s strengths and concerns.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is consistently strong, with nuanced reflections that validate and deepen understanding of the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist embodies the spirit of MI by maintaining a fully collaborative, autonomy-supportive, and evocative stance. The session opens with an invitation to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on?”), emphasizing patient control. Throughout, the psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritative language, instead drawing out the patient’s own motivations and values. The psychiatrist supports autonomy explicitly (“How important would you say it is…?” “How confident do you feel…?”) and reinforces the patient’s agency. The conversation flows naturally, with the psychiatrist skillfully evoking change talk and exploring ambivalence without pressure.

**Summary:**  
The session exemplifies MI spirit, with strong collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently and skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk throughout the session. Early reflections highlight the patient’s desire and reasons for change (“Wanting to find something that truly fits your life…”). The psychiatrist asks open-ended questions that invite elaboration on motivations and past successes (“What do you think helped you keep going during that period?”). Change talk is amplified and linked to the patient’s values and strengths (“You’ve already identified some great strengths…”). The psychiatrist also elicits commitment language and confidence ratings, then explores strategies to maintain motivation. This consistent focus on change talk helps the patient articulate and strengthen their readiness.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is evoked frequently and skillfully amplified, supporting the patient’s movement toward change.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without reinforcing it. Early in the session, the patient expresses barriers and doubts (“I can’t afford to take an hour to go to the gym every day”). The psychiatrist reflects these concerns empathetically but redirects focus toward manageable, enjoyable activities. While sustain talk is not explicitly reframed using double-sided reflections, the psychiatrist effectively softens its impact by emphasizing strengths and feasible alternatives. There is room for more explicit use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to further minimize sustain talk’s influence.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged and gently redirected, though more explicit softening techniques could enhance effectiveness.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a fully collaborative stance, minimizing power differentials and working as an equal partner. The session begins with an open invitation to set the agenda, and the psychiatrist consistently invites the patient’s input and perspective. Language is inclusive and non-expert driven (“What do you think…?” “How important would you say…?”). The psychiatrist respects the patient’s autonomy and expertise in their own life, co-creating goals and plans. The patient is empowered to identify small, manageable steps, reflecting a true partnership.

**Summary:**  
Excellent partnership is demonstrated, with clear respect for patient autonomy and shared decision-making.
