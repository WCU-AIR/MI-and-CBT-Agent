## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s guarded stance without judgment (“I appreciate you sharing that with me—it sounds like you’re coming in with a clear idea…”). The tone remains warm, supportive, and validating, especially when affirming the patient’s care for her son and her thoughtful approach to change. There is no evidence of labeling, disapproval, or pressure. The psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence and respects the patient’s pace, which is essential for acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Exemplary acceptance is shown by the psychiatrist’s respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating approach throughout the session.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. Reflections accurately capture the patient’s ambivalence and emotional state, such as recognizing the patient’s stress management through smoking and her motivation as a mother concerned about cancer. For example, “It sounds like smoking has become a kind of companion for you during those busy, stressful moments…” reflects understanding of the patient’s lived experience. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally formulaic. There is room to deepen emotional nuance, for example by exploring the patient’s feelings about stress or fear more explicitly.

**Summary:**  
Good empathy is evident, but reflections could be more emotionally nuanced and less formulaic to deepen connection.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s perspective and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would make that evening cutback feel doable or meaningful for you?”). The session avoids directive or persuasive language and respects the patient’s readiness and limits. The psychiatrist evokes the patient’s own motivations and strategies, such as linking past success with night classes to smoking reduction. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, lacking more dynamic evocation or explicit partnership language that would fully embody the MI spirit. There is some missed opportunity to more explicitly co-create the agenda or plan.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more evocative and less formulaic to fully embody MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient expresses desire, reasons, and ability for change (“I could maybe try to cut back in the evenings a little bit,” “Being a mother, I don’t want something to happen to me,” “I’d say about a 5” confidence). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, reinforcing the patient’s motivation and realistic goals. However, while change talk is consistently evoked, the psychiatrist could more actively amplify and explore commitment language or confidence in greater depth, for example by asking more about what would increase confidence or what commitment might look like.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but missed opportunities to deepen commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in the patient’s minimization of smoking and reluctance to discuss drinking (“I don’t smoke or drink that much compared to people I work with,” “I’m not really looking to talk about my drinking”). The psychiatrist acknowledges these statements neutrally and does not confront or reinforce sustain talk. However, there is limited use of strategic techniques such as double-sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence to soften sustain talk. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to gently reframe or shift sustain talk toward change, instead mostly maintaining a neutral stance.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites the patient’s input and respects her autonomy, using inclusive language and open questions. The patient is encouraged to set her own goals and identify strategies, reflecting a collaborative approach. However, the partnership is somewhat implicit rather than explicitly negotiated or co-created. There is little explicit sharing of power or explicit agenda-setting with the patient, which would elevate the partnership to a fully equal level.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be more explicitly co-created and power-sharing emphasized.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist demonstrates excellent acceptance and respect.  
- Good use of open questions and affirmations.  
- Maintains focus on patient priorities and supports autonomy.  
- Effectively evokes change talk and collaboratively develops a realistic plan.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are often surface-level; more complex, emotionally nuanced reflections are needed.  
- Sustain talk is not skillfully softened or redirected; use of double-sided reflections or reframing would improve this.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly co-created with the patient, including agenda-setting and power-sharing.  
- Summaries are implicit rather than explicit; more structured summaries would consolidate motivation and plan.  
- Evocation of commitment and confidence could be more strategic and explicit.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values rather than restating content. For example, reflect the patient’s fear, stress, and hope more explicitly.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and gentle reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence and validate the difficulty of change.

3. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Explicitly negotiate agenda and co-create the plan with the patient. Use language that shares power and emphasizes the patient’s expertise in her own life.

4. **Use Explicit Summaries:**  
   Summarize key change talk and the agreed plan clearly and explicitly to reinforce motivation and commitment.

5. **Amplify Commitment and Confidence:**  
   When the patient expresses tentative commitment or moderate confidence, explore what would increase these and reinforce strengths and past successes.

6. **Personalize and Vary Questions:**  
   Avoid formulaic phrasing; tailor questions to the patient’s unique context and emotional state to deepen engagement.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This diagnostic session demonstrates a solid and respectful application of motivational interviewing principles, with strong acceptance, empathy, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk and collaboratively develops a realistic plan aligned with the patient’s priorities. However, the session falls short of excellence due to somewhat formulaic reflections, limited handling of sustain talk, and implicit rather than explicit partnership and summarizing. Addressing these areas will enhance the depth, responsiveness, and collaborative spirit of future sessions, ultimately improving patient motivation and outcomes.
