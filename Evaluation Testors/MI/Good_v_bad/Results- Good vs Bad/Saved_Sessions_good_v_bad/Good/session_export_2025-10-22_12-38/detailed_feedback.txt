# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Luis Ramirez Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Luis’s frustration and overwhelm without dismissing or minimizing his feelings (e.g., “That’s a lot to carry,” “That makes a lot of sense”). The psychiatrist avoids labeling or blaming and validates the patient’s experience of complexity and burden. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard that might help Luis feel fully accepted despite his resistance and sarcasm. For example, the psychiatrist does not explicitly normalize Luis’s ambivalence or defensiveness, which could have further enhanced acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive stance is evident, but the acceptance could be deepened with more explicit normalization and warmth to fully embrace Luis’s resistance and frustration.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Luis’s feelings of overwhelm, confusion, and frustration (e.g., “feeling pretty worn down,” “sense of being misunderstood,” “feeling overwhelmed by a flood of different treatments”). The reflections are generally accurate and validate the patient’s experience. However, the empathy is mostly cognitive and surface-level; there is limited exploration of the emotional nuance behind Luis’s sarcasm, defensiveness, or deeper fears about illness and loss of control. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect the emotional subtext more deeply, such as the patient’s possible fear or resignation.

**Summary:**  
Good understanding and validation of patient’s perspective, but empathy could be enhanced by deeper emotional attunement and exploration of underlying feelings.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Luis’s input and emphasizing autonomy (e.g., “What would you like to focus on?”, “What do you think would make this feel less like drowning?”). There is no evidence of persuasion or directive advice, and the psychiatrist supports Luis’s own ideas for manageable steps. The session reflects a partnership approach, with the psychiatrist eliciting Luis’s thoughts and preferences. However, the spirit is somewhat formulaic and lacks more evocative techniques to draw out deeper motivation or values. The psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce Luis’s autonomy and evoke his own reasons for change beyond practical steps.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more evocative and less formulaic to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk by asking open-ended questions about what might be doable and what steps Luis might take (e.g., “What do you think would be the one thing…?”, “What might help you remember?”). Luis offers several statements of desire, ability, and reasons for change, which the psychiatrist reflects and reinforces. The psychiatrist also explores confidence with a scaling question, which is a good MI technique. However, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to amplify change talk more strongly or to explore commitment language in greater depth. The change talk is present and supported but could be more actively cultivated and reinforced.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation of change talk with practical focus, but could be strengthened by more strategic amplification and exploration of commitment.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Luis expresses sustain talk through sarcasm, defensiveness, and minimization (“I don’t think you get my situation,” “I’m not promising anything,” “It’s not some big magic fix”). The psychiatrist acknowledges these statements neutrally and reflects them without reinforcing sustain talk. However, there is limited evidence of active softening or strategic redirection of sustain talk. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly validate the difficulty of change while gently guiding Luis toward change. Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or minimized.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is recognized but not actively softened or redirected; more skillful handling of ambivalence is needed.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently works as a partner with Luis, inviting his perspective and choices throughout the session. The language is inclusive and collaborative (“What do you think…?”, “What might help you…?”, “What do you think would be the very next step?”). The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance and respects Luis’s autonomy and expertise about his own life. This partnership is a clear strength of the session.

**Summary:**  
Excellent collaborative stance with clear respect for client autonomy and expertise.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist demonstrates a respectful, collaborative, and autonomy-supportive style.  
- Good use of open questions and reflective listening to engage a resistant patient.  
- Effectively elicits and supports change talk focused on practical, manageable steps.  
- Maintains clear focus on asthma management and behavior change.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Acceptance could be warmer and more explicitly unconditional to fully embrace resistance and frustration.  
- Empathy should be deepened to explore emotional nuances behind sarcasm and defensiveness.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; use of double-sided reflections and normalization would help.  
- Affirmations are generic; more specific, personalized affirmations would strengthen motivation.  
- Planning could be more concrete with explicit commitment and follow-up.  
- Summarizing is minimal; explicit summaries pulling together change talk would reinforce motivation.  
- The session occasionally feels formulaic; more evocative and personalized responses would enhance MI spirit.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Acceptance:**  
   - Explicitly normalize ambivalence, frustration, and resistance to foster unconditional positive regard.  
   - Use warm, personalized affirmations that validate the patient’s experience beyond surface-level respect.

2. **Enhance Empathy:**  
   - Reflect not only content but also underlying emotions such as fear, frustration, or resignation.  
   - Explore the emotional subtext behind sarcasm and defensiveness to build deeper connection.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   - Use double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.  
   - Normalize the difficulty of change and validate ambivalence to reduce resistance.

4. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   - When change talk emerges, reflect and amplify it to deepen motivation.  
   - Explicitly elicit and reinforce commitment and confidence language.

5. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   - Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing the patient’s strengths, efforts, and values.  
   - Avoid generic praise; focus on what is meaningful and unique to the patient.

6. **Improve Planning and Summarizing:**  
   - Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with explicit commitment and follow-up.  
   - Use structured summaries to pull together change talk and reinforce motivation.

7. **Increase Evocation and Personalization:**  
   - Move beyond formulaic responses to more evocative, personalized reflections and questions.  
   - Actively draw out the patient’s own motivations, values, and ideas for change.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates solid motivational interviewing skills with a respectful, collaborative approach and effective elicitation of change talk focused on practical steps. However, to reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen acceptance and empathy, skillfully soften sustain talk, amplify change talk more actively, and strengthen planning and summarizing. Greater personalization and evocation would also enhance the MI spirit and better engage a resistant patient like Luis Ramirez.
