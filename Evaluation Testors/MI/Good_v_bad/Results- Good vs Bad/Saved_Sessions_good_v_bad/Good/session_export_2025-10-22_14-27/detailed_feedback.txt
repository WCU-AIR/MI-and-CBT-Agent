# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening, the psychiatrist warmly invites the patient’s agenda (“What’s on your mind…?”), validating Samuel’s progress and struggles without judgment (“that’s a real accomplishment,” “it’s understandable that it sometimes feels like you’re just delaying”). The tone remains supportive and respectful, affirming the patient’s efforts and ambivalence without any hint of criticism or pressure. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the difficulty of the patient’s situation and normalizes ambivalence, which is a hallmark of high acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, nonjudgmental environment that encourages openness and honesty.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the patient’s experience and emotional state. For example, reflections such as “those work breaks have become more than just a pause—they’re almost like a little ritual” and “that mix of wanting change but feeling uncertain” show nuanced understanding. The psychiatrist consistently paraphrases and reflects the patient’s ambivalence and frustration. However, while reflections are accurate and empathetic, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally formulaic. There is room to deepen emotional resonance by exploring the underlying feelings more explicitly (e.g., the emotional toll of stress or the fear of failure).

**Summary:**  
Good empathy and understanding, but reflections could be more emotionally evocative and less formulaic.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input and supporting autonomy throughout (“Would it be okay if I offered some ideas…?”). The conversation avoids directive or authoritative language and respects the patient’s pace and readiness. The psychiatrist evokes the patient’s own motivations and strategies, reinforcing self-efficacy. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and scripted, lacking spontaneous evocation or deeper exploration of the patient’s values and motivations beyond the immediate strategies. The partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of the plan and more evocative questions.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more evocative and less formulaic.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient articulates desire, ability, reasons, and need for change, and the psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements (“that shows a lot of insight,” “you’ve already tapped into some effective tools”). The psychiatrist also invites the patient to rate confidence and explore past successes, which helps deepen change talk. However, there are missed opportunities to more actively amplify or summarize change talk to build momentum toward commitment. The psychiatrist could more explicitly elicit commitment language or confidence-building statements.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could be more strategic and explicit in amplifying and consolidating it.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without judgment but does not consistently use strategic techniques to soften or redirect it. For example, the patient expresses skepticism about new strategies and doubts about their effectiveness, which the psychiatrist validates but does not actively reframe or use double-sided reflections to gently shift toward change. The sustain talk is met with understanding but remains largely neutral rather than skillfully minimized or redirected.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully but not skillfully softened or redirected to reduce its impact.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered approach, frequently inviting the patient’s perspective and permission before offering suggestions. The language is inclusive and avoids expert-driven directives. However, the partnership could be strengthened by more explicit sharing of power, such as co-creating the agenda or plan more actively and explicitly inviting the patient to lead the process. The session occasionally feels somewhat clinician-led in structure.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for autonomy, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and shared decision-making.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections could be more emotionally rich and less formulaic.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- More explicit evocation and amplification of commitment language needed.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly co-created and less clinician-led.

## Summary of Feedback

- Good use of open questions and reflective listening.  
- Consistent respect for autonomy and collaboration.  
- Effective evocation and reinforcement of change talk.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections often lack emotional depth and complexity; more complex reflections needed.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- More explicit evocation and amplification of commitment language needed.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak; need more explicit consolidation of next steps and motivation.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly co-created and less clinician-led.  
- Some responses feel formulaic and could be more personalized and evocative.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex and amplified reflections that capture underlying emotions, values, and ambivalence. Avoid formulaic phrasing.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and gentle redirection to reduce the impact of sustain talk and guide toward change.

3. **Amplify Commitment Language:**  
   Explicitly elicit and reinforce commitment and confidence statements to build momentum toward change.

4. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Use explicit summaries to consolidate change talk and collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with the patient.

5. **Strengthen Partnership:**  
   More actively share power by co-creating the agenda and plan, inviting the patient to lead and make choices.

6. **Increase Evocation and Personalization:**  
   Use more evocative questions and personalized responses to deepen motivation and engagement.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Be present and responsive to the unique flow of the conversation, avoiding repetitive or scripted language.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates solid motivational interviewing skills with strong acceptance, empathy, and collaboration. The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk and respects autonomy. However, the session falls short of excellence due to somewhat formulaic reflections, insufficient handling of sustain talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully soften sustain talk, explicitly amplify commitment language, and more actively co-create the plan with the patient.
