# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, warmly validating the patient’s progress and struggles without judgment. The tone remains supportive and respectful throughout, creating a safe environment for openness.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident, with nuanced reflections capturing the patient’s ambivalence and frustration. However, reflections are somewhat surface-level and could be deepened to better evoke underlying emotions.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting patient input and supporting autonomy. The session respects the patient’s pace and readiness, though it occasionally feels somewhat structured and could benefit from more spontaneous evocation.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
Strong collaboration is maintained with frequent invitations for patient perspective and permission before suggestions. Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of agenda and plan to further share power.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent, moderate evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, including desire, ability, reasons, and need. There is room to more actively amplify and consolidate commitment language to build momentum toward change.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo)  
☑ 3 (acknowledges sustain talk neutrally)  
⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully but not skillfully softened or redirected. The psychiatrist validates doubts and skepticism without actively reframing or gently steering toward change.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist establishes strong rapport and maintains patient engagement with open questions and reflective listening, avoiding confrontational or directive language.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The session maintains clear focus on tobacco reduction and managing urges. However, the psychiatrist could more explicitly negotiate and revisit the agenda to sharpen focus.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively draws out the patient’s motivations and past successes, affirming autonomy. More active evocation of commitment and confidence language would strengthen this process.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
⬜ Strengthens commitment language  
**Comments:**  
Some tentative planning occurs with discussion of reminders and small steps. The plan is not fully consolidated or collaboratively developed; more explicit summarizing and strengthening of commitment is needed.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Open-ended questions are used effectively to explore the patient’s experience and ambivalence, though some questions are somewhat predictable.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are frequent and accurate but tend to be simple and surface-level. There are few complex or amplified reflections capturing deeper emotions or values.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☐ Pulls together change talk  
☑ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Summaries are implicit rather than explicit. The psychiatrist could use more structured summaries to consolidate change talk and reinforce motivation.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Affirmations are genuine and supportive, recognizing the patient’s insight and effort. They could be more specific and personalized.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently asks permission before offering advice or suggestions, respecting patient autonomy.

---

- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not dwelled on excessively.

- Commitment language is present but could be strengthened toward session close.
