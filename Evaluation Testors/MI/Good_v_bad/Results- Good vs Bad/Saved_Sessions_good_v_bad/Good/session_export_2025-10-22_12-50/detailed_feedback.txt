# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect toward the patient throughout the session. From the opening question, “What would you like to focus on or talk about during our session today?” the psychiatrist invites the patient’s agenda, signaling respect for his autonomy. The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s feelings without judgment, for example, “Thanks for sharing that with me. It sounds like smoking plays an important role for you…” and “That tension between knowing you could change and feeling unsure about the disruption it might bring is really understandable.” These statements validate the patient’s experience and ambivalence without any hint of criticism or pressure. The tone remains warm and supportive, fostering a safe environment for open dialogue.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in communicating acceptance, creating a nonjudgmental atmosphere that encourages honest disclosure.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a high level of empathic understanding, accurately reflecting the patient’s perspective and emotional experience. Complex reflections capture both content and feeling, such as, “It sounds like smoking is woven into your daily rhythm and serves as a meaningful way to decompress…” and “That tension between wanting to keep that comfort and facing the reality that things might have to shift.” These reflections show nuanced understanding of the patient’s ambivalence and emotional conflict. The psychiatrist also validates the patient’s concerns about stress management and disruption, which deepens rapport and trust.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is consistently accurate, nuanced, and emotionally attuned, enhancing the therapeutic alliance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, frequently inviting the patient’s input and supporting autonomy (“What would you like to focus on…?”, “What do you think about how smoking fits into your life right now?”). The session avoids directive or authoritarian language, and the psychiatrist skillfully evokes the patient’s own motivations and ideas for change. However, while collaboration is strong, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, particularly in the repeated use of scaling questions (“On a scale from zero to ten…”). Although these are useful tools, their frequent use can risk making the conversation feel less spontaneous or fully co-created. The psychiatrist could further enhance evocation by explicitly inviting the patient to set the agenda or co-design the plan more actively.

**Summary:**  
Strong partnership and autonomy support, but the interaction could be more fluid and less formulaic to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient offers several statements indicating desire, ability, and reasons for change (“Sure, I can quit,” “I think I could do it if I really try,” “Maybe cutting back a few cigarettes a day…”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, reinforcing the patient’s self-efficacy and readiness (“It sounds like you recognize your ability to quit or cut down…”). The use of scaling questions helps quantify importance and confidence, which can deepen motivation. However, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to more explicitly evoke commitment language or to explore the patient’s values and goals in greater depth. For example, the patient mentions stress relief and work policy as motivators but these could be more deeply explored to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could more actively deepen commitment and link change to core values.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation or judgment. The patient’s statements about liking smoking and its role in stress management are met with reflective and validating responses (“That tension between wanting to keep that comfort and facing the reality…”). The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk but rather gently redirects focus toward change possibilities. However, the session lacks explicit use of double-sided reflections that simultaneously acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting change talk, which is a hallmark of advanced MI skill. Incorporating such reflections could further soften sustain talk and reduce resistance.

**Summary:**  
Effective neutralization of sustain talk, though more strategic use of double-sided reflections would enhance this skill.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a collaborative, client-centered approach. The patient is invited to set the agenda, share his thoughts, and co-create a plan. The psychiatrist avoids expert-driven or directive language and instead uses inclusive phrasing (“What do you think would be a good first small step…?”, “What do you think might help boost that confidence?”). The patient is positioned as the expert in his own life, and the psychiatrist respects his autonomy throughout. This approach minimizes power differentials and fosters a strong therapeutic alliance.

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership, with genuine collaboration and respect for patient autonomy.

### Areas for Improvement

- Provide more explicit summaries to consolidate motivation and plan.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Reduce Formulaic Language:**  
   Limit repetitive use of scaling questions and scripted phrases to maintain conversational spontaneity and deeper engagement.

2. **Deepen Commitment Evocation:**  
   Actively elicit commitment statements and explore what change means to the patient’s core values and identity.

3. **Use Double-Sided Reflections:**  
   Incorporate reflections that acknowledge both sustain and change talk simultaneously to soften resistance and highlight ambivalence.

4. **Enhance Summarizing:**  
   Provide explicit summaries that integrate change talk and reinforce the patient’s motivation and plan, checking for agreement.

5. **Increase Specific Affirmations:**  
   Offer more frequent and detailed affirmations that recognize the patient’s strengths, past successes, and efforts.

6. **Co-Create the Plan More Explicitly:**  
   Invite the patient to actively shape the plan and next steps, reinforcing autonomy and ownership.

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a strong foundation in motivational interviewing with excellent acceptance, empathy, and partnership. The psychiatrist skillfully navigates ambivalence and elicits change talk while respecting sustain talk. To elevate the session to an expert level, the psychiatrist should reduce formulaic elements, deepen commitment evocation, strategically soften sustain talk, and provide more explicit summaries and affirmations. These improvements will enhance the patient’s motivation and readiness for change, ultimately leading to more effective outcomes.
