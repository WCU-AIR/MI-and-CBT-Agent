# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and supportive tone throughout, validating the patient’s ambivalence and readiness without judgment. However, the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and could benefit from deeper warmth and more explicit unconditional positive regard.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking are demonstrated with accurate paraphrasing of the patient’s ambivalence and feelings. Reflections tend to be surface-level and occasionally repetitive, lacking deeper emotional nuance.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist supports autonomy and collaboration, inviting the patient’s input and ideas. The style is non-directive and evokes patient motivation, though it remains somewhat formulaic and could be more evocative and deeply collaborative.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist invites patient perspectives and avoids an expert-driven approach. Partnership is present but somewhat superficial, lacking explicit co-creation or negotiation of agenda and plan.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (consistent, deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is effectively evoked through open questions and reflections, with the patient expressing desire, ability, and tentative commitment. However, opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk, especially commitment language, are missed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo)  
☑ 3 (some acknowledgment)  
⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged indirectly by reflecting ambivalence but not actively softened or redirected. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to reduce sustain talk’s impact.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist establishes and maintains rapport with polite, respectful interaction. Open questions encourage patient elaboration, and reflections are used consistently, though they could be deeper.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☑ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The session maintains focus on smoking cessation and ambivalence. The psychiatrist guides the conversation toward change but does not explicitly negotiate or clarify agenda beyond initial questions.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist effectively draws out reasons for change and past successes, affirming autonomy. Evocation is consistent but could be more strategic and deeper to elicit stronger commitment and confidence.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
A concrete, manageable plan is collaboratively developed (gum, reminders, social support). However, the plan is not explicitly consolidated or summarized with a clear commitment statement.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Several open-ended questions invite elaboration and reflection. Some questions are somewhat formulaic but generally effective in eliciting patient thoughts.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are frequent and accurate but mostly simple paraphrases. Few complex or amplified reflections that deepen meaning or evoke stronger motivation.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☑ Pulls together change talk  
⬜ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Implicit summaries occur but no explicit, structured summaries that consolidate change talk or reinforce motivation.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Affirmations are present but somewhat generic and lack specificity or personalization.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist avoids unsolicited advice and respects autonomy, though explicit permission for advice is not always sought.

---

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Reflects more than asks, maintaining good R:Q ratio.  
- Does not dwell excessively on sustain talk but could soften it more actively.  
- Affirms autonomy consistently.  
- Commitment language is emerging but not strongly consolidated at session close.

---

## 5. Overall Evaluation

### MI Spirit (Collaboration, Evocation, Autonomy): (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support with consistent evocation, though somewhat formulaic and lacking deeper emotional attunement.

### Empathy: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and understanding, but reflections could be deeper and more nuanced emotionally.

### Partnership: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Collaborative stance maintained, but partnership could be more explicitly equal and co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
Change talk is effectively evoked but could be amplified and deepened, especially commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk: (3/5)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not actively softened or redirected to pivot toward change.

### Overall MI Adherence: (4/5)  
**Comments:**  
The session adheres well to MI principles with respectful, supportive, and collaborative style, though improvements in reflection depth, sustain talk handling, and summarizing would enhance adherence.
