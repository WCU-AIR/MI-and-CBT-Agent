# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Medication Adherence Session with Mai Nguyen

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect toward Mai throughout the session. From the opening, the psychiatrist validates Mai’s uncertainty and frustration without judgment (“Thank you for sharing that—it sounds like there’s some uncertainty and maybe even a bit of frustration…”). The tone remains warm, supportive, and patient-centered, acknowledging Mai’s concerns and values without dismissing or minimizing them. There is no evidence of labeling or disapproval. The psychiatrist explicitly affirms Mai’s thoughtful approach and autonomy repeatedly, which reflects a high level of acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, respectful environment that encourages openness and trust. This is a clear strength of the session.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Mai’s ambivalence and concerns about medication necessity and side effects (“It sounds like you’ve been putting effort into healthy habits… and that makes it feel confusing…”). Reflections are generally accurate and empathetic, showing understanding of Mai’s values and fears. However, the reflections tend to be somewhat surface-level and formulaic, often restating content without deeper exploration of emotional nuance or underlying fears (e.g., anxiety about medication dependency or loss of control). There is room to deepen empathic reflections by exploring emotional subtext more fully.

**Summary:**  
Good empathic engagement is present, but reflections could be more nuanced and emotionally attuned to fully capture Mai’s internal experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Mai’s input and supporting her autonomy throughout (“What do you think would help you feel more comfortable…?” “How do you feel about us reviewing that together?”). The conversation avoids directive or authoritarian language and respects Mai’s right to make informed decisions. The psychiatrist evokes Mai’s own motivations and values, particularly her desire to balance health maintenance with minimizing medication. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, lacking spontaneous evocation or deeper exploration of Mai’s intrinsic motivations beyond surface-level reasons. The partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of the agenda and plan.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support are evident, but the session could be more evocative and less scripted to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk related to medication adherence and health goals (“What are some reasons you might want to consider this medication…?” “How confident do you feel about starting with these steps?”). Mai offers multiple statements of desire, reasons, need, and commitment. The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, helping Mai articulate her readiness and practical plans. However, there is a missed opportunity to deepen commitment language further or explore confidence and ability in more detail. The evocation is consistent but could be more strategic and nuanced.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is well cultivated and reinforced, but the psychiatrist could more actively deepen and amplify commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Mai expresses sustain talk primarily through skepticism about medication necessity and concerns about side effects. The psychiatrist acknowledges these concerns respectfully but tends to reflect them neutrally rather than actively softening or redirecting sustain talk. For example, the psychiatrist could have used more double-sided reflections (“On one hand, you want to avoid too many medications, and on the other, you want to protect your heart health…”) or normalized ambivalence to reduce resistance. The session lacks strategic efforts to minimize the impact of sustain talk or gently guide Mai toward change.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected, representing a key area for improvement.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered stance, inviting Mai’s perspectives and preferences throughout. Language such as “How do you feel about us reviewing that together?” supports shared decision-making. However, the partnership occasionally feels somewhat one-sided, with the psychiatrist leading the agenda and offering solutions rather than explicitly inviting Mai to co-create the plan or set the agenda. More explicit sharing of power and negotiation of next steps would strengthen the partnership.

**Summary:**  
Good collaboration and respect for autonomy, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and client leadership.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Consistent acceptance and respect.  
- Good empathic listening and validation.  
- Strong collaboration and autonomy support.  
- Effective evocation of change talk and practical planning.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections could be deeper and more emotionally nuanced.  
- Sustain talk is not actively softened or redirected.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly co-created.  
- Evocation of commitment and confidence could be more strategic and amplified.

### Areas for Improvement

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflective Listening:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values rather than simple paraphrasing.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and gently guide toward change.

3. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Explicitly invite the patient to co-create the agenda and plan, sharing power more fully.

4. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   Strategically reinforce and deepen commitment and confidence language to strengthen motivation.

5. **Use Explicit Summaries:**  
   Summarize key change talk and plans clearly to consolidate motivation and commitment.

6. **Increase Spontaneity and Evocation:**  
   Move beyond formulaic questions and reflections to more personalized, evocative dialogue.

7. **Explicit Permission for Advice:**  
   Consistently ask permission before providing information or suggestions to respect autonomy fully.
