# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

## 1. Global Ratings (Counselor)

### Acceptance (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Conveys unconditional positive regard  
☑ High: consistently respectful and supportive  
⬜ Low: judgmental, disapproving  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect throughout the session. Validates patient’s uncertainty and frustration without judgment, creating a safe and supportive environment that encourages openness and trust.

---

### Empathy (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Accurately understands client perspective  
☑ High: active reflective listening  
⬜ Low: little effort to understand  
**Comments:**  
Good reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident, with accurate reflections of the patient’s ambivalence and concerns. However, reflections tend to be somewhat surface-level and formulaic, lacking deeper emotional nuance or exploration of underlying fears.

---

### Spirit (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Collaboration, evocation, autonomy support  
☑ High: avoids persuasion, emphasizes client choice  
⬜ Low: authoritarian stance  
**Comments:**  
Maintains a collaborative stance and supports autonomy effectively. Invites patient input and respects her right to make informed decisions. The session occasionally feels structured and formulaic, with less spontaneous evocation of intrinsic motivations.

---

### Partnership (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Works as equal partner  
☑ High: minimizes power differential  
⬜ Low: expert-driven  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist invites patient perspectives and preferences, supporting shared decision-making. However, the agenda and plan are somewhat led by the psychiatrist rather than explicitly co-created, limiting full partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Encourages client change language  
☑ 4 (consistent, moderate evocation)  
⬜ 1 (none) → ⬜ 5 (deep evocation)  
**Comments:**  
Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk related to medication adherence and health goals. Patient expresses desire, reasons, need, and commitment. Opportunities exist to deepen and amplify commitment and confidence language further.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Reduces focus on resistance  
⬜ 1 (reinforces status quo) → ☑ 3 (acknowledges but limited softening) → ⬜ 5 (consistently pivots to change)  
**Comments:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully but mostly reflected neutrally. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to soften or redirect sustain talk through double-sided reflections or normalization, representing an area for improvement.

---

## 2. Core MI Processes

### Engaging (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Build rapport, acceptance, empathy  
☑ Uses open questions  
☑ Reflects more than asks  
☑ Avoids roadblocks  
**Comments:**  
Rapport is well established and maintained with warm, respectful tone. Open questions invite elaboration, and reflections support engagement. Some reflections are surface-level but do not block communication.

---

### Focusing (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Maintain direction toward change goal  
☑ Confirms agreed goal  
☐ Redirects drifting conversation  
**Comments:**  
The session maintains clear focus on medication adherence and ambivalence. However, explicit negotiation and confirmation of agenda and goals with the patient could sharpen focus further.

---

### Evoking (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Draw out client’s reasons for change  
☑ Explores values, hopes, past successes  
☑ Affirms autonomy  
**Comments:**  
The psychiatrist draws out patient’s reasons for change and readiness, exploring values and hopes. Evocation is consistent but could be more strategic and deeper to fully engage intrinsic motivation.

---

### Planning (5-point Likert)  
Expectation: Consolidates commitment, next steps  
☑ Strengthens commitment language  
☑ Elicits concrete steps  
**Comments:**  
A practical plan is collaboratively developed, including medication timing, juice avoidance, and symptom monitoring. The plan could be more explicitly consolidated with a clear summary and commitment check.

---

## 3. Counselor Behaviors (Behavior Counts – MISC/MITI)

### Open Questions (5-point Likert)  
☑ Draw out narrative, values  
⬜ Overuse of closed questions  
**Comments:**  
Several open-ended questions effectively invite elaboration and reflection. Questions could be more varied and strategically targeted to deepen motivation.

---

### Reflections (5-point Likert)  
☑ More reflections than questions; complex reflections add meaning  
⬜ Few or no reflections  
**Comments:**  
Reflections are frequent and generally accurate but tend to be simple and surface-level, mostly paraphrasing content without capturing deeper emotions or ambivalence.

---

### Summarizing (5-point Likert)  
☐ Pulls together change talk  
☑ Omits or distorts client language  
**Comments:**  
Implicit summaries and reflections of key points occur, but no explicit, structured summaries that consolidate change talk or reinforce motivation and commitment.

---

### Affirmations (5-point Likert)  
☑ Recognizes strengths, autonomy  
⬜ Judgmental, minimizing  
**Comments:**  
Affirmations recognize patient’s thoughtful approach and planning. They are genuine and supportive but could be more specific and varied to highlight a broader range of strengths.

---

### Persuade with Permission (5-point Likert)  
☑ Provides advice/info only after asking  
⬜ Directs, warns, or advises without permission  
**Comments:**  
Information and suggestions are offered with implicit permission and collaboration. Explicit permission requests before providing advice could be more consistently used.

---

## 4. Self-Audit Prompts (Clinician Reflection)

- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not deeply explored or softened.  
- Autonomy is consistently affirmed and supported.  
- Commitment language is elicited but could be strengthened toward session close.

### Empathy: (4/5)

---
