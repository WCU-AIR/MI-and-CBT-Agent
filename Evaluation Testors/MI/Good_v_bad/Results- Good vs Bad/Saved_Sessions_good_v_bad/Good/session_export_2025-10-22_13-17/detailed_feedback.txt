# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Session with Malik Okoro

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. Early on, the psychiatrist acknowledges Malik’s defensiveness and external motivation (“Thanks for sharing that with me—it sounds like you’re feeling a bit pushed into this…”), which demonstrates acceptance of Malik’s perspective. The psychiatrist avoids labeling or confrontation and validates Malik’s feelings and experiences (e.g., “That makes a lot of sense, wanting to feel understood rather than judged”). However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard that might help reduce Malik’s guardedness further. There is no explicit normalization of ambivalence or struggle, which would have strengthened acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive stance is maintained, but the acceptance could be warmer and more explicitly unconditional to better engage a defensive adolescent.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing Malik’s ambivalence and social context (e.g., “It sounds like smoking is tied up with those moments of connection and relaxation with your friends…”). However, reflections tend to be surface-level and descriptive rather than deeply empathic or emotionally nuanced. For example, the psychiatrist reflects content but misses opportunities to explore Malik’s underlying feelings of tension, frustration, or fear of exclusion more deeply. The tone remains somewhat clinical and lacks emotional attunement that might have helped Malik feel more truly understood.

**Summary:**  
Adequate understanding and paraphrasing of content, but reflections lack emotional depth and fail to fully capture Malik’s internal experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist generally adopts a collaborative stance, inviting Malik’s input and supporting autonomy (e.g., “If it’s okay with you, I’d like to share…”). There is some evocation of Malik’s own reasons and ambivalence, and the psychiatrist avoids overt persuasion or confrontation. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks the full MI spirit of evocation and partnership. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Malik’s own motivations or values beyond surface-level statements, nor does the psychiatrist explicitly co-create the agenda or plan. The autonomy support is present but could be more explicit and empowering.

**Summary:**  
Moderate collaboration and autonomy support, but the session lacks the evocative, fully client-centered spirit characteristic of high-quality MI.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist makes some attempts to evoke change talk by asking about reasons for change and readiness (e.g., “On a scale from zero to ten… where would you say you are?”). Malik offers minimal change talk, mostly acknowledging tension with his mother. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify this change talk. There are missed opportunities to reflect or explore the ambivalence more deeply (e.g., “What would it mean for you if the tension with your mom eased?”). The psychiatrist does not strategically respond to or build on change talk to deepen motivation or commitment.

**Summary:**  
Rare and weak evocation of change talk; missed opportunities to amplify and explore emerging motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation and avoids reinforcing it. For example, the psychiatrist reflects Malik’s minimization and peer influence without judgment. There is some attempt to balance sustain talk with recognition of tension at home. However, the responses are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly normalize ambivalence to reduce resistance.

**Summary:**  
Some neutral acknowledgment of sustain talk, but lacks skillful softening or strategic redirection to reduce sustain talk’s impact.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist invites Malik’s perspective and occasionally asks permission before sharing information, which supports partnership. However, the collaboration is somewhat superficial and formulaic. The psychiatrist does not explicitly negotiate the agenda or co-create a plan with Malik. The power differential remains somewhat unaddressed, and Malik’s expertise in his own life is acknowledged but not fully leveraged to build a sense of equal partnership.

**Summary:**  
Partial collaboration and some autonomy support, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation and power sharing.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations that recognize Malik’s strengths, skills, and values to build rapport and confidence.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize key points, pull together change talk, and collaboratively develop concrete next steps or plans.

6. **Increase Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out Malik’s own motivations and values. Share power by explicitly co-creating agenda and plans.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and be more emotionally attuned to Malik’s unique experience and ambivalence.

8. **Deepen Engagement:**  
   Use empathic statements and emotional reflections to build trust and reduce defensiveness.
