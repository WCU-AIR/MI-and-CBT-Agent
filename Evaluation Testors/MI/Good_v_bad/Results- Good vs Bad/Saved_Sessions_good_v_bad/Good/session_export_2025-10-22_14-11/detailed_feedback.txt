# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening, the tone is warm and inviting (“Hello, it’s good to see you today”), and the psychiatrist validates the patient’s experiences and efforts without judgment. Affirmations such as “That kind of self-monitoring, even if it feels imperfect, is a meaningful shift” and “Thank you for sharing that so honestly” reinforce acceptance. The psychiatrist avoids any labeling or disapproval, instead normalizing ambivalence and struggle (“That mix of hope and worry seems like a powerful place to be”). This consistent warmth and respect meet the highest standard for acceptance.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, nonjudgmental environment that encourages openness and honesty.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Empathy is demonstrated at a high level with accurate and nuanced reflections of the patient’s feelings and perspectives. The psychiatrist captures both content and emotional undertones, such as when reflecting the patient’s ambivalence (“That mix of hope and worry seems like a powerful place to be”) and self-perception shifts (“It sounds like you’re stepping into a new way of being with yourself—more control, less self-judgment”). The reflections are complex and validate the patient’s experience without minimizing challenges. The psychiatrist also picks up on subtle cues, such as the patient’s hesitation and cautious optimism, and reflects these back effectively.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist shows a deep understanding of the patient’s internal experience, fostering trust and engagement.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The session strongly embodies the spirit of motivational interviewing: collaboration, evocation, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist consistently invites the patient’s perspective (“What do you think has helped you most…?”, “What do you think would be a manageable next step?”), avoids directive or authoritative language, and supports the patient’s autonomy (“How do you feel about starting with one or two of those steps?”). The psychiatrist skillfully evokes the patient’s own motivations and strengths, reinforcing self-efficacy and partnership. There is no evidence of persuasion or pressure; instead, the psychiatrist respects the patient’s pace and readiness.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates a fully collaborative, evocative, and autonomy-supportive approach throughout.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist actively and skillfully cultivates change talk by eliciting the patient’s reasons, abilities, and desires for change. Questions such as “What do you think keeps you motivated…?” and reflections that amplify change talk (“Noticing those moments where you surprise yourself suggests a resilience…”) effectively deepen the patient’s commitment language. The psychiatrist also explores confidence levels and helps the patient articulate manageable next steps, reinforcing the patient’s agency. Change talk is consistently reinforced and expanded upon, with no missed opportunities evident.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is skillfully and consistently evoked, amplified, and reinforced.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged and gently reframed without confrontation. For example, the psychiatrist reflects the patient’s ambivalence (“That mix of hope and doubt about your ability to sustain change is really understandable”) and normalizes the struggle. However, while sustain talk is not reinforced, the psychiatrist could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and gently guide toward change. The approach is mostly neutral-positive but could be more strategic in softening sustain talk’s impact.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is handled respectfully and neutrally, but opportunities exist to more actively soften and redirect it.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a clear partnership stance, consistently inviting the patient’s input and co-creating the agenda and plan. The use of inclusive language (“What do you think would be a manageable next step?”) and checking in on readiness and confidence exemplify a client-centered approach. The psychiatrist avoids expert-driven or directive language and explicitly supports the patient’s autonomy and expertise in their own life.

**Summary:**  
The session reflects a fully equal, collaborative partnership.

## Recommendations

1. **Enhance Summarizing:**  
   Incorporate explicit summaries that pull together key change talk and reinforce commitment before moving to planning or closing.

2. **Softening Sustain Talk:**  
   Use more double-sided reflections that acknowledge sustain talk while gently highlighting reasons for change, helping the patient resolve ambivalence.

3. **Maintain Strengths:**  
   Continue the high level of acceptance, empathy, and autonomy support demonstrated here.

4. **Expand Evocation:**  
   When appropriate, deepen evocation by exploring values and long-term goals connected to change.
