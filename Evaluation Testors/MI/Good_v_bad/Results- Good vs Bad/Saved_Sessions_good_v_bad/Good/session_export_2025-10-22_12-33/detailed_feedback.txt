# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Diagnostic Session with Tyler Johnson

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. Early on, the psychiatrist acknowledges Tyler’s resistance and external motivation without confrontation (“Thanks for sharing that with me,” “It sounds like you’re feeling a bit pushed…”). The psychiatrist avoids labeling or blaming Tyler and validates his feelings of frustration and desire for autonomy. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or personalized. There is limited explicit normalization of Tyler’s ambivalence or struggle, which would elevate this to a 5. The psychiatrist could have more explicitly conveyed unconditional positive regard, especially given Tyler’s guarded and defensive stance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive, but lacks the full warmth and unconditional positive regard that would better engage a resistant adolescent.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Tyler’s feelings about his mom’s behavior and his desire for peace and autonomy (“It sounds like keeping the peace at home…,” “trust and respect feel pretty central to you”). However, the reflections tend to be surface-level and somewhat repetitive, focusing mainly on content rather than deeper emotional nuance. For example, Tyler’s frustration, defensiveness, and possible feelings of being misunderstood or boxed in are acknowledged but not deeply explored or validated. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect the emotional complexity behind Tyler’s resistance and sustain talk, such as his possible fear, shame, or confusion.

**Summary:**  
Adequate understanding and reflections, but lacks emotional depth and nuanced empathy that could better connect with Tyler’s guarded affect.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Tyler’s input and emphasizing autonomy (“What would you like to talk about…?”, “What do you think might help…”). There is no evidence of persuasion or directive advice, and the psychiatrist respects Tyler’s pace and readiness. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks strong evocation of Tyler’s own motivations or values beyond the surface level. The psychiatrist does not explicitly explore Tyler’s personal goals or values beyond conflict avoidance, nor does he actively reinforce Tyler’s agency beyond acknowledging his control over change. The spirit of MI is present but not fully embodied.

**Summary:**  
Collaborative and autonomy-supportive but lacks deeper evocation and partnership to fully engage Tyler’s intrinsic motivation.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Change talk is minimal and mostly tentative, reflecting Tyler’s low readiness to change (“Maybe if things got worse, I’d think about it,” “I guess I can try that”). The psychiatrist attempts to evoke change talk by asking about past successes and possible small steps, but these efforts are limited in frequency and depth. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify Tyler’s tentative change talk, nor does he strategically explore desire, ability, reasons, or need beyond superficial mentions. There is no explicit elicitation of commitment language or confidence. Overall, change talk is weakly cultivated.

**Summary:**  
Rare and shallow evocation of change talk; missed opportunities to deepen and reinforce Tyler’s tentative statements about change.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation and avoids reinforcing it. For example, Tyler’s insistence on maintaining his current behavior and blaming his mom is met with reflections that validate his feelings (“You’re clear about wanting to keep your friendships…,” “That makes a lot of sense…”). However, the psychiatrist does not actively use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to gently shift sustain talk toward change. The responses are mostly neutral rather than skillfully softening or redirecting sustain talk. There is room to better normalize ambivalence and reduce the impact of sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Acknowledges sustain talk neutrally but lacks consistent, skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Tyler’s perspective and choices, minimizing power differentials (“What would you like to talk about…?”, “What do you think might help…”). The language is inclusive and client-centered. Tyler is given control over the pace and content of the session. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic, lacking explicit co-creation of goals or plans. The psychiatrist could more explicitly share power by negotiating agenda and collaboratively developing next steps with Tyler.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaborative stance and autonomy support, but partnership could be deepened through explicit co-creation and shared decision-making.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive stance.  
- Adequate reflective listening and acknowledgment of sustain talk.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and nuance.  
- Change talk is weakly evoked and not amplified.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Partnership is present but could be more explicitly co-created.  
- The session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks dynamic evocation of intrinsic motivation.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3/5**

- Uses open questions and reflections to engage Tyler.  
- Supports autonomy and avoids confrontation or persuasion.  
- Acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and complexity; more complex reflections needed.  
- Change talk is weakly evoked and not amplified; missed opportunities to deepen motivation.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Affirmations are sparse and generic; more specific, strength-based affirmations needed.  
- Planning and summarizing are minimal; no clear consolidation of goals or commitments.  
- Engagement could be enhanced with more warmth and personalized connection.  
- The session feels formulaic and lacks dynamic evocation of Tyler’s intrinsic motivation.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex and amplified reflections that capture underlying emotions, values, and ambivalence to better connect with Tyler’s guarded affect.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When Tyler expresses tentative change talk, reflect and reinforce it to deepen motivation. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and strategic reframing to gently shift sustain talk toward change while validating Tyler’s feelings.

4. **Increase Affirmations:**  
   Offer more frequent, specific affirmations that recognize Tyler’s strengths, past successes, and efforts to manage conflict.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with Tyler and summarize key points to reinforce motivation and commitment.

6. **Improve Engagement:**  
   Increase warmth, personalized connection, and emotional attunement to better engage a resistant adolescent.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive or scripted phrasing to maintain a dynamic and authentic conversation.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing principles, with consistent respect, autonomy support, and avoidance of confrontation. However, it falls short of excellence due to limited emotional depth in reflections, weak evocation and amplification of change talk, minimal planning and summarizing, and a somewhat formulaic style. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully handle sustain talk, and more actively evoke and reinforce Tyler’s own motivations and strengths to foster intrinsic motivation and readiness for change.
