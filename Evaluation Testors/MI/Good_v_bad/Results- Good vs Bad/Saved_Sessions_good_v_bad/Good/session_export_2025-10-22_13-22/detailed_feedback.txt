# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Medication Education Session

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect toward Alessia throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on…?”) to affirming her concerns and questions, the psychiatrist maintains a warm, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges Alessia’s feelings of uncertainty and caution without minimizing or dismissing them (“That’s completely understandable,” “You’re clearly focused on safety…”). There is no evidence of judgment or labeling. The tone remains collaborative and validating, which is essential given Alessia’s vulnerability around medication safety.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a safe and respectful environment that encourages open dialogue.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Alessia’s concerns and emotional state. For example, reflections such as “You’re carrying a lot of caution and responsibility…” and “You’re really valuing understanding…” show an understanding of the patient’s mindset. The psychiatrist also validates the patient’s proactive approach and responsibility. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and formulaic, lacking deeper emotional nuance or exploration of underlying fears or ambivalence. The psychiatrist could have probed more into Alessia’s feelings about uncertainty or fear of adverse effects to deepen empathy.

**Summary:**  
Good empathy and understanding are demonstrated, but reflections could be more nuanced and emotionally rich to fully capture the patient’s experience.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Alessia to guide the session’s focus and explicitly asking permission before providing information (“If it’s okay with you, I can share some information…”). Autonomy is supported throughout, and the psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritative language. The session evokes Alessia’s own concerns and plans, such as when discussing symptom diaries and follow-up preferences. However, the session is somewhat didactic at times, especially during the medication explanation, which, while necessary, limits the evocation of Alessia’s own motivations or values related to medication adherence. The psychiatrist could have more actively elicited Alessia’s reasons for wanting to manage her medications safely or explored her values around health and control.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more evocative and less didactic to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk indirectly by asking about confidence and next steps (“On a scale from 0 to 10, how confident do you feel…?” and “What might be a first small step…”). Alessia expresses willingness and plans to engage in symptom tracking and communication with providers. However, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explicitly reinforce or amplify this change talk. For example, when Alessia describes her proactive strategies and desire for control, the psychiatrist could have reflected and amplified these statements to strengthen motivation. The change talk is present but not consistently deepened or strategically reinforced.

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk is present, but the psychiatrist could more actively amplify and reinforce it to strengthen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
There is minimal sustain talk in this session, as Alessia is mostly inquisitive and open. The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges any ambivalence or caution without reinforcing it. For example, the psychiatrist normalizes caution (“That 6 really reflects a thoughtful balance…”) and validates uncertainty without pushing for premature commitment. This approach effectively softens any potential sustain talk or resistance by respecting the patient’s pace and concerns.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist effectively softens any ambivalence or sustain talk, maintaining a supportive and non-pressuring stance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently fosters a strong partnership, inviting Alessia to set the agenda, asking permission before sharing information, and collaboratively discussing plans. Language such as “How does that sound?” and “What might work best for you?” emphasizes shared decision-making. The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven approach and respects Alessia’s expertise in managing her health. This equalizing stance is maintained throughout the session.

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership and client-centered collaboration.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist creates a respectful, safe, and collaborative environment.  
- Clear, jargon-free explanations tailored to patient concerns.  
- Strong partnership and autonomy support.  
- Thoughtful affirmations and engagement.  
- Effective softening of ambivalence and sustain talk.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are often surface-level and formulaic; more complex, emotionally attuned reflections are needed.  
- The psychiatrist should more actively evoke, reinforce, and amplify change talk to strengthen motivation.  
- The session occasionally becomes didactic; balancing information delivery with evocation of patient values and motivations would improve MI spirit.  
- Summaries could be more explicit and strategic to consolidate motivation and plan.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use complex reflections that capture emotional nuance, ambivalence, and underlying values, not just content.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses motivation or plans, reflect and reinforce these statements to build momentum.

3. **Balance Information with Evocation:**  
   While medication education requires factual information, intersperse this with questions that evoke the patient’s own reasons, hopes, and concerns.

4. **Use Explicit Summaries:**  
   Summarize key change talk and plans explicitly to consolidate motivation and commitment.

5. **Continue Strong Partnership and Autonomy Support:**  
   Maintain the collaborative stance and respect for patient expertise and choice.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid application of motivational interviewing principles, particularly in acceptance, partnership, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist provides clear, patient-centered medication education while maintaining engagement and respect. However, to reach a higher level of MI proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen reflective listening, more actively evoke and amplify change talk, and balance didactic information with greater evocation of the patient’s own motivations and values. Explicit summarizing and consolidation of plans would further strengthen the session’s impact.
