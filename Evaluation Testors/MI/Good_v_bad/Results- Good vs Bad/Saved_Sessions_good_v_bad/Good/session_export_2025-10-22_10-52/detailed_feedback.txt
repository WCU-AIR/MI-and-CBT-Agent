## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on or talk about during our session today?”) to the affirming reflections, the psychiatrist avoids judgment or pressure. The patient’s fears and ambivalence are met with warmth and validation, such as “It’s understandable that this feels like a tough balance to find,” and “You’re holding two very real and important truths.” There is no evidence of labeling or disapproval, and the psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the difficulty of the patient’s situation. This level of acceptance is consistent and genuine, meeting the highest standard.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist creates a safe, supportive environment that encourages open sharing and respects the patient’s experience fully.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the patient’s perspective. Complex reflections capture both the content and emotional nuance of the patient’s ambivalence, such as “You’re carrying a lot of weight—not just the fear of gaining pounds, but the emotional weight of feeling isolated or misunderstood during withdrawal.” The psychiatrist skillfully reflects the patient’s fears, hopes, and internal conflict, validating the emotional experience without minimizing or dismissing it. The reflections go beyond surface content to convey deep understanding, which is evident in the patient’s responsive and engaged answers.

**Summary:**  
Empathy is a clear strength, with reflections that accurately and sensitively convey the patient’s emotional state and perspective.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist embodies the spirit of motivational interviewing by maintaining a fully collaborative, evocative, and autonomy-supportive stance throughout. The session is patient-centered, with frequent invitations for the patient to share her thoughts and feelings (“What do you think would need to happen for you to feel more confident…?”). The psychiatrist avoids directive or persuasive language, instead evoking the patient’s own motivations and ideas for change. Autonomy is emphasized explicitly, and the patient is positioned as the expert in her own life. The psychiatrist also supports the patient’s pace and readiness, acknowledging ambivalence without pressure.

**Summary:**  
The session exemplifies MI spirit, fostering partnership, evocation, and autonomy support consistently.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. Early reflections highlight the patient’s reasons for change (“You want to be healthier for your family and yourself”), and the psychiatrist repeatedly invites elaboration on motivations (“What do you think are some reasons that make quitting smoking important enough to consider despite these fears?”). The patient’s change talk is amplified and deepened, with the psychiatrist reflecting and summarizing it to strengthen commitment (“You’re holding two very real and important truths…”). The psychiatrist also explores readiness with a scaling question and follows up thoughtfully. The patient’s statements of desire, reasons, and need are consistently nurtured.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is evoked frequently and skillfully, with strategic reflections and questions that deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk and ambivalence without reinforcing it. The patient’s fears about weight gain, withdrawal, and lifestyle changes are validated and explored. While the psychiatrist does not explicitly use double-sided reflections, the approach effectively normalizes ambivalence and gently redirects focus toward change. For example, the psychiatrist reflects the patient’s fears but follows with questions about reasons for change and past successes. However, there is a slight missed opportunity to more explicitly soften sustain talk by naming the ambivalence in a double-sided reflection (“On one hand you feel… but on the other hand…”). Overall, sustain talk is handled well but could be softened more strategically.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged and managed respectfully, with room for more explicit softening techniques.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a fully collaborative stance, minimizing power differentials and working as an equal partner. The patient is invited to set the agenda, share her thoughts, and co-create the plan. Language is inclusive and empowering (“What do you think…?”, “How do you imagine…?”). The psychiatrist avoids expert-driven or directive approaches, instead supporting the patient’s autonomy and expertise. The session flows as a genuine dialogue rather than a clinician-led interrogation.

**Summary:**  
Partnership is exemplary, with clear respect for the patient’s expertise and active collaboration.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Use Double-Sided Reflections:**  
   Explicitly name ambivalence by reflecting both sustain and change talk in the same statement to soften sustain talk and help the patient resolve ambivalence.

2. **Enhance Affirmations:**  
   Offer more frequent and specific affirmations that highlight the patient’s strengths, courage, and past successes to build confidence.

3. **Summarize Explicitly:**  
   Provide clear, structured summaries that pull together key change talk and plan elements to reinforce motivation and clarify next steps.

4. **Consolidate Planning:**  
   Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with the patient, including specific goals, timelines, and support mechanisms, and check for patient buy-in.

5. **Maintain MI Spirit:**  
   Continue to foster autonomy, collaboration, and evocation, ensuring the patient remains the expert and driver of change.
