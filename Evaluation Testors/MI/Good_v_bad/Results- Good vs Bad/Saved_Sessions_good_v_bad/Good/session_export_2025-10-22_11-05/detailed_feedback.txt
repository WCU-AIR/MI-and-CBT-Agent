# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. They acknowledge Andrea’s perspective without criticism or labeling, for example, “I really appreciate how clearly you’re sharing where you’re at.” The psychiatrist avoids pushing for change or expressing disappointment, which supports acceptance. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and lacks warmth or explicit expressions of unconditional positive regard that would elevate this to a 5. There is no explicit normalization of Andrea’s current stance or struggles, which would deepen acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive, but somewhat formal and lacking deeper warmth or normalization.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and attempts to understand Andrea’s experience, as seen in reflections like “That steady pace might bring a sense of reliability, but maybe it also leaves some space where excitement or deeper meaning could be missing.” However, many reflections are surface-level paraphrases rather than deep empathic reflections that capture underlying emotions or ambivalence. For example, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Andrea’s possible feelings of boredom, resignation, or subtle distress beneath her “just doing what I have to” stance. The empathy is consistent but lacks emotional depth and nuance.

**Summary:**  
Adequate understanding and reflection, but reflections are mostly factual and lack emotional depth.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Andrea’s perspective and avoiding directive or authoritarian language. Autonomy is supported, e.g., “That makes it easier to not feel overwhelmed.” However, the session is largely exploratory without strong evocation of Andrea’s own motivations or values. The psychiatrist does not actively elicit change talk or explore ambivalence beyond surface-level. The conversation remains somewhat neutral and lacks the evocative, client-centered spirit characteristic of high-quality MI. There is little evidence of strategic evocation or partnership beyond basic collaboration.

**Summary:**  
Moderate collaboration and autonomy support, but limited evocation and depth of MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
Change talk is minimal and not actively cultivated. The psychiatrist asks a readiness ruler question (“On a scale from 0 to 10…”) but does not follow up to explore or amplify any change talk. When Andrea expresses low readiness (“Probably close to a 0 or 1”), the psychiatrist accepts this without attempting to evoke reasons for change or explore discrepancies. There are missed opportunities to gently explore what might motivate Andrea or what small changes could be meaningful. The session lacks strategic efforts to elicit or reinforce change talk.

**Summary:**  
Rare and weak efforts to evoke change talk; missed opportunities to deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 2/5**

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is present in Andrea’s statements about sticking with routine and not wanting change (“I’m fine with how things are,” “Changing stuff sounds more complicated than it’s worth”). The psychiatrist acknowledges this stance respectfully but does not actively soften or reframe sustain talk. There are no double-sided reflections or strategic responses to reduce resistance or ambivalence. The sustain talk remains largely unchallenged and unaddressed beyond neutral acceptance.

**Summary:**  
Rare and weak attempts to soften sustain talk; sustain talk is mostly accepted without redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Andrea’s input and respects her autonomy. The language is inclusive and non-expert driven (“What do you think might need to happen…?” “What feels most helpful for you…”). The psychiatrist avoids pushing or directing, maintaining a collaborative stance. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and lacks explicit co-creation of goals or plans. The psychiatrist does not explicitly negotiate agenda or share power beyond basic invitations.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit co-creation.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone.  
- Consistent collaboration and autonomy support.  
- Adequate reflective listening and engagement.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and nuance.  
- Minimal evocation and reinforcement of change talk.  
- Sustain talk is accepted but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Partnership is present but not fully equal or co-created.  
- The session lacks the evocative, dynamic spirit of motivational interviewing.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and collaborative stance.  
- Uses open questions and reflections to engage the patient.  
- Supports autonomy and avoids pressure or confrontation.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are mostly surface-level; need to deepen emotional resonance and explore ambivalence.  
- Change talk is not actively evoked or reinforced; missed opportunities to explore motivation.  
- Sustain talk is accepted but not skillfully softened or reframed.  
- Affirmations are sparse and generic; more specific recognition of strengths needed.  
- No planning or goal-setting; session ends without concrete next steps.  
- The overall spirit of MI is underdeveloped; the session feels neutral and exploratory rather than evocative and motivational.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying feelings, values, and ambivalence. Reflect not only content but also emotional subtext.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When any change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it. Use strategic questions to evoke desire, ability, reasons, and need for change.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change.

4. **Increase Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing Andrea’s strengths, resilience, and efforts to manage her life.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Collaboratively develop small, manageable goals or next steps, even if minimal. Use explicit summaries to consolidate motivation.

6. **Strengthen Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out Andrea’s own motivations and values. Share power by co-creating agenda and plans.

7. **Increase Emotional Attunement and Warmth:**  
   Personalize responses and express warmth to deepen engagement and trust.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a foundational understanding of motivational interviewing principles, with respectful collaboration and autonomy support. However, it falls short of excellence due to superficial reflections, minimal evocation of change talk, weak handling of sustain talk, and lack of planning. To improve, the psychiatrist must deepen emotional attunement, actively evoke and reinforce motivation, and collaboratively develop concrete next steps. Without these improvements, the session risks remaining neutral and exploratory without fostering meaningful movement toward change.
