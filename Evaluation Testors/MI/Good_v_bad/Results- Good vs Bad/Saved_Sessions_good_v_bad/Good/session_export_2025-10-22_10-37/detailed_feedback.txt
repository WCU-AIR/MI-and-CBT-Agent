# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation – Vaccine Hesitancy Session with Jordan Reyes

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. Early on, the psychiatrist explicitly invites the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on?”), which supports acceptance and client-centeredness. The psychiatrist validates the patient’s perspective multiple times, such as acknowledging the patient’s confidence in his health and concerns about side effects. There is no evidence of labeling or judgment. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and lacks deeper warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard that might help the patient feel fully embraced despite his hesitancy. For example, the psychiatrist could have normalized the patient’s ambivalence more explicitly or affirmed the difficulty of making health decisions under uncertainty.

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive, but somewhat formulaic and lacking deeper warmth or explicit normalization of ambivalence.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing the patient’s ambivalence and concerns (e.g., “You’re really weighing the risks and benefits carefully…”). However, reflections tend to be surface-level and primarily paraphrasing content rather than capturing emotional nuance or underlying values. The patient’s flat affect and hesitancy are not deeply explored or reflected back with emotional attunement. For example, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect the patient’s subtle worry or frustration about the trade-offs involved. The empathy is consistent but lacks depth and emotional resonance.

**Summary:**  
Adequate understanding and paraphrasing, but limited emotional depth and nuance in reflections.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting the patient’s input and respecting autonomy (e.g., “Would it be okay if I shared…”). There is clear effort to evoke the patient’s own motivations and concerns, and the psychiatrist avoids directive or authoritarian language. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks a fully evocative style that draws out the patient’s deeper values or ambivalence. The psychiatrist does not explicitly reinforce the patient’s autonomy beyond basic invitations, nor does the conversation fully co-create the agenda or plan. The spirit of MI is present but not fully embodied.

**Summary:**  
Moderate collaboration and autonomy support, but limited evocation and partnership depth.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, particularly when discussing the patient’s concern for his grandparents and the desire to avoid downtime from illness. The patient offers statements of desire and reasons for change (e.g., “I do worry about my grandparents getting sick”). However, the psychiatrist misses opportunities to amplify or deepen this change talk. For example, the psychiatrist could have reflected and amplified the patient’s expressed care for family or explored what “staying active” means in more detail to evoke stronger motivation. There is no explicit elicitation of commitment or confidence language, and change talk is not strategically reinforced.

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but lacks consistent amplification and strategic reinforcement.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk (e.g., concerns about side effects, doubts about vaccine effectiveness) without reinforcing it. However, responses are mostly neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or explicitly normalize ambivalence to reduce resistance. For example, the patient’s worry about side effects disrupting his routine is acknowledged but not reframed or gently challenged to reduce sustain talk impact.

**Summary:**  
Occasional acknowledgment of sustain talk but lacks skillful softening or strategic redirection.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites the patient’s perspective and avoids an expert-driven approach. The use of permission-seeking language (“Would it be okay if I shared…”) and agenda-setting (“What would you like to focus on?”) supports partnership. However, the collaboration remains somewhat superficial and procedural rather than fully equal or co-created. The psychiatrist does not explicitly share power or negotiate the process beyond initial agenda-setting.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaborative stance with good client-centered language, but partnership could be deepened by more explicit co-creation.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary

**Strengths:**  
- Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive language.  
- Some evocation of change talk related to family concerns and personal health priorities.

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and fail to capture underlying feelings or values.  
- Change talk is not consistently amplified or strategically reinforced.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Partnership is present but could be more fully co-created and empowering.  
- The session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks the dynamic, evocative spirit characteristic of expert MI.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.3/5** (rounded down to 3 for strictness)

- Appropriately asks permission before sharing information.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are mostly surface-level; need more complex, emotionally attuned reflections.  
- Affirmations are minimal and generic; should affirm patient’s strengths and values more explicitly.  
- Change talk is not consistently amplified or deepened.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or reframed.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak; no clear next steps or consolidation of motivation.  
- Engagement could be improved by deeper emotional attunement and personalized connection.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feelings and meaning.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it to deepen motivation. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and gently guide toward change.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations recognizing the patient’s thoughtful consideration, care for family, and openness.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize key points and change talk. Collaboratively develop concrete next steps and check for patient buy-in.

6. **Increase Emotional Engagement:**  
   Attend more closely to the patient’s affect and emotional cues to build rapport and deepen connection.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive or scripted language to maintain a natural, dynamic conversation flow.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing principles, with consistent respect, collaboration, and some evocation of change talk. However, it falls short of excellence due to formulaic responses, limited emotional depth in reflections, minimal affirmations, and weak planning. To improve, the psychiatrist must deepen emotional attunement, skillfully handle ambivalence, amplify change talk, and collaboratively develop clear next steps. These improvements will better support the patient’s motivation and decision-making regarding vaccination.
