# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation – Marcus Rivera Diabetes Management Session

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect and a supportive stance toward Marcus. Early on, the psychiatrist validates Marcus’s feelings of overwhelm and uncertainty without judgment (“It makes sense that you’d want to focus on the part that feels most uncertain right now”). The tone remains warm and encouraging throughout, affirming Marcus’s willingness to try despite hesitations. However, the acceptance is somewhat surface-level and formulaic; there is limited explicit normalization of the struggle with chronic illness management or deeper expressions of unconditional positive regard. For example, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly acknowledged the difficulty of adapting to a new diagnosis and the emotional burden Marcus carries. This would elevate acceptance to a 5.

**Summary:**  
Generally respectful and supportive, but lacks deeper normalization and explicit unconditional positive regard.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Marcus’s ambivalence and emotional state. Reflections such as “It sounds like having a clear, simple plan… could really ease some of that overwhelm” and “That shows a lot of practical insight and a realistic sense of pacing yourself” show understanding of Marcus’s experience. The psychiatrist also acknowledges Marcus’s concerns about motivation and consistency. However, reflections tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally restate content rather than exploring underlying emotions or fears more deeply (e.g., anxiety about failure or feelings of discouragement). There is room to deepen emotional attunement and validate the emotional toll of managing a chronic illness.

**Summary:**  
Good understanding and reflective listening, but could deepen emotional nuance and validation.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, inviting Marcus’s input and supporting autonomy (“What do you think would help you feel more comfortable or confident…?” “Would it be okay if I offered a bit of information…?”). The session avoids directive or authoritarian language and respects Marcus’s pace and readiness. The psychiatrist evokes Marcus’s own ideas for manageable steps and acknowledges his expertise in his daily routine. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks a fully evocative style that draws out deeper motivations or values beyond practical concerns. There is limited explicit reinforcement of Marcus’s autonomy beyond general invitations, and the partnership, while present, could be more explicitly co-created.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and explicitly co-creative.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as Marcus’s willingness to try blood sugar monitoring and recognition of the need to learn. However, the evocation is limited in depth and frequency. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to amplify or reinforce change talk more robustly (e.g., when Marcus says “I’m willing to give that a shot,” the psychiatrist could have reflected and amplified this commitment). There is no explicit elicitation of commitment language or exploration of Marcus’s confidence beyond a single scaling question. The psychiatrist could have probed more to deepen Marcus’s reasons for change and to evoke stronger commitment or confidence statements.

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk, but inconsistent and shallow; missed opportunities to amplify and deepen motivation.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**

**Explanation:**  
Marcus expresses sustain talk and ambivalence, such as concerns about forgetting or losing motivation. The psychiatrist acknowledges these concerns neutrally but does not actively soften or redirect sustain talk. For example, when Marcus expresses uncertainty about keeping up with the routine, the psychiatrist reflects but does not use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to reduce the impact of sustain talk. The session lacks explicit normalization of ambivalence or gentle guidance toward resolving sustain talk.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected; lacks strategic handling of ambivalence.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Marcus’s perspective and preferences, using inclusive language and avoiding an expert-driven approach. The psychiatrist asks permission before offering information and checks in on Marcus’s readiness. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic. There is limited explicit sharing of power or co-creation of the agenda or plan beyond general invitations. The psychiatrist could more explicitly acknowledge Marcus’s expertise and invite him to lead the process more fully.

**Summary:**  
Good collaboration and client-centeredness, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit co-creation and power sharing.

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect feelings and meaning, not just content.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk emerges, reflect and reinforce it explicitly. Ask questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and gentle reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, personalized affirmations recognizing Marcus’s strengths, efforts, and insights.

5. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Explicitly summarize change talk and collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan. Check for commitment and confidence.

6. **Increase Evocation and Collaboration:**  
   More actively draw out Marcus’s own motivations, values, and ideas. Share power by explicitly inviting Marcus to lead and co-create the process.

7. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses and avoid repetitive phrasing. Be present and responsive to the unique flow of the conversation.
