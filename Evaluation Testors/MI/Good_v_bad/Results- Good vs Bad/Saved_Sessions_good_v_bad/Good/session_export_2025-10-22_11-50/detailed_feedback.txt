### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections often simple and formulaic; need more emotional depth and complexity.  
- Sustain talk handled neutrally rather than skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly co-created with shared agenda and power.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feelings such as shame, fear, or hope.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and gentle reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence and validate the difficulty of change.

3. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Explicitly negotiate agenda and co-create plans with the client. Use language that shares power and acknowledges the client’s expertise.

4. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific, genuine affirmations that recognize the client’s courage, insight, and strengths. Avoid generic praise.

5. **Ask Permission Before Advice:**  
   Before offering suggestions or information, explicitly ask for permission to maintain collaboration and respect autonomy.

6. **Improve Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Provide explicit, structured summaries that pull together change talk and clarify next steps. Collaboratively develop concrete, actionable plans and check for client buy-in.

7. **Increase Evocation of Commitment and Confidence:**  
   When change talk emerges, amplify and explore it further, especially commitment and confidence language, to strengthen motivation.

8. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize questions and reflections to the client’s unique context and emotional state. Be present and responsive rather than relying on scripted phrasing.

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a solid foundation in motivational interviewing, with consistent respect, empathy, and collaboration. The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk and supports autonomy. However, the session falls short of excellence due to formulaic reflections, neutral handling of sustain talk, and weak planning and summarizing. To improve, the psychiatrist should deepen emotional attunement, skillfully manage ambivalence, and more actively co-create plans and evoke commitment. These improvements will enhance the motivational interviewing spirit and effectiveness, ultimately better supporting Adrian’s readiness and capacity for change.
