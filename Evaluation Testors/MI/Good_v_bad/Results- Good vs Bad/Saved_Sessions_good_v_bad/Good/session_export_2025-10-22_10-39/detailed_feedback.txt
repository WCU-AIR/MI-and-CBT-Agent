## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the session. From the opening question—“What would you like to focus on or talk about during our session today?”—the psychiatrist invites the patient’s agenda, showing respect for autonomy. The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s reflections and concerns without judgment, e.g., “Thank you for sharing that with me,” and “It sounds like you’re really navigating a tricky balance.” There is no evidence of labeling, disapproval, or minimizing the patient’s experience. The tone remains warm, supportive, and validating, which fosters a safe environment for honest disclosure.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, creating a respectful and supportive atmosphere that encourages openness.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. Reflections accurately capture the patient’s ambivalence and emotional experience, such as “That tension between wanting to belong and wanting to protect your future must feel pretty challenging.” The psychiatrist also reflects the patient’s values and concerns effectively, e.g., linking soccer goals to identity and future security. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and formulaic. There is limited exploration of deeper emotional nuances or underlying fears (e.g., the patient’s anxiety about social rejection or parental consequences). The empathy is consistent but could be deepened by more complex reflections that capture emotional subtext.

**Summary:**  
Good empathic engagement with accurate reflections, but lacks consistent depth and emotional nuance.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, frequently inviting the patient’s input and emphasizing autonomy (“What would you like to focus on?” “What feels doable and fitting for you?”). The session avoids directive or authoritarian language and supports the patient’s self-efficacy. The psychiatrist evokes the patient’s own motivations and strategies, reinforcing personal values and strengths. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat formulaic and lacks dynamic evocation or explicit partnership in co-creating the agenda or plan. The psychiatrist could more explicitly share power by inviting the patient to lead agenda-setting or decision-making more fully.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but could be more evocative and explicitly co-creative.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk throughout the session. The patient articulates desire, reasons, ability, and commitment language (e.g., “I don’t wanna become addicted,” “I want to stick to my goals,” “I’d probably say around a 5” confidence). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, linking them to the patient’s values and goals. There is a clear focus on strengthening motivation and confidence. However, while change talk is frequently evoked, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to deepen or amplify it further, such as exploring the patient’s confidence fluctuations or eliciting more detailed commitment language.

**Summary:**  
Effective evocation and reinforcement of change talk, though with room to deepen and amplify commitment language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk (e.g., patient’s minimization: “I’ve only tried drinking once or twice,” “It’s tough because it feels like everyone’s doing it”), but responses tend to be neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. The psychiatrist validates the patient’s ambivalence but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or reframe sustain talk to reduce its impact. For example, the tension between social pressure and goals is acknowledged but not explicitly normalized or reframed to gently shift the patient toward change. This limits the session’s ability to resolve ambivalence more effectively.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is recognized but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, client-centered stance, inviting the patient’s perspective and choices throughout. Language such as “What feels doable and fitting for you?” and “How can I or others support you?” reflects partnership. However, the psychiatrist occasionally leads the session with structured questions and summaries, which, while supportive, limit full co-creation. There is little explicit negotiation of agenda or shared decision-making beyond the initial question. The partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit sharing of power and inviting the patient to direct the flow or priorities more actively.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and respect for autonomy, but partnership could be deepened with more explicit co-creation.

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- The psychiatrist creates a warm, accepting, and nonjudgmental environment.  
- Uses open questions effectively to engage the patient.  
- Reflects and reinforces patient’s values and motivations well.  
- Supports autonomy and partnership consistently.  
- Elicits meaningful change talk and guides toward practical planning.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are often simple and lack emotional depth; more complex reflections are needed to capture ambivalence and emotional nuance.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or reframed; use of double-sided reflections or normalization could improve ambivalence resolution.  
- Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation and shared agenda-setting.  
- Change talk evocation could be deepened, especially around commitment and confidence language.  
- Summaries and planning could be more integrative and personalized rather than formulaic.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex and amplified reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feeling and meaning.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and normalization to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Reframe sustain talk to reduce its impact.

3. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Invite the patient to co-create the agenda and plan more explicitly. Share power by asking what the patient wants to focus on next or how they want to proceed.

4. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk arises, reflect and amplify it strategically, especially commitment and confidence language. Ask questions like, “What makes you feel hopeful about this?” or “What would help you feel more confident?”

5. **Strengthen Summarizing and Planning:**  
   Use summaries to integrate change talk and reinforce motivation. Collaboratively develop concrete, actionable plans with clear next steps and check for patient buy-in.

6. **Avoid Formulaic Language:**  
   Personalize reflections, affirmations, and summaries to the patient’s unique experience. Avoid repetitive phrasing to maintain engagement and authenticity.

Improving these areas will better resolve ambivalence and strengthen the patient’s motivation and confidence for change.
