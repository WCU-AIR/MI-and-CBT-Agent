## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Empathy:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture the patient’s feelings and underlying concerns, not just factual content. For example, reflect possible anxiety or worry about medication safety.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When the patient expresses motivation or commitment, reflect and reinforce it to strengthen resolve. Ask evocative questions like, “What makes you feel confident about telling your doctor?”

3. **Address Sustain Talk and Barriers:**  
   Acknowledge the patient’s time constraints and possible reluctance to engage more deeply. Use double-sided reflections to validate ambivalence while gently guiding toward change.

4. **Enhance Partnership:**  
   Invite the patient to co-create the agenda and next steps explicitly. Recognize the patient’s expertise and preferences more fully.

5. **Strengthen Affirmations:**  
   Offer specific affirmations that recognize the patient’s proactive behavior and clarity, e.g., “It’s great that you’re taking the initiative to ask about medication safety despite your busy schedule.”

6. **Improve Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Summarize key points and change talk explicitly. Collaboratively develop a clear plan or next steps and check for patient agreement.

7. **Balance Information with Evocation:**  
   While providing necessary information, continue to evoke the patient’s own motivations, concerns, and ideas to maintain the spirit of MI.
