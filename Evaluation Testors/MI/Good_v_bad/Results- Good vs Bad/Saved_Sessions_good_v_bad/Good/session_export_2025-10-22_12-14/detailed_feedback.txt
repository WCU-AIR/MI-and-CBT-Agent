## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect toward Elena throughout the session. From the outset, the psychiatrist validates Elena’s concerns without judgment (“Thank you for sharing that with me—it sounds like you’ve been carrying some real concerns…”), normalizes ambivalence (“It’s completely understandable to feel uncertain…”), and explicitly honors her perspective as a parent (“I really appreciate how thoughtfully you’re approaching this…”). There is no evidence of labeling, disapproval, or pressure. The psychiatrist’s tone is warm, supportive, and patient-centered, creating a safe space for Elena’s hesitancy and doubts.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in acceptance, providing a consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental environment that encourages openness.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Elena’s ambivalence and emotional experience. For example, reflections such as “It sounds like you’re really weighing the potential risks…” and “That tension must feel like walking a tightrope…” show good understanding of Elena’s internal conflict. The psychiatrist also acknowledges Elena’s skepticism and desire for trustworthy information. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally formulaic. There is room to deepen emotional nuance, for example by exploring Elena’s underlying fears or anxieties more explicitly or validating the emotional burden of her decision-making process.

**Summary:**  
Good empathic engagement with accurate reflections, but could be enhanced by deeper emotional attunement and more nuanced exploration of feelings.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance throughout, inviting Elena’s input and supporting her autonomy (“Would it be okay if I shared some information…?”). The session avoids directive or authoritarian approaches, instead evoking Elena’s own motivations and concerns. The psychiatrist skillfully balances evocation and education, respecting Elena’s pace and readiness. However, the interaction occasionally feels somewhat scripted or formulaic, with repeated phrasing (“It sounds like…”) and limited spontaneous exploration. The psychiatrist could further enhance the evocative spirit by more explicitly eliciting Elena’s values and personal reasons for change, and by co-creating next steps more actively.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support, but the session could be more dynamic and evocative to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, such as Elena’s tentative openness (“I’m willing to listen and learn a bit more…”) and recognition of illness risks (“Wow, I didn’t realize it was so dangerous.”). The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these statements, encouraging further exploration of readiness and confidence. However, while change talk is present and supported, the psychiatrist misses some opportunities to deepen commitment language or explore Elena’s confidence and ability more explicitly. For example, the session could include more questions like “What would make you feel more confident about vaccinating?” or “How might you take a first step when you’re ready?”

**Summary:**  
Good evocation and reinforcement of change talk, but could be strengthened by more strategic elicitation of commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**

The use of double-sided reflections helps validate Elena’s fears about side effects while gently guiding toward consideration of vaccination benefits. The psychiatrist could improve by more explicitly normalizing ambivalence and gently shifting sustain talk toward change talk with strategic questions or affirmations.

**Summary:**  
Effective acknowledgment and neutral softening of sustain talk, but could be more actively strategic in redirecting sustain talk toward change.

### 6. Partnership

### Areas for Improvement

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, values, and ambivalence. Reflect feelings and meaning, not just content.

2. **Enhance Evocation of Commitment and Confidence:**  
   Explicitly ask about readiness, confidence, and specific intentions to strengthen commitment language.

3. **Strategically Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and gentle redirection to move sustain talk toward change talk.

4. **Strengthen Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan and summarize key change talk to reinforce motivation and commitment.

5. **Increase Spontaneity and Personalization:**  
   Avoid formulaic phrasing; tailor responses dynamically to the client’s unique expressions and emotional cues.

6. **Expand Affirmations:**  
   Offer more specific affirmations recognizing client strengths, past successes, and values.

---

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a strong foundation in motivational interviewing with excellent acceptance, partnership, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk and respects client pace. However, to reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen reflections, more actively elicit commitment and confidence, and strengthen planning and summarizing. Avoiding formulaic responses and enhancing emotional attunement will further improve the quality and impact of the session.
