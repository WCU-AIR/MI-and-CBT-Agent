# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. They acknowledge the patient’s perspective without overt criticism or disapproval, for example, “Thanks for sharing that—it sounds like the drinking at the social gathering feels pretty normal to you,” and “I appreciate how grounded you seem in your current experience.” These statements convey respect and validation of Milan’s experience. However, the acceptance sometimes feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or fully unconditional. There is little explicit normalization of ambivalence or struggle, and the psychiatrist does not explicitly affirm Milan’s worth or intrinsic value beyond his drinking behavior.  

**Summary:**  
Respectful and supportive overall, but lacks the warmth and explicit unconditional positive regard that would elevate this to a 5.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and attempts to understand Milan’s perspective, as seen in reflections like, “It sounds like your drinking fits into a pattern that feels pretty typical and manageable for you.” However, the reflections tend to be surface-level and factual rather than deeply empathic or emotionally nuanced. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore Milan’s underlying feelings, such as defensiveness, social pressure, or possible fears about change. For example, when Milan expresses defensiveness (“I really don’t think it’s that bad”), the psychiatrist does not reflect the emotional tone or validate the difficulty of facing concerns about drinking. The empathy is present but inconsistent and somewhat mechanical.  

**Summary:**  
Adequate understanding of content but lacks depth in emotional attunement and perspective-taking.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and supports autonomy, frequently inviting Milan’s input (“What do you think might be some reasons…?” “What do you imagine would be the best way for you to bring that plan into action?”). There is no evidence of overt persuasion or directive advice, which is positive. However, the session feels somewhat formulaic and lacks strong evocation of Milan’s own motivations or values. The psychiatrist does not actively explore Milan’s ambivalence beyond surface-level questioning, nor do they explicitly reinforce Milan’s autonomy with statements like “It’s completely your choice.” The spirit of MI is present but not fully embodied or dynamic.  

**Summary:**  
Collaborative and autonomy-supportive but lacks strong evocation and deeper partnership.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as Milan’s recognition of limits (“I guess being careful about not going overboard is something I do”) and tentative plans (“just staying aware and not letting things get out of hand”). However, these instances are not consistently amplified or reinforced. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to deepen change talk by asking evocative follow-ups or reflecting with emphasis to strengthen motivation (e.g., “What makes you feel confident about keeping things chill?”). Commitment language is weak and not explicitly elicited or consolidated. The evocation of change talk is present but shallow and inconsistent.  

**Summary:**  
Some evocation of change talk but lacks strategic amplification and reinforcement.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 2/5**  

**Explanation:**  
Sustain talk is frequent in Milan’s responses (“I don’t really have any reasons to change,” “I just don’t see what’s wrong with how it is”). The psychiatrist acknowledges this but does not actively soften or redirect sustain talk. There are no double-sided reflections or reframing attempts to gently shift Milan’s perspective. Instead, the psychiatrist often neutrally reflects sustain talk without challenging or exploring it further. This approach risks allowing sustain talk to dominate without resolution or movement toward change.  

**Summary:**  
Rarely softens or redirects sustain talk; mostly neutral reflections that do not reduce sustain talk’s impact.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently invites Milan’s input and avoids an expert-driven stance (“What do you think might be different for you?” “What feels most doable for you?”). This demonstrates a strong collaborative approach. However, the partnership is somewhat superficial and formulaic, lacking explicit negotiation of agenda or shared decision-making language that would demonstrate full equality. The psychiatrist does not explicitly acknowledge Milan’s expertise in his own life beyond general invitations to speak.  

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and client-centeredness but could deepen power-sharing and co-creation.

---

## Overall Global Ratings Summary  

**Strengths:**  
- Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone.  
- Collaborative and autonomy-supportive stance.  
- Some elicitation of change talk and client input.  

**Areas for Improvement:**  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and nuanced empathy.  
- Sustain talk is not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Change talk is not consistently amplified or consolidated.  
- Partnership is present but could be more explicitly equal and co-created.  
- The session feels formulaic and lacks dynamic evocation of motivation.  

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3/5**

## Summary of Feedback

### Strengths  
- Maintains respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout.  
- Uses open questions to invite client perspective.  
- Supports autonomy and collaboration.  
- Elicits some change talk and tentative plans.  

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections lack emotional depth and complexity.  
- Sustain talk is not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- Affirmations are sparse and generic; need to highlight strengths more.  
- No explicit permission sought when offering suggestions or reflections.  
- Planning and summarizing are weak or absent.  
- Evocation of motivation is superficial and formulaic.  
- Partnership could be more explicitly equal and co-created.  

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex and amplified reflections that capture underlying emotions, ambivalence, and values. Reflect not only content but also feelings and meaning.

2. **Amplify Change Talk:**  
   When change talk arises, reflect it with emphasis, ask evocative follow-ups, and strategically reinforce motivation. Explicitly elicit commitment and confidence language.

3. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Use double-sided reflections and reframing to acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. Normalize ambivalence and validate the difficulty of change.

4. **Increase Specific Affirmations:**  
   Offer frequent, genuine affirmations that recognize Milan’s strengths, self-awareness, and efforts. Avoid generic praise.

5. **Seek Permission for Advice:**  
   When offering suggestions or information, explicitly ask permission to respect autonomy and collaboration.

6. **Enhance Planning and Summarizing:**  
   Develop concrete, actionable plans collaboratively. Use structured summaries to consolidate change talk and reinforce commitment.

7. **Strengthen Partnership:**  
   Explicitly acknowledge Milan’s expertise and co-create the agenda and plan. Use language that minimizes power differentials.

8. **Avoid Formulaic Responses:**  
   Personalize responses to Milan’s unique context and emotional tone. Be responsive and present rather than relying on scripted questions or reflections.

---

## Overall Score: **3/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates a foundational understanding of motivational interviewing principles, with consistent respect, collaboration, and some elicitation of change talk. However, it falls short of excellence due to superficial reflections, weak handling of sustain talk, minimal affirmations, and lack of concrete planning or summarizing. To improve, the psychiatrist must deepen emotional attunement, skillfully manage ambivalence, and more actively evoke and reinforce Milan’s own motivations and strengths.
