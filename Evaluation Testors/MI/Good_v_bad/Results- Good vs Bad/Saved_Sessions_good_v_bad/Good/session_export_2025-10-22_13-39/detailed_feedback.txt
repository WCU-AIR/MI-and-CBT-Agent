# Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation of Asthma Management Session

---

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient throughout the session. From the opening question inviting the patient to set the agenda (“What would you like to focus on…?”) to affirming the patient’s concerns and ambivalence, the psychiatrist maintains a warm, supportive tone. The patient’s worries about inhalers and side effects are validated repeatedly without judgment or dismissal (“It makes sense that you’d want to explore…”; “It sounds like you’re carrying a lot of understandable concern…”). The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the patient’s autonomy and right to make decisions, which further supports acceptance. There are no instances of labeling, judgment, or pressure.

**Summary:**  
The psychiatrist excels in creating a safe, nonjudgmental environment that encourages open sharing and respects the patient’s perspective.

---

### 2. Empathy  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking. Reflections accurately capture the patient’s ambivalence and emotional state (“You’re caught between wanting to reduce your reliance on inhalers and the fear of what might happen…”; “You’re valuing structure and connection…”). The psychiatrist also acknowledges the patient’s fears and the difficulty of balancing health and anxiety. However, while reflections are accurate and supportive, they tend to be somewhat surface-level and occasionally formulaic. There is limited exploration of deeper emotional nuances, such as the patient’s anxiety or fear beyond the cognitive level. For example, the psychiatrist could have reflected more on the patient’s anxiety or fear of “freaking out” or the emotional toll of feeling “all over the place.”  

**Summary:**  
Good empathic understanding is evident, but reflections could be deepened to capture more emotional nuance and complexity.

---

### 3. Spirit of MI  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist consistently adopts a collaborative stance, inviting the patient to set the agenda and express concerns. Autonomy is emphasized (“It sounds like honesty and feeling respected in your choices are really important to you”). The psychiatrist evokes the patient’s own motivations and ideas for change, such as asking about manageable first steps and supports the patient’s control over the pace of change. There is no evidence of persuasion or directive advice without permission. However, the session occasionally feels somewhat structured and formulaic, especially in the way information is presented and questions are posed. The psychiatrist could enhance evocation by more explicitly eliciting the patient’s values or goals related to asthma management and by inviting the patient to elaborate on their hopes or fears more deeply.

**Summary:**  
Strong collaboration and autonomy support are present, but the session could be more evocative and less formulaic to fully embody the MI spirit.

---

### 4. Cultivating Change Talk  
**Score: 4/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk by asking about the importance of managing asthma differently (“On a scale from 0 to 10, how important…?”), past successes (“Have there been times when you felt more in control?”), and manageable next steps (“What might be a manageable first step?”). The patient offers several statements of desire, ability, reasons, and need for change. The psychiatrist reflects and reinforces these statements, helping to amplify motivation. However, there is a missed opportunity to more explicitly evoke commitment language or confidence statements. For example, the psychiatrist could have asked about the patient’s confidence in carrying out the plan or explored what would help increase confidence.

**Summary:**  
Change talk is well evoked and reinforced, but could be deepened by explicitly eliciting commitment and confidence language.

---

### 5. Softening Sustain Talk  
**Score: 3/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s sustain talk and ambivalence (“I’m still a bit worried about what happens if I try to cut back too quickly…”), but responses tend to be neutral reflections rather than active softening or strategic redirection. There is little use of double-sided reflections that acknowledge sustain talk while gently guiding toward change. The psychiatrist does not explicitly normalize ambivalence or validate the difficulty of change in a way that reduces resistance. For example, the psychiatrist could have said, “It’s completely normal to feel nervous about making changes when you’ve seen others struggle,” which might soften sustain talk more effectively.

**Summary:**  
Sustain talk is recognized but not skillfully softened or redirected; more strategic handling is needed to reduce resistance.

---

### 6. Partnership  
**Score: 5/5**  

**Explanation:**  
The psychiatrist maintains a fully collaborative stance, inviting the patient to guide the conversation and respecting her choices throughout. The patient is invited to set the agenda, choose topics, and decide on next steps. The psychiatrist explicitly offers to share information only with permission and checks in frequently to ensure the patient’s comfort and agreement. The language used minimizes power differentials and promotes equality (“If it’s okay with you, I can share some information…”). The patient’s expertise in her own experience is consistently acknowledged.

**Summary:**  
Excellent demonstration of partnership, with clear client-centeredness and shared decision-making.

---

**Strengths:**  
- Consistent acceptance and respect.  
- Good empathic listening and reflective statements.  
- Strong partnership and autonomy support.

**Areas for Improvement:**

- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not actively softened or redirected.  
- More explicit elicitation of commitment and confidence language would strengthen change talk cultivation.

- Good use of open questions and reflections to explore ambivalence and motivation.

- Permission sought before providing information.

### Areas for Improvement  
- Reflections are often surface-level; more complex, emotionally nuanced reflections are needed.  
- Sustain talk is acknowledged but not skillfully softened or redirected.  
- More explicit elicitation of commitment and confidence language would strengthen motivation.  
- Summaries are implicit; explicit, structured summaries would consolidate gains.  
- The session occasionally feels formulaic; more fluid, personalized evocation would enhance MI spirit.  
- Planning could be more concrete and collaboratively consolidated.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Deepen Reflections:**  
   Use more complex reflections that capture underlying emotions, fears, and ambivalence to deepen patient engagement and understanding.

2. **Skillfully Soften Sustain Talk:**  
   Employ double-sided reflections and normalization to reduce resistance and gently guide the patient toward change.

3. **Explicitly Elicit Commitment and Confidence:**  
   Ask direct questions about the patient’s confidence and readiness to carry out plans, and reinforce commitment language.

4. **Enhance Summarizing:**  
   Provide clear, structured summaries that pull together change talk, reinforce motivation, and clarify next steps.

5. **Personalize and Evoke More Deeply:**  
   Avoid formulaic phrasing; tailor questions and reflections to the patient’s unique experience and values to evoke richer motivation.

6. **Consolidate Planning:**  
   Collaboratively develop a concrete, actionable plan with clear steps and supports, checking for patient buy-in and confidence.

## Overall Score: **4/5**

**Summary:**  
This session demonstrates strong foundational MI skills, with excellent acceptance, partnership, and autonomy support. The psychiatrist effectively explores ambivalence and elicits change talk, maintaining a clear focus on asthma management. However, to reach a higher level of proficiency, the psychiatrist should deepen reflections, skillfully soften sustain talk, explicitly elicit commitment and confidence, and provide more structured summaries and concrete planning. Greater personalization and fluidity would also enhance the evocative spirit of MI and better support the patient’s motivation for change.
