Psychiatry Clinical Evaluation – Core of Psychiatry

This document outlines a structured framework for assessing clinical interviewing skills in psychiatry. It is intended for use in training, supervision, and evaluation of learners such as medical students, residents, or clinicians in psychiatric settings. The framework focuses on three core categories:

1. Rapport Building
2. Interview Technique
3. Interview Content

Each subcategory is scored on a 1–5 scale, with clear descriptions and behavioral examples provided for each level. This tool emphasizes not just the collection of clinical information, but also the quality of engagement, communication, cultural sensitivity, and the therapeutic use of the interview process.

CATEGORY 1: RAPPORT BUILDING

1. Establishes Relationship

Score 5:
- Introduces self and role clearly
- Explains interview purpose
- Respectful tone/body language
- Starts with warm, open-ended question
Example: “Hi, I’m Dr. Lee, a psychiatry resident. I’d like to spend the next hour learning more about you and what brought you in today. Is that okay?”

Score 4:
- Introduces self and purpose
- Respectful and professional
- Some open-ended questions
Example: “I’m Dr. Lee. I’m here to hear your story. What’s been going on lately?”

Score 3:
- Basic intro, minimal warmth
Example: “I'm Dr. Lee. What brings you in?”

Score 2:
- Rushed/unclear intro, closed tone
Example: “You’re here for an evaluation, correct?”

Score 1:
- No proper introduction
Example: “Start talking.”

---

2. Develops and Sustains Rapport

Score 5:
- Consistently empathic and engaged
- Verbal/non-verbal cues, acknowledges distress
Example: “That sounds incredibly difficult. I’m really sorry you’ve had to go through that.”

Score 4:
- Mostly empathic, minor lapses
Example: “That must’ve been tough. Thanks for sharing that.”

Score 3:
- Mixed engagement, rote tone
Example: “Okay. I understand.”

Score 2:
- Detached tone, weak responses
Example: “So that happened. What next?”

Score 1:
- Cold, judgmental
Example: “You shouldn’t feel that way.”

---

3. Maintains Control of the Interview

Score 5:
- Respectfully redirects and organizes conversation
Example: “That's important—let's come back to that…”

Score 4:
- Mostly manages flow; minor lapses
Example: “We’ll get to that soon…”

Score 3:
- Moderate control, allows tangents
Example: “Okay... let’s go back to...”

Score 2:
- Struggles to redirect
Example: “Wait, what were we talking about?”

Score 1:
- No effort to control or redirect

---

4. Demonstrates Cultural Sensitivity

Score 5:
- Integrates cultural understanding proactively
Example: “Are there any cultural or spiritual beliefs…”

Score 4:
- Mostly culturally aware
Example: “How does your background affect...”

Score 3:
- Responds to cultural cues when prompted
Example: “You mentioned your religion...”

Score 2:
- Minimal or dismissive
Example: “That’s not really relevant...”

Score 1:
- Disregards cultural context
Example: “That stuff doesn’t matter here.”

---

5. Ends the Interview Smoothly

Score 5:
- Warm, professional closure with summary
Example: “Thank you for being so open today…”

Score 4:
- Organized wrap-up, slightly awkward
Example: “We’ve talked about your mood...”

Score 3:
- Basic closure, no summary
Example: “Okay, I think we’re done for today.”

Score 2:
- Abrupt or unclear
Example: “That’s it.”

Score 1:
- Ends abruptly without closing
Example: Leaves without a word

---

CATEGORY 2: INTERVIEW TECHNIQUE

1. Information Gathering with Open Exploration

Score 5:
- Attentive body language, open prompts
Example: “Tell me about what that was like for you.”

Score 4:
- Mostly attentive, mild distraction
Example: “Can you describe that more?”

Score 3:
- Variable listening, minor distraction
Example: “Uh-huh... go on.”

Score 2:
- Poor engagement, note-taking dominates
Example: “When did that happen?”

Score 1:
- No engagement
Example: No follow-ups

---

2. Facilitative Questioning Style

Score 5:
- Logical, open, non-leading
Example: “What was going through your mind…”

Score 4:
- Logical but minor clarity issues
Example: “Did anything specific happen...”

Score 3:
- Mixed style, clinical tone
Example: “Rate the intensity of your anhedonia.”

Score 2:
- Rigid, leading
Example: “You’re sad because of your mom, right?”

Score 1:
- Disjointed or overly technical
Example: “When did symptom cluster one begin?”

---

3. Pursues Important Information

Score 5:
- Follows up on cues deeply
Example: “You mentioned you stopped sleeping…”

Score 4:
- Picks up most cues
Example: “You said you felt off…”

Score 3:
- Responds only to obvious cues
Example: “Okay. Anything else?”

Score 2:
- Misses cues
Example: “Explain that.”

Score 1:
- Ignores major disclosures

---

4. Maintains Flow

Score 5:
- Seamlessly manages and reframes
Example: “Let’s pause here—you’ve said so much…”

Score 4:
- Mostly smooth
Example: “You’re doing well—take your time.”

Score 3:
- Awkward transitions
Example: “Okay… let’s talk about something else…”

Score 2:
- Disorganized, emotionally unresponsive
Example: “I don’t know where to go from here.”

Score 1:
- Avoids or disrupts flow
Example: “Let’s skip that part.”

---

CATEGORY 3: INTERVIEW CONTENT

1. Identifies the Person

Score 5:
- Complete demographic info
Example: “Name, age, occupation, who do you live with…”

Score 4:
- Mostly complete
Example: “What’s your age and occupation?”

Score 3:
- Some demographics covered
Example: “Your age?”

Score 2:
- Very limited
Example: “Name?”

Score 1:
- No demographic info collected

---

2. Identifies Presenting Complaints and History

Score 5:
- Detailed and structured
Example: Timeline, symptoms, impact, stressors, past episodes, treatments

Score 4:
- Mostly complete
Example: “How long have you felt this way?”

Score 3:
- Moderate detail
Example: “What’s wrong?”

Score 2:
- Superficial
Example: “Tell me about your illness.”

Score 1:
- Fragmented or missing

---

3. Screens for Symptoms and Differential Diagnosis

Score 5:
- Covers psychosis, mania, substance use, etc.
Example: “Heard voices? Drink alcohol? Motivation to change?”

Score 4:
- Mostly thorough
Example: “Do you drink? Any panic attacks?”

Score 3:
- Obvious issues only
Example: “Do you use alcohol?”

Score 2:
- Minimal screening
Example: “Any mood problems?”

Score 1:
- No screening done

---

4. Ensures Safety

Score 5:
- Full risk and safety screen
Example: “Thoughts of harm? Medications? Allergies?”

Score 4:
- Good, with some gaps
Example: “Any meds? Harm thoughts?”

Score 3:
- Basic check
Example: “Any thoughts of harm?”

Score 2:
- Incomplete
Example: “Are you okay?”

Score 1:
- No safety check

---

5. Identifies Relevant Past History

Score 5:
- Covers medical, psychiatric, family, forensic
Example: “Any past illnesses? Family mental health issues?”

Score 4:
- Most domains covered
Example: “Any history of mental illness?”

Score 3:
- Basic history
Example: “Any past illnesses?”

Score 2:
- Patchy info
Example: “Any medical problems?”

Score 1:
- No past history asked

---

6. Identifies Developmental and Psychosocial History

Score 5:
- Comprehensive psychosocial/developmental history
Example: Family, childhood, trauma, supports, work, spirituality, beliefs

Score 4:
- Most domains touched
Example: “How’s family? Any trauma?”

Score 3:
- General background only
Example: “Tell me about your childhood.”

Score 2:
- Brief or inconsistent
Example: “Any family?”

Score 1:
- No psychosocial history
