Category 1: Rapport Building
Establishes Relationship
Score	Description
5	Clearly introduces self and role, explains interview purpose thoroughly, uses respectful tone and body language, and begins with a warm, open-ended question. Example: "Hi, I’m Dr. Lee, a psychiatry resident. I’d like to spend the next hour learning more about you and what brought you in today. Is that okay?" Expected instances: All 4 behaviors present at the beginning of the interview (1 full instance).
4	Introduces self and purpose, respectful and professional, asks some open-ended questions. Example: "I’m Dr. Lee. I’m here to hear your story. What’s been going on lately?" Expected instances: At least 3 of 4 behaviors demonstrated (1 brief but adequate instance).
3	Basic introduction, somewhat respectful, minimal open-ended questioning. Example: "I'm Dr. Lee. What brings you in?" Expected instances: 2 of 4 behaviors, somewhat incomplete introduction.
2	Introduction unclear or rushed, occasionally disrespectful or too formal, starts with closed or overly clinical questions. Example: "You're here for an evaluation, correct?" Expected instances: 0–1 behaviors; introduction lacks clarity or warmth.
1	Fails to introduce self or explain purpose, appears dismissive or abrupt. Example: "Start talking." Expected instances: No meaningful introduction.

Develops and Sustains Rapport
Score	Description
5	Consistently empathic, verbally and non-verbally engaged, acknowledges distress effectively. Example: "That sounds incredibly difficult. I’m really sorry you’ve had to go through that." Expected instances: 5+ empathic acknowledgments and consistent engagement throughout.
4	Mostly empathic and engaged; occasional lapses but recovers well. Example: "That must’ve been tough. Thanks for sharing that." Expected instances: 3–4 instances of empathy or engagement, with brief lapses.
3	Variable empathy and engagement; some mechanical responses. Example: "Okay. I understand." Expected instances: 2–3 instances; tone or words may feel rote or limited.
2	Detached or inconsistent empathy; limited emotional acknowledgment. Example: "So that happened. What next?" Expected instances: 1–2 instances of weak or inconsistent engagement.
1	Cold, judgmental, or disengaged throughout. Example: "You shouldn’t feel that way." Expected instances: No empathic responses; responses may be critical or disinterested.

Maintains Control of the Interview
Score	Description
5	Skillfully redirects and organizes conversation while remaining respectful and calm. Example: "That's important—let's come back to that. Right now, I’d like to understand what happened just before this episode began." Expected instances: At least 3 effective redirections, including management of tangents or disorganization.
4	Mostly manages flow well; occasional difficulty with redirection. Example: "We’ll get to that soon, but could you first tell me about your sleep lately?" Expected instances: 2–3 instances; mostly effective redirection.
3	Moderate control; sometimes allows tangents or disorganization. Example: "Okay... let's go back to the part where you mentioned your job." Expected instances: 1–2 attempts at control, not always timely or smooth.
2	Struggles to redirect or structure the conversation effectively. Example: "Wait, what were we talking about?" Expected instances: 0–1 weak redirections, disorganized thread.
1	Interview feels chaotic or uncontrolled; fails to redirect. Example: No redirection efforts made during entire interview. Expected instances: None.

Demonstrates Cultural Sensitivity
Score	Description
5	Fully integrates cultural awareness, shows humility and adapts language/approach respectfully. Example: "Are there any cultural or spiritual beliefs that shape how you see what’s happening?" Expected instances: 2+ proactive references to culture, identity, or beliefs.
4	Culturally aware; mostly sensitive in language and approach. Example: "How does your background affect how you think about this experience?" Expected instances: 1–2 respectful inquiries or acknowledgments.
3	Acknowledges cultural identity when prompted; little proactive adaptation. Example: "Oh, I see—you mentioned your religion. Can you tell me more?" Expected instances: 1 instance, usually in response to patient’s lead.
2	Minimal acknowledgment of culture; potential insensitivity. Example: "That’s not really relevant right now." Expected instances: 0–1 dismissive or neutral responses.
1	Disregards or disrespects cultural background. Example: "That stuff doesn’t matter here." Expected instances: None; may actively alienate.

Ends the Interview Smoothly
Score	Description
5	Ends with clarity, warmth, and professionalism; summarizes and transitions naturally. Example: "Thank you for being so open today. You’ve shared a lot, and I’ll be reviewing this with my team to ensure we support you well moving forward." Expected instances: 1 complete, clear, and supportive ending.
4	Organized ending with a summary; minor awkwardness. Example: "Alright, we’ve talked about your mood, sleep, and stress. We’ll review this and talk more next time." Expected instances: 1 structured wrap-up with minor missing element.
3	Basic closure, limited summary or warmth. Example: "Okay, I think we’re done for today." Expected instances: 1 basic ending lacking warmth or clarity.
2	Abrupt or unclear ending; no summary. Example: "That’s it." Expected instances: No summarization, closure feels rushed.
1	Ends interview abruptly or without any closure. Example: Leaves room or ends video call without saying anything. Expected instances: None.

Category 2: Interview Technique
Information Gathering with Open Exploration
Score	Description
5	Highly attentive, responsive body language, seamless non-distracting note-taking. Example: "Tell me about what that was like for you." Expected instances: 5+ instances of open prompts with sustained attention.
4	Mostly attentive; note-taking mildly noticeable but not disruptive. Example: "Can you describe that more for me?" Expected instances: 3–4 instances of exploratory engagement.
3	Variable listening; note-taking sometimes distracts. Example: "Uh-huh... go on." Expected instances: 2–3 instances, with signs of distraction.
2	Poor engagement; relies heavily on note-taking, limiting connection. Example: "When did that happen?" (without follow-up) Expected instances: 1–2 perfunctory attempts; largely disengaged.
1	Appears uninterested or disengaged; note-taking hinders rapport. Example: No engagement or follow-up questions. Expected instances: None.

Facilitative Questioning Style
Score	Description
5	Questions flow logically, use plain language, foster emotional openness without leading. Example: "What was going through your mind when that happened?" Expected instances: 5+ well-sequenced, non-leading, open or reflective questions.
4	Logical flow; some minor issues with question types or clarity. Example: "Did anything specific happen before that?" Expected instances: 3–4 well-structured questions with 1 minor issue.
3	Mixed questioning style; sometimes confusing or overly clinical. Example: "Can you rate the intensity of your anhedonia?" Expected instances: 2–3 mixed-type questions, some unclear.
2	Rigid or disorganized questions; some leading or stacked questions. Example: "You’re sad because of your mom, right?" Expected instances: 1–2 problematic questions.
1	Disjointed questioning, overly technical, or emotionally blunting. Example: "When did symptom cluster one begin?" Expected instances: None; ineffective throughout.

Pursues Important Information
Score	Description
5	Proactively identifies cues, explores deeply, and clarifies appropriately. Example: "You mentioned you stopped sleeping—what was happening around that time?" Expected instances: 5+ cue-driven follow-ups.
4	Picks up on most cues; pursues clarification with some gaps. Example: "You said you felt off—what do you mean by that?" Expected instances: 3–4 instances of appropriate exploration.
3	Responds to obvious cues; limited exploration. Example: "Okay. Anything else?" Expected instances: 2–3 follow-ups, may miss nuance.
2	Often misses or ignores cues; vague clarification. Example: "Explain that." Expected instances: 1–2 missed or weak attempts.
1	Ignores relevant information; fails to pursue important leads. Example: No follow-up on major disclosures. Expected instances: None.

Maintains Flow
Score	Description
5	Seamlessly manages flow, reframes, and summarizes appropriately; handles distress calmly. Example: "Let’s pause here—you’ve said so much. If I understand correctly..." Expected instances: 4+ moments of summarizing, reframing, or calming redirection.
4	Generally smooth with some minor hiccups; manages emotions well. Example: "You’re doing well—take your time." Expected instances: 3–4 flow-preserving moments.
3	Some awkward transitions; struggles slightly with emotional or complex content. Example: "Okay... let’s talk about something else now." Expected instances: 2–3 attempts, with some clumsiness.
2	Often stuck or disorganized; poor response to emotional content. Example: "I don’t know where to go from here." Expected instances: 1–2 weak attempts.
1	Disruptive flow; avoids or mishandles emotionally charged topics. Example: "Let’s skip that part." Expected instances: None.

Category 3: Interview Content
Identifies the Person

Score	Description
5	Obtains complete demographic information. Example: "Can you tell me your full name and age? What do you do for work? Who do you live with?" Expected instances: At least 5 demographic questions covering name, age, gender, occupation, living situation, and marital status.
4	Mostly complete; minor omissions. Example: "What's your age and occupation? Are you married?" Expected instances: 4 demographic questions covering most key areas.
3	Basic information gathered; some gaps. Example: "Your age?" Expected instances: 3 questions, minimal demographic coverage.
2	Incomplete or disorganized demographic data. Example: "Name?" Expected instances: 1–2 questions, limited coverage.
1	Fails to obtain or document demographic info. Example: [No demographic questions] Expected instances: 0 questions.

Identifies the Presenting Complaints or Problems and Their History

Score	Description
5	Obtains detailed, structured history including timeline, symptoms, impact, premorbid state, stressors, previous episodes, treatments, and responses. Example: "When did you first notice these symptoms? How have they changed over time? What was your mood like before this started? Have you had any stress recently? Did you have similar problems before? What treatments have you tried and did they help?" Expected instances: 5+ detailed queries covering all key domains.
4	Mostly thorough; a few details underexplored. Example: "How long have you been feeling this way? Any stressful events? Have you been treated for this before?" Expected instances: 4 queries covering majority of relevant aspects.
3	Moderate detail; some key areas omitted. Example: "What's wrong?" Expected instances: 3 questions, limited depth.
2	Superficial history; lacks depth in multiple domains. Example: "Tell me about your illness." Expected instances: 1–2 general questions.
1	Fragmented or unclear account of presenting complaint. Example: [No relevant questions] Expected instances: 0 questions.

Screens for Symptoms Relevant to Differential Diagnosis

Score	Description
5	Reviews primary criteria for other diagnoses, substance use, impact, and motivation to change comprehensively. Example: "Have you heard voices or seen things others don’t? Do you ever feel excessively energetic or need less sleep? Do you drink alcohol or use drugs? How does that affect you and those around you? Are you interested in changing your use?" Expected instances: 5+ targeted questions across differential diagnoses and substance use.
4	Screens most areas well; minor omissions. Example: "Do you drink or use drugs? Any panic attacks or unusual moods?" Expected instances: 4 queries covering most relevant comorbidities.
3	Screens obvious comorbidities; others missed. Example: "Do you use alcohol?" Expected instances: 2–3 questions, limited scope.
2	Minimal screening; misses key differential areas. Example: "Any mood problems?" Expected instances: 1 question.
1	Fails to screen or consider differential diagnoses. Example: [No screening questions] Expected instances: 0 questions.

Ensures Safety

Score	Description
5	Completes thorough risk assessment (self-harm, aggression, self-care, competency), reviews medications (dose/response), OTC products, side effects, and allergy status. Example: "Have you thought about hurting yourself? Are you taking any medications or supplements? Any allergies we should know about?" Expected instances: 5+ questions covering full safety domains.
4	Good safety assessment; some areas briefly addressed. Example: "Are you taking any medications? Have you had thoughts of harm?" Expected instances: 4 queries with minor gaps.
3	Basic risk assessment; notable gaps in medical safety. Example: "Any thoughts of harm?" Expected instances: 2–3 questions, incomplete safety review.
2	Superficial or incomplete safety coverage. Example: "Are you okay?" Expected instances: 1 question.
1	No safety assessment performed. Example: [No safety questions] Expected instances: 0 questions.

Identifies Relevant Past History

Score	Description
5	Reviews past medical, psychiatric, family psychiatric, and forensic history comprehensively. Example: "Do you have any history of medical illnesses? Has anyone in your family had mental health problems? Any legal or forensic issues we should know about?" Expected instances: 5+ questions across all domains.
4	Covers most domains; some areas glossed over. Example: "Any medical or psychiatric history? Family history of mental illness?" Expected instances: 4 queries, minor omissions.
3	Covers basics; lacks detail or context. Example: "Any past illnesses?" Expected instances: 3 questions, limited depth.
2	History is patchy or one-dimensional. Example: "Any medical problems?" Expected instances: 1–2 questions.
1	Major omissions in past history. Example: [No questions about past history] Expected instances: 0 questions.

Identifies the Developmental and Psychosocial History

Score	Description
5	Rich history including family dynamics, gestational/perinatal, childhood/adolescent development, occupational/relationship history, abuse history, supports, cultural identity, trauma, spirituality, and explanatory illness model. Example: "Tell me about your family growing up. Any difficulties during childhood or adolescence? What is your current work like? Have you experienced any abuse? What supports do you have now? How do you understand your illness?" Expected instances: 6+ comprehensive questions covering multiple psychosocial domains.
4	Covers most domains with reasonable detail. Example: "How’s your family? Work okay? Any history of trauma or abuse?" Expected instances: 4–5 queries across psychosocial areas.
3	General background collected; several domains minimally explored. Example: "Tell me about your childhood. Do you have any support?" Expected instances: 3 questions, limited scope.
2	Brief or inconsistent psychosocial history. Example: "Any family?" Expected instances: 1–2 questions.
1	Little or no developmental/psychosocial history obtained. Example: [No psychosocial questions] Expected instances: 0 questions.
