Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale (CTRS)
This document outlines a structured framework for assessing therapist competence in Cognitive Behavior Therapy (CBT). 
It is used for training, supervision, and evaluation. Each item is scored on a 0–6 scale:

0 = Poor
2 = Barely Adequate
4 = Good
6 = Excellent

Odd numbers (1, 3, 5) represent intermediate ratings between descriptors. Ratings are based on the therapist’s skill level while accounting for patient difficulty.

Category 1: General Therapeutic Skills

Agenda
Score    Description
6    Therapist worked with patient to set an appropriate agenda with target problems, suitable for the available time. Established priorities and then followed agenda.
4    Therapist worked with patient to set a mutually satisfactory agenda that included specific target problems (e.g., anxiety at work, dissatisfaction with marriage).
2    Therapist set agenda that was vague or incomplete.
0    Therapist did not set agenda.

Feedback
Score    Description
6    Therapist was especially adept at eliciting and responding to verbal and non-verbal feedback throughout the session (e.g., elicited reactions to session, regularly checked for understanding, helped summarize main points at end of session).
4    Therapist asked enough questions to be sure that the patient understood the therapist’s line of reasoning throughout the session and to determine the patient’s reactions. Adjusted behavior when appropriate.
2    Therapist elicited some feedback, but not enough to ensure understanding or satisfaction.
0    Therapist did not ask for feedback.

Understanding
Score    Description
6    Therapist thoroughly understood patient’s “internal reality” and communicated this through verbal and non-verbal empathy. Excellent listening and empathic skills.
4    Therapist generally grasped the patient’s “internal reality” with good listening and empathy.
2    Therapist usually reflected or rephrased explicit statements but missed subtle communication. Limited empathy.
0    Therapist repeatedly failed to understand explicit statements; consistently missed the point. Poor empathy.

Interpersonal Effectiveness
Score    Description
6    Therapist displayed optimal warmth, concern, confidence, genuineness, and professionalism, tailored to patient and session.
4    Therapist displayed satisfactory warmth, concern, confidence, genuineness, and professionalism, with no significant problems.
2    Therapist was not destructive but had noticeable interpersonal problems (e.g., aloof, insincere, impatient).
0    Therapist had poor interpersonal skills (e.g., hostile, demeaning, destructive).

Collaboration
Score    Description
6    Collaboration was excellent; therapist encouraged patient to take active role and function as a “team.”
4    Therapist collaborated, focused on mutually important problem, and established rapport.
2    Therapist attempted collaboration but struggled to define a problem or establish rapport.
0    Therapist did not attempt to collaborate.

Pacing and Efficient Use of Time
Score    Description
6    Therapist used time efficiently, tactfully limiting peripheral discussion and pacing appropriately.
4    Therapist was reasonably successful at using time efficiently, maintaining control over discussion flow and pacing.
2    Therapist had significant problems with pacing (too little structure, too rigid, too slow/fast).
0    Therapist made no attempt to structure time; session seemed aimless.

Category 2: Conceptualization, Strategy, and Technique

Guided Discovery
Score    Description
6    Therapist was especially adept at guided discovery, helping patient generate conclusions through skillful questioning.
4    Therapist generally used guided discovery (evidence examination, considering alternatives, weighing pros/cons).
2    Therapist relied too much on persuasion/debate but remained supportive.
0    Therapist primarily lectured, debated, or cross-examined, putting patient on the defensive.

Focusing on Key Cognitions or Behaviors
Score    Description
6    Therapist skillfully focused on the most relevant cognitions/behaviors, offering strong potential for progress.
4    Therapist focused on relevant cognitions/behaviors but could have chosen more central ones.
2    Therapist attempted focus but targeted irrelevant or secondary issues.
0    Therapist did not attempt to elicit cognitions or behaviors.

Strategy for Change (Evaluate quality of strategy, not outcome)
Score    Description
6    Therapist followed a consistent, promising change strategy using the most appropriate CBT techniques.
4    Therapist used a coherent strategy for change with reasonable promise, incorporating CBT techniques.
2    Therapist selected CBT techniques but overall strategy was vague or unpromising.
0    Therapist did not select CBT techniques.

Application of Cognitive-Behavioral Techniques (Evaluate skill of application)
Score    Description
6    Therapist very skillfully and resourcefully applied CBT techniques.
4    Therapist applied CBT techniques with moderate skill.
2    Therapist applied CBT techniques with significant flaws.
0    Therapist did not apply CBT techniques.

Homework
Score    Description
6    Therapist reviewed prior homework and assigned carefully tailored, detailed new homework relevant to session (e.g., testing hypotheses, experimenting with new behaviors).
4    Therapist reviewed prior homework and assigned standard CBT homework relevant to session, explained sufficiently.
2    Therapist had significant difficulty (e.g., skipped review, unclear instructions, inappropriate homework).
0    Therapist did not attempt to assign homework.

Scoring Summary
Total Score = Sum of all items (0–66 possible).

- Part I: General Therapeutic Skills → Items 1–6
- Part II: Conceptualization, Strategy, and Technique → Items 7–11

Therapists are evaluated on both foundational skills and their ability to apply CBT methods effectively.
